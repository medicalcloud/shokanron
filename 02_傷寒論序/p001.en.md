# §1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The *Shanghan lun* indeed transmits and elaborates the intentions of the great sages, and none of the various schools can rival it. Thus Huangfu Mi of the Jin, in his preface to the *Jiayi zhenjing*, says: "Yi Yin, with the talent of a primordial sage, selected and used Shennong's materia medica to make decoctions. Zhang Zhongjing of the Han expanded the discussion of decoctions into some ten-odd scrolls, and using them was often effective. In recent times the Imperial Physician Wang Shuhe compiled Zhongjing's surviving treatise very carefully, and all of it can be applied." This means Zhongjing based himself on the methods of Yi Yin, and Yi Yin on the classic of Shennong—can one not say it transmits the intentions of the great sages? Zhang Zhongjing has no biography in the *Hanshu*, but appears in the *Mingyi lu*, which says: "A man of Nanyang, named Ji; Zhongjing was his courtesy name. He was recommended as filial and incorrupt, and his office reached Governor of Changsha. He first received the art from Zhang Bozu of the same commandery; people of the time said his insight and application were so refined that he surpassed his teacher." In the treatise he wrote, the words are refined and profound, the methods concise yet detailed, beyond the reach of those of shallow learning and narrow experience. In the more than eight hundred years from Zhongjing to now, only Wang Shuhe was able to learn it; in between, men such as Ge Hong, Tao Jing, Hu Qia, Xu Zhicai, and Sun Simiao were not without talent, but each merely founded his own school and could not cultivate and elucidate it. In the Kaibao era, the military governor Gao Jichong once compiled and presented it, but its text was confused and erroneous and had never been examined and corrected; although successive dynasties stored it in the archives, it lacked collation. Thus among those who treat disease, none in all the realm knew it. The state ordered Confucian officials to collate and correct medical books, and your servant [Sun] Qi was subsequently among those selected. Considering that among the urgent of all diseases none is more urgent than cold damage, we have now first collated and settled Zhang Zhongjing's *Shanghan lun* in ten scrolls: twenty-two chapters in all, with, apart from the signs, three hundred and ninety-seven methods in total, and, removing duplicates, one hundred and twelve formulas established. We now request that it be promulgated.

## Notes

The preface written when the Northern Song Bureau for Revising Medical Texts collated and presented the *Shanghan lun* (the so-called "Song officials' preface"). There is no Cheng Wuji commentary.

- It places the *Shanghan lun* in the orthodox line of the decoction tradition, Shennong → Yi Yin → Zhang Zhongjing, and introduces Zhang Zhongjing's career from the *Mingyi lu*.
- Since there is no biography in the official histories, whether Zhang Zhongjing was Governor of Changsha is debated.
- It states that the edition presented by Gao Jichong in the Kaibao era (968–976) was used as the base for collation, and gives the structure of ten scrolls, twenty-two chapters, 397 methods, and 112 formulas. These "397 methods" and "112 formulas" later became stock phrases for the scope of the *Shanghan lun*.
- "Tao Jing" is understood to refer to Tao Hongjing (said to be a form avoiding the taboo character "hong" in the Song).
