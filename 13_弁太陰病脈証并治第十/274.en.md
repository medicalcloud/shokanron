# Clause 274 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In taiyin wind strike, with vexing pain in the four limbs, when the yang [pulse] is faint and the yin [pulse] rough yet long, it is about to recover.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Taiyin is the spleen, which governs nourishing the four extremities. Vexing pain of the four limbs in taiyin wind strike is wind spreading to the extremities and causing disease. When the exterior evil is slight [the yang pulse] is faint; when the interior tends toward harmony [the yin pulse] is rough yet long. Long is yang. When a yang pulse is seen in yin disease, there is life; when yin obtains yang, it resolves; therefore it is said "about to recover." [The end of the commentary breaks off as "故云欲" in the base; it is translated in the sense of "故云欲愈."]

## Notes

In taiyin wind strike (wind evil reaching the four limbs governed by the spleen), the appearance of a long (yang) pulse is regarded as a sign of recovery of upright qi. The faintness at yang (superficial) is said to show decline of the external evil, and the roughness at yin (deep) insufficiency of the spleen; even if rough, being long is interpreted as the qi recovering.
