# Clause 274 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In Taiyin wind attack, with vexing pain in the four limbs, when the yang [pulse] is feeble and the yin [pulse] hesitant yet long, it is about to recover.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Taiyin is the spleen, which governs nourishing the four extremities. Vexing pain of the four limbs in Taiyin wind attack is wind spreading to the extremities and causing disease. When the exterior pathogen is slight [the yang pulse] is feeble; when the interior tends toward harmony [the yin pulse] is hesitant yet long. Long is yang. When a yang pulse is seen in yin disease, there is life; when yin obtains yang, it resolves; therefore it is said "about to recover." [The end of the commentary breaks off as "故云欲" in the base; it is translated in the sense of "故云欲愈."]

## Notes

In Taiyin wind attack (pathogenic wind reaching the four limbs governed by the spleen), the appearance of a long (yang) pulse is regarded as a sign of recovery of healthy qi. The feebleness at yang (superficial) is said to show decline of the external pathogen, and the hesitancy at yin (deep) insufficiency of the spleen; even if hesitant, being long is interpreted as the qi recovering.
