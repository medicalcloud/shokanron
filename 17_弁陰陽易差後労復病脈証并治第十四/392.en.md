# Clause 392 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The disease of yin-yang exchange in cold damage: when the person's body is heavy, there is shortage of qi, internal tension of the lower abdomen, perhaps pulling into the genitals with spasm, heat surging up into the chest, a heavy head that one does not wish to lift, flowers in the eyes (one version writes "?" for "flowers"), and tension of the knees and shins, Shaokun san (Burnt Underwear Powder) governs it. Formula 1.

Take the crotch of a woman's underwear, near the private parts, and burn it to ash.

Take a square-inch spoonful of the one ingredient above with water, three times a day. Urination becomes free at once, and when the glans swells slightly, this is recovery. For a woman's illness, take a man's underwear, burn it, and take it.

[The character after "one version writes" is an illegible character ("?") in the base. It is generally given as "眵" (eye discharge).]

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

When one who has just recovered from a major illness, with blood and qi not yet restored and residual heat not yet exhausted, forcibly unites yin and yang (has intercourse) and contracts disease, it is called exchange. When a man has just recovered from illness and is not yet back to normal, and a woman has intercourse with him and contracts disease, it is called yang exchange; when a woman has just recovered from illness and is not yet back to normal, and a man has intercourse with her and contracts disease, it is called yin exchange. Because yin and yang stimulate each other and the residual toxin is transmitted between them, it is like an exchange. The person's illness with heavy body and shortage of qi is the true qi being damaged and stirred. Internal tension of the lower abdomen, pulling into the genitals with spasm, and tension of the knees and shins mean yin qi has reached its extreme. Heat surging up into the chest, a heavy head one does not wish to lift, and flowers in the eyes mean the stimulated toxin and the exchanged qi are fuming and steaming upward. Shaokun san is given to conduct the yin qi.

The underwear should be burnt to ash.

## Notes

The clause describes the disease of "yin-yang exchange," in which intercourse soon after recovering from cold damage was believed to transmit the illness to the partner (or cause relapse).

- Shaokun san, taking the ash of the crotch of the opposite sex's underwear, is a formula of strongly magical character.
- From a modern medical viewpoint there is no basis for its efficacy, and it is often read as a historical record.
- Clauses 393–398 that follow deal with relapse after illness (taxation relapse, food relapse) and convalescent care.
