# Clause 395 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When after recovery from a major illness there is water qi from the waist down, Muli Zexie san governs it. Formula 4.

Muli (dry-fried); zexie; shuqi (washed in warm water to remove the fishy smell); tinglizi (dry-fried); shanglugen (dry-fried); haizao (washed to remove the salt); gualougen; equal parts each.

Pound the seven ingredients above separately, sift into powder, process further in a mortar, and take a square-inch spoonful mixed with white drink, three times a day. When urination becomes free, stop the remaining doses.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

After recovery from a major illness, the spleen and stomach qi is vacuous and cannot restrain kidney water; water overflows into the lower burner, so there is swelling from the waist down. The *Jingui Yaolüe* says: "For swelling from the waist down, one should promote urination." Muli Zexie san is given to promote urination and dissipate water.

(Nature and flavor) Muli: salty, neutral. Zexie: salty, cold. Gualougen: bitter, cold. Shuqi: acrid, neutral. Tingli: bitter, cold. Shanglugen: acrid-sour-salty, neutral. Haizao: salty, cold.

The salty flavor drains boldly—the saltiness of muli, zexie, and haizao drains water qi. The *Neijing* says: "When dampness spreads within, level it with bitter, assist with sour and acrid, and drain it with bitter." The sourness, acridity, and bitterness of shuqi, tingli, gualou[gen], and shanglu conduct out the swelling and dampness.

## Notes

For edema of the lower body (water qi) remaining after illness, Muli Zexie san, a collection of strong water-disinhibiting and water-expelling medicinals, is used. Because shanglugen, tinglizi, and others are potent, it is instructed to stop once urination is freed.
