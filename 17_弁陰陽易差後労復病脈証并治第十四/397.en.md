# Clause 397 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When after cold damage has resolved there are vacuity emaciation, shortage of qi, and qi counterflow with a desire to vomit, Zhuye Shigao tang governs it. Formula 6.

Zhuye (bamboo leaves) 2 handfuls; shigao 1 jin; banxia half a sheng (washed); maimendong 1 sheng (remove the core); renshen 2 liang; gancao 2 liang (mix-fried); jingmi half a sheng.

Boil the seven ingredients above in 1 dou of water to obtain 6 sheng, remove the dregs, add the jingmi, boil until the rice is cooked; when the decoction is done, remove the rice, and take 1 sheng warm, three times a day.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

After cold damage has resolved, the fluids are insufficient and there is vacuity emaciation, and residual heat is not yet exhausted. Heat damages qi, so there are shortage of qi and qi counterflow with a desire to vomit. Zhuye Shigao tang is given to regulate the stomach and dissipate heat.

(Nature and flavor) Zhuye: acrid, neutral. Shigao: sweet, cold. Banxia: acrid, warm. Renshen: sweet, warm. Gancao: sweet, neutral. Jingmi: sweet, slightly cold. Maimendong: sweet, neutral.

Acrid and sweet effuse and dissipate, removing heat—the sweetness and acridity of zhuye, shigao, and gancao effuse and dissipate the residual heat. Sweet moderates the spleen and boosts qi—the sweetness of maimendong, renshen, and jingmi supplements insufficiency. Acrid dissipates; counterflow qi needs to be dissipated—the acridity of banxia dissipates counterflow qi.

## Notes

In the recovery period of febrile disease, when residual heat remains and both qi and fluids are damaged (dual vacuity of qi and yin), with emaciation, shortness of breath, and nausea, Zhuye Shigao tang clears residual heat while supplementing qi and yin. It is said to be Baihu jia Renshen tang minus zhimu plus zhuye, maimendong, and banxia, and in later times it is widely used for convalescence after febrile disease.
