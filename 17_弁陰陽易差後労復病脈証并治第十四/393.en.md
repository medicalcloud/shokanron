# Clause 393 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When after recovery from a major illness there is taxation relapse, Zhishi Zhizi Chi tang governs it. Formula 2.

Zhishi 3 pieces (mix-fried); zhizi 14 pieces (broken); fermented soybeans 1 sheng (wrapped in cotton).

Take the three ingredients above with 7 sheng of clear sour rice water; first boil it empty to obtain 4 sheng, add the zhishi and zhizi, boil to obtain 2 sheng, add the soybeans, boil five or six more times, remove the dregs, and take warm in two divided doses; cover to induce a slight sweat. If there is abiding food, add five or six pieces of dahuang the size of game pieces, and taking it cures.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Add five or six pieces of dahuang the size of game pieces. [This phrase appears to be text from after the formula that has mixed into the commentary.] Disease has taxation relapse and food relapse. When, just after recovering from cold damage, with blood and qi not yet settled and residual heat not yet exhausted, one works early and falls ill, it is called taxation relapse. When the disease heat has slightly abated and one forces oneself to eat, the heat has a place to hide, and through the grain qi it lodges and contends, the two yang combine and one falls ill—this is called food relapse. In taxation relapse the heat qi floats upward, so Zhishi Zhizi Chi tang is given to resolve it. In food relapse there is abiding accumulation in the stomach, so dahuang is added to purge it.

(Nature and flavor) Zhishi: bitter, cold. Zhizi: bitter, cold. Fermented soybeans: bitter, cold.

Zhishi Zhizi Chi tang should be an emetic, but here it says "cover to induce a slight sweat" because when the heat gathers above, bitterness causes vomiting, and when the heat is scattered in the exterior, bitterness effuses it. The *Neijing* says: "When fire prevails, effuse it with bitter." This is what is meant.

## Notes

For "taxation relapse," in which heat returns from overexertion after illness, a formula adding zhishi to Zhizi Chi tang removes heat and qi stagnation in the chest.

- When there is abiding food ("food relapse" from overeating), dahuang is added to purge.
- Clear sour rice water is interpreted as a sour water made by cooking and fermenting rice.
