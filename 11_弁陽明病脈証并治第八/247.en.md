# Clause 247 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The fuyang pulse is floating and rough. Floating means the stomach qi is strong; rough means urination is frequent. When floating and rough contend, the stool becomes hard; the spleen is restrained, and Maziren wan (Hemp Seed Pill) governs it. Formula 31.

Maziren (hemp seed) 2 sheng; shaoyao half a jin; zhishi half a jin (mix-fried); dahuang 1 jin (remove the bark); houpo 1 chi (mix-fried, remove the bark); xingren 1 sheng (remove the skins and tips, dry-fry, and make separately into a paste).

Mix the six ingredients above with honey into pills the size of firmiana seeds; take ten pills with a drink, three times a day, gradually increasing until there is an effect.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Fuyang is the pulse for examining the spleen and stomach. Floating is yang, showing that the stomach qi is strong; rough is yin, showing that the spleen is restrained (*yue*). *Yue* means the *yue* of frugality and also the *yue* of binding. The *Neijing* says: "Drink enters the stomach, overflows with essential qi, and is conveyed upward to the spleen; the spleen qi spreads the essence, which returns upward to the lung; [the lung] regulates the waterways and conveys downward to the bladder; the water essence spreads in four directions and the five channels move together." This means the spleen governs moving the fluids for the stomach. Here the stomach is strong and the spleen weak; the fluids are bound and cannot spread in four directions but are conveyed only to the bladder, so urination is frequent and the stool difficult. Piyue wan is given to free the intestines and moisten dryness.

(Nature and flavor) Maziren: sweet, neutral. Shaoyao: sour, neutral. Zhishi: bitter, cold. Dahuang: bitter, cold. Houpo: bitter, cold. Xingren: sweet, warm.

The *Neijing* says: "The spleen desires moderation; quickly eat sweet to moderate it." The sweetness of maziren and xingren moderates the spleen and moistens dryness. When fluids are insufficient, contract them with sour—the sourness of shaoyao astringes the fluids. When the intestines are dry and the stomach strong, discharge with bitter—the bitterness of zhishi, houpo, and dahuang purges dry binding and discharges the strength of the stomach.

## Notes

The clause on Maziren wan (Piyue wan), used to treat the "spleen restraint" (taiyang yangming) of Clause 179.

- It is explained that with heat in the stomach, the spleen fails to move the fluids and sends them only to the bladder, so urination is copious and the stool hard.
- It is Xiao Chengqi tang plus maziren, xingren, and shaoyao made into honey pills, moistening while gently freeing the bowels; it instructs starting with small doses and gradually increasing. In later times it has been widely applied to habitual constipation in the elderly and weak.
