# Clause 191 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In Yangming disease, one with cold-strike cannot eat, has inhibited urination, and sweats continuously from the hands and feet; this is about to form a solid conglomeration (*gujia*), and the stool will certainly be hard at first and sloppy afterward. The reason is that the stomach is cold and water and grain are not separated.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Inability to eat in Yangming cold-strike means the cold does not digest grain. Inhibited urination means the fluids are not transformed. Yangming disease by rule has much sweating, all over the body; here there is continuous sweating from the hands and feet with no sweating on the body—Yangming cold-strike. A solid conglomeration is a binding accumulation of cold qi. When the cold in the stomach is severe and tends to remain and bind into a solid conglomeration, the fluids cannot flow, and the stool will certainly be hard. If there were sweating with inhibited urination, it would be excess. Here urination is inhibited and water and grain are not separated, so although the stool is hard at first, it will certainly be sloppy afterward.

## Notes

A clause on the cold-strike (stomach cold) pattern of Yangming disease. Sweating only from the hands and feet differs from Yangming excess heat (sweating all over); the stomach is cold and cannot separate water and grain, so the stool is "hard at first and sloppy afterward." It is regarded as a caution not to purge it mistakenly as excess heat.
