# Clause 185 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Originally, when Taiyang first contracted the disease, sweating was induced, but although the sweat came out at first it was insufficient, and consequently [the disease] shifted to belong to the Yangming. In cold damage with fever, no sweating, and retching with inability to eat, when, on the contrary, there is continuous sweating, it has shifted to belong to the Yangming.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

When cold damage is transmitted through the meridians, the first day is Taiyang and the second Yangming. Here the Taiyang has been transmitted through the meridians; hence "shifted to belong to the Yangming."

Fever, no sweating, and retching with inability to eat in cold damage mean the Taiyang has contracted the disease. If, on the contrary, there is continuous sweating, the Taiyang pathogen has shifted to belong to the Yangming. The Classic says, "In Yangming disease, by rule, there is much sweating."

## Notes

A clause giving two routes by which Taiyang disease shifts into Yangming: when sweating was insufficient and the remaining pathogen entered inward, and when sweating begins in the natural course (continuous sweating). The onset of sweating is taken as a sign of shift into Yangming.
