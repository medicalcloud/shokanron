# Clause 210 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Repletion causes delirious speech (*zhanyu*); vacuity causes muttering (*zhengsheng*). Muttering is repetitive speech. Fixed stare with delirious speech: when there are panting and fullness, [the patient] dies; when there is diarrhea, he also dies.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The *Neijing* says: "When evil qi is exuberant, there is repletion; when essential qi is robbed, there is vacuity." Delirious speech arises from exuberant evil qi clouding the spirit and consciousness; muttering arises from robbed essential qi so that the voice is incomplete. Delirious speech is speech without order; muttering (*zhengsheng*, "the sound of Zheng") is improper tone. The *Analects* says, "I hate the music of Zheng disturbing the classical music," and also, "Banish the music of Zheng and keep away from flatterers; the music of Zheng is licentious and flatterers are dangerous"—speaking of the improper sound of Zheng. Now, one whose voice changes because of qi vacuity just after recovering from illness is what is called repetitive speech; a repeated voice is also a changed voice.

A fixed stare with delirious speech means the evil prevails. Panting and fullness mean the qi deserts upward; diarrhea means the qi deserts downward; both portend death.

## Notes

This clause distinguishes two kinds of disordered speech—"delirious speech" (repletion: disordered speech due to evil heat) and "muttering" (vacuity: weak repetition due to declining essential qi)—and states that delirious speech with a fixed stare plus panting and fullness or diarrhea is desertion of the upright qi with poor prognosis.
