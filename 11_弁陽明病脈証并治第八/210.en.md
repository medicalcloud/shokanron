# Clause 210 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Excess causes delirium (*zhanyu*); deficiency causes muttering (*zhengsheng*). Muttering is repetitive speech. Fixed stare with delirium: when there are dyspnea and fullness, [the patient] dies; when there is diarrhea, he also dies.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The *Neijing* says: "When pathogenic qi is exuberant, there is excess; when essential qi is robbed, there is deficiency." Delirium arises from exuberant pathogenic qi clouding the spirit and consciousness; muttering arises from robbed essential qi so that the voice is incomplete. Delirium is speech without order; muttering (*zhengsheng*, "the sound of Zheng") is improper tone. The *Analects* says, "I hate the music of Zheng disturbing the classical music," and also, "Banish the music of Zheng and keep away from flatterers; the music of Zheng is licentious and flatterers are dangerous"—speaking of the improper sound of Zheng. Now, one whose voice changes because of qi deficiency just after recovering from illness is what is called repetitive speech; a repeated voice is also a changed voice.

A fixed stare with delirium means the pathogen prevails. Dyspnea and fullness mean the qi deserts upward; diarrhea means the qi deserts downward; both portend death.

## Notes

This clause distinguishes two kinds of disordered speech—"delirium" (excess: disordered speech due to pathogenic heat) and "muttering" (deficiency: weak repetition due to declining essential qi)—and states that delirium with a fixed stare plus dyspnea and fullness or diarrhea is desertion of the healthy qi with poor prognosis.
