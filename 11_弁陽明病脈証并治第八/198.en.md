# Clause 198 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In yangming disease (one version says "winter yangming"), when there is only dizziness without aversion to cold, one can therefore eat and coughs, and the throat will certainly be sore; if there is no cough, the throat is not sore.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Yangming disease without heaviness or pain of the body, with only dizziness and no aversion to cold, is yangming wind-strike, with wind qi attacking within. The Classic says, "In yangming disease, if one can eat, it is called wind-strike." When wind evil attacks the stomach and the stomach qi counterflows upward, there is cough. The throat gate is the connection of the stomach; when the cough is severe, it damages the throat, so the throat will certainly be sore. If the stomach qi does not counterflow, there is no cough, and the throat does not hurt either.

## Notes

This describes yangming wind-strike (stomach heat) in which heat counterflows upward causing cough and sore throat, contrasted with the cold-strike of Clause 197 (headache). In the Daizhige edition the note "one version says winter yangming" is placed at the end of the clause (see the textual notes).
