# Clause 217 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

One who sweats (one version writes "lies down" instead of "sweats") and has delirium has dry stool in the stomach; this is wind. When it must be purged, it may be purged only after the meridian [period] has passed. If it is purged too early, the speech will certainly be deranged, because the exterior is deficient and the interior excess. Purging it cures it; Da Chengqi tang (Daijokito) is appropriate. 8. Use the second formula above. (One version says Da Chaihu tang [Daisaikoto].)

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Dry stool in the stomach causes delirium. Sweating means the exterior has not yet ended; hence "this is wind." Dry stool in the stomach should be purged, but while the exterior is not yet harmonized, it must not yet be purged; only after the Taiyang meridian [period] has passed and there is no exterior pattern may it be purged. If purged too early, although the dry stool is removed, the exterior pathogen takes advantage of the deficiency to sink into the interior again, the exterior deficient and the interior excess; the stomach is deficient and the heat severe, and the speech will certainly be deranged. Giving Da Chengqi tang to purge the pathogenic heat in the stomach will stop it.

## Notes

One who sweats with delirium has dry stool, but while "wind" (exterior pathogen) remains one must not purge; only after the exterior pattern is gone is Da Chengqi tang used. It warns that purging too early instead worsens the derangement of speech. An instance of the principle "exterior first, then interior."
