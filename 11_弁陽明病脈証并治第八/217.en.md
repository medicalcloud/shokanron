# Clause 217 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

One who sweats (one version writes "lies down" instead of "sweats") and has delirious speech has dry stool in the stomach; this is wind. When it must be purged, it may be purged only after the channel [period] has passed. If it is purged too early, the speech will certainly be deranged, because the exterior is vacuous and the interior replete. Purging it cures it; Da Chengqi tang is appropriate. 8. Use the second formula above. (One version says Da Chaihu tang.)

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Dry stool in the stomach causes delirious speech. Sweating means the exterior has not yet ended; hence "this is wind." Dry stool in the stomach should be purged, but while the exterior is not yet harmonized, it must not yet be purged; only after the taiyang channel [period] has passed and there is no exterior pattern may it be purged. If purged too early, although the dry stool is removed, the exterior evil takes advantage of the vacuity to sink into the interior again, the exterior vacuous and the interior replete; the stomach is vacuous and the heat severe, and the speech will certainly be deranged. Giving Da Chengqi tang to purge the evil heat in the stomach will stop it.

## Notes

One who sweats with delirious speech has dry stool, but while "wind" (exterior evil) remains one must not purge; only after the exterior pattern is gone is Da Chengqi tang used. It warns that purging too early instead worsens the derangement of speech. An instance of the principle "exterior first, then interior."
