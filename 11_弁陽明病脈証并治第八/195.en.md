# Clause 195 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In yangming disease with a slow pulse, it is hard to eat one's fill; when full, there are slight vexation and dizziness, and urination will certainly be difficult; this is about to become grain jaundice (*gudan*). Although it is purged, the abdominal fullness remains as before. The reason is that the pulse is slow.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

A slow pulse in yangming disease means the evil has just entered the interior and the heat has not yet become repletion. Food enters the yin and grows qi in the yang. With heat in the stomach, it is hard to eat one's fill; slight vexation and dizziness when full mean grain qi and heat qi contend. The two heats combine and consume the fluids, so urination will certainly be difficult. When it is free, there cannot be yellowing, because the heat can drain. When urination is inhibited, the heat cannot drain, and the body will certainly turn yellow. *Dan* means yellow; since it arises from the heat of grain qi, it is called grain jaundice. When the heat is replete, purging cures it, but a slow pulse means the heat qi is not yet replete, so even if purged the abdominal fullness does not diminish. The Classic says, "When the pulse is slow, one must not yet attack."

## Notes

One with a slow pulse who has vexation, dizziness, and difficult urination after eating has dampness and heat trapped in the middle burner and is about to develop jaundice (grain jaundice); purging will not relieve the abdominal fullness. The slow pulse is often interpreted as indicating cold-damp (Cheng Wuji says the heat is not yet replete), and the clause is taken as an example to be distinguished from yangming replete heat.
