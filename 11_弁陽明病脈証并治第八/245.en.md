# Clause 245 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the yang pulse is faint and sweating is slight, there is spontaneous harmony (one version writes "as if"); when sweating is profuse, it is excessive. When the yang pulse is replete and sweating is therefore induced and profuse, it is also excessive. Excess means the yang is cut off in the interior, the fluids are lost, and the stool consequently becomes hard.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

A faint yang pulse means the evil qi is slight; slight sweating is appropriate, so there is spontaneous harmony. Profuse sweating damages the upright qi instead; this is excessive sweating.

A replete yang pulse means the exterior heat is severe. When sweating is therefore induced, the heat takes advantage of the vacuity to steam the fluids and drain them outward, causing excessive sweating. Profuse sweating loses the yang; the yang is cut off in the interior, the intestines and stomach become dry, and the stool consequently becomes hard.

## Notes

The clause judges, together with the pulse, whether sweating is appropriate or excessive, and states that excessive sweating loses the fluids and hardens the stool (shift to yangming). "The yang is cut off in the interior" is often interpreted not as yang qi being exhausted but as yang (heat) being isolated in the interior through loss of fluids.
