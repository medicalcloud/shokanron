# Clause 229 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In Yangming disease with tidal fever, sloppy stool, spontaneously normal urination, and chest and rib-side fullness that does not go away, give Xiao Chaihu tang (Shosaikoto). Formula 16.

Chaihu (saiko) half a jin; huangqin (ogon) 3 liang; renshen (ninjin) 3 liang; banxia (hange) half a sheng (washed); gancao (kanzo) 3 liang (mix-fried); shengjiang (shokyo) 3 liang (sliced); dazao (taiso) 12 pieces (split).

Of the seven ingredients above, boil with 1 dou 2 sheng of water down to 6 sheng, remove the dregs, decoct again down to 3 sheng, and take 1 sheng warm, three times a day.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

[Xiao Chaihu tang governs it.] Tidal fever in Yangming disease is stomach excess; the stool should be hard and urination frequent. Here the stool is sloppy and urination spontaneously normal, meaning the stomach heat is not yet excess and water and grain are not separated. With sloppy stool, the qi should descend and the chest and rib-side fullness go away; that it does not go away means the pathogenic qi is still between half exterior and half interior. Xiao Chaihu tang is given to remove the pathogen of exterior and interior.

## Notes

Even with tidal fever, if the stool is sloppy and chest and rib-side fullness remains, it is not Yangming fu organ excess but remaining Shaoyang pathogen, and Xiao Chaihu tang is given instead of purging. It is taken as an example of recognizing a Shaoyang pattern within the Yangming chapter.
