# Clause 192 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In Yangming disease, at first there is a desire to eat, urination is, on the contrary, inhibited, and the stool is spontaneously normal; the person's joints ache, there is a feather-warm, fever-like state, suddenly there is mania, and then with continuous sweating it resolves: this is water not overcoming the grain qi, coming out together with the sweat; if the pulse is tight, [the patient] recovers.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

In yang[ming] disease, when visiting heat is first transmitted into the stomach, the hot stomach digests grain and there is a desire to eat. When the heat of Yangming disease becomes excess, urination should be frequent and the stool hard. Here urination is inhibited and the stool spontaneously normal, meaning the heat qi is diffuse and has not become excess. With a desire to eat, there is much grain in the stomach. The *Neijing* says: "Food enters the yin and grows qi in the yang." With much grain, yang qi prevails; when heat consumes the fluids, water becomes scant. The Classic says: "Water enters the meridians, and then blood is formed." When water is scant, yin blood weakens. The *Jingui yaolüe* says: "When yin qi does not flow, the bones ache." The person's joint pain means insufficient yin qi. When heat is severe in the exterior there is feather-warm fever; when severe in the interior there is steaming fever. Here the heat qi is diffuse and not fixed in exterior or interior, hence "a feather-warm, fever-like state." *Yan* means suddenly. Sudden mania means yin does not overcome yang. The *Neijing* says: "When yin does not overcome its yang, the pulse flows pressing and fast, and when they merge there is mania." When Yangming heat accumulates into excess, purging cures it; when heat qi is diffuse and does not become excess, one must wait for sweating to cure it—hence "with continuous sweating it resolves." Equality of water and grain means the qi of yin and yang is balanced; water not overcoming grain qi means yin not overcoming yang. Sweating makes the yang qi decline; a tight pulse means yin qi arises. When the qi of yin and yang is balanced and neither prevails, [the patient] recovers—hence "together with the sweat."

## Notes

A difficult clause describing how Yangming disease with diffuse heat that does not become excess cures spontaneously with sweating. The interpretation of "water not overcoming grain qi" is debated; Cheng Wuji explains it by the balance of yin and yang. Sudden mania followed by sweating and recovery is regarded as a reaction akin to "shiver-sweating," when the healthy qi overcomes the pathogen.
