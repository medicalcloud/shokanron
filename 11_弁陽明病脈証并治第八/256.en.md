# Clause 256 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In combined disease of yangming and shaoyang, there will certainly be diarrhea. When the pulse is not defeated, it is favorable; when defeated, it is a loss. Mutual restraint and injury is called defeat. When the pulse is slippery and rapid, there is abiding food; it should be purged. Da Chengqi tang is appropriate. 40. Use the second formula above.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Yangming is earth and shaoyang is wood. When the two channels have combined disease and their qi is not in harmony, there will certainly be diarrhea. When the shaoyang pulse does not overcome the yangming, [the yangming] is not defeated; they do not restrain each other, which is favorable. If the shaoyang pulse overcomes and the yangming pulse is defeated, it is the mutual restraint of "ghost and thief," and the upright qi is lost. The *Maijing* says: "A slippery pulse is disease of food," and also: "Slippery and rapid means the stomach qi is replete." In diarrhea the pulse should be faint with reversal cold, [but] the pulse is slippery and rapid, so one knows there is abiding food in the stomach; Da Chengqi tang is given to purge and remove it. [The phrase "下利者脈当微厥冷脈滑数" is translated as in the base; it is understood in the sense of "the pulse should be faint with reversal, but now it is slippery and rapid."]

## Notes

For diarrhea from combined yangming–shaoyang disease, the prognosis is judged by the five-phase restraint cycle (wood restrains earth), and purgation is permitted when the pulse is slippery and rapid with abiding food.

- "Defeat" is generally interpreted as the shaoyang (wood) pulse overcoming the yangming (earth) pulse, i.e. the adverse state of earth being restrained by wood.
- It is cited as an example of the idea that even with diarrhea, if there is repletion from abiding food with a slippery rapid pulse, purgation removes the cause ("treating flow by promoting flow").
