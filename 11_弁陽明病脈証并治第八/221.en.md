# Clause 221 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Yangming disease with a floating and tight pulse, dry throat, bitter taste in the mouth, abdominal fullness with dyspnea, fever with sweating, no aversion to cold but, on the contrary, aversion to heat, and heaviness of the body. If sweating is induced, there will be agitation, confusion of the heart (*kui* is the reverse spelling of *gong-dui*), and, on the contrary, delirium. If warm needling is added, there will certainly be fearfulness, restlessness, and inability to sleep. If it is purged, the stomach becomes empty, visiting qi stirs the diaphragm, there is anguish in the heart; when there is a coating on the tongue, Zhizi Chi tang governs it. Formula 11.

Plump zhizi 14 fruits (split); xiangchi 4 ge (wrapped in cotton).

Of the two ingredients above, with 4 sheng of water boil the zhizi down to 2.5 sheng, remove the dregs, add the chi, boil again down to 1.5 sheng, remove the dregs, divide into two doses, and give one dose warm. If satisfactory vomiting occurs, stop the later dose.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

A floating pulse with fever means the pathogen is in the exterior. Dry throat and bitter taste mean the heat is in the meridian. A tight pulse, abdominal fullness with dyspnea, sweating without aversion to cold but with aversion to heat, and heaviness of the body mean the pathogen is in the interior. Since there is pathogen in both exterior and interior, both should be resolved. If sweating is induced to attack the exterior, although the exterior heat is removed, the internal heat becomes even more severe, hence dryness, confusion of the heart, and delirium; *kuikui* means confusion of the heart. The Classic says, "When red-hot needling is applied to one whose ying-nutrient qi is feeble, the blood does not move, and there are further fever, agitation, and vexation." Here there is heat in exterior and interior; adding red-hot needling damages and stirs the yin qi, hence fearfulness, vexation and dryness, and inability to sleep. If purged, although the interior heat goes, the stomach becomes empty, and the visiting pathogenic qi of the exterior takes advantage of the deficiency to sink into the upper jiao, vexing and stirring the diaphragm, causing anguish in the heart without relief. A yellow tongue coating means heat qi lodges in the stomach; a white coating shows heat qi lodges in the chest. Zhizi Chi tang is given to make [the patient] vomit the pathogen in the chest.

## Notes

A clause listing the deteriorations from mistakenly applying sweating, warm needling, or purgation to a Yangming heat pattern, and using Zhizi Chi tang when after purgation heat is trapped in the chest with anguish. It is taken as an example that even in Yangming disease, if heat remains in the chest (upper jiao), it is cleared with Zhizi Chi tang as for deficiency vexation in the Taiyang chapter (Clause 76). Together with Clauses 222 (Baihu jia Renshen tang) and 223 (Zhuling tang), it is read as choosing formulas by the location of Yangming heat (upper, middle, lower jiao).
