# Clause 262 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In cold damage, when stagnant heat is in the interior, the body will certainly be yellow. Mahuang Lianyao Chixiaodou tang governs it. Formula 44.

Mahuang 2 liang (remove the nodes); lianyao 2 liang (it is the root of forsythia); xingren 40 pieces (remove the skins and tips); chixiaodou 1 sheng; dazao 12 pieces (broken); fresh catalpa root bark (cut) 1 sheng; shengjiang 2 liang (cut); gancao 2 liang (mix-fried).

Take the eight ingredients above and, with 1 dou of puddle water, first boil the mahuang until it boils twice, remove the foam on top, add the other medicinals, boil to obtain 3 sheng, remove the dregs, divide into three warm portions, and finish taking them within half a day.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

When dampness and heat interact, many people suffer from *dan*; *dan* is yellowing. Cold damage means cold-damp in the exterior; yellowing means stagnant heat in the interior. Mahuang Lianyao Chixiaodou tang is given to remove heat and dissipate dampness.

(Nature and flavor) Mahuang: sweet, warm. Chixiaodou: sweet, neutral. Lianyao: bitter, cold. Xingren: sweet, warm. Dazao: sweet, warm. Fresh catalpa root bark: bitter, cold. Shengjiang: acrid, warm. Gancao: sweet, neutral.

The *Neijing* says: "When dampness is severe above with heat, treat with sweet-warm, assist with sweet-neutral, and take sweating as the basis." This is exactly what is meant. Also, puddle water is used for decoction because its flavor is thin and so it does not assist the damp qi.

## Notes

For jaundice with cold-damp in the exterior (an exterior pattern) and stagnant heat in the interior, mahuang induces sweating while lianyao, catalpa root bark, and chixiaodou remove heat and dampness.

- "Lianyao" is glossed in the interlinear note as the root of forsythia, but forsythia fruit (lianqiao) is now often substituted.
- Catalpa root bark is hard to obtain, and later practice sometimes substitutes mulberry root bark and the like.
- Puddle water is understood as water collected in puddles after rain.
