# Clause 257 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the patient has no exterior or interior pattern and has had fever for seven or eight days, even if the pulse is floating and rapid, it may be purged. Suppose it has already been purged and the rapid pulse does not resolve; with combined heat, grain is dispersed and one is prone to hunger; when by the sixth or seventh day there is no bowel movement, there is static blood; Didang tang is appropriate. 41. Use the twenty-fourth formula above.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Seven or eight days is when the evil enters the bowel. When the patient has no exterior or interior pattern but only fever, even with a floating rapid pulse Da Chengqi tang may still be given to purge it. Floating means heat lodged in the qi; rapid means heat lodged in the blood. When purged and the evil heat departs, both the floating and rapid pulses should resolve. If after purgation the rapid pulse goes and the pulse is only floating, the heat in the nutritive blood has merged into the defensive qi; it should be a pattern in which the evil qi alone remains in the heart causing hunger, the evil heat does not disperse grain, and there are tidal fever and thirst. Here after purgation the floating pulse goes but the rapid one does not resolve, so the heat in the defensive qi has combined with the nutritive blood. The combined heat qi presses the blood to move downward; the stomach is vacuous and carries heat, dispersing grain so that one is prone to hunger. The blood reaches the lower burner; if the stool is free, discharging the blood cures it. If there is no bowel movement for six or seven days, the blood cannot move and accumulates below as static blood; Didang tang is given to purge and remove it.

## Notes

For fever without clear exterior or interior patterns where the rapid pulse persists after purgation, with increased appetite and no bowel movement, the heat is regarded as having entered the blood aspect and formed static blood, and Didang tang is used.

- It is often read together with the next clause (258), contrasted as: in the same situation, if diarrhea does not stop there will be stool with pus and blood.
- It uses the same Didang tang as the blood amassment patterns in the taiyang chapter (Clauses 124–126).
