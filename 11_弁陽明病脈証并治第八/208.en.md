# Clause 208 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In yangming disease with a slow pulse, when despite sweating there is no aversion to cold, the body will certainly be heavy, with shortness of breath, abdominal fullness, and panting; when there is tidal fever, the exterior is about to resolve and the interior may be attacked. When the hands and feet sweat continuously, the stool is already hard; Da Chengqi tang (Major Qi-Coordinating Decoction) governs it. If there is much sweating with slight fever and aversion to cold, the exterior has not yet resolved (one method gives Guizhi tang); if the fever is not tidal, Chengqi tang must not yet be given. If the abdomen is greatly full and blocked, Xiao Chengqi tang (Minor Qi-Coordinating Decoction) may be given to harmonize the stomach qi slightly; do not let it reach the point of heavy purgation. Da Chengqi tang formula. 2.

Dahuang 4 liang (washed in wine); houpo half a jin (mix-fried, remove the bark); zhishi 5 pieces (mix-fried); mangxiao 3 ge.

Of the four ingredients above, with 1 dou of water first boil two of them down to 5 sheng, remove the dregs, add the dahuang, boil again down to 2 sheng, remove the dregs, add the mangxiao, put on a gentle fire again and boil once or twice, and take warm in two doses. Once purgation is obtained, do not take the rest.

Xiao Chengqi tang
Dahuang 4 liang; houpo 2 liang (mix-fried, remove the bark); zhishi 3 pieces (large ones, mix-fried).
Of the three ingredients above, boil with 4 sheng of water down to 1 sheng 2 ge, remove the dregs, and take warm in two doses. After the first dose there should be a bowel movement; if not, drink all of it; if there is a bowel movement, do not take [the rest].

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

In yangming disease with a slow pulse, if there are much sweating and slight fever with aversion to cold, the exterior has not yet resolved. If the pulse is slow and, despite sweating, there is no aversion to cold, the exterior pattern has ended. Heaviness of the body, shortness of breath, abdominal fullness with panting, and tidal fever mean the heat has entered the bowel. The four limbs are the root of all yang. When the fluids are sufficient and steamed by heat, the whole body sweats; when the fluids are insufficient and steamed by heat, only the hands and feet sweat continuously, so one knows the stool is already hard. Da Chengqi tang is given to purge the stomach heat. The Classic says, "Tidal fever is repletion." When the fever is not tidal, the heat has not yet become repletion, so Da Chengqi tang must not be given at once. Even with the urgency of great abdominal fullness and blockage, Da Chengqi tang must not be given; Xiao Chengqi tang is given to harmonize the stomach qi slightly.

(Nature and flavor) Dahuang: bitter, cold. Houpo: bitter, warm. Zhishi: bitter, cold. Mangxiao: salty, cold.

The *Neijing* says: "Where dryness excess prevails, purge it with bitter"—the bitterness of dahuang and zhishi moistens dryness and removes heat. Also: "When dryness runs to excess within, treat with bitter and warm"—the bitterness of houpo purges bound dryness. Also: "Where heat excess prevails, treat with salty and cold"—the saltiness of mangxiao attacks accumulated heat.

For great heat bound into repletion, Da Chengqi tang is given; for slight heat slightly bound, Xiao Chengqi tang. Because the heat is not very severe, mangxiao is removed from Da Chengqi tang; and because the binding is not hard, houpo and zhishi are reduced.

## Notes

One of the central clauses of the yangming chapter, stating the indications for Da Chengqi tang in yangming bowel repletion and the choice between Da and Xiao Chengqi tang.

- No aversion to cold (no exterior pattern), tidal fever, and continuous sweating from the hands and feet are taken as signs that the stool is hard and bound (dry stool).
- While an exterior pattern remains or there is no tidal fever, Da Chengqi tang is not used; even with strong abdominal fullness, Xiao Chengqi tang lightly harmonizes the stomach qi.
- Da Chengqi tang (dahuang, mangxiao, houpo, zhishi) is regarded as a drastic purgative and Xiao Chengqi tang (without mangxiao, with less houpo and zhishi) as a milder one; both carry instructions to stop once purgation occurs.
