# §6 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the stirring qi is on the right, sweating must not be induced. If sweating is induced, there will be nosebleed and thirst, the heart will suffer vexation, and on drinking, water will be vomited at once.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Stirring qi is thumping movement of qi. "On the right" means to the right of the navel. The *Nanjing* says: "The internal sign of the lung is stirring qi to the right of the navel, firm or painful on pressure." The lung qi is not governing, the upright qi is vacuous within, and the qi stirs to the right of the navel. Sweating stirs the lung qi. The lung governs qi and opens at the nose; when qi is vacuous it cannot guard the blood, the blood overflows and moves recklessly, and following the qi it comes out of the nose as nosebleed. With loss of fluids and a dry stomach, there are vexation and thirst, and the heart suffers vexation. The lung dislikes cold; drinking cold damages the lung, so on drinking, water is vomited at once.

## Notes

Paragraphs 6–9 are a group that judges vacuity of the lung, liver, heart, or kidney by the position (right, left, above, below) of a pulsation felt around the navel (stirring qi), and prohibit sweating. They are based on the internal signs of each viscus (stirring qi around the navel) in the Sixteenth Difficulty of the *Nanjing*. This paragraph describes stirring qi to the right of the navel (lung).
