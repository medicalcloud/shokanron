# §12 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The pulse is soft and weak: weak, on the contrary, at the guan; soft, on the contrary, at the top; wiry, on the contrary, above; feeble, on the contrary, below. Wiry means yang is in motion; feeble means yin cold. Above is in excess and below deficient, and one wishes to be warm. Feeble and wiry mean deficiency; sweating must not be induced. If sweating is induced, there will be cold shuddering from which one cannot recover by oneself.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Wiry above means wind damages qi; when wind prevails, yang is set in motion. Feeble below means cold damages blood; when blood is damaged, the interior becomes yin-cold. The outer qi boiling and depressed is excess above; yin cold in the interior is deficiency below. With exterior heat and interior cold, one wishes to be warm. If, on the contrary, sweating is induced, yang collapses and only yin remains, so there is cold shuddering from which one cannot recover by oneself.

## Notes

In a form similar to paragraph 5, the clause states that sweating one with excess above and deficiency below—wiry above (exterior excess from wind) and feeble below (interior cold)—loses yang and causes cold shuddering.
