# §12 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The pulse is soft and weak: weak, on the contrary, at the guan; soft, on the contrary, at the top; wiry, on the contrary, above; faint, on the contrary, below. Wiry means yang is in motion; faint means yin cold. Above is replete and below vacuous, and one wishes to be warm. Faint and wiry mean vacuity; sweating must not be induced. If sweating is induced, there will be cold shuddering from which one cannot recover by oneself.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Wiry above means wind damages qi; when wind prevails, yang is set in motion. Faint below means cold damages blood; when blood is damaged, the interior becomes yin-cold. The outer qi boiling and depressed is repletion above; yin cold in the interior is vacuity below. With exterior heat and interior cold, one wishes to be warm. If, on the contrary, sweating is induced, yang collapses and only yin remains, so there is cold shuddering from which one cannot recover by oneself.

## Notes

In a form similar to paragraph 5, the clause states that sweating one with repletion above and vacuity below—wiry above (exterior repletion from wind) and faint below (interior cold)—loses yang and causes cold shuddering.
