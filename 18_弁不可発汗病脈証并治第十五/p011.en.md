# §11 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When any pulse shows rapid, stirred, faint, or weak, sweating must not be induced. If sweating is induced, there will be difficult defecation, dryness in the abdomen (one version says "difficult urination and dryness in the bladder"), and agitation and vexation of the stomach. Their forms resemble each other, but their roots have different origins.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

A stirred rapid pulse means heat in the exterior; a faint weak pulse means heat in the interior. When sweating loses the fluids, the heat qi becomes more severe and the stomach dries, so there are difficult defecation, dryness in the abdomen, and dryness and vexation of the stomach. Although the roots differ as exterior and interior, after adverse treatment the transmission of heat is the same; therefore the forms of disease resemble each other.

## Notes

The clause states that sweating those with rapid or stirred (exterior heat) or faint or weak (interior vacuity) pulses causes loss of fluids with constipation and vexation in either case; the signs resemble each other but the causes differ.
