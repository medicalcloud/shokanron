# §7 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the stirring qi is on the left, sweating must not be induced. If sweating is induced, there will be dizziness of the head, unceasing sweating, and twitching of the sinews and jerking of the flesh.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The *Nanjing* says: "The internal sign of the liver is stirring qi to the left of the navel, firm or painful on pressure." The liver qi is not governing, the upright qi is vacuous within, and the qi stirs to the left of the navel. The liver is the master of yin. When sweating is induced and does not stop, yang collapses and the exterior is vacuous, so there are dizziness and twitching of the sinews and jerking of the flesh. The *Zhenjing* says: "Vacuity above produces dizziness."

When the stirring qi is above, sweating must not be induced; if sweating is induced, qi surges up right at the tip of the heart. The *Nanjing* says: "The internal sign of the heart is stirring qi above the navel, firm or painful on pressure." The heart qi is not governing, the upright qi is vacuous within, and the qi stirs above the navel. The heart is yang; sweating that collapses yang further damages the heart qi. The kidney takes advantage of the heart's vacuity and tries to rise and encroach on the heart, so the qi rises. [The second half of this commentary consists of the text of the next paragraph (paragraph 8) and its commentary.]

## Notes

The clause states the prohibition of sweating for one with stirring qi to the left of the navel (liver). The second half of Cheng Wuji's commentary is the text and commentary of the next paragraph (stirring qi above the navel), recorded continuously.
