# §5 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The pulse is soft and weak: weak, on the contrary, at the guan; soft, on the contrary, at the top; faint, on the contrary, above; rough, on the contrary, below. Faint means yang qi is insufficient; rough means there is no blood. When yang qi is, on the contrary, faint, one is struck by wind, sweats, and, on the contrary, is agitated and vexed. Rough means there is no blood, with reversal and cold. If sweating is induced when yang is faint, there is agitation with inability to sleep.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The cun and guan are yang, and the pulse should be floating and exuberant. Weak, on the contrary, at the guan means the interior qi falls short; soft, on the contrary, at the top means the exterior qi falls short. The defensive moves outside the vessels; floating is above and examines the defensive; faint, on the contrary, above means yang qi is insufficient. The nutritive moves within the vessels; sunken is below and examines the nutritive; rough, on the contrary, below means there is no blood. When yang is faint and cannot secure the outside, the interstices open and become loose, and wind lodges there, causing sweating with agitation and vexation. Without blood, yin is vacuous and does not connect smoothly with yang, so there are reversal and cold. When yang is faint and there are no fluids, one cannot sweat; if sweating is induced, yang will surely collapse and there will be agitation. The classic says: "Profuse sweating collapses yang, leading to vacuity, aversion to wind, and vexation and agitation with inability to sleep."

## Notes

The clause judges insufficiency of yang qi and of blood from the pulse quality at each position, and prohibits sweating, since sweating such a person causes agitation and sleeplessness. It is not found in the three yin and three yang chapters and is one of the clauses unique to the "may / may not" chapters.
