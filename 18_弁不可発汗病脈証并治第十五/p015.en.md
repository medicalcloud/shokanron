# §15 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In all cases of adverse sweating, mild diseases become hard to cure; severe ones have disordered speech, and those with dizzy vision die (one version says: "delirious speech, dizzy vision, and disordered pupils are fatal"); life will hardly be preserved.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

When sweating is forcibly induced where it must not be, light cases become heavy from the sweating and are hard to cure; heavy cases lose the qi of yin and yang, with disordered speech and dizzy vision, and die. The *Nanjing* says: "One who loses yang sees ghosts"—this is the disordered speech. "One who loses yin goes blind"—this is the dizzy vision. Dizziness is seeing darkness where there is none, which is close to blindness. [The commentary's "目肓" is read as "目盲" (blindness).]

## Notes

The clause summarizes the results of mistakenly sweating those for whom it is contraindicated: in severe cases, the qi of yin and yang deserts, causing disordered speech and dizzy vision, leading to death.
