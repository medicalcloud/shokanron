# §9 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the stirring qi is below, sweating must not be induced. If sweating is induced, no sweat comes out, there are great vexation in the heart, distressing pain in the bones and joints, dizzy vision, and aversion to cold; on eating, one, on the contrary, vomits, and grain cannot go forward.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The *Nanjing* says: "The internal sign of the kidney is stirring qi below the navel, firm or painful on pressure." The kidney qi is not governing, the upright qi is vacuous within, and the stirring qi arises below the navel. The kidney governs water; no sweat on sweating means water is insufficient. Great vexation in the heart means the vacuous kidney cannot restrain heart fire. Distressing pain in the bones and joints is because the kidney governs the bones. Dizzy vision is because when the kidney is diseased the eyes are blurred as if seeing nothing. Aversion to cold is because the kidney governs cold. On eating, vomiting instead, with grain unable to go forward, means kidney water is dried up. Wang Bing says: "Retching and vomiting food that comes back up after a long time means there is no water."

## Notes

The clause states the prohibition of sweating for one with stirring qi below the navel (kidney). Because kidney fluids are insufficient, sweating produces no sweat and causes various vacuity signs.
