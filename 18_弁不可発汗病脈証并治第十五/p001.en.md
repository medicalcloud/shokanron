# §1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Considering that disease is most urgent, and that in hasty searching the essentials are hard to find, the formulas and treatments of what may and may not be done have been collected again. Compared with the chapters on the three yin and three yang, these are easy to see. Moreover, some items are not limited to the three yang and three yin and appear among the "may" and "may not."

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Of the patterns and formulas of "must not sweat" and "must not purge," those whose text and commentary are already provided in the preceding chapters on the three yin and three yang are not repeated. Others not found there are given here with commentary after the text.

## Notes

A paragraph serving as the preface of the so-called "may / may not" chapters (Chapters 15–22) beginning with "Distinguishing Pulse, Signs, and Treatment of Diseases That Must Not Be Sweated." It states that, for easy reference in urgent cases, clauses and formulas have been regrouped according to whether sweating, vomiting, or purgation is permitted, and that some clauses not found in the three yin and three yang chapters are included. Cheng Wuji states that he does not repeat commentary on clauses already annotated in the three yin and three yang chapters; hence many of the following paragraphs have no Cheng Wuji commentary.
