# §10 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the throat is blocked, sweating must not be induced. If sweating is induced, there will be vomiting of blood, the qi feeble and verging on expiry, cold hands and feet, a desire to lie curled up, and inability to warm oneself.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Cold limbs, a desire to lie curled up, and inability to warm oneself. The throat is connected with the stomach; when the stomach meridian is not harmonious, the throat is not free. Inducing sweating attacks yang, and the blood rises following the effusion, so there will certainly be vomiting of blood. When the stomach meridian is not harmonious and one, on the contrary, attacks the exterior, yang becomes deficient outside, so the qi is about to expire, the hands and feet are cold, and one wants to curl up but cannot [warm oneself]. [The opening of the commentary is thought to be a duplication of the final phrase of the text.]

## Notes

The clause prohibits sweating for one whose throat is blocked (whose stomach meridian is not harmonious), since sweating would cause vomiting of blood and decline of yang qi.
