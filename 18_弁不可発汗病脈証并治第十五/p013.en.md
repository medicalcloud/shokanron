# §13 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

One who coughs becomes severe, frequently vomits drool and foam, the throat will certainly be dry, urination is inhibited, the heart is hungry and vexed, and it arises every full day; its form resembles malaria, with cold but no heat, vacuity and cold shuddering. If sweating is induced in one with cough, one curls up and suffers fullness, and the abdomen becomes hard again.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

When the lung is cold and qi counterflows, one who coughs becomes severe, vomits drool and foam, and loses fluids, so the throat will certainly be dry and urination inhibited. When the yang qi in the diaphragm is vacuous, the heart is hungry and vexed. In one day and night the qi greatly converges at the lung, and the evil and upright strike each other, so it arises every full day; its form is like cold malaria, with only cold and no heat, vacuity and cold shuddering. When sweating attacks yang, yang qi becomes more vacuous and yin cold more severe, so one curls up, suffers fullness, and the abdomen becomes hard again.

## Notes

Thought to be a continuation of the previous paragraph (12), the clause states that sweating one who coughs and spits foam from lung cold further depletes yang qi and worsens the condition.
