# §22 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the fuyang pulse is floating and rough and the shaoyin pulse is as normal, the disease is in the spleen, and by rule there should be diarrhea. How is this known? If the pulse were floating and large, it would be qi repletion with blood vacuity. Now the fuyang pulse is floating and rough, so one knows the spleen qi is insufficient and the stomach qi vacuous. Because the shaoyin pulse is wiry and floating (one version writes "sunken"), this is regarded as a regulated pulse, so it is called "as normal." If, on the contrary, it is slippery and rapid, one knows there should be pus in the stool. (The *Yuhan* writes "urine.")

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Fuyang is the pulse for examining the stomach. Obtaining floating and rough means the spleen and stomach are insufficient. To take floating as qi repletion and rough as blood vacuity is wrong. The classic says: "When the pulse is floating and large, floating is qi repletion and large is blood vacuity." If the pulse were floating and large, it should be taken as qi repletion and blood vacuity; now the fuyang pulse is floating and rough, so floating is stomach vacuity and rough is spleen cold. When the spleen and stomach are vacuous and cold, grain is not digested and water is not separated, so by rule there should be diarrhea. Shaoyin is the kidney pulse. The kidney is the child of the lung and the mother of the liver. Floating is the lung pulse; wiry is the liver pulse. The shaoyin pulse being wiry and floating means child and mother engender each other, so it is called a regulated pulse. If it is slippery and rapid, guest heat is in the lower burner, making the blood flow and putrefy into pus, so there is pus in the stool.

## Notes

The clause compares the foot fuyang pulse (stomach pulse) and shaoyin pulse (kidney pulse) to judge diarrhea from spleen-stomach vacuity cold and pus in the stool from heat in the lower burner. The use of pulses at sites other than the cun kou (fuyang, shaoyin) is regarded as one characteristic of the chapters "Distinguishing the Pulse" and "Balancing the Pulse."
