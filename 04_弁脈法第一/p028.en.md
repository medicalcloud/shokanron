# §28 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The cun kou pulse is floating and large, yet the physician, on the contrary, purged it; this is a great adverse treatment. Floating means there is no blood; large means cold. When cold qi contends, there is rumbling of the intestines. The physician, not knowing this, on the contrary gave cold water to drink to cause great sweating. The water obtains the cold qi, the cold will certainly contend, and the person at once chokes (*ye*). (Pronounced *ye*; likewise below.)

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The classic says: "When the pulse is floating and large, sweating should be induced; if, on the contrary, it is purged, it is a great adverse treatment." A floating large pulse means the evil is in the exterior. Sweating should be induced; if, on the contrary, it is purged, the upright qi is attacked and the evil qi is able to enter deeply, so it is a great adverse treatment. "Floating means there is no blood" means blood was lost after purgation. "Large means cold" means the evil qi alone remains. The cold evil enters through the interior vacuity, the cold qi contends, and there is rumbling of the intestines. The physician, seeing the large pulse, thought there was heat and gave cold water to drink, wishing the cold of the water to overcome the heat and produce great sweating. The interior was already vacuous and cold; receiving cold water as well, water and cold contend and make the qi of the middle burner rough and stagnant, so there is choking.

## Notes

The clause states that mistaken treatment—purging a pulse of exterior pattern and then giving cold water to induce sweating—chills the interior, producing intestinal rumbling and choking (*ye*).
