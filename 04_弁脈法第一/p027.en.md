# §27 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the pulse is floating and surging, the sweat on the body is like oil, there is unceasing panting, water and drink do not go down, the body is numb, and one is alternately quiet and disordered, this is the expiry of life. Further, when it is not known which viscus first suffered the calamity: if sweat comes out moistening the hair with unceasing panting, the lung has expired first. If yang, on the contrary, remains alone, the body looks as if smoked, and there are staring eyes and a shaking head, the heart has expired. If the lips are, on the contrary, green-blue and the four limbs twitch, the liver has expired. If the area around the mouth is dark black and there is soft (cold) sweat with yellowing, the spleen has expired. If urine and stool are lost involuntarily, with manic speech and eyes, on the contrary, staring, the kidney has expired. Further, when it is not known whether the yin or yang of the viscera expired first: if yang qi expires first and yin qi is exhausted afterward, when the person dies the body color will certainly be green-blue; if yin qi expires first and yang qi is exhausted afterward, when the person dies the body color will certainly be red, with warmth under the armpits and heat below the heart.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

That some diseases cannot be treated is because the evil qi overcomes the upright qi. The *Neijing* says: "Large means the evil arrives," and also: "Large means the disease advances." A floating surging pulse means the evil qi prevails. Sweat on the body like oil and unceasing panting mean the upright qi is deserting. In all four seasons stomach qi is the root; water and drink not going down means the stomach qi is exhausted. The whole body is filled by the nutritive and defensive; numbness of the body means the nutritive and defensive are cut off. Numbness means feeling neither pain nor itch. The *Zhenjing* says: "When the nutritive and defensive do not move, there is numbness." Struggle produces disorder; calm produces quiet. Being alternately quiet and disordered means the upright struggles with the evil, the upright is defeated and the evil prevails. The upright qi has already deserted, the stomach qi is exhausted, the nutritive and defensive are both cut off, and the evil qi alone prevails; therefore it is called the expiry of life. [The commentary's "巳脱" is read as "已脱."]

The lung is the master of qi and the commander of the fluids. Sweat moistening the hair is desertion of the fluids; unceasing panting is desertion of qi.

The lung governs qi; the heart governs blood. Qi is yang; blood is yin. "Yang remaining alone" means great heat of the body; the blood has expired first and only the qi remains. The body looking as if smoked means there is no luster on the body; the blood has expired and does not nourish the body. The heart vessel flanks the throat and connects to the eye system; staring eyes mean the heart channel has expired. The head is the meeting place of all yang; a shaking head means yin has expired and yang has no root.

The lips are where the spleen is observed. The color of the liver is green-blue; when the liver expires, its true color appears in the region of what it overcomes. The four limbs are governed by the spleen; the liver governs the sinews. When the liver expires, the sinews and vessels become tense, appearing in the part of what it overcomes. Twitching means shaking, as if the hands and feet periodically jerk and contract.

The spleen governs the mouth and lips; when it expires, the luster departs, so the area around the mouth is dark black. Soft is yin; soft sweat is cold sweat. The spleen and stomach are the root of the fluids and the source of yang qi. Soft sweat with yellowing means the spleen has expired, the yang has deserted, and the true color appears.

The kidney governs opening and closing and holds in urine and stool. Involuntary loss of urine and stool means the kidney has expired and cannot restrain. The kidney stores the will; manic speech means the will is not kept. The *Neijing* says: "Manic speech is loss of the will; one who has lost the will dies." The *Zhenjing* says: "The essential qi of the five viscera all pours up into the eyes; the essence of the bones becomes the pupils." Eyes, on the contrary, staring means that when the kidney expires, the essence of the bones does not nourish the pupils, and the pupils do not turn.

Yang governs heat and its color is red; yin governs cold and its color is green-blue. When the person dies and the body color is green-blue, yin has not yet left the body, so it is said "yin qi is exhausted afterward." When the body color is red, with warmth under the armpits and heat below the heart, yang has not yet left the body, so it is said "yang qi is exhausted afterward." The *Zhenjing* says "people have two kinds of death but not two kinds of life"; this is what is meant.

## Notes

The clause describes the pulse and signs of "the expiry of life" when death is imminent, the signs when each of the five viscera expires (lung, heart, liver, spleen, kidney expiry), and a method of judging from the body color after death whether yin or yang expired first. It is based on the correspondence of five-phase colors with the five viscera.
