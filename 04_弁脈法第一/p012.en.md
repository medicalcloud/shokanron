# §12 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the pulse is floating and tight, it is called wiry. Wiry is shaped like a bowstring and does not shift when pressed. A tight pulse is like a twisting rope, without constancy.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The *Maijing* says: "Wiry and tight resemble each other." Wiry is taken as vacuity, so although tight and like a string, it does not shift when pressed; not shifting is insufficiency. The classic says: "Wiry means reduction." Tight is taken as repletion, so when palpated it is like a twisting rope, without constancy, and does not dissipate. The *Jingui Yaolüe* says: "When the pulse is tight like a twisting rope without constancy, there is abiding food."

## Notes

The clause states the difference between the similar "wiry" and "tight" pulses. Wiry is said to be straight like a bowstring and does not move when pressed; tight is said to flick from side to side like a twisting rope.
