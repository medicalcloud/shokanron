# §36 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The pulse is floating and slippery. Floating is yang; slippery is repletion. When yang and repletion contend, the pulse is rapid and racing, and the defensive qi loses its measure. When the floating slippery pulse is rapid and racing, with fever and sweating, it is untreatable.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Floating means the evil qi has merged with the defensive and the defensive qi prevails; slippery means the evil qi has merged with the nutritive and the nutritive qi is replete. When the evil qi prevails and is replete, hemming in the nutritive and defensive, their movement becomes fast, so the pulse is rapid and racing. Six beats per breath is called rapid. In a normal person the pulse beats four times per breath and the defensive qi moves six cun; now with six beats per breath the defensive qi moves nine cun, exceeding the normal by half; thus one knows the pulse is rapid and racing and the defensive qi has lost its normal measure. With a floating, slippery, rapid, racing pulse, fever and sweating should resolve it; if it does not resolve, the essential qi is deserting, and it certainly cannot be treated. The classic says: "When the pulse is exuberant at both yin and yang and great sweating does not resolve it, it is fatal."

## Notes

A floating, slippery, rapid, racing pulse indicates exuberant yang evil that should normally resolve with sweating; if it does not resolve despite sweating, the essential qi is deserting, and the clause deems it untreatable.
