# §36 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The pulse is floating and slippery. Floating is yang; slippery is excess. When yang and excess contend, the pulse is rapid and racing, and the wei-defensive qi loses its measure. When the floating slippery pulse is rapid and racing, with fever and sweating, it is untreatable.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Floating means the pathogenic qi has merged with the wei-defense and the wei-defensive qi prevails; slippery means the pathogenic qi has merged with the ying-nutrient and the ying-nutrient qi is in excess. When the pathogenic qi prevails and is in excess, hemming in the ying-nutrient and wei-defense, their movement becomes fast, so the pulse is rapid and racing. Six beats per breath is called rapid. In a normal person the pulse beats four times per breath and the wei-defensive qi moves six cun; now with six beats per breath the wei-defensive qi moves nine cun, exceeding the normal by half; thus one knows the pulse is rapid and racing and the wei-defensive qi has lost its normal measure. With a floating, slippery, rapid, racing pulse, fever and sweating should resolve it; if it does not resolve, the essential qi is deserting, and it certainly cannot be treated. The classic says: "When the pulse is exuberant at both yin and yang and great sweating does not resolve it, it is fatal."

## Notes

A floating, slippery, rapid, racing pulse indicates exuberant yang pathogen that should normally resolve with sweating; if it does not resolve despite sweating, the essential qi is deserting, and the clause deems it untreatable.
