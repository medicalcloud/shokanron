# §34 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the pulse is tight at both yin and yang, even after vomiting and diarrhea, the pulse alone may not resolve; when the tightness goes, one becomes calm, and it is about to resolve. If the pulse is slow and by the sixth or seventh day there is no desire to eat, it is a late-onset [disease] due to collected water, and it has not yet resolved; if eating is spontaneously possible, it is about to resolve. When after six or seven days of illness the pulses at the three positions of the hands and feet all arrive, and there is great vexation with clenched jaw and inability to speak, and the person is agitated and restless, it will certainly resolve. If the pulse is harmonious and the person has great vexation, heavy eyes, and yellowness at the inner margins of the eyelids, it is about to resolve.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

A pulse tight at both yin and yang means cold qi is severe above and below. When even after vomiting and diarrhea the tight pulse does not cease, it is "the pulse alone not resolving." When the tightness goes, one becomes calm; it is about to resolve. If the pulse is slow and by the sixth or seventh day there is no desire to eat, the spleen and stomach are greatly vacuous after vomiting and diarrhea. The *Neijing* says: "Drink enters the stomach, overflows with essential qi, and is conveyed up to the spleen; the spleen qi spreads the essence, which returns up to the lung; [the lung] regulates the waterways and conveys down to the bladder; the water essence spreads in four directions and the five channels move together." When the spleen and stomach qi is strong, it can convey and disperse the qi of water-rheum; when the spleen and stomach qi is vacuous, water-rheum collects within. What is called late onset is a disease that comes afterward. If by the sixth or seventh day one desires to eat, the spleen and stomach are already harmonized and the cold evil already dissipated, so it is said to be about to resolve.

Vexation is heat. At the time of transmission through the channels, when the patient's body is greatly vexed, the jaw clenched and unable to speak, and there is inner agitation and restlessness, yin and yang are contending for victory. If the pulses at the three positions of the hands and feet all arrive, the upright prevails and the evil is faint; the yang qi returns and the cold qi dissipates, so it will certainly resolve.

The *Maijing* says: "When yellowness arises at the canthi of both eyes of a patient, the disease is about to recover." In disease the pulse is primary. If there are yellow eyes and great vexation but the pulse is not harmonious, the evil prevails and the disease is advancing. Yellow eyes and great vexation with a harmonious pulse mean the upright qi is already harmonized, so it is said to be about to resolve. [The commentary's "巳" is read as "已."]

## Notes

The clause judges whether a disease will recover from vomiting and diarrhea, appetite, vexation and agitation, eye color, and so on. Even when intense vexation and agitation appear, if the pulse has recovered and is harmonious, it is regarded as a sign of resolution, a precursor of the upright qi struggling with and overcoming the evil (such as the state before so-called "shiver sweating"). "臉" is understood as the eyelid.
