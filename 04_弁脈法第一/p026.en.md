# §26 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the pulse is floating and large, yet the area below the heart is, on the contrary, hard, with heat: if it belongs to the viscera, attack it and do not induce sweating; if it belongs to the bowels, do not make urination frequent—frequent urination makes the stool hard. When sweating is profuse, the heat recovers; when sweating is scant, the stool is difficult. With a slow pulse, it still must not be attacked.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

A floating large pulse should be taken to mean the evil is in the exterior. If, on the contrary, the area below the heart is hard, the heat is already severe and bound within. "Having heat and belonging to the viscera" means there is no other vacuity cold, only interior heat. The viscera belong to yin and are all in the interior, so it may be purged; "attack" means purge. One must not, because the pulse is floating and large, further induce sweating. The *Bingyuan* says: "When heat-toxin qi assails the heart, with glomus and fullness below the heart, there is repletion; it should be purged promptly."

Although the area below the heart is hard, if there are no other interior signs but only exterior signs, the disease is in the yang; this is called belonging to the bowels. One should first resolve the exterior and afterward attack the glomus. "Sou" is urine. One must not, thinking it is bound rheum, promote urination and make it frequent, because the stool will certainly become hard. The classic says "when urination is frequent, the stool will certainly be hard," meaning that the fluids are drained off. When sweating is profuse, the evil qi is removed and the heat recovers; when sweating is scant, the evil heat is not exhausted, and since the fluids are also drained off, the stool will certainly be difficult. One with hardness should be purged, but if the pulse is slow it must not yet be attacked, because slow is insufficiency, meaning the interior qi is not yet replete.

## Notes

The clause divides treatment, for hardness below the heart with a floating large pulse, according to whether the disease belongs to the "viscera" (interior) or "bowels" (exterior, yang). The use of the terms viscera and bowels differs from usual, and interpretations of the passage vary.
