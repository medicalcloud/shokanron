# §23 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The cun kou pulse is floating and tight. Floating means wind; tight means cold. Wind damages the defensive; cold damages the nutritive. When both the nutritive and the defensive are diseased, there is vexing pain in the bones and joints; sweating should be induced.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The *Maijing* says: "Wind damages yang; cold damages yin." The defensive is yang; the nutritive is yin. Wind is yang; cold is yin. Each damages according to its kind. This is what the *Changes* means by "water flows to the damp; fire goes to the dry." When the defensive gets wind there is heat; when the nutritive gets cold there is pain. Both nutritive and defensive are diseased, so there is vexing pain in the bones and joints. Give Mahuang tang to induce sweating, and there will be recovery.

## Notes

The clause states the theory that "wind damages the defensive, cold damages the nutritive." In later times it was regarded as one basis for the theory distinguishing taiyang wind strike (Guizhi tang pattern) from cold damage (Mahuang tang pattern), and further led to the "three-pillar" theory, which adds damage to both nutritive and defensive (Da Qinglong tang pattern).
