# §23 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The cun kou pulse is floating and tight. Floating means wind; tight means cold. Wind damages the wei-defense; cold damages the ying-nutrient. When both the ying-nutrient and the wei-defense are diseased, there is vexing pain in the bones and joints; sweating should be induced.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The *Maijing* says: "Wind damages yang; cold damages yin." The wei-defense is yang; the ying-nutrient is yin. Wind is yang; cold is yin. Each damages according to its kind. This is what the *Changes* means by "water flows to the damp; fire goes to the dry." When the wei-defense gets wind there is heat; when the ying-nutrient gets cold there is pain. Both ying-nutrient and wei-defense are diseased, so there is vexing pain in the bones and joints. Give Mahuang tang (Maoto) to induce sweating, and there will be recovery.

## Notes

The clause states the theory that "wind damages the wei-defense, cold damages the ying-nutrient." In later times it was regarded as one basis for the theory distinguishing Taiyang wind attack (Guizhi tang [Keishito] pattern) from cold damage (Mahuang tang pattern), and further led to the "three-pillar" theory, which adds damage to both ying-nutrient and wei-defense (Da Qinglong tang [Daiseiryuto] pattern).
