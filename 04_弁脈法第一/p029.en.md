# §29 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The fuyang pulse is floating; floating means deficiency. When floating and deficiency contend, the qi is made to choke; this means the stomach qi is deficient and exhausted. When the pulse is slippery there is hiccup. This is the physician's fault: treating deficiency as excess, guarding emptiness and pressing the blood. When the pulse is floating and the inside of the nose is dry, there will certainly be nosebleed.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

A floating fuyang pulse means choking; a slippery pulse means hiccup. Both are the physician's fault, the error of treating deficiency as excess. The *Neijing* says: "Yin is inside as the guard of yang; yang is outside as the envoy of yin." Inducing sweating to attack yang, losing fluids so that yang qi is insufficient, is called guarding emptiness. The classic says: "The exterior qi is slightly deficient and the interior qi does not guard, so the pathogen strikes the yin." Yin does not serve as the guard of yin, the pathogenic qi is thereby able to enter, contends within with the yin blood, the yin loses what it guards, and the blood moves recklessly; it is not known by which path it will come out. If the pulse is floating and the nose dry, one knows the blood will certainly come out from the nose.

## Notes

Continuing from the previous paragraph, it states that mistaken treatment makes the stomach qi deficient producing choking or hiccup, and further that the blood moves recklessly causing nosebleed. Interpretations of the phrase "責虚取実，守空迫血" vary.
