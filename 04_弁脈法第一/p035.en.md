# §35 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The pulse is floating and rapid. Floating is wind; rapid is vacuity. Wind is heat; vacuity is cold. When wind and vacuity contend, there is shivering aversion to cold.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The *Neijing* says: "What is present is repletion; what is absent is vacuity. When qi is merged, there is no blood; when blood is merged, there is no qi." Wind damages the defensive; rapid means there is no blood. A floating rapid pulse means wind evil has merged with the defensive; when the defensive prevails, the nutritive is vacuous. The defensive is yang; wind contends with the defensive, so there is heat. The nutritive is yin; the nutritive qi is vacuous, so there is cold. When wind merges with the defensive, the signs of fever and aversion to cold are fully present.

## Notes

The clause explains the mechanism of simultaneous fever and aversion to cold by interpreting a floating rapid pulse as a combination of wind (heat of the defensive) and vacuity (cold of the nutritive).
