# §35 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The pulse is floating and rapid. Floating is wind; rapid is deficiency. Wind is heat; deficiency is cold. When wind and deficiency contend, there is shivering aversion to cold.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The *Neijing* says: "What is present is excess; what is absent is deficiency. When qi is merged, there is no blood; when blood is merged, there is no qi." Wind damages the wei-defense; rapid means there is no blood. A floating rapid pulse means pathogenic wind has merged with the wei-defense; when the wei-defense prevails, the ying-nutrient is deficient. The wei-defense is yang; wind contends with the wei-defense, so there is heat. The ying-nutrient is yin; the ying-nutrient qi is deficient, so there is cold. When wind merges with the wei-defense, the signs of fever and aversion to cold are fully present.

## Notes

The clause explains the mechanism of simultaneous fever and aversion to cold by interpreting a floating rapid pulse as a combination of wind (heat of the wei-defense) and deficiency (cold of the ying-nutrient).
