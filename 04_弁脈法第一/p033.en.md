# §33 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the pulse is tight at both yin and yang, with qi coming out of the mouth, dry lips and mouth, lying curled up with cold feet, nasal discharge, and slippery tongue fur, do not treat rashly. When by around the seventh day the person has slight fever and the hands and feet are warm, it is about to resolve; when by the eighth day or later there is, on the contrary, great fever, it is difficult to treat. If there is aversion to cold, there will certainly be a desire to retch; if there is pain within the abdomen, there will certainly be a tendency to diarrhea.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

A pulse tight at both yin and yang means cold lodges in both exterior and interior. Cold is yin and resolves when it obtains yang. Qi coming out of the mouth and dry lips and mouth mean yang qi is gradually returning and the upright qi is just becoming warm. Even so, the yin has not yet fully dissipated: lying curled up with cold feet, nasal discharge, and slippery tongue fur show that yin still remains. When yin and yang have not yet separated, one must not treat rashly and bias the qi of yin and yang. When by around the seventh day the person has slight fever and the hands and feet are warm, the yin qi has already been cut off and the yang qi has been able to return; it is about to resolve. If it does not resolve after the seventh day and by the eighth day or later great fever, on the contrary, arises, yin has reached its extreme and transformed into heat, and the evil qi overcomes the upright, so it is said to be difficult to treat. A tight yang pulse means the cold evil arises in the upper burner, which governs the outside; a tight yin pulse means the cold evil arises in the lower burner, which governs the inside. If there is aversion to cold, the cold qi of the upper burner prevails, and there will certainly be a desire to retch. If there is pain within the abdomen, the cold qi of the lower burner prevails, and there will certainly be a tendency to diarrhea. [The commentary's "巳来" is read as "已来."]

## Notes

The clause states that when cold evil has been contracted in both exterior and interior, during the period when yin and yang are not yet settled one should not treat rashly but observe the course, and judges prognosis by the degree of fever after the seventh day.
