# §24 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the fuyang pulse is slow and moderate, the stomach qi is normal. When the fuyang pulse is floating and rapid, floating damages the stomach and rapid stirs the spleen; this is not the original disease but the result of the physician's purgation. The nutritive and defensive sink inward, the rapidity first becomes faint, and the pulse, on the contrary, is only floating; the person will certainly have hard stool, and the qi is removed by belching. Why so? Originally the spleen was stirred by the rapid pulse; the rapidity first becomes faint, so one knows the spleen qi is not governing, the stool is hard, and the qi is removed by belching. Now the pulse, on the contrary, is floating and the rapidity changes to faint; the evil qi alone remains, the heart is hungry, but the evil heat does not digest grain, and there are tidal fever and thirst. A rapid pulse should become slow and moderate; when the pulse's measure before and after is as it should be, the patient is hungry. When a rapid pulse is out of season, malign sores arise.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

"Jing" means normal. The fuyang pulse examines the spleen and stomach, so a slow moderate pulse is normal. If the pulse is floating and rapid, the physician has recklessly purged, damaging the stomach and stirring the spleen, and the evil qi has taken advantage of the vacuity to sink inward. When the evil is in the exterior, yang pulses are seen; when the evil is in the interior, yin pulses are seen. When the evil is in the exterior, the pulse is floating and rapid. Because purgation made the interior vacuous, the nutritive and defensive sink inward and the evil lodges in the spleen. Rapid stirs the spleen. Now the rapidity first becoming faint means the spleen evil has first sunk into the interior. When the stomach is vacuous and the spleen hot, the fluids dry and become scant, and the stool will certainly be hard. The *Zhenjing* says: "Spleen disease is prone to belching; when stool or flatus comes out, one feels relieved and it abates." Now evil heat lodges in the spleen, so the qi is removed by belching. The spleen can digest water and grain; now the evil qi alone remains in the spleen and the spleen qi does not govern, so although the heart is hungry, grain cannot be digested. The spleen governs moving the fluids for the stomach; the spleen is scorched by heat, so there are tidal fever and thirst. The fuyang pulse was originally slow and moderate; after purgation it changed to floating and rapid, the nutritive and defensive sank inward, and the rapidity again changed to faint. This is "the pulse's measure before and after is as it should be"; the evil heat has sunk inward into the spleen, so the heart is prone to hunger. "A rapid pulse out of season" means the rapidity should change to faint but does not; in this case the evil qi is not transmitted inward but is depressed within the nutritive and defensive, and will certainly come out through the flesh and skin as malign sores.

## Notes

The clause discusses, by changes in the fuyang pulse, the course in which evil heat sinks into the spleen after mistaken purgation and the subsequent prognosis (the development of malign sores, etc.). Many passages are hard to construe, and punctuation and interpretation vary. In particular, there is no settled view on punctuating "邪気独留，心中則飢" (some read "邪気独留心中，則飢") or on the meaning of "脈因前後度数如法." The closing phrase of this clause, "when a rapid pulse is out of season, malign sores arise," is also quoted in Cheng Wuji's commentary on Clause 332 of the jueyin chapter.
