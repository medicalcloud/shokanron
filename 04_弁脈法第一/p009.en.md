# §9 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the pulse comes moderately and stops once at times and then comes again, it is called bound. When the pulse comes rapidly and stops once at times and then comes again, it is called a skipping (one version writes "loose") pulse. When yang is exuberant it skips; when yin is exuberant it binds. These are all disease pulses.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Four beats per breath is called normal; three beats per breath is called slow; slightly faster than slow is called moderate; six beats per breath is called rapid. Stopping once at times means the qi of yin and yang cannot continue each other. Yang moves fast; yin moves slowly. Moderate is used to examine yin: if yin qi prevails and yang cannot continue, the pulse comes moderately and stops once at times. Rapid is used to examine yang: if yang qi prevails and yin cannot continue, the pulse comes rapidly and stops once at times. Cold damage has bound and intermittent pulses; one that moves, stops in the middle, and cannot return by itself is a death pulse. These bound and skipping pulses merely stop once at times due to one-sided prevalence of yin or yang and are not desertion or expiry, so it is simply said "these are all disease pulses."

## Notes

The clause defines, among irregular pulses, the "bound pulse," which is slowish and stops at times, and the "skipping pulse," which is rapid and stops at times. Both are disease pulses but are distinguished from the "intermittent pulse" (a death pulse) that does not recover by itself after stopping.
