# §25 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The master said: When a patient's pulse is faint and rough, this is a disease caused by the physician. The physician greatly induced sweating and also repeatedly purged heavily, so the person lost blood; the disease should involve aversion to cold, and afterward fever without any time of cessation. In the hot months of summer, one wishes to wear layered clothing; in the cold months of winter, one wishes to bare the body. The reason is that when yang is faint there is aversion to cold, and when yin is weak there is fever. Here the physician induced sweating, making the yang qi faint, and also purged heavily, making the yin qi weak. In the fifth month, the yang qi is in the exterior and the stomach is vacuous and cold; because the yang qi is faint within and cannot overcome the cold, one wishes to wear layered clothing. In the eleventh month, the yang qi is in the interior and the stomach is vexed with heat; because the yin qi is weak within and cannot overcome the heat, one wishes to bare the body. Also, the yin pulse is slow and rough, so one knows there is blood loss.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Faint means yang collapse; rough means there is no blood. When sweating is forcibly induced where it should not be, the yang qi is made faint, yin qi rises into the yang, and there is aversion to cold; hence "when yang is faint there is aversion to cold." When purgation is forcibly applied where it should not be, the yin qi is made weak, yang qi sinks into the yin, and there is fever; hence "when yin is weak there is fever." Qi is yang; blood is yin. The yang pulse examines qi; the yin pulse examines blood. A slow rough yin pulse means the nutritive blood is insufficient, so one knows there is blood loss. The classic says: "When the chi pulse is slow, sweating must not be induced, because the nutritive qi is insufficient and blood scant." [The "盥" in the commentary's "強盥下之" is considered an erroneous character and is read in the sense of "強与下之" (forcibly purge it).]

## Notes

The clause explains, by where yang qi resides inside or outside according to the season, why one whose yang qi and yin blood have both been damaged by repeated mistaken sweating and purgation shows sensations of cold and heat contrary to the season (feeling cold in summer and hot in winter). For the phrase from "無休止時," there is also a reading punctuated "後乃発熱無休止，時夏月盛熱…" ("afterward fever without cessation; at times, in the hot months of summer…").
