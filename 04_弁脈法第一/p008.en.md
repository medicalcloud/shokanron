# §8 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

A pulse that is continuous and soft like pouring lacquer that breaks off means loss of blood.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

*Mianmian* means continuous and soft. "Like pouring lacquer that breaks off" means large in front and fine behind. The *Zhengli lun* says: "The celestial pivot opens and effuses, essence shifts and qi transforms, yin and yang meet, the stomach is harmonized and the pulse arises, and the pulse arises again." Yang qi arrives first and yin qi arrives after, so the front of the pulse is yang qi and the back is yin qi. A pulse coming large in front and fine behind means yang qi is in surplus and yin qi insufficient; from this one knows there is blood loss.

## Notes

A pulse that, like poured lacquer, flows thick at first and finally thins and breaks off indicates insufficiency of yin blood (blood loss).
