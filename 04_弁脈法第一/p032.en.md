# §32 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the cun kou pulse is tight at both yin and yang, by rule clear evil has struck the upper burner and turbid evil has struck the lower burner. Clear evil striking above is called clean; turbid evil striking below is called muddy. When yin is struck by evil, there will certainly be internal shuddering; the exterior qi is slightly vacuous and the interior qi does not guard, so the evil strikes the yin. When yang is struck by evil, there will certainly be fever, headache, stiff nape, neck spasm, lumbar pain, and aching shins, because yang has been struck by the qi of mist and dew; hence it is said: clear evil strikes above, turbid evil strikes below. Yin qi produces shuddering, counterflow cold of the feet and knees, and reckless discharge of stool and urine. The exterior qi is slightly vacuous and the interior qi slightly urgent; the triple burner becomes muddled together and inside and outside do not communicate. When the upper burner is depressed (*fu*, pronounced *fo*; likewise below) and the visceral qi fumes, the mouth ulcerates and the gums are eroded. When the middle burner is not governed, stomach qi surges upward, spleen qi does not turn, the stomach becomes turbid, the nutritive and defensive do not flow, and blood congeals and does not move. If the defensive qi flows through first, the urine is red-yellow; it contends with the heat, and acting as an envoy through the heat, roams the channels and network vessels and goes in and out of the viscera and bowels; where the heat qi passes, there are welling-abscesses with pus. If the yin qi flows through first, the yang qi is reversed and faint, yin has nothing to employ, guest qi enters within and is expelled by sneezing, the voice is choked (*wa*, pronounced yi-gu) and the throat obstructed; cold and reversal chase each other, are hemmed in by heat, and congealed blood descends of itself, looking like pig's liver. When yin and yang are both reversed, the spleen qi is isolated and weak, the five fluids pour downward, the lower burner does not close (one version writes 闔), there are bowel movements with rectal heaviness, making defecation frequent and difficult, and the navel pounds with gripping pain; life will hardly be preserved.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Floating is yang; sunken is yin. When the yang pulse is tight, the qi of mist and dew has struck the upper burner; when the yin pulse is tight, cold evil has struck the lower burner. The upper burner is the taiyang; the lower burner is the shaoyin. Fever, headache, stiff nape, neck spasm, lumbar pain, and aching shins mean the qi of mist and dew has struck the taiyang channel. Turbid evil striking below, with yin qi producing shuddering, counterflow cold of the feet and shins, and reckless discharge of stool and urine, means cold evil has struck the shaoyin. Because the exterior qi is slightly vacuous, the evil enters and lodges there; and because the interior qi does not guard, the evil takes advantage of the interior weakness and finally strikes the yin. When yin is vacuous and meets the evil, there is internal fear and shuddering, leading to slight urgency of qi. The *Neijing* says: "Yang disease goes up to the extreme and then descends; yin disease goes down to the extreme and then ascends." Here, when the evil of the upper burner is severe, it descends to invade the middle burner; when the evil of the lower burner is severe, it ascends to invade the middle burner; thereby the triple burner becomes muddled and disordered. The triple burner governs and holds all qi; once it is muddled and disordered, the qi of inside and outside cannot communicate. The *danzhong* is the sea of yang qi; since the qi cannot communicate inside and outside, it is depressed in the upper burner and becomes heat, fuming with the viscera, so the mouth ulcerates and the gums are eroded. The *Neijing* says: "When the diaphragm is hot and [the bowels] do not move, it rises to become erosion of the mouth." The middle burner is where the evils of the upper and lower burners are muddled, so it cannot be calmly governed. The middle burner is within the stomach; when the middle burner loses its governance, the stomach qi surges upward. The spleen is *kun*; *kun* assists the stomach qi to grind water and grain. When the spleen qi does not turn, the water and grain in the stomach are not ground and digested, so the stomach is turbid. The *Jingui Yaolüe* says: "When grain qi is not dispersed, the stomach suffers from turbidity." The nutritive is the essential qi of water and grain; the defensive is the fierce qi of water and grain. When qi cannot spread and disperse, the nutritive and defensive do not flow and blood congeals and does not move. The defensive qi is yang qi; the nutritive blood is yin qi. Yang governs heat; yin governs cold. "The defensive qi flowing through first" means the yang qi flows through first and the heat qi is able to move. The *Neijing* says: "The bladder stores the fluids; when qi transformation occurs, they can come out." From the urine being red-yellow, one knows the defensive qi has flowed through first. The heat qi contends with the stomach qi and moves, going in and out of the viscera and bowels and roaming the channels and network vessels. When heat lodges in the channels and network vessels, blood congeals and flesh putrefies into welling-abscesses with pus; this shows that the heat qi has been able to move. If the yin qi flows through first, it is otherwise. Yang is outside as the envoy of yin; because the yang qi is reversed and faint, yin has nothing to employ, and so the yin qi flows through first. The *Neijing* says: "Yang qi guards the outside and makes it secure." When the yang qi is reversed and faint, it cannot guard the outside, and cold qi accordingly lodges there. The nose is where the lung is observed, and the lung governs the voice; cold qi entering within lodges in the lung channel, so there is sneezing to expel it, the voice is choked and the throat obstructed. Cold is the outer evil; reversal is the inner evil. The evils of outside and inside combine and chase each other. When it becomes heat, blood congeals and does not move; now, being hemmed in by heat, the blood is made to congeal and descend of itself, like pig's liver. When the yang qi of the upper burner is reversed and the yin qi of the lower burner is reversed, and the two qi are both reversed and do not connect smoothly, the spleen qi alone is weak and cannot move and transform qi and blood to nourish the five viscera, so the five viscera are all vacuous and the five fluids pour downward. The *Zhenjing* says: "When the five viscera are not harmonized, the fluids overflow and flow down into the yin." *He* means closing. *Qing* is *qing* (privy). The qi of the lower burner deserts and does not close, so there are frequent bowel movements with rectal heaviness. The navel is the source of the generative qi; when the navel pounds with gripping pain, the generative qi is about to be cut off; therefore it is said that life will hardly be preserved.

## Notes

One of the longest and most difficult clauses of the "Distinguishing the Pulse" chapter. It begins with the qi of mist and dew (clear evil) striking the upper burner and cold-damp evil (turbid evil) striking the lower burner, and describes the pathological changes arising in each of the upper, middle, and lower burners from the disorder of triple-burner qi, as well as the outcomes when yang qi flows through first (abscesses) and when yin qi flows through first (bloody discharge, etc.). There are many textual and punctuation problems, and interpretations vary. "斉" is understood to mean "臍" (navel).
