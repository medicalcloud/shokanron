# §14 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Question: Why is it that some diseases resolve through shivering followed by sweating? Answer: When the pulse is floating and tight, and on pressure is, on the contrary, scallion-stalk, there is original vacuity, so there should be shivering and then sweating. Because the person is originally vacuous, shivering arises; because the pulse is floating, there should be sweating and resolution. If the pulse is floating and rapid and not scallion-stalk on pressure, the person is not originally vacuous; when about to resolve spontaneously, there is only sweating and no shivering.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Floating is yang, tight is yin, scallion-stalk is vacuity. When yin and yang struggle there is shivering. When the evil qi is about to exit and the evil struggles with the upright, if the person is originally vacuous, shivering arises. When the upright qi prevails there is shivering; after the shivering there is fever again, and great sweating resolves it.

Floating and rapid are yang. When one is originally replete and yang prevails, the evil cannot struggle with the upright, so no shivering arises.

## Notes

The clause describes the mechanism of "shiver sweating," in which a disease resolves through shivering (chills and rigor) followed by sweating. Those whose upright qi is originally vacuous shiver as they struggle with the evil, while those not vacuous resolve with sweating alone without shivering.
