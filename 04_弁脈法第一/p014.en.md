# §14 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Question: Why is it that some diseases resolve through shivering followed by sweating? Answer: When the pulse is floating and tight, and on pressure is, on the contrary, hollow, there is original deficiency, so there should be shivering and then sweating. Because the person is originally deficient, shivering arises; because the pulse is floating, there should be sweating and resolution. If the pulse is floating and rapid and not hollow on pressure, the person is not originally deficient; when about to resolve spontaneously, there is only sweating and no shivering.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Floating is yang, tight is yin, hollow is deficiency. When yin and yang struggle there is shivering. When the pathogenic qi is about to exit and the pathogen struggles with the healthy qi, if the person is originally deficient, shivering arises. When the healthy qi prevails there is shivering; after the shivering there is fever again, and great sweating resolves it.

Floating and rapid are yang. When one is originally excess and yang prevails, the pathogen cannot struggle with the healthy qi, so no shivering arises.

## Notes

The clause describes the mechanism of "shiver sweating," in which a disease resolves through shivering (chills and rigor) followed by sweating. Those whose healthy qi is originally deficient shiver as they struggle with the pathogen, while those not deficient resolve with sweating alone without shivering.
