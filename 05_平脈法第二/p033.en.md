# §33 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The cun kou pulse is feeble and hesitant. Feeble means the wei-defensive qi does not move; rough means the ying-nutrient qi does not reach. When the ying-nutrient and wei-defense cannot accompany each other, the sanjiao has nothing to rely on, and the body becomes numb and insensate. When the ying-nutrient qi is insufficient, there are vexing pains and difficulty speaking; when the wei-defensive qi is deficient, there are aversion to cold and frequent yawning. When the sanjiao does not return to its regions: when the upper jiao does not return, there are belching and swallowing of sourness; when the middle jiao does not return, one cannot digest grain or take in food; when the lower jiao does not return, there is enuresis.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

What nourishes the sanjiao is blood; what protects it is qi. When the ying-nutrient and wei-defense are both damaged and cannot move together, the sanjiao has nothing to rely on, and the body becomes stubbornly numb and insensate. The *Neijing* says: "When the ying-nutrient qi is deficient there is numbness." The *Zhenjing* says: "When the wei-defensive qi does not move there is numbness." The ying-nutrient is blood; when blood is insufficient there are vexing pains. The ying-nutrient belongs to the heart; when the ying-nutrient is weak and the heart deficient, it is hard to speak. The wei-defense is yang; when yang is feeble there is aversion to cold. The wei-defense is qi; when qi is deficient there is frequent yawning. Because the ying-nutrient and wei-defense are insufficient, the sanjiao has nothing to rely on and its qi cannot return to its regions. The *Jingui Yaolüe* says: "When the upper jiao is exhausted, there is frequent belching; the upper jiao receives the qi of the middle jiao, and the middle jiao is not yet harmonious and cannot digest grain, hence belching. When the lower jiao is exhausted, there are enuresis and fecal incontinence." The upper jiao is above the diaphragm, the region where things are not yet transformed. "Not returning" means not arriving. When the qi of the upper jiao does not reach its region, things cannot be conveyed and transformed, so there are belching and swallowing of sourness. The middle jiao is within the stomach and governs rotting and ripening water and grain; when water and grain are transformed, one thinks of food. When [the qi of] the middle jiao does not return to its region, water and grain are not transformed; hence it is said one cannot digest grain or take in food. The lower jiao is at the upper opening of the bladder and governs separating the clear from the turbid. "Sou" is urine. When the lower jiao does not return to its region, it cannot restrain urine and stool, so there is enuresis.

## Notes

The clause describes symptoms arising because, with insufficiency of the ying-nutrient and wei-defense, the qi of the sanjiao does not reach its respective regions ("not returning to its regions"), divided into the upper jiao (belching, acid swallowing), middle jiao (indigestion, poor appetite), and lower jiao (enuresis). Related descriptions appear in the *Jingui Yaolüe* chapter on wind, cold, accumulations, and gatherings of the five zang organs.
