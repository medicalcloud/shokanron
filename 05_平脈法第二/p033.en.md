# §33 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The cun kou pulse is faint and rough. Faint means the defensive qi does not move; rough means the nutritive qi does not reach. When the nutritive and defensive cannot accompany each other, the triple burner has nothing to rely on, and the body becomes numb and insensate. When the nutritive qi is insufficient, there are vexing pains and difficulty speaking; when the defensive qi is vacuous, there are aversion to cold and frequent yawning. When the triple burner does not return to its regions: when the upper burner does not return, there are belching and swallowing of sourness; when the middle burner does not return, one cannot digest grain or take in food; when the lower burner does not return, there is enuresis.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

What nourishes the triple burner is blood; what protects it is qi. When the nutritive and defensive are both damaged and cannot move together, the triple burner has nothing to rely on, and the body becomes stubbornly numb and insensate. The *Neijing* says: "When the nutritive qi is vacuous there is numbness." The *Zhenjing* says: "When the defensive qi does not move there is numbness." The nutritive is blood; when blood is insufficient there are vexing pains. The nutritive belongs to the heart; when the nutritive is weak and the heart vacuous, it is hard to speak. The defensive is yang; when yang is faint there is aversion to cold. The defensive is qi; when qi is vacuous there is frequent yawning. Because the nutritive and defensive are insufficient, the triple burner has nothing to rely on and its qi cannot return to its regions. The *Jingui Yaolüe* says: "When the upper burner is exhausted, there is frequent belching; the upper burner receives the qi of the middle burner, and the middle burner is not yet harmonious and cannot digest grain, hence belching. When the lower burner is exhausted, there are enuresis and fecal incontinence." The upper burner is above the diaphragm, the region where things are not yet transformed. "Not returning" means not arriving. When the qi of the upper burner does not reach its region, things cannot be conveyed and transformed, so there are belching and swallowing of sourness. The middle burner is within the stomach and governs rotting and ripening water and grain; when water and grain are transformed, one thinks of food. When [the qi of] the middle burner does not return to its region, water and grain are not transformed; hence it is said one cannot digest grain or take in food. The lower burner is at the upper opening of the bladder and governs separating the clear from the turbid. "Sou" is urine. When the lower burner does not return to its region, it cannot restrain urine and stool, so there is enuresis.

## Notes

The clause describes symptoms arising because, with insufficiency of the nutritive and defensive, the qi of the triple burner does not reach its respective regions ("not returning to its regions"), divided into the upper burner (belching, acid swallowing), middle burner (indigestion, poor appetite), and lower burner (enuresis). Related descriptions appear in the *Jingui Yaolüe* chapter on wind, cold, accumulations, and gatherings of the five viscera.
