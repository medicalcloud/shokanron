# §32 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The fuyang pulse is tight and floating. Floating is qi; tight is cold. Floating means abdominal fullness; tight means wringing pain. When floating and tight contend, the intestines rumble and [qi] turns over; when it turns, the qi moves and the diaphragm qi then descends. When the shaoyin pulse does not emerge, the genitals are swollen, enlarged, and vacuous.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Floating is vacuity of stomach qi; tight is cold in the spleen. Stomach vacuity produces fullness; spleen cold produces pain. When vacuity and cold contend, the intestines rumble and turn over; when they turn, the qi within the diaphragm accordingly drains downward. If the shaoyin pulse does not emerge, the vacuity-cold qi reaches the lower burner, binds in the shaoyin, and gathers in the genitals, unable to effuse and drain, making the genitals swollen, enlarged, and vacuous.

## Notes

The clause describes abdominal fullness, abdominal pain, and intestinal rumbling from spleen-stomach vacuity cold, and how that qi reaches the lower burner to produce swelling of the genitals (perhaps of the kind later called *shan* qi).
