# §5 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the master takes the pulse, a patient who yawns has no disease. One who groans when the pulse is taken has disease. One who speaks slowly has wind. One who speaks with a shaking head has interior pain. One who walks slowly has exterior stiffness. One who sits bent forward has shortness of breath. One who sits with one leg lowered has lumbar pain. One with interior repletion who guards the abdomen as if holding an egg has heart pain.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The *Zhenjing* says: "Yang pulls upward and yin pulls downward; yin and yang pull each other, hence yawning." When yin and yang do not pull each other there is disease; when they pull each other there is harmony. Therefore one who yawns has no disease.

Groaning is the sound of moaning, which occurs when the body has suffering.

When wind lodges within, the channels and network vessels are tense and the tongue is stiff and hard to move.

When there is disease in the interior, the head shakes when one tries to speak.

Exterior stiffness means the sinews and network vessels are tense and walking is impaired.

Shortness of breath means the interior is not harmonious, so one sits and likes to bend forward.

The *Neijing* says: "The lumbus is the great joint of the body." Lumbar pain means the great joint is impaired, so one cannot sit upright, and lowers one leg to ease the pain in the lumbus.

With heart pain one cannot stretch or lean back, so one guards the abdomen to press on the pain.

## Notes

The clause lists concrete examples of observation, discerning the condition from the patient's movements, posture, and manner of speech while the pulse is taken.
