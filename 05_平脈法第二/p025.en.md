# §25 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The fuyang pulse is slippery and tight. Slippery means the stomach qi is replete; tight means the spleen qi is strong. Using repletion to strike strength, the pain turns back to injure oneself, like grasping a blade with the hand and thereby making a wound.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The fuyang pulse examines the spleen and stomach. Slippery means grain qi is replete, which is stomach repletion; tight means yin qi prevails, which is spleen strength. Because one of spleen and stomach is replete and the other strong, and they strike each other, pain results. If one were strong and the other weak, their contention could not produce pain. Here both spleen and stomach are strong and replete and strike each other, so the bowels and viscera injure themselves and there is pain—like grasping a blade with the hand and making a wound; is this not bringing harm on oneself?

## Notes

The clause likens abdominal pain arising from stomach repletion and spleen strength striking each other to injuring oneself by grasping a blade.
