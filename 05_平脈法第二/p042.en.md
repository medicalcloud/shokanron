# §42 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The cun kou pulse is faint and the chi pulse tight. The person is vacuous and depleted with profuse sweating; one knows that yin is constantly present and yang is not seen at all.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

A faint cun means yang collapse; a tight chi means yin prevails. Yang is faint and yin prevails, so it is called vacuity and depletion. When profuse sweating is added, the yang qi is further depleted. This is yin constantly present and [yang] not seen at all. [The commentary ends with "絶不見"; it is understood as "絶不見陽" (yang not seen at all).]

## Notes

When a faint cun (declining yang) and tight chi (exuberant yin cold) are compounded by profuse sweating, yang qi is lost further, leaving a state of yin alone.
