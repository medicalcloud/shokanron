# §12 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Question: What is meant by the pulse having "cruel thieves"? The master said: The pulse has wiry, tight, floating, slippery, deep, and hesitant; these six pulses are called cruel thieves, which can cause disease in all the vessels.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

What becomes the pulse of a person's disease is called the eight pathogens: wind, cold, summer heat, and dampness damage from outside; hunger, satiety, overwork, and idleness damage from inside. The meridian vessels are the ying-nutrient and wei-defense; the ying-nutrient and wei-defense are yin and yang. That they cause disease in all the meridian vessels is surely because wind, cold, summer heat, and dampness damage the ying-nutrient and wei-defense and lodge within yin and yang. With wind the pulse is floating; with cold, tight; struck by summer heat, slippery; struck by dampness, rough; when yin is damaged, deep; when yang is damaged, floating. They are called cruel thieves because harming the good is called cruel and injuring the good is called thieving, since they can damage the healthy qi.

## Notes

The clause collectively names "cruel thieves" the six pulses (wiry, tight, floating, slippery, deep, hesitant) that appear due to pathogens and damage the healthy qi.
