# §12 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Question: What is meant by the pulse having "cruel thieves"? The master said: The pulse has wiry, tight, floating, slippery, sunken, and rough; these six pulses are called cruel thieves, which can cause disease in all the vessels.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

What becomes the pulse of a person's disease is called the eight evils: wind, cold, summerheat, and dampness damage from outside; hunger, satiety, overwork, and idleness damage from inside. The channel vessels are the nutritive and defensive; the nutritive and defensive are yin and yang. That they cause disease in all the channel vessels is surely because wind, cold, summerheat, and dampness damage the nutritive and defensive and lodge within yin and yang. With wind the pulse is floating; with cold, tight; struck by summerheat, slippery; struck by dampness, rough; when yin is damaged, sunken; when yang is damaged, floating. They are called cruel thieves because harming the good is called cruel and injuring the good is called thieving, since they can damage the upright qi.

## Notes

The clause collectively names "cruel thieves" the six pulses (wiry, tight, floating, slippery, sunken, rough) that appear due to pathogens and damage the upright qi.
