# §20 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The master said: When the pulse is diseased but the person is not, it is called a walking corpse; lacking prime qi, one suddenly becomes dizzy and collapses, failing to recognize people, and with a short life dies. When the person is diseased but the pulse is not, it is called interior vacuity; lacking grain spirit, though distressed there is no [great] suffering.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The pulse is a person's root. When the pulse is diseased but the person is not, the root is cut off within; although the body is for the time being strong, when the qi is suddenly cut off one becomes dizzy, stiffly collapses, and dies—what is this if not a walking corpse? When the person is diseased but the pulse is not, the root is secure within; although the body is for the time being emaciated, it is merely interior vacuity. Grain spirit is grain qi; when grain qi is sufficient, one naturally becomes calm. The *Neijing* says: "When the bodily qi is in surplus and the pulse qi insufficient, one dies; when the pulse qi is in surplus and the bodily qi insufficient, one lives."

## Notes

The clause shows the idea of valuing the pulse over outward appearance. Even if one looks healthy, a diseased pulse means the root is damaged—a "walking corpse" at risk of sudden death; even if one looks ill, a normal pulse means it is merely insufficiency of stomach qi (interior vacuity) and of little concern.
