# §41 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the shaoyin pulse does not arrive, the kidney qi is faint and essence and blood are scant. Running qi presses urgently, rising into the chest and diaphragm; the ancestral qi, on the contrary, gathers; blood binds below the heart; yang qi retreats downward; heat returns to the inner thighs and stirs with the yin, making the body insensate. This is corpse reversal; one should needle Qimen and Juque. (The ancestral qi is the qi to which the triple burner returns; it has a name but no form, and is the spirit envoy of qi. Descending, it nourishes the penis; hence the ancestral sinew gathers and contracts.)

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Corpse reversal arises from reversal; the body has no awareness and looks like a corpse; hence it is called corpse reversal. When the shaoyin pulse does not emerge, reversal qi lodges in the kidney, and the kidney qi is faint and essence and blood scant. The reversal qi runs upward, filling and blocking the chest and diaphragm, hemming in and obstructing the yang qi, so the ancestral qi, on the contrary, gathers and blood binds below the heart. The *Zhenjing* says: "When the five grains enter the stomach, their dregs, fluids, and ancestral qi divide into three tunnels; the ancestral qi accumulates in the chest, emerges at the throat, penetrates the heart vessel, and moves the breath." And: "The nutritive qi must pour its fluids into the vessels, transform into blood, and nourish the four extremities." Now the reversal qi is extremely severe; when the ancestral qi, on the contrary, gathers and does not move, breathing is cut off; when blood binds below the heart and does not flow, the four limbs become insensate. The yang qi is hemmed in by the reversal qi, cannot diffuse and effuse, retreats downward to the inner thighs, and stirs with the yin. *Ren* means soft; *buren* means not soft and harmonious—feeling neither cold nor heat, pain nor itch. The yang qi does not serve as envoy outside and cannot pass inside; the nutritive and defensive both cannot move; the body is insensate and looks like a corpse. The *Neijing* says: "When reversal qi moves upward, it fills the vessels and departs from the body." Needling Qimen frees the blood bound below the heart; needling Juque moves the ancestral qi in the chest. When blood and qi flow and the reversal qi retreats, one revives.

## Notes

The clause describes the mechanism and needle treatment (Qimen, Juque) of "corpse reversal," in which one loses consciousness and lies motionless like a corpse. The disease name is associated with the story of the crown prince of Guo's "corpse reversal" in the biography of Bian Que in the *Shiji*. The closing interlinear note explains the ancestral qi.
