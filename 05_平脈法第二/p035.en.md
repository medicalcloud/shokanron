# §35 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The cun kou pulse is feeble and hesitant. Feeble means the wei-defensive qi is debilitated; hesitant means the ying-nutrient qi is insufficient. When the wei-defensive qi is debilitated, the complexion is yellow; when the ying-nutrient qi is insufficient, the complexion is green-blue. The ying-nutrient is the root; the wei-defense is the leaves. When both are feeble, root and leaves wither, and there are cold shuddering, cough with counterflow, spitting of fishy-smelling matter, and vomiting of drool and foam.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

When both are feeble, root and leaves wither, and there are cold shuddering, cough with counterflow, spitting of fishy matter, and vomiting of drool and foam. The wei-defense is qi; a yellow complexion means the wei-defensive qi is debilitated. The ying-nutrient is blood; a green-blue complexion means the ying-nutrient blood is debilitated. The ying-nutrient moves within the vessels as the root; the wei-defense moves outside the vessels as the leaves. The ying-nutrient is yin; the wei-defense is yang. The ying-nutrient is the root; the wei-defense is the leaves. When root and leaves are both feeble, the qi of yin and yang declines within, leading to cold shuddering, cough with counterflow, spitting of fishy matter, and vomiting of drool and foam.

## Notes

Likening the ying-nutrient (root) and wei-defense (leaves) to a plant, the clause states that when both decline, symptoms such as changes in complexion, cold, cough, and spitting of fishy phlegm or foam arise. The symptoms resemble the description of atrophy of the lung lobes in the *Jingui Yaolüe*.
