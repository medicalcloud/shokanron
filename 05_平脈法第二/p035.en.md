# §35 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The cun kou pulse is faint and rough. Faint means the defensive qi is debilitated; rough means the nutritive qi is insufficient. When the defensive qi is debilitated, the complexion is yellow; when the nutritive qi is insufficient, the complexion is green-blue. The nutritive is the root; the defensive is the leaves. When both are faint, root and leaves wither, and there are cold shuddering, cough with counterflow, spitting of fishy-smelling matter, and vomiting of drool and foam.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

When both are faint, root and leaves wither, and there are cold shuddering, cough with counterflow, spitting of fishy matter, and vomiting of drool and foam. The defensive is qi; a yellow complexion means the defensive qi is debilitated. The nutritive is blood; a green-blue complexion means the nutritive blood is debilitated. The nutritive moves within the vessels as the root; the defensive moves outside the vessels as the leaves. The nutritive is yin; the defensive is yang. The nutritive is the root; the defensive is the leaves. When root and leaves are both faint, the qi of yin and yang declines within, leading to cold shuddering, cough with counterflow, spitting of fishy matter, and vomiting of drool and foam.

## Notes

Likening the nutritive (root) and defensive (leaves) to a plant, the clause states that when both decline, symptoms such as changes in complexion, cold, cough, and spitting of fishy phlegm or foam arise. The symptoms resemble the description of lung atrophy in the *Jingui Yaolüe*.
