# §10 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Question: The classic says the pulse has weights of three beans, six beans, and so on—what does this mean? The master said: When the one taking the pulse presses with the fingers at a weight like three beans, it is lung qi; like six beans, heart qi; like nine beans, spleen qi; like twelve beans, liver qi; pressing down to the bone, kidney qi. (*Shu* means small beans.) Suppose there is diarrhea and no pulse is seen at all at the cun kou, guan, and chi, but at the chi it occasionally appears slightly once, and the pulse lifts its head again (one version says "press-throw"); this is kidney qi. If a diminished pulse is seen to arrive, it is difficult to treat. (The kidney is overcome by the spleen; the spleen's prevailing does not accord with the season.)

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

*Shu* means beans. The *Nanjing* says: "At a weight like three beans, matching the skin and hair, is the lung region; at a weight like six beans, matching the blood vessels, is the heart region; at a weight like nine beans, matching the flesh, is the spleen region; at a weight like twelve beans, level with the sinews, is the liver region; pressing to the bone, and when the finger is lifted it comes fast, is the kidney region." The visceral qi is examined according to the part each governs.

The *Maijing* says: "Cold qi in the stomach makes the pulse not flow." Diarrhea with no pulse seen means cold qi lodges in the spleen and stomach. Now the pulse occasionally appearing slightly once at the chi means the spleen is vacuous and assailed by kidney qi; the pulse lifting its head again means the spleen is assailed by the kidney. If the chi pulse becomes further diminished, the kidney qi is also debilitated and the spleen again overcomes it; ghost and thief punish each other, so it is said to be difficult to treat. This is "the spleen's prevailing does not accord with the season."

## Notes

The clause describes the "bean method" of pulse diagnosis, examining the qi of the five viscera by the pressure of the fingers (from the weight of three to twelve small beans, down to the bone). It is based on the Fifth Difficulty of the *Nanjing*. The latter description of the pulse vanishing with diarrhea is given an interpretation based on the five-phase restraint cycle (earth restrains water).
