# §4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The master said: Someone from the patient's family comes to request [a visit], saying: "The patient has fever and extreme vexation." The next day the master arrives and the patient is lying facing the wall; this means the heat has already gone. Even if the pulse is not harmonious, one may say it has already recovered. Suppose the patient lies facing the wall and, hearing the master arrive, does not start up in surprise but glances sideways, three times begins to speak and three times stops, and swallows saliva when the pulse is taken—this is feigned illness. If the pulse is spontaneously harmonious, say: "This illness is very serious; one must take emetic and purgative medicines and have acupuncture and moxibustion at several dozen to a hundred places; then it will recover."

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

With fever and extreme vexation, one cannot lie quietly. Now the patient lies quietly facing the wall, so one knows the heat has already gone. [The commentary's "巳去" is read as "已去."]

One who feigns illness is not a good person. Frighten them with words so that they become fearful, and they will recover. "Medicine is intention"—is this not what is meant?

## Notes

The clause describes an example of judging from the patient's appearance that the heat has already gone, and the wit of seeing through feigned illness and "curing" it by frightening the patient with talk of severe treatment. It is regarded as an anecdotal passage showing a practitioner's power of observation and psychological insight.
