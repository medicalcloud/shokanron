# §13 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Question: What is meant by the pulse having "calamitous oddity"? The master said: Suppose a person is ill, the pulse shows taiyang, corresponding to the bodily signs, so a decoction is made accordingly; when the decoction is sent back, after about a mealtime the patient vomits heavily or has diarrhea with pain in the abdomen. The master says: "When I came before, I did not see these signs; now they have changed strangely. This is called calamitous oddity." Again it was asked: "Why did this vomiting and diarrhea occur?" The answer: "Perhaps some medicine was taken earlier, and it has now taken effect; that is why it is a calamitous oddity."

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

When the physician matched the pulse, signs, and medicine, yet, on the contrary, a strange change occurred, the calamity is to be wondered at; therefore it is called calamitous oddity.

## Notes

Regarding "calamitous oddity," in which unexpected symptoms arise even though a medicine matching the pulse and signs was given, the clause points to the possibility that the action of a previously taken medicine has appeared late. It is read as showing the importance of checking the history (medication history).
