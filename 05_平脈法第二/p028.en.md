# §28 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The pulse is floating and large. Floating is wind vacuity; large is strong qi. When wind and qi contend, there will certainly be dormant papules, and the body itches. Itching is called draining wind; if prolonged, it becomes scabby *lai*. (The eyebrows become sparse and the hair thin, there are dry sores on the body, and they smell foul.)

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Scabby *lai* means sparse eyebrows and thin hair, with dry sores on the body that smell foul. The *Neijing* says: "Wind in the vessels becomes *li* (pestilential wind)." [The commentary's "醒臭" is read as "腥臭" (foul smell).]

## Notes

The clause states that wind evil and qi contend to produce papules and itching (draining wind), which if prolonged become scabby *lai*. Some view the description of scabby *lai* (loss of eyebrows and hair, dry sores, foul smell) as referring to leprosy (Hansen's disease).
