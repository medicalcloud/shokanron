# §43 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

At the cun kou, all feebleness is yang collapse; all softness is blood collapse; all weakness is fever; all tightness is cold. All that are assailed by cold have syncope, depressive veiling, and insensibility. Because the stomach has no grain qi and the spleen is rough and blocked, the mouth is tense and one cannot speak, and there are shivering and shuddering.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The wei-defense is yang; feeble means the wei-defensive qi is feeble, so it is called yang collapse. The ying-nutrient is blood; soft means the ying-nutrient qi is weak, so it is called blood collapse. Weak is yin deficiency; deficiency produces fever. Tight is yin prevailing, so it is cold. "Assailed by cold" means both yin and yang are deficient and pathogenic cold assails them. When cold assails the qi deficiency, suppressing and subduing the yang qi so that it cannot diffuse and effuse, syncope results. Depressive veiling means clouding so that one does not recognize people. Insensibility means rigidity without sensation. This is sudden syncope. Because the stomach has no grain qi, the spleen becomes rough and does not pass up and down, so the mouth becomes tense and one cannot speak. Shivering means cold is in the exterior; shuddering means cold is in the interior.

## Notes

The clause summarizes the conditions indicated by feeble, soft, weak, and tight cun kou pulses, and states that pathogenic cold assailing deficiency produces syncope, depressive veiling, and insensibility (a state close to the sudden syncope of the previous paragraph).
