# §43 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

At the cun kou, all faintness is yang collapse; all softness is blood collapse; all weakness is fever; all tightness is cold. All that are assailed by cold have reversal, depressive veiling, and insensibility. Because the stomach has no grain qi and the spleen is rough and blocked, the mouth is tense and one cannot speak, and there are shivering and shuddering.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The defensive is yang; faint means the defensive qi is faint, so it is called yang collapse. The nutritive is blood; soft means the nutritive qi is weak, so it is called blood collapse. Weak is yin vacuity; vacuity produces fever. Tight is yin prevailing, so it is cold. "Assailed by cold" means both yin and yang are vacuous and cold evil assails them. When cold assails the qi vacuity, suppressing and subduing the yang qi so that it cannot diffuse and effuse, reversal results. Depressive veiling means clouding so that one does not recognize people. Insensibility means rigidity without sensation. This is corpse reversal. Because the stomach has no grain qi, the spleen becomes rough and does not pass up and down, so the mouth becomes tense and one cannot speak. Shivering means cold is in the exterior; shuddering means cold is in the interior.

## Notes

The clause summarizes the conditions indicated by faint, soft, weak, and tight cun kou pulses, and states that cold evil assailing vacuity produces reversal, depressive veiling, and insensibility (a state close to the corpse reversal of the previous paragraph).
