# §36 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The fuyang pulse is floating and hollow. Floating means the wei-defensive qi is deficient; hollow means the ying-nutrient qi is damaged. The body is emaciated and the skin is scaly. When floating and hollow contend, the ancestral qi is feeble and debilitated, and the four belongings are severed. (The four belongings are skin, flesh, fat, and marrow; when all are exhausted, the ancestral qi declines.)

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The classic says: "Exuberant wei-defensive qi is called lofty—lofty means suddenly wild and stout; exuberant ying-nutrient qi is called brilliant—brilliant means suddenly moist and lustrous." That the body is emaciated and not stout means the wei-defensive qi is debilitated; that the skin is scaly and not lustrous means the ying-nutrient qi is damaged. The ancestral qi is the qi to which the sanjiao returns. The four belongings are skin, flesh, fat, and marrow. When the ying-nutrient and wei-defense are debilitated and damaged, the ancestral qi too becomes feeble, and the four belongings lose their nourishment, leading to severance.

## Notes

The clause explains emaciation and roughened skin (scaliness), and further the decline of the ancestral qi and of the four belongings (skin, flesh, fat, marrow), by the fuyang pulse being floating (wei-defense deficiency) and hollow (ying-nutrient damage). It is read in contrast with "lofty" and "brilliant" of paragraph 23.
