# §36 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The fuyang pulse is floating and scallion-stalk. Floating means the defensive qi is vacuous; scallion-stalk means the nutritive qi is damaged. The body is emaciated and the skin is scaly. When floating and scallion-stalk contend, the ancestral qi is faint and debilitated, and the four belongings are severed. (The four belongings are skin, flesh, fat, and marrow; when all are exhausted, the ancestral qi declines.)

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The classic says: "Exuberant defensive qi is called lofty—lofty means suddenly wild and stout; exuberant nutritive qi is called brilliant—brilliant means suddenly moist and lustrous." That the body is emaciated and not stout means the defensive qi is debilitated; that the skin is scaly and not lustrous means the nutritive qi is damaged. The ancestral qi is the qi to which the triple burner returns. The four belongings are skin, flesh, fat, and marrow. When the nutritive and defensive are debilitated and damaged, the ancestral qi too becomes faint, and the four belongings lose their nourishment, leading to severance.

## Notes

The clause explains emaciation and roughened skin (scaliness), and further the decline of the ancestral qi and of the four belongings (skin, flesh, fat, marrow), by the fuyang pulse being floating (defensive vacuity) and scallion-stalk (nutritive damage). It is read in contrast with "lofty" and "brilliant" of paragraph 23.
