# §14 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Question: What does the eastern liver pulse resemble? The master said: The liver is wood and is called jueyin. Its pulse is slightly wiry, soft, weak, and long; this is the liver pulse. In liver disease, spontaneously obtaining a soft weak pulse means recovery. If a purely wiry pulse is obtained, it is fatal. How is this known? Because the pulse is straight like a bowstring, the liver viscus is damaged; thus one knows it is fatal.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The *Nanjing* says: "The spring pulse is wiry because the liver is eastern wood; the myriad things begin to grow and have no branches or leaves yet, so the pulse comes soft, weak, and long; hence it is called wiry." This is the normal pulse of the liver. In liver disease, obtaining this pulse means the liver qi is already harmonized. [The commentary's "巳和" is read as "已和."]

Purely wiry means straight like a bowstring and not soft; it has no stomach qi within and is a true-visceral pulse. The *Neijing* says: "The dying liver pulse comes urgent and ever more forceful, like a newly strung bowstring."

## Notes

Paragraphs 14–17 are regarded as a group describing the normal form of the visceral pulses (liver, heart, lung) and the prognosis of their abnormalities. This paragraph states that for the liver pulse, a slight wiriness accompanied by softness (stomach qi) is normal, while a purely wiry pulse without softness is a "true-visceral pulse" in which the stomach qi is exhausted, and is fatal.
