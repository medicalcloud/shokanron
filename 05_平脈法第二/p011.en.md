# §11 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Question: The pulse has mutual assailing—there is the vertical and the horizontal, the counter and the compliant. What does this mean? The master said: When water goes to assail fire, or metal goes to assail wood, it is called vertical. When fire goes to assail water, or wood goes to assail metal, it is called horizontal. When water goes to assail metal, or fire goes to assail wood, it is called counter. When metal goes to assail water, or wood goes to assail fire, it is called compliant.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Metal overcomes wood; water overcomes fire. "Vertical" means giving free rein to its qi and assailing what it overcomes. "Horizontal" means its qi goes perversely and, on the contrary, assails what it cannot overcome. Vertical and horizontal share the sense of unrestrained license. Water is the child of metal; fire is the child of wood. The child going to assail the mother is counter in qi; the mother going to assail the child is compliant in qi.

## Notes

The clause classifies "mutual assailing"—the pulse of one viscus appearing in the position or season of another—into four types according to five-phase restraint and generation: vertical (restrainer → restrained), horizontal (restrained → restrainer), counter (child → mother), and compliant (mother → child). It relates to ideas such as those in the Fifty-Third Difficulty of the *Nanjing*.
