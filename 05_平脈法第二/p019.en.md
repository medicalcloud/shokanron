# §19 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The master said: When the cun pulse does not reach down to the guan, it is yang expiry; when the chi pulse does not reach up to the guan, it is yin expiry. These are all untreatable and certainly fatal. If one calculates the time of life or death for the remaining life, it is set at the seasonal period that overcomes it.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The *Maijing* says: "Yang arises at the cun and stirs at the chi; yin arises at the chi and stirs at the cun." When the cun pulse does not reach down to the guan, it is yang expiry, unable to correspond downward with the chi. When the chi pulse does not reach up to the guan, it is yin expiry, unable to correspond upward with the cun. The *Neijing* says: "When yin and yang separate and are lacking, the essential qi is then cut off." Here yin or yang is one-sidedly cut off, so death is certain in all cases. "Set at the seasonal period that overcomes it" means, for example, that yang expiry dies in spring and summer, and yin expiry dies in autumn and winter.

## Notes

When the pulse exists only at the cun or only at the chi and does not reach the guan, it is a fatal pattern of separation of yin and yang; the clause estimates the time of death in relation to the seasons.
