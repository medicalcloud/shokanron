# §24 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The cun kou pulse is moderate and slow. Moderate means yang qi grows: the complexion is fresh, the face lustrous, the voice in the *shang* tone, and the hair grows long. Slow means yin qi is exuberant: the bone marrow is generated, the blood is full, and the flesh is firm, thin, fresh, and hard. Yin and yang embrace each other, the nutritive and defensive move together, and hard and soft complement each other; this is called strong.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Moderate is the stomach pulse; the stomach unites with the defensive qi. The defensive warms the parting flesh, fills the skin and hair, fattens the interstices, and governs opening and closing. When the defensive is harmonious and the qi relaxed, the complexion is lustrous, the voice clear, and the hair glossy. Slow is the spleen pulse; the spleen unites with the nutritive qi. The nutritive nourishes the bone marrow, fills the flesh, moistens the sinews and network vessels, and frees the joints. When the nutritive is harmonious and the blood full, the bones are upright, the marrow is generated, and the flesh is firm and hard. Yin and yang are in accord, the two qi embrace without opposing each other, the nutritive and defensive flow freely, and hard and soft complement each other—this is robustness.

## Notes

The clause names "strong" the healthy state in which moderate (yang qi, defensive) and slow (yin qi, nutritive) both appear appropriately and yin and yang are in harmony. Moderate and slow here are understood not as disease pulses but as a relaxed, settled pulse.
