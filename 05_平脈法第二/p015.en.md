# §15 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

What does the southern heart pulse resemble? The master said: The heart is fire and is called shaoyin. Its pulse is surging, large, and long; this is the heart pulse. In heart disease, spontaneously obtaining a surging large pulse means recovery. Suppose the pulse comes faint and goes large—this is therefore called reversed, and the disease is in the interior. When the pulse comes with a small head and a large root, it is therefore called overturned, and the disease is in the exterior. When the upper part is faint with a small head, there is sweating. When the lower part is faint with a large root, there is block and repulsion with no passage, and no urination. If there is no sweat on the head, it can be treated; if there is sweat, it is fatal.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The heart is in its prime in summer. In summer yang prevails outside and qi and blood overflow, so its pulse comes surging, large, and long.

The heart pulse is normal when it comes exuberant and goes debilitated. Coming faint and going large is contrary to its proper pulse. The *Neijing* says: "Large means the evil arrives; small means calm." Faint is upright qi; large is evil qi. The coming examines the exterior; coming faint shows the exterior is harmonious. The going examines the interior; going large shows the interior is diseased. The *Neijing* says: "When the heart pulse comes not exuberant and goes, on the contrary, exuberant, this is insufficiency, and the disease is in the center." A small head and a large root mean small in front and large behind. Small is upright qi; large is evil qi. Thus the evil qi was first in the interior and has now returned to the exterior; hence it is called overturned. It speaks only of coming and not going because it is known to be in the exterior. The *Maijing* says: "What is above is exterior; what is below is interior." Sweat is the fluid of the heart. "The upper part faint" means faint on floating [palpation]; "small head" means small in front; thus the qi within the exterior is vacuous, so it governs sweating. "The lower part faint" means faint on deep [palpation]; "large root" means large behind; sunken means in the interior, and large means the disease is advancing. The *Neijing* says: "The heart is a male viscus, and the small intestine is its envoy." Now the evil is severe and moves downward, blocking and closing the small intestine so that the upright qi does not pass; therefore there is no urination; this is called block and repulsion. The *Maijing* says: "When yang qi goes out upward, sweat is seen on the head." Now with block and repulsion the upright qi does not pass; if in addition there is sweat on the head, the yang qi cannot pass downward and is deserting upward. When there is no sweat, although there is block and repulsion, the yang is not yet debilitated and it can still be treated.

## Notes

The clause distinguishes the normal heart pulse (surging, large, and long) from its abnormalities ("reversed" and "overturned") by the coming and going of the pulse and the size of front and back, and judges the prognosis of block and repulsion (a severe condition with urinary retention, vomiting, etc.) by the presence or absence of sweat on the head. Many passages are difficult to construe.
