# §26 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The cun kou pulse is floating and large. Floating is deficiency; large is excess. At the chi it is block; at the cun it is repulsion. With block there is no urination; with repulsion there is vomiting counterflow.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The classic says: "Floating is deficiency." The *Neijing* says: "Large means the disease advances." Floating means the healthy qi is deficient; large means the pathogenic qi is in excess. At the chi, the pathogenic qi blocks and closes the lower jiao, and the interior qi cannot pass downward, so there is no urination. At the cun, the pathogenic qi repels at the upper jiao, preventing food from entering, so there is vomiting counterflow.

## Notes

The clause explains the disease of "block and repulsion," combining urinary retention (block) and vomiting (repulsion), by the position in the cun kou pulse. It pairs with the next paragraph (block and repulsion by the fuyang pulse).
