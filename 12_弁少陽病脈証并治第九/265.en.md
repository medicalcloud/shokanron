# Clause 265 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Cold damage with a wiry fine pulse, headache, and fever belongs to the shaoyang. Shaoyang must not be sweated; if sweated there will be delirious speech. This belongs to the stomach; when the stomach is harmonized there is recovery; when the stomach is not harmonized, there are vexation and palpitations. (One version says "agitation.")

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The classic says: "When all three positions are wiry, the shaoyang has contracted disease." A fine pulse means the evil is gradually transmitted inward. Although there are headache and fever and the exterior is not yet resolved, the evil is lodged in the shaoyang, half in the exterior and half in the interior, so it must not be sweated. Sweating loses the fluids and dries the stomach, and the shaoyang evil is thereby transmitted into the stomach, certainly producing delirious speech; Tiaowei Chengqi tang should be given to purge it. When the stomach is harmonized there is recovery. If not purged, the stomach is invaded by the shaoyang wood evil, so there are vexation and palpitations.

## Notes

Even with headache and fever, a wiry fine pulse indicates shaoyang rather than taiyang, and sweating is contraindicated (one of the "three prohibitions" of shaoyang). Mistaken sweating is said to lose the fluids and shift the disease to the yangming (stomach), producing delirious speech.
