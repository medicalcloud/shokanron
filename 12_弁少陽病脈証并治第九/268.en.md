# Clause 268 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In combined disease of the three yang, the pulse is floating and large, rising up to the guan; one only wishes to sleep, and when the eyes close there is sweating.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The guan pulse is used to examine the qi of the shaoyang. The taiyang pulse is floating; the yangming pulse is large. Because the pulse is floating and large and rises up to the guan, one knows it is combined disease of the three yang. When the gallbladder is hot there is sleep. In shaoyin disease one also only wishes to sleep, but there is no sweating when the eyes close, because yin cannot produce sweat. Since one only wishes to sleep and there is sweating when the eyes close, one knows it is combined disease of the three yang with heat in the gallbladder.

## Notes

The clause gives the pulse and signs of combined disease in which all three yang (taiyang, yangming, shaoyang) are affected at once. The desire to sleep resembles shaoyin disease (Clause 281, "only desire to sleep"), but it is distinguished by sweating on closing the eyes (night sweating) due to heat. Interpretations of "rising up to the guan" vary: that the pulse is especially strong at the guan position, or that it overflows toward the cun, among others.
