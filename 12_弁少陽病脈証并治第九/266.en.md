# Clause 266 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When a disease originally Taiyang does not resolve and shifts into the Shaoyang, with hardness and fullness below the rib-sides, dry retching and inability to eat, alternating fever and chills, not yet treated by vomiting or purgation, and a deep tight pulse, give Xiao Chaihu tang. Formula 1.

Chaihu 8 liang; renshen 3 liang; huangqin 3 liang; gancao 3 liang (mix-fried); banxia half a sheng (washed); shengjiang 3 liang (cut); dazao 12 pieces (broken).

Boil the seven ingredients above in 1 dou 2 sheng of water to obtain 6 sheng, remove the dregs, decoct again to obtain 3 sheng, and take 1 sheng warm, three times a day.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Shifting from Taiyang into Shaoyang means the exterior pathogen enters the interior. Hardness and fullness below the rib-sides, inability to eat, and alternating fever and chills mean the pathogen is between the half-exterior and half-interior. If vomiting or purgation had already been used and the pulse were deep and tight, the pathogenic qi would have entered the bowel as interior excess. [Here] vomiting and purgation have not yet been used, yet the pulse is deep and tight; although the transmission inward is deep, it has not fully entered the bowel, and the exterior is still unresolved. Xiao Chaihu tang is given to harmonize and resolve it. [The commentary's "巳経" is read as "已経" (already).]

## Notes

The clause describes the typical Xiao Chaihu tang pattern following the shift from Taiyang to Shaoyang disease.

- A deep tight pulse usually indicates the interior or cold, but here, since there has been no vomiting or purgation, it is interpreted as the stage where the pathogen is heading inward but has not yet entered the bowel, so Xiao Chaihu tang is used.
- The decoction method of "removing the dregs and decocting again" is considered characteristic of the Chaihu formulas.
