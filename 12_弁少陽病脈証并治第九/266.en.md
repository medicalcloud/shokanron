# Clause 266 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When a disease originally taiyang does not resolve and shifts into the shaoyang, with hardness and fullness below the rib-sides, dry retching and inability to eat, alternating chills and fever, not yet treated by vomiting or purgation, and a sunken tight pulse, give Xiao Chaihu tang. Formula 1.

Chaihu 8 liang; renshen 3 liang; huangqin 3 liang; gancao 3 liang (mix-fried); banxia half a sheng (washed); shengjiang 3 liang (cut); dazao 12 pieces (broken).

Boil the seven ingredients above in 1 dou 2 sheng of water to obtain 6 sheng, remove the dregs, decoct again to obtain 3 sheng, and take 1 sheng warm, three times a day.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Shifting from taiyang into shaoyang means the exterior evil enters the interior. Hardness and fullness below the rib-sides, inability to eat, and alternating chills and fever mean the evil is between the half-exterior and half-interior. If vomiting or purgation had already been used and the pulse were sunken and tight, the evil qi would have entered the bowel as interior repletion. [Here] vomiting and purgation have not yet been used, yet the pulse is sunken and tight; although the transmission inward is deep, it has not fully entered the bowel, and the exterior is still unresolved. Xiao Chaihu tang is given to harmonize and resolve it. [The commentary's "巳経" is read as "已経" (already).]

## Notes

The clause describes the typical Xiao Chaihu tang pattern following the shift from taiyang to shaoyang disease.

- A sunken tight pulse usually indicates the interior or cold, but here, since there has been no vomiting or purgation, it is interpreted as the stage where the evil is heading inward but has not yet entered the bowel, so Xiao Chaihu tang is used.
- The decoction method of "removing the dregs and decocting again" is considered characteristic of the Chaihu formulas.
