# Clause 304 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Shaoyin disease, one or two days after contracting it, with harmony in the mouth and aversion to cold on the back: it should be moxaed, and Fuzi tang governs it. Formula 4.

Fuzi 2 pieces (blast-fried, remove the skin, break into eight pieces); fuling 3 liang; renshen 2 liang; baizhu 4 liang; shaoyao 3 liang.

Boil the five ingredients above in 8 sheng of water to obtain 3 sheng, remove the dregs, and take 1 sheng warm, three times a day.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

When heat lodges in the shaoyin, the mouth is dry, the tongue parched, and there is thirst. Harmony in the mouth means neither bitter nor dry, so there is no heat. The back is yang; aversion to cold on the back means the yang qi is weak and the yin qi prevails. The classic says: "Aversion to cold without fever arises in the yin." Moxa it to assist yang and disperse yin, and give Fuzi tang to warm the channels and dissipate cold.

(Nature and flavor) Fuzi: acrid, hot. Fuling: sweet, neutral. Renshen: sweet, warm. Baizhu: sweet, warm. Shaoyao: sour, neutral.

Dissipate with acrid—the acridity of fuzi dissipates cold. Moderate with sweet—the sweetness of fuling, renshen, and baizhu supplements yang. Contract with sour—the sourness of shaoyao supports yin. The reason is that leaning toward yin or toward yang becomes disease; when fire tends to repletion, water should level it; one does not want either to prevail one-sidedly.

## Notes

The clause warms shaoyin yang vacuity with exuberant cold using Fuzi tang.

- Harmony in the mouth (neither bitter nor dry) indicates no interior heat, and aversion to cold on the back is attributed to yang vacuity.
- Fuzi tang is close in composition to Zhenwu tang, with renshen instead of shengjiang and increased fuzi and baizhu, and is explained as having stronger warming-supplementing power.
- The moxa site is not specified; various views name Dazhui (GV14), Guanyuan (CV4), Geshu (BL17), and others.
