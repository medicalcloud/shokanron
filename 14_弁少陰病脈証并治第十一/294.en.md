# Clause 294 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Shaoyin disease with only cold limbs and no sweating: if sweating is forcibly induced, it will certainly stir the blood. It is not known by which path it will come out; it may come from the mouth and nose or from the eyes. This is called cold limbs below with exhaustion above and is difficult to treat.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Only cold limbs and no sweating mean heat is moving in the interior. When sweating is nevertheless forced, it makes the meridians and collaterals deficient; the heat takes advantage of the meridian deficiency, presses the blood to move recklessly, and it comes out where there is deficiency—from the mouth and nose, or from the eyes. All cold limbs belongs to the lower; only cold limbs is cold limbs below, and loss of blood above is upper exhaustion. Qi is damaged and blood is harmed; the pathogen is severe and the healthy qi deficient, so it is difficult to treat.

## Notes

When sweating is forced in Shaoyin disease with cold extremities and no sweat, yang qi becomes deficient below (cold limbs below) and blood comes out from the upper orifices (mouth, nose, eyes), exhausting yin blood (upper exhaustion), a dangerous state. It is regarded as reiterating the prohibition of sweating in Shaoyin disease (Clauses 285, 286).
