# Clause 300 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Shaoyin disease with a faint, fine, sunken pulse, only a desire to lie down, sweating without vexation, and a spontaneous desire to vomit: when by the fifth or sixth day there is spontaneous diarrhea and again vexation and agitation with inability to lie down and sleep, it is fatal.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Although the yin qi is just exuberant, when by the fifth or sixth day the transmission through the channels is complete and the yang qi is able to return, there is recovery. When, on the contrary, there is further spontaneous diarrhea with vexation-agitation and inability to lie down and sleep, the upright qi is weak and the yang cannot return; the disease overcomes the viscus, so it is fatal.

## Notes

When typical shaoyin disease after five or six days develops vexation and agitation with sleeplessness in addition to diarrhea, it is not yang returning but a sign of yin and yang separating and vacuous yang in disorder; the clause deems it fatal. It is the last of the group of fatal shaoyin patterns (Clauses 295–300).
