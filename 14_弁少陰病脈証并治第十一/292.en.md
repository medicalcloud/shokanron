# Clause 292 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Shaoyin disease with vomiting and diarrhea, when the hands and feet are not counterflow cold and, on the contrary, there is fever, is not fatal. When the pulse does not arrive (one version writes "is insufficient"), moxa the shaoyin with seven cones.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The classic says: "Shaoyin disease with vomiting and diarrhea, agitation and vexation, and counterflow cold of the four limbs is fatal." When there are vomiting and diarrhea but the hands and feet are not reversal-cold, the yang qi has not declined, so even with fever, on the contrary, it is not fatal. When the pulse does not arrive, it is sudden vacuity from vomiting and diarrhea. Moxa the shaoyin with seven cones to free its pulse.

## Notes

In shaoyin vomiting and diarrhea, the hands and feet not being cold and rather having fever is a sign that yang qi remains, and the prognosis is good. When the pulse suddenly cannot be felt due to vomiting and diarrhea, moxibustion is used to free the yang qi. The "shaoyin" site to moxa is not specified; one view is that it is Taixi (KI3), among others.
