# Clause 319 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Shaoyin disease with diarrhea for six or seven days, cough with retching and thirst, and heart vexation with inability to sleep: Zhuling tang governs it. Formula 18.

Zhuling (remove the skin), fuling, ejiao, zexie, and huashi, 1 liang each.

Take the five ingredients above with 4 sheng of water; first boil four of them to obtain 2 sheng, remove the dregs, add the ejiao and melt it completely, and take 7 ge warm, three times a day.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Diarrhea without thirst means interior cold. The classic says: "Spontaneous diarrhea without thirst belongs to the taiyin, because its viscus is cold." Here there are diarrhea, retching, and thirst, so one knows it is not interior cold; heart vexation with inability to sleep shows heat is involved. Zhuling tang is given to percolate and drain the urine, separating water and grain. The classic says: "If it still does not stop, one should promote urination." Is this not what is meant?

## Notes

The clause uses Zhuling tang for the shaoyin pattern in which water and heat combine with yin vacuity. Diarrhea, cough, retching, thirst, and vexation with sleeplessness are explained as water-heat moving up and down. Zhuling tang also appears in Clause 223 of the yangming chapter. It is cited as an example of the idea of promoting urination to separate the water from the stool ("promoting urination to firm the stool").
