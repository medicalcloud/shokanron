# Clause 313 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Shaoyin disease with pain in the throat: Banxia san and [Banxia] tang govern it. Formula 12.

Banxia (washed); guizhi (remove the bark); gancao (mix-fried).

Take equal parts of the three ingredients above, pound each separately into powder, then combine and prepare; take a square-inch spoonful mixed with white drink, three times a day. If one cannot take it as powder, bring 1 sheng of water to a boil seven times, add two square-inch spoonfuls of the powder, boil three more times, take it off the fire, let cool slightly, and swallow a little at a time. Banxia is toxic and should not be taken as powder.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Gancao tang governs sore throat from heat lodged in the shaoyin; Jiegeng tang governs sore throat from cold and heat contending in the shaoyin; Banxia san and tang govern sore throat from cold lodged in the shaoyin.

(Nature and flavor) Banxia: acrid, warm. Guizhi: acrid, hot. Gancao: sweet, neutral.

The *Neijing* says: "When cold prevails, level it with acrid-hot and assist with sweet-bitter." The acridity of banxia and guizhi dissipates channel cold; the sweetness of gancao moderates the upright qi.

## Notes

For sore throat from cold evil lodged in the throat, banxia, guizhi, and gancao are used, in both powder and decoction forms. Many view the final "banxia is toxic and should not be taken as powder" as a later annotation that entered the text. Cheng Wuji's commentary distinguishes the three sore-throat formulas (Gancao tang, Jiegeng tang, Banxia san and tang) as for heat, cold-heat, and cold.
