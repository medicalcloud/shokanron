# Clause 317 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Shaoyin disease with diarrhea of undigested grain, interior cold and exterior heat, reversal counterflow of the hands and feet, a faint pulse verging on expiry, the body on the contrary without aversion to cold, a red facial complexion, and perhaps abdominal pain, or dry retching, or sore throat, or the diarrhea stopping but the pulse not emerging: Tongmai Sini tang governs it. Formula 16.

Gancao 2 liang (mix-fried); fuzi, a large one, 1 piece (used raw, remove the skin, break into eight pieces); ganjiang 3 liang (4 liang may be used for a strong person).

Boil the three ingredients above in 3 sheng of water to obtain 1 sheng 2 ge, remove the dregs, and take warm in two divided doses. When the pulse emerges at once, there is recovery. When the facial complexion

is red, add 9 stalks of scallion. For pain in the abdomen, remove the scallion and add shaoyao 2 liang. For retching, add shengjiang 2 liang. For sore throat, remove the shaoyao and add jiegeng 1 liang. When the diarrhea stops but the pulse does not emerge, remove the jiegeng and add renshen 2 liang. Take it only when the disease fully corresponds to the formula.

[In the base the paragraph breaks between "面色" (facial complexion) and "赤者" (is red), but "面色赤者" is one continuous phrase.]

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Diarrhea of undigested grain, reversal counterflow of the hands and feet, and a faint pulse verging on expiry are interior cold. Body heat without aversion to cold and a red complexion are exterior heat. This is yin severe within, repelling yang to the outside so that they do not communicate. Tongmai Sini tang is given to dissipate yin and free yang.

Scallion is acrid in flavor and frees the yang qi.

The sourness of shaoyao frees cold and eases [pain]; pain in the abdomen means the qi is not flowing.

Dissipate with acrid; retching means the qi is not dissipated.

When there is something like a lump in the throat, adding jiegeng can dissipate it.

When the diarrhea stops but the pulse does not emerge, there is blood loss; renshen is added to supplement it. The classic says: "A faint pulse with diarrhea is blood loss; Sini jia Renshen tang governs it." 脈病皆与 [The commentary breaks off here; it appears to be a fragment of the clause's final words "病皆与方相応者，乃服之" that has mixed in.]

## Notes

The clause uses Tongmai Sini tang for "yin exuberance repelling yang" (true cold with false heat), in which yin cold is extremely exuberant and the slight remaining yang is pushed outward.

- The feeling of body heat, facial flushing, and absence of aversion to cold are false heat; the essence is interior cold with diarrhea of undigested grain, reversal of the limbs, and a faint pulse verging on expiry.
- Tongmai Sini tang has the same composition as Sini tang, with increased fuzi and ganjiang to strengthen its power to restore the pulse.
- The closing "take it only when the disease fully corresponds to the formula" is interpreted as a caution to take it only when pattern and formula match.
