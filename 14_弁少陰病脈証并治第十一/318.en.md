# Clause 318 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Shaoyin disease with counterflow cold of the four limbs, when the person may have cough, or palpitations, or inhibited urination, or pain in the abdomen, or diarrhea with rectal heaviness: Sini san governs it. Formula 17.

Gancao (mix-fried); zhishi (broken, soaked in water, mix-fried dry); chaihu; shaoyao.

Pound and sift ten fen each of the four ingredients above, and take a square-inch spoonful mixed with white drink, three times a day. For cough, add wuweizi and ganjiang, five fen each; this also governs diarrhea. For palpitations, add guizhi five fen. For inhibited urination, add fuling five fen. For pain in the abdomen, add one fuzi blast-fried until it cracks. For diarrhea with rectal heaviness, first boil 3 sheng of xiebai in 5 sheng of water to obtain 3 sheng, remove the dregs, put three square-inch spoonfuls of the powder into the decoction, boil to obtain 1.5 sheng, and take warm in two divided doses.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Counterflow of the four limbs means the four limbs are not warm. When the evil of cold damage is in the three yang, the hands and feet are certainly hot; when transmitted to the taiyin, the hands and feet are spontaneously warm; when it reaches the shaoyin, the evil heat gradually deepens, so the four limbs are counterflow and not warm; when it reaches the jueyin, the hands and feet are reversal-cold, which is more severe than counterflow. Sini san dissipates the heat transmitted to the yin.

(Nature and flavor) Gancao: sweet, neutral. Zhishi: bitter, cold. Chaihu: bitter, cold. Shaoyao: sour, slightly cold.

The *Neijing* says: "When heat spreads within, assist with sweet and bitter, contract it with sour, and effuse it with bitter." The sweetness and bitterness of zhishi and gancao drain interior heat; the sourness of shaoyao contracts the yin qi; the bitterness of chaihu effuses exterior heat.

When the lung is cold and qi counterflows, there is cough. The sourness of wuweizi contracts the counterflow qi; the acridity of ganjiang dissipates lung cold. It also governs diarrhea because the lung and large intestine are exterior–interior partners; cough above and diarrhea below are treated in much the same way.

Palpitations mean the qi is vacuous and cannot flow, so there is thumping palpitation below the heart. *Gui* (cinnamon) is like *gui* (jade tablet); it guides the yang qi, as if using heat as the messenger. [The phrase "若熱以使" is hard to construe; this is a tentative translation.]

Fuling is sweet and bland in flavor and is used to percolate and drain [water].

When the interior is vacuous and meets evil, there is pain; fuzi is added to supplement the vacuity.

Diarrhea with rectal heaviness means the qi of the lower burner is stagnant; xiebai is added to drain the qi stagnation.

## Notes

Although in the shaoyin chapter, the cold limbs here are often interpreted not as due to yang vacuity but as qi stagnation preventing yang qi from reaching the four limbs.

- Composed of chaihu, shaoyao, zhishi, and gancao, it is regarded in later times as the basic formula for coursing constrained liver qi, and explained as the source of formulas such as Xiaoyao san.
- Cheng Wuji explains it as "dissipating the heat transmitted to the yin."
- The modification amounts are given in fen because it is a powder, it is said.
