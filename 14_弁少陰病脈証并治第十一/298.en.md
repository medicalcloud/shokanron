# Clause 298 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Shaoyin disease with counterflow of the four limbs, aversion to cold with a curled-up body, the pulse not arriving, and agitation without vexation is fatal. (One version writes: "vomiting and diarrhea with agitation and counterflow is fatal.")

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Counterflow of the four limbs, aversion to cold, and a curled-up body mean the cold is severe; the pulse not arriving means the true qi is cut off. Vexation is heat; agitation is disorder, like the agitation of indignant restlessness. Proceeding from vexation to agitation means the heat comes gradually, which is still acceptable. Agitation without vexation means the qi is about to desert and is struggling. It is like a lamp about to go out suddenly flaring bright—how can it last long?

## Notes

The clause deems fatal the sudden appearance of agitation (restless thrashing) without vexation (internal distress from heat), a phenomenon just before yang qi deserts. Cheng Wuji's simile of "a lamp flaring before it goes out" is well known.
