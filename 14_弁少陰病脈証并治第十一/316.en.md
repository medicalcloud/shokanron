# Clause 316 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Shaoyin disease that has not ceased in two or three days: when by the fourth or fifth day there are abdominal pain, inhibited urination, heavy aching pain of the four limbs, and spontaneous diarrhea, there is water qi. When the person may have cough, or free urination, or diarrhea, or retching, Zhenwu tang (Shinbuto) governs it. Formula 15.

Fuling (bukuryo) 3 liang; shaoyao (shakuyaku) 3 liang; baizhu (byakujutsu) 2 liang; shengjiang (shokyo) 3 liang (cut); fuzi (bushi) 1 piece (blast-fried, remove the skin, break into eight pieces).

Boil the five ingredients above in 8 sheng of water to obtain 3 sheng, remove the dregs, and take 7 ge warm, three times a day. For cough, add wuweizi (gomishi) half a sheng, xixin (saishin) 1 liang, and ganjiang (kankyo) 1 liang. For free urination, remove the fuling. For diarrhea, remove the shaoyao and add ganjiang 2 liang. For retching, remove the fuzi and add shengjiang to make half a jin together with the previous amount.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

At two or three days of Shaoyin disease the pathogenic qi is still shallow; by the fourth or fifth day the pathogenic qi is already deep. The kidney governs water; when the kidney is diseased it cannot control water, and water-fluid retention collects as water qi. Abdominal pain means cold-damp is severe within. Heavy aching pain of the four limbs means cold-damp is severe without. Inhibited urination with spontaneous diarrhea means dampness prevails and water and grain are not separated. The *Neijing* says: "When dampness prevails there is soggy diarrhea." Zhenwu tang is given to boost yang qi and dissipate cold-damp.

(Nature and flavor) Fuling: sweet, neutral. Shaoyao: sour, neutral. Shengjiang: acrid, warm. Baizhu: sweet, warm. Fuzi: acrid, hot.

The spleen dislikes dampness, and sweet first enters the spleen—the sweetness of fuling and baizhu boosts the spleen and expels water. When cold prevails, level it with acrid-hot; when dampness prevails, assist with sour-neutral—the sourness and acridity of fuzi, shaoyao, and shengjiang warm the meridians and dissipate dampness.

Modifications after taking:

For cough from qi counterflow, the sourness of wuweizi contracts the counterflow qi. When water and cold contend there is cough; the acridity of xixin and ganjiang dissipates water and cold.

When urination is free there is no hidden water, so fuling is removed.

The sourness of shaoyao drains qi; the acridity of ganjiang dissipates cold.

Qi counterflow causes retching. Fuzi supplements qi; shengjiang dissipates qi. The *Qianjin* says: "Those prone to retching should take much shengjiang; it is the sacred medicine for those prone to retching."

[The commentary's "巳深" is read as "已深" and "五味予" as "五味子."]

## Notes

The clause uses Zhenwu tang for Shaoyin yang deficiency with collected water qi (yang deficiency with water flooding).

- It is a representative Zhenwu tang clause alongside Clause 82 of the Taiyang chapter (yang deficiency with water flooding from excessive sweating).
- Abdominal pain, inhibited urination, heavy limb pain, and diarrhea are explained as kidney yang declining so that it cannot govern water, and water-damp overflowing inside and out.
- The Song edition names it "Zhenwu tang," but it is said to have originally been "Xuanwu tang," changed in the Song to avoid a taboo character.
