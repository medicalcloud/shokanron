# Clause 325 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Shaoyin disease with diarrhea, a feeble hesitant pulse, retching with sweating, and certainly frequent trips to the privy, yet with, on the contrary, little [stool]: one should warm its upper part by moxibustion. (The *Maijing* says: "Moxa the Jueyin, up to fifty cones.")

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

A feeble pulse means yang collapse; a hesitant pulse means blood collapse. Diarrhea, retching, and sweating are yang collapse and blood collapse. The fluids are insufficient and there is deficiency cold in the interior, so there are certainly frequent trips to the privy, yet with, on the contrary, little [stool]. Warm the upper part to assist the yang, and moxa it to disperse the yin.

## Notes

For Shaoyin disease in which both yang qi and yin blood are deficient and diarrhea is frequent but scant (qi sinking downward), the upper part is moxaed to raise the yang qi. The site of "its upper part" is not specified; one view identifies it as Baihui (GV20), among others. It is the last clause of the Shaoyin chapter.
