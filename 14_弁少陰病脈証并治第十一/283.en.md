# Clause 283 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the patient's pulse is tight at both yin and yang, and on the contrary there is sweating, it is yang collapse. This belongs to the Shaoyin; by rule there should be sore throat and also vomiting and diarrhea.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

A pulse tight at both yin and yang is Shaoyin cold damage, and by rule there should be no sweating. Sweating, on the contrary, means the yang is deficient and cannot secure. Therefore it is called yang collapse. Since there is no yang and only yin, it belongs to the Shaoyin. The *Neijing* says: "When the pathogen lodges in the collaterals of the Shaoyin, it causes pain of the throat and inability to take food." The Shaoyin cold is severe, so there should be sore throat and also vomiting and diarrhea.

## Notes

Sweating despite a tight (cold) pulse is not exterior-pattern sweating but "yang collapse," the leaking out of collapsing yang qi, belonging to Shaoyin disease. Sore throat is explained by the Shaoyin meridian passing through the throat, and vomiting and diarrhea by exuberant yin cold.
