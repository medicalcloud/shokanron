# Clause 314 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Shaoyin disease with diarrhea: Baitong tang governs it. Formula 13.

Congbai (scallion white) 4 stalks; ganjiang 1 liang; fuzi 1 piece (raw, remove the skin, break into eight pieces).

Boil the three ingredients above in 3 sheng of water to obtain 1 sheng, remove the dregs, and take warm in two divided doses.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The shaoyin governs water. When cold lodges in the shaoyin and it cannot control water, there is spontaneous diarrhea. Baitong tang warms the interior and dissipates cold.

(Nature and flavor) Congbai: acrid, warm. Ganjiang: acrid, hot. Fuzi: acrid, hot.

The *Neijing* says: "The kidney suffers from dryness; quickly eat acrid to moisten it." The acridity of congbai frees the yang qi; the acridity of ganjiang and fuzi dissipates yin cold.

## Notes

The clause uses Baitong tang for shaoyin diarrhea due to vacuity cold. Baitong tang is Sini tang with congbai replacing gancao, and congbai is said to give it strong power to free yang qi. Since the clause is terse, it is often interpreted together with the next clause (315) as used for the pattern in which exuberant yin pushes yang upward ("upcast yang").
