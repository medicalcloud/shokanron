# Clause 306 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Shaoyin disease with diarrhea and stool with pus and blood: Taohua tang (Peach Blossom Decoction) governs it. Formula 6.

Chishizhi (halloysite) 1 jin (use one half whole, sift one half into powder); ganjiang 1 liang; jingmi (nonglutinous rice) 1 sheng.

Take the three ingredients above with 7 sheng of water, boil until the rice is cooked, remove the dregs, and take 7 ge warm, adding a square-inch spoonful of chishizhi powder, three times a day. If one dose cures, do not take the rest.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

In yang disease, diarrhea with pus and blood involves heat. In shaoyin disease, diarrhea with pus and blood means the lower burner is not contained and the interior is cold. Taohua tang is given to secure the lower and dissipate cold.

(Nature and flavor) Chishizhi: sweet, warm. Ganjiang: acrid, hot. Jingmi: sweet, neutral.

Astringency can remove desertion—the astringency of chishizhi secures the intestines and stomach. Dissipate with acrid—the acridity of ganjiang dissipates interior cold. The sweetness of jingmi supplements the upright qi.

## Notes

For diarrhea with pus and blood due to shaoyin vacuity cold (slippery desertion with the lower burner not contained), Taohua tang uses chishizhi to astringe the intestines and ganjiang to warm. It is contrasted with pus and blood from heat (Clause 258; Baitouweng tang in the jueyin chapter, etc.). The formula name is said to derive from the color of chishizhi. Taking half the chishizhi as powder in the decoction is explained as making it stay directly in the intestines.
