# Clause 341 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In cold damage with fever for four days, cold limbs, on the contrary, for three days, and heat again for four days, the coldness of the limbs is less and the heat more, so the disease should recover. When from the fourth to the seventh day the heat is not removed, there will certainly be stool with pus and blood.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The disease should recover, but when from the fourth to the seventh day the heat is not removed, there will certainly be stool with pus and blood afterward. Heat first and cold limbs afterward means the pathogen [with] the yang qi is transmitted inward. Fever means the pathogenic qi is in the exterior; cold limbs after the fourth day means it has been transmitted to the yin. When after three days it is transmitted again to the yang meridians, there is heat again. Less cold limbs means the pathogen is slight; more heat means yang prevails, so the disease recovers. When by the seventh day the transmission through the meridians is complete and the heat is removed, there is recovery. When the heat is not removed, the surplus heat qi contends internally with the blood of the Jueyin, and there will certainly be pus and blood in the stool afterward. [The opening phrase of the commentary looks like a summary of the clause, and the phrase "陽気邪伝裏" is hard to construe; this is a tentative translation.]

## Notes

One of the clauses judging prognosis by the days of alternating cold limbs and fever. If the days of heat exceed those of cold limbs, yang prevails and there is recovery, but if heat remains too long, yang becomes excessive and causes stool with pus and blood (the same purport as Clause 334).
