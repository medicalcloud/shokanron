# Clause 357 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In cold damage of six or seven days, after great purgation, when the cun pulse is sunken and slow, the hands and feet show reversal counterflow, the pulse of the lower part does not arrive, the throat is inhibited, pus and blood are spat, and diarrhea does not stop, it is difficult to treat; Mahuang Shengma tang governs it. Formula 9.

Mahuang 2.5 liang (remove the nodes); shengma 1 liang 1 fen; danggui 1 liang 1 fen; zhimu 18 zhu; huangqin 18 zhu; weirui 18 zhu (one version writes changpu); shaoyao 6 zhu; tianmendong 6 zhu (remove the core); guizhi 6 zhu (remove the bark); fuling 6 zhu; gancao 6 zhu (mix-fried); shigao 6 zhu (crushed, wrapped in cotton); baizhu 6 zhu; ganjiang 6 zhu.

Take the fourteen ingredients above with 1 dou of water; first boil the mahuang to "one liang" boils, remove the foam on top, add the other medicinals, boil to obtain 3 sheng, remove the dregs, and take warm in three divided doses, spaced apart by about the time it takes to cook 3 dou of rice, finishing it all. When sweat comes out, there is recovery. [As in Clause 302, "一両沸" is generally read as "一二沸" (bring to a boil once or twice).]

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Six or seven days of cold damage is when the evil is transmitted to the jueyin. After great purgation, the qi of the lower burner is vacuous and the yang qi sinks inward, so the cun pulse is slow, the hands and feet show reversal counterflow, and the pulse of the lower part does not arrive. The jueyin vessel penetrates the diaphragm, rises to pour into the lung, and follows the throat. [The evil] in the jueyin follows the channel and shoots into the lung; through loss of fluids, it eventually forms lung atrophy, so the throat is inhibited and pus and blood are spat. The *Jingui Yaolüe* says: "From where is the disease of lung atrophy contracted? From diarrhea caused by drastic medicines, doubly losing the fluids, it is contracted." If the diarrhea does not stop, the interior qi is greatly vacuous, so it is said to be difficult to treat. Mahuang Shengma tang is given to regulate the qi of the liver and lung.

(Nature and flavor) Mahuang: sweet, warm. Shengma: sweet, neutral. Danggui: acrid, warm. Zhimu: bitter, cold. Huangqin: bitter, cold. Weirui: sweet, neutral. Shigao: sweet, cold. Baizhu: sweet, warm. Ganjiang: acrid, hot. Shaoyao: sour, neutral. Tianmendong: sweet, neutral. Guizhi: acrid, hot. Fuling: sweet, neutral. Gancao: sweet, neutral.

The *Yuhan* says: "Qi of great heat is taken with cold; qi of extreme heat is effused by sweating." The sweetness of mahuang and shengma effuses floating heat. When the upright qi is vacuous, moisten it with acrid—the acridity of danggui, guizhi, and ganjiang dissipates cold. When there is heat above, drain it with bitter—the bitterness of zhimu and huangqin cools the heart and removes heat. When fluids are scant, moisten with sweet—the sweetness of fuling and baizhu moderates the spleen and generates fluids. When the lung is dry and the qi hot, contract it with sour and moderate it with sweet—the sourness of shaoyao astringes counterflow qi, and the sweetness of weirui, tianmendong, shigao, and gancao moistens the lung and removes heat. [The commentary's "知毋" is read as "知母" (zhimu).]

## Notes

The clause uses Mahuang Shengma tang for a severe mixed cold-heat condition in which mistaken purgation has caused yang qi to sink inward, producing heat above (inhibited throat, spitting pus and blood) and cold below (unceasing diarrhea, reversal of the limbs).

- Composed of fourteen ingredients, it is a complex formula combining dispersing, heat-clearing, center-warming, and yin-nourishing actions.
- Since antiquity some have doubted that it is Zhongjing's formula because its composition seems disorderly.
- That a formula is given even though the condition is called "difficult to treat" is also debated.
