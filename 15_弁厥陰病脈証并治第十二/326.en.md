# Clause 326 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Jueyin disease is wasting and thirst, qi rising up to strike the heart, pain and heat in the heart, hunger without desire to eat, and vomiting of roundworms upon eating. If it is purged, the diarrhea will not stop.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

When the pathogen is transmitted to the Jueyin, the heat is already deep. When the pathogen is transmitted from the Taiyang to the Taiyin, there are abdominal fullness and dry throat, but thirst has not yet formed. When the pathogen reaches the Shaoyin, the mouth is dry, the tongue parched, and there is thirst, but dispersion has not yet formed. Wasting and thirst forms upon reaching the Jueyin because the heat is severe and can disperse water. Drinking much water with scant urine is called wasting and thirst. Wood generates fire, and liver qi communicates with the heart; when heat lodges in the Jueyin, qi rises to strike the heart, and there are pain and heat in the heart. Six or seven days of cold damage is when the Jueyin contracts disease; when transmission through the meridians is complete, it should enter the bowel. The stomach is deficient and heat lodges there, so there is hunger without desire to eat. Roundworms are in the stomach; when there is no food they stir, and smelling food they come out, so upon eating roundworms are vomited. This is heat in the Jueyin meridian. If it is purged at once, the stomach qi is made deficient, the Jueyin wood pathogen assails [earth], and there will certainly be vomiting and diarrhea that do not stop.

## Notes

Regarded as the outline clause of Jueyin disease. It is often interpreted as a condition of "heat above and cold below" (mixed cold and heat).

- Wasting and thirst, qi rising to strike the heart, and pain and heat in the heart are said to indicate heat above, while hunger without being able to eat and vomiting of roundworms indicate cold in the stomach (middle jiao).
- It warns that purgation would intensify the cold below and cause unceasing diarrhea.
- The mention of vomiting roundworms connects to the Wumei wan (Ubaigan) pattern of Clause 338.
