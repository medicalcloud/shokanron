# Clause 333 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In cold damage with a slow pulse for six or seven days, Huangqin tang was, on the contrary, given to clear away the heat. A slow pulse means cold; now giving Huangqin tang further removes the heat, so the abdomen should be cold and one should be unable to eat. Now one, on the contrary, can eat; this is called removal of the center, and it is certainly fatal.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

A slow pulse for six or seven days in cold damage means the cold qi is already deep. When, on the contrary, Huangqin tang, a cold medicine, is given, the two colds contend and the abdomen should be cold; cold cannot disperse grain, so one cannot eat. One who, on the contrary, can eat has removal of the center. In all four seasons, stomach qi is the root; since the stomach qi is already cut off, it is said to be certainly fatal.

## Notes

The clause describes a fatal pattern in which, because of fever, the cold-natured Huangqin tang was mistakenly used to remove heat in a patient with a slow (cold) pulse, exhausting stomach yang and producing removal of the center. It is regarded as a concrete example of the removal of the center in the previous clause.
