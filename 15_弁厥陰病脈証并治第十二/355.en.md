# Clause 355 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the patient's hands and feet are reversal-cold and the pulse is suddenly tight, the evil is bound in the chest. When there are fullness and vexation below the heart and hunger with inability to eat, the disease is in the chest and must be treated by vomiting; Guadi san is appropriate. Formula 7.

Guadi (melon stalk); chixiaodou.

Take equal parts of the two ingredients above, pound and sift them separately, put them together into a mortar and process further. Separately, boil 1 ge of fragrant fermented soybeans in 7 ge of hot water to make a thin gruel, remove the dregs, take the liquid and mix with one qian-spoonful of the powder, and take it warm in one dose. If there is no vomiting, add a little more; stop once satisfactory vomiting is obtained. Those with blood loss and those of vacuous constitution must not be given Guadi san.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Reversal-cold hands and feet mean the evil qi has sunk inward. A tight, firm pulse indicates repletion. When the evil qi enters the bowel, the pulse becomes sunken; now the pulse is suddenly tight, so one knows the evil is bound in the chest as repletion. Therefore there are fullness and vexation below the heart. When there is no evil in the stomach, one is prone to hunger; because the disease is in the chest, though hungry one cannot eat. Guadi san is given to induce vomiting of the evil in the chest.

## Notes

For cold extremities due to replete evil such as phlegm or food lodged in the chest, preventing yang qi from reaching the limbs (phlegm reversal), Guadi san induces vomiting. The Guadi san pattern also appears in Clause 166 of the taiyang chapter. It is added that it is contraindicated for those with blood loss or weak constitution.
