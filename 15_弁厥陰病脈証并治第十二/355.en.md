# Clause 355 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the patient's hands and feet are cold and the pulse is suddenly tight, the pathogen is bound in the chest. When there are fullness and vexation below the heart and hunger with inability to eat, the disease is in the chest and must be treated by vomiting; Guadi san (Kateisan) is appropriate. Formula 7.

Guadi (katei; melon stalk); chixiaodou (sekishozu).

Take equal parts of the two ingredients above, pound and sift them separately, put them together into a mortar and process further. Separately, boil 1 ge of fragrant fermented soybeans in 7 ge of hot water to make a thin gruel, remove the dregs, take the liquid and mix with one qian-spoonful of the powder, and take it warm in one dose. If there is no vomiting, add a little more; stop once satisfactory vomiting is obtained. Those with blood loss and those of deficient constitution must not be given Guadi san.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Cold hands and feet mean the pathogenic qi has sunk inward. A tight, firm pulse indicates excess. When the pathogenic qi enters the bowel, the pulse becomes deep; now the pulse is suddenly tight, so one knows the pathogen is bound in the chest as excess. Therefore there are fullness and vexation below the heart. When there is no pathogen in the stomach, one is prone to hunger; because the disease is in the chest, though hungry one cannot eat. Guadi san is given to induce vomiting of the pathogen in the chest.

## Notes

For cold extremities due to excess pathogen such as phlegm or food lodged in the chest, preventing yang qi from reaching the limbs (phlegm cold limbs (WHO: phlegm syncope)), Guadi san induces vomiting. The Guadi san pattern also appears in Clause 166 of the Taiyang chapter. It is added that it is contraindicated for those with blood loss or weak constitution.
