# Clause 363 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Diarrhea with the cun pulse, on the contrary, floating and rapid, and the chi spontaneously hesitant, will certainly involve discharging pus and blood.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

In diarrhea the pulse should be deep and slow; floating and rapid, on the contrary, means there is heat in the interior. Hesitant means there is no blood; the chi being spontaneously hesitant means the blood of the intestines and stomach is scattered. It goes down with the diarrhea, and there will certainly be stool with pus and blood. "清" is interchangeable with "圊" (privy). The *Maijing* says: "清 means privy."

## Notes

In diarrhea, a floating rapid cun pulse (heat) and a hesitant chi pulse (damage to blood) mean heat is damaging the blood, causing stool with pus and blood. "清" is said to have shifted from meaning "privy" to meaning defecation.
