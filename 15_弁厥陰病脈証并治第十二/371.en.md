# Clause 371 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Heat diarrhea with rectal heaviness: Baitouweng tang (Pulsatilla Decoction) governs it. Formula 12.

Baitouweng 2 liang; huangbai 3 liang; huanglian 3 liang; qinpi 3 liang.

Boil the four ingredients above in 7 sheng of water to obtain 2 sheng, remove the dregs, and take 1 sheng warm. If not recovered, take another sheng.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Diarrhea reduces the fluids, and heat damages qi. When qi is vacuous with diarrhea, rectal heaviness results. Baitouweng tang is given to dissipate heat and thicken the intestines.

(Nature and flavor) Baitouweng: bitter, cold. Huangbai: bitter, cold. Huanglian: bitter, cold. Qinpi: bitter, cold.

The *Neijing* says: "The kidney desires firmness; quickly eat bitter to firm it." Diarrhea makes the lower burner vacuous, so a preparation of pure bitter firms it.

## Notes

For heat diarrhea with tenesmus (urgency and rectal heaviness)—sometimes explained as corresponding to later "heat dysentery," bacillary dysentery, and the like—Baitouweng tang uses four bitter-cold ingredients to clear heat and remove dampness. It is contrasted with Taohua tang (Clause 306), used for vacuity-cold stool with pus and blood.
