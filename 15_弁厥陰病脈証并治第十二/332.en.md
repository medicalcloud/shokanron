# Clause 332 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In cold damage, fever at first for six days, then reversal, on the contrary, for nine days with diarrhea. As a rule, one with reversal and diarrhea should be unable to eat; now if one, on the contrary, can eat, it is feared to be "removal of the center" (one version says "dispersion of the center"). Feed noodles; if there is no fever, one knows the stomach qi is still present, and there will certainly be recovery—one fears that sudden heat comes out and then departs again. Feel the pulse three days later; if the heat persists, expect recovery at midnight of the following day. The reason is that originally there was fever for six days and reversal, on the contrary, for nine days; with fever again for three days, together with the earlier six days it also makes nine days, corresponding to the reversal; therefore expect recovery at midnight of the following day. If three days later the pulse is felt and is rapid and the heat does not cease, this is surplus heat qi, and there will certainly be welling-abscess with pus.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Fever at first means the evil is in the exterior. By the sixth day the evil is transmitted to the jueyin, and one in whom yin qi prevails has reversal and diarrhea. When reversal continues, on the contrary, for nine days, yin-cold qi is abundant, and one should be unable to eat; when, on the contrary, one can eat, it is feared to be "removal of the center." "Removal" means taking away; "center" is the stomach qi. When the evil qi is extremely severe and removes the stomach qi, the stomach draws in food to rescue itself, so one suddenly can eat; this is [the yang qi] trying to prevail. Feed noodles to test it: if the stomach qi is cut off, on obtaining flour there will certainly be fever; if there is no fever, the stomach qi is still present. It may be that cold reached its extreme and transformed into heat, so that sudden heat came and departed again, enabling one to eat; [then] it is not removal of the center. The *Jingui Yaolüe* says: "When a patient normally unable to eat, on the contrary, suddenly craves food, there will certainly be fever." Feeling the pulse three days later, if the heat persists, the yang qi prevails; expect recovery at midnight of the following day. If not recovered the following day, and three days later the pulse is rapid and the heat does not cease, there is surplus heat qi, and there will certainly be welling-abscess with pus. The classic says: "When a rapid pulse [appears] out of season, malign sores arise."

## Notes

A long clause judging prognosis by comparing the days of reversal and heat, and describing how to recognize "removal of the center."

- "Removal of the center" is regarded as a dangerous state in which appetite temporarily appears just before the stomach qi is exhausted.
- One tests by feeding noodles: sudden fever indicates removal of the center; its absence indicates the stomach qi remains.
- When the days of fever balance those of reversal there is recovery; when heat is in surplus, welling-abscesses arise.
- Phrases in Cheng Wuji's commentary such as "此欲勝也" and "若胃気絶得麫則必発熱" are hard to construe; a literal translation has been attempted.
