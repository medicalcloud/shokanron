# Clause 338 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In cold damage with a faint pulse and reversal, when by the seventh or eighth day the skin is cold and the person is agitated without a moment's peace, this is visceral reversal, not roundworm reversal. In roundworm reversal, the person should vomit roundworms. When the patient is quiet yet at times again vexed, this is visceral cold; the roundworms rise into the diaphragm, hence the vexation, which stops again after a short while. When one retches upon obtaining food and is vexed again, it is because the roundworms smell the food and come out; such a person often vomits roundworms spontaneously. Roundworm reversal is governed by Wumei wan (Mume Pill). It also governs chronic diarrhea. [The "令" in "令病者静" is generally read as "今" (now), and is so translated here.]

Wumei 300 pieces; xixin 6 liang; ganjiang 10 liang; huanglian 16 liang; danggui 4 liang; fuzi 6 liang (blast-fried, remove the skin); shujiao 4 liang ("sweated," i.e. fried to release its oil); guizhi (remove the bark) 6 liang; renshen 6 liang; huangbai 6 liang.

Pound and sift the ten ingredients above separately, then combine and prepare. Soak the wumei in vinegar overnight, remove the pits, and steam them under 5 dou of rice; when the rice is cooked, pound into a paste, mix with the medicinals to combine, put into a mortar, and pestle with honey two thousand times; make pills the size of firmiana seeds. Before meals take ten pills with a drink, three times a day, gradually increasing to twenty pills. Avoid raw and cold, slippery, and strong-smelling foods.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Visceral reversal is fatal, because the yang qi is cut off. In roundworm reversal, though there is reversal, there is vexation, and once the roundworms are vomited there is quiet—unlike visceral reversal with agitation and not a moment's peace. The patient's viscera are cold and the stomach vacuous; the roundworms stir and rise to the diaphragm, smell the food and come out, and so roundworms are vomited. Wumei pill is given to warm the viscera and calm the worms. [The commentary's "巳" is read as "已."]

(Nature and flavor) Wumei: sour in flavor, warm. Xixin: acrid, hot. Ganjiang: acrid, hot. Huanglian: bitter, cold. Danggui: acrid, warm. Fuzi: acrid, hot. Shujiao: acrid, hot. Guizhi: acrid, hot. Renshen: sweet, warm. Huangbo: bitter, cold.

The lung governs qi; the lung desires contraction, so quickly eat sour to contract it—the sourness of wumei contracts lung qi. The spleen desires moderation, so quickly eat sweet to moderate it—the sweetness of renshen moderates spleen qi. When cold spreads within, moisten it with acrid and firm it with bitter—the acridity of danggui, guizhi, shujiao, and xixin moistens internal cold. When cold prevails, level it with acrid-hot—the acrid heat of ganjiang and fuzi overcomes cold. Roundworms stir on obtaining sweet and are calmed on obtaining bitter—the bitterness of huanglian and huangbo calms the roundworms.

## Notes

The clause distinguishes "visceral reversal," a fatal pattern with exhaustion of visceral yang qi, from "roundworm reversal" due to roundworms, and uses Wumei wan for the latter.

- Visceral reversal involves incessant agitation, whereas roundworm reversal alternates between quiet and vexation, worsens with eating, and involves vomiting roundworms.
- Wumei wan combines sour (wumei), acrid-warm (xixin, ganjiang, fuzi, shujiao, guizhi, danggui), bitter-cold (huanglian, huangbai), and sweet (renshen), using cold and hot together, and is also regarded as the representative formula for the heat-above, cold-below pattern of jueyin disease.
- It says "it also governs chronic diarrhea," and in later times it is used for chronic diarrhea as well.
