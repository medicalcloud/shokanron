# Clause 343 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In cold damage of six or seven days with a faint pulse, reversal-cold hands and feet, and vexation and agitation, moxa the jueyin. If the reversal does not return [to warmth], it is fatal.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

At six or seven days of cold damage, the upright qi should return and the evil qi cease. A floating pulse with body heat means it is about to resolve. If, on the contrary, the pulse is faint with reversal, yin is overcoming yang. Vexation and agitation mean the yang is vacuous and struggling. Moxa the jueyin to restore its yang. If the reversal does not return, the yang qi is already cut off and cannot restore the upright, and one dies. [The commentary's "巳絶" is read as "已絶."]

## Notes

Clauses 343–348 are regarded as a group listing fatal and difficult patterns of jueyin disease. This clause states that if the hands and feet do not warm even after moxibustion for declining yang qi, it is fatal. The "jueyin" moxa site is not specified; one view says Taichong (LR3), among others.
