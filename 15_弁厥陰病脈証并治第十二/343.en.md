# Clause 343 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In cold damage of six or seven days with a feeble pulse, cold hands and feet, and restlessness, moxa the Jueyin. If the coldness of the limbs does not return [to warmth], it is fatal.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

At six or seven days of cold damage, the healthy qi should return and the pathogenic qi cease. A floating pulse with body heat means it is about to resolve. If, on the contrary, the pulse is feeble with cold limbs, yin is overcoming yang. Restlessness means the yang is deficient and struggling. Moxa the Jueyin to restore its yang. If the coldness of the limbs does not return, the yang qi is already cut off and cannot restore the healthy qi, and one dies. [The commentary's "巳絶" is read as "已絶."]

## Notes

Clauses 343–348 are regarded as a group listing fatal and difficult patterns of Jueyin disease. This clause states that if the hands and feet do not warm even after moxibustion for declining yang qi, it is fatal. The "Jueyin" moxa site is not specified; one view says Taichong (LR3), among others.
