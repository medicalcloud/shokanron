# Clause 375 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When after diarrhea there is further vexation and the area below the heart is soft on pressure, it is vacuity vexation; Zhizi Chi tang is appropriate. Formula 16.

Plump zhizi 14 pieces (broken); fragrant fermented soybeans 4 ge (wrapped in cotton).

Take the two ingredients above with 4 sheng of water; first boil the zhizi to obtain 2.5 sheng, add the soybeans, boil again to obtain 1.5 sheng, remove the dregs, and take in two divided doses. If one dose produces vomiting, stop the remaining dose.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Absence of vexation after diarrhea means it is about to resolve. If there is further vexation with hardness below the heart, it is feared to be grain vexation. Here there is vexation with softness below the heart; evil heat has taken advantage of the vacuity to lodge in the chest, and it is vacuity vexation. Zhizi Chi tang is given to induce vomiting, and there is recovery. ["谷煩" is read as "穀煩" (vexation from food).]

## Notes

Lingering vexation in the chest after diarrhea has stopped, with softness (no repletion) below the heart on pressure, is vacuity vexation, and Zhizi Chi tang removes the heat in the chest. It is the same formula as in Clause 76 of the taiyang chapter and elsewhere. "Vacuity" is interpreted as meaning there is no tangible replete evil.
