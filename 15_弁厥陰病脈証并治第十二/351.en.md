# Clause 351 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Reversal cold of the hands and feet with a fine pulse verging on expiry: Danggui Sini tang governs it. Formula 3.

Danggui 3 liang; guizhi 3 liang (remove the bark); shaoyao 3 liang; xixin 3 liang; gancao 2 liang (mix-fried); tongcao 2 liang; dazao 25 pieces (broken) (one method: 12 pieces).

Boil the seven ingredients above in 8 sheng of water to obtain 3 sheng, remove the dregs, and take 1 sheng warm, three times a day.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Reversal cold of the hands and feet means yang qi is vacuous outside and does not warm the four extremities. A fine pulse verging on expiry means yin blood is weak within and the movement of the pulse is not free. Danggui Sini tang is given to assist yang and generate yin.

(Nature and flavor) Danggui: acrid, warm. Guizhi: acrid, hot. Shaoyao: sour, cold. Xixin: acrid, hot. Dazao: sweet, warm. Gancao: sweet, neutral. Tongcao: sweet, neutral.

The *Neijing* says: "The vessels are the storehouse of blood." All blood belongs to the heart. To free the vessels, one must first supplement the heart and boost the blood. Bitter first enters the heart—the bitterness of danggui assists heart blood. The heart suffers from slackness, so quickly eat sour to contract it—the sourness of shaoyao contracts heart qi. The liver suffers from tension, so quickly eat sweet to moderate it—the sweetness of dazao, gancao, and tongcao moderates yin blood. [The interlinear note gives danggui as "acrid, warm," but the explanation in the text speaks of "the bitterness of danggui."]

## Notes

The clause uses Danggui Sini tang for cold extremities with a fine, weak pulse due to blood vacuity with added cold (blood vacuity cold reversal).

- Although it bears the name Sini tang, it contains no fuzi or ganjiang; it is Guizhi tang minus shengjiang, plus danggui, xixin, and tongcao, with increased dazao.
- It is regarded as a formula that nourishes blood and warms and frees the channels, and in later times is widely applied to chilblains and cold hands and feet.
- Many views hold that "tongcao" corresponds to present-day mutong (akebia).
