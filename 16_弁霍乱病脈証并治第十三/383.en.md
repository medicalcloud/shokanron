# Clause 383 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Question: When a disease has fever, headache, body aches, aversion to cold, vomiting, and diarrhea, to what disease does it belong? Answer: This is called sudden turmoil. In sudden turmoil there are spontaneous vomiting and diarrhea, and also when the diarrhea stops there is fever once again.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Fever, headache, body aches, and aversion to cold are originally cold damage. Because the evil entered the interior and damaged the spleen and stomach, there are vomiting above and diarrhea below, which makes it sudden turmoil. When the diarrhea stops and the interior is harmonized, fever arising once again means it has returned to cold damage, and sweating will certainly resolve it.

## Notes

The clause concerns sudden turmoil accompanied by an exterior pattern (fever, headache, body pain, aversion to cold). Fever arising again after vomiting and diarrhea stop is attributed to the interior disease having gone while the exterior evil remains. In Cheng Wuji's commentary, "令為霍乱" is understood from context in the sense of "今為霍乱" (now it is sudden turmoil).
