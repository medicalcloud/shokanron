# Clause 389 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Having vomited and also with diarrhea, free urination again, great sweating, diarrhea of undigested grain, internal cold and external heat, and a faint pulse verging on expiry: Sini tang governs it. 5. Use the fourth formula above.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

When vomiting and diarrhea lose fluids, urine should be scant. Free urination again with great sweating means the fluids are not contained and the yang qi is greatly vacuous. A faint pulse means yang collapse. If there were no external heat but only internal cold with diarrhea of undigested grain, it would be pure yin. Here, since there is external heat, the yang is not yet cut off, and Sini tang can still be given to rescue it.

## Notes

For a severe case of sudden turmoil in which vomiting, diarrhea, excessive urination, and great sweating have markedly depleted both yin and yang and the pulse is verging on expiry, Sini tang urgently restores yang. The external heat is regarded as floating vacuous yang, but Cheng Wuji takes it as evidence that yang is not yet cut off, a basis for its being rescuable.
