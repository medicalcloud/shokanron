# Clause 382 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Question: What is the disease called sudden turmoil (*huoluan*)? Answer: Vomiting with diarrhea—this is called sudden turmoil.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The triple burner is the pathway of water and grain. When the evil is in the upper burner, there is vomiting without diarrhea; when the evil is in the lower burner, there is diarrhea without vomiting; when the evil is in the middle burner, there are both vomiting and diarrhea. Because eating and drinking are immoderate and cold and heat are unregulated, the clear and turbid invade each other, yin and yang are estranged and separated, and sudden turmoil results. Mild cases are simply called vomiting and diarrhea; severe ones, with flailing and disorderly distress, are called sudden turmoil.

## Notes

A clause defining sudden turmoil in question-and-answer form. It is a disease with abrupt vomiting and diarrhea as its main signs, often explained as corresponding to modern acute gastroenteritis or food poisoning (not necessarily limited to cholera). Cheng Wuji explains that the term *huoluan* derives from *huihuo liaoluan* (sudden violent disorder).
