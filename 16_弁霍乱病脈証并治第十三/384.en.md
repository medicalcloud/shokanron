# Clause 384 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In cold damage, when the pulse is faint and rough, it was originally sudden turmoil and is now cold damage. After four or five days, reaching the yin channels and shifting into the yin, there will certainly be diarrhea; one who originally retched and has diarrhea cannot be treated. When there seems to be a need to defecate but, on the contrary, only flatus is passed and still there is no bowel movement, it belongs to the yangming; the stool will certainly be hard, and recovery comes on the thirteenth day, because the channels are completed. After diarrhea the stool should become hard, and one whose stool is hard and who can eat recovers. Now if, on the contrary, one cannot eat, but upon reaching the next channel cycle can eat fairly well, and after passing one more channel can eat, then one day after that there should be recovery. If there is no recovery, it does not belong to the yangming.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Faint means yang collapse; rough means blood collapse. A faint rough pulse in cold damage means it was originally sudden turmoil, with vomiting and diarrhea losing yang and blood. Although the vomiting and diarrhea have stopped, the evil of cold damage has not yet ended, so it returns to cold damage. After four or five days, when the evil is transmitted to the yin channels, the vacuous interior meets the evil and there will certainly be spontaneous diarrhea. Original retching means the evil is severe above; diarrhea as well means the evil is severe below. First, sudden turmoil left the interior qi greatly vacuous, and then the evil of cold damage is transmitted again causing vomiting and diarrhea—this is double vacuity, so it is untreatable. When there seems to be a need to defecate but, on the contrary, only flatus is passed and still there is no bowel movement—[diarrhea is vacuity; no bowel movement is repletion]—wanting to defecate but passing flatus instead means the interior qi is hot. This belongs to the yangming, and the stool will certainly be hard. Recovery on the thirteenth day is because cold damage passes through all three yin and three yang in six days, and in the next six days passes again and completes the channels; then the qi of yin and yang harmonize, the qi of the great evil departs, and there is recovery.

After diarrhea, fluids are lost, so the stool should be hard. Being able to eat means the stomach is harmonized, and there will certainly be spontaneous recovery; being unable to eat means it is not yet harmonized. "Reaching the next channel cycle" means passing one more channel, that is, going through the channels again after seven days. Being able to eat fairly well means the stomach qi is just becoming harmonized, so after one more day there should be recovery. If there is no recovery, it is sudden heat that has enabled eating, not the harmonization of yangming qi. [The commentary's "巳" is read as "已."]

## Notes

A long clause judging the course and prognosis after sudden turmoil has shifted to cold damage, by day count, appetite, and stool. Many passages are hard to construe and have been interpreted variously since antiquity. The key point is said to be that after fluids are lost in sudden turmoil, hardening of the stool is the natural course, and when appetite returns the stomach qi is harmonized and there is recovery.
