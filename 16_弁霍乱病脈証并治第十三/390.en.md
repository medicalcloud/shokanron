# Clause 390 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When vomiting has ended and diarrhea has ceased, yet there are sweating with reversal, unresolved tension of the four limbs, and a faint pulse verging on expiry, Tongmai Sini jia Zhudanzhi tang governs it. Formula 6.

Gancao 2 liang (mix-fried); ganjiang 3 liang (4 liang may be used for a strong person); fuzi, a large one, 1 piece (raw, remove the skin, break into eight pieces); pig bile half a ge.

Boil the four ingredients above in 3 sheng of water to obtain 1 sheng 2 ge, remove the dregs, add the pig bile, and take warm in two divided doses; the pulse will come at once. If there is no pig bile, substitute sheep bile.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

When vomiting has ended, diarrhea has ceased, and the fluids are exhausted within, there should be no sweating; and one who sweats should not have reversal. Now sweating with reversal, unresolved tension of the four limbs, and a faint pulse verging on expiry mean the yang qi is greatly vacuous and yin qi alone prevails. If pure yang medicines were given, it is feared the yin would repel them, causing retching or agitation, so that [the medicine] could not enter again. Tongmai Sini tang with added pig bile is given. Bile, being bitter, enters the heart and frees the pulse; bile, being cold, supplements the liver and harmonizes yin, guiding the decoction in so that it is not repelled. The *Neijing* says: "Go against what is slight; follow what is severe." This is what is meant. [The commentary's "巳" is read as "已."]

## Notes

The cessation of vomiting and diarrhea here is not recovery but because there is nothing left to discharge; for this critical state with sweating, reversal, cramps, and a pulse verging on expiry, Tongmai Sini tang with pig bile is used. Like Baitong jia Zhudanzhi tang in the shaoyin chapter (Clause 315), it is regarded as the "contrary assistant" method preventing hot medicines from being rejected.
