# Clause 29 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In cold damage with a floating pulse, spontaneous sweating, frequent urination, vexation of the heart, slight aversion to cold, and hypertonicity of the legs, to give Guizhi [tang] instead, seeking to attack the exterior, is a mistake. When, upon taking it, there are at once reversal [cold], dryness in the throat, vexation and agitation, and vomiting counterflow, make Gancao Ganjiang tang (Licorice and Dried Ginger Decoction) and give it to restore the yang. If the reversal is cured and the feet become warm, make Shaoyao Gancao tang (Peony and Licorice Decoction) and give it, and the legs will stretch at once. If the stomach qi is not in harmony and there is delirious speech, give a little Tiaowei Chengqi tang (Stomach-Regulating Qi-Coordinating Decoction). If sweating has been repeatedly induced and red-hot needling has also been applied, Sini tang (Counterflow Cold Decoction) governs it. Formula 16.

Gancao Ganjiang tang
Gancao 4 liang (mix-fried); ganjiang (dried ginger) 2 liang.
Of the two ingredients above, boil with 3 sheng of water down to 1 sheng 5 ge, remove the dregs, and take warm in two doses.

Shaoyao Gancao tang
Shaoyao and gancao (mix-fried), 4 liang each.
Of the two ingredients above, boil with 3 sheng of water down to 1 sheng 5 ge, remove the dregs, and take warm in two doses.

Tiaowei Chengqi tang
Dahuang (rhubarb) 4 liang (remove the bark, wash in clear wine); gancao 2 liang (mix-fried); mangxiao (mirabilite) half a sheng.
Of the three ingredients above, boil with 3 sheng of water down to 1 sheng, remove the dregs, add the mangxiao, put on the fire again and simmer until it boils, and take warm in small sips.

Sini tang
Gancao 2 liang (mix-fried); ganjiang 1.5 liang; fuzi 1 piece (used raw, remove the skin, broken into eight pieces).
Of the three ingredients above, boil with 3 sheng of water down to 1 sheng 2 ge, remove the dregs, and take warm in two doses. For a strong person, a large piece of fuzi and 3 liang of ganjiang may be used.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

A floating pulse, spontaneous sweating, frequent urination, and aversion to cold mean insufficient yang qi. Vexation of the heart and hypertonicity of the legs mean insufficient yin qi. When yin and yang, blood and qi are all vacuous, sweating must not be induced. If Guizhi tang is given to attack the exterior, it further damages the yang qi; hence it is a mistake. When, upon taking it, there are at once reversal, dryness in the throat, vexation and agitation, and vomiting counterflow, first make Gancao Ganjiang tang to restore the yang qi; once the reversal is cured and the feet are warm, give Shaoyao Gancao tang to boost the yin blood, and the legs and shins can stretch. Even when yin and yang have been restored, if there is stomach dryness with delirious speech, give a little Tiaowei Chengqi tang to make the stool slightly loose and harmonize the stomach. Repeated sweating causes loss of yang, and adding red-hot needling damages the yin. The *Neijing* says: "If red-hot needling is applied when the nutritive qi is faint, the blood will not flow." With repeated sweating and red-hot needling, the qi of yin and yang is greatly vacuous; Sini tang restores the qi of yin and yang.

(Nature and flavor noted in the interlinear notes on the ingredients) Gancao: sweet, neutral. Ganjiang: acrid, hot.
The *Neijing* says: "Acrid and sweet disperse and are yang." Gancao and ganjiang are combined to restore the yang.

(Nature and flavor) White shaoyao: sour, slightly cold. Gancao: sweet, neutral.
Of shaoyao, the white supplements and the red drains; the white contracts and the red disperses. Sour contracts and sweet moderates; sour and sweet combined are used to supplement yin blood.

(Nature and flavor) Gancao: sweet, neutral. Mangxiao: salty and bitter, very cold.
The *Neijing* says: "When heat runs to excess within, treat with salty and cold, assisted by bitter and sweet." Mangxiao, salty and cold, removes heat; dahuang, bitter and cold, flushes out repletion; gancao, sweet and neutral, assists the two to push out the old and moderate the middle.

(Nature and flavor) Gancao: sweet, neutral. Ganjiang: acrid, hot. Fuzi: acrid, very hot.
The *Neijing* says: "When cold runs to excess within, treat with sweet and hot," and also, "Where cold excess prevails, calm it with acrid and hot." Gancao combined with ginger and aconite forms a sweet, acrid, very hot preparation that can effuse the qi of yin and yang.

## Notes

This clause describes the changes after Guizhi tang has been mistakenly given to someone deficient in both yin and yang, and the treatment for each stage. Four formulas appear: Gancao Ganjiang tang, Shaoyao Gancao tang, Tiaowei Chengqi tang, and Sini tang.

- If mistreatment leads to reversal cold, dry throat, vexation and agitation, and vomiting, the order "yang first, then yin" is shown: first restore the yang with Gancao Ganjiang tang, then supplement the yin (blood and fluids) with Shaoyao Gancao tang to relax the hypertonicity of the legs.
- Shaoyao Gancao tang became well known in later times as a formula for muscle cramps, such as calf cramps.
- When the stomach is dry with delirious speech, a little Tiaowei Chengqi tang is given; when the yang is greatly vacuous after repeated sweating and red-hot needling, Sini tang is used—the formula changes with the course of the disease.
- Clause 30 explains the course of this clause in question-and-answer form.
