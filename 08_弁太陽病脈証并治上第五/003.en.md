# Clause 3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Taiyang disease—whether fever has already appeared or not yet—with certain aversion to cold, body pain, retching counterflow, and a pulse that is tight at both yin and yang, is called cold damage (*shanghan*).

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The Classic says: "Whenever one is damaged by cold, the disease becomes heat." This is because when cold qi lodges in the channels, the yang channels become constrained and bound and produce heat. In wind-strike there is fever at once because wind is yang. That cold damage is described as "whether fever has already appeared or not yet" is because cold is a yin evil and does not become heat at once; only after it is constrained does it turn into heat. Wind damages the defensive [qi]; cold damages the nutritive [qi]. When the defensive is vacuous there is aversion to wind; when the nutritive is vacuous there is aversion to cold. One whose nutritive [qi] is damaged by cold must have aversion to cold. When qi is diseased there is numbness; when blood is diseased there is pain. Wind slackens qi; cold makes qi counterflow. Body pain and retching counterflow mean that the nutritive [qi] has been struck by cold. The Classic says: "A full pulse with a cold body is contracted from cold damage." A pulse tight at both yin and yang shows that it is cold damage.

## Notes

The definition of "cold damage" within taiyang disease. Its signs are aversion to cold, body pain, retching counterflow, and a pulse tight at both yin and yang; it pairs with wind-strike in Clause 2.

- "Whether fever has already appeared or not yet": fever does not necessarily appear at once, but aversion to cold is always present.
- "Tight at both yin and yang": generally read as tight at both cun and chi (or at both superficial and deep levels). A tight pulse is taken to indicate cold evil.
- Absence of sweating is not stated here, but read together with clauses such as the Mahuang tang clause (Clause 35), it is understood as characteristic of cold damage.
