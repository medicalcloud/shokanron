# Clause 14 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Taiyang disease with stiffness of the nape and back, *jiji* (stiff and awkward), and, on the contrary, sweating and aversion to wind: Guizhi jia Gegen tang (Cinnamon Twig Decoction plus Pueraria) governs it. Formula 3.

Gegen (pueraria) 4 liang; mahuang (ephedra) 3 liang (remove the nodes); shaoyao 2 liang; shengjiang 3 liang (sliced); gancao 2 liang (mix-fried); dazao 12 pieces (split); guizhi 2 liang (remove the bark).

Of the seven ingredients above, with 1 dou of water first boil the mahuang and gegen, reducing by 2 sheng; remove the froth on top, add the other ingredients, boil down to 3 sheng, remove the dregs, and take 1 sheng warm. Cover to obtain a slight sweat. There is no need to sip porridge. Otherwise follow the method for Guizhi tang for care and prohibitions. Your servants [Lin] Yi and others respectfully note: in Zhongjing's original treatise, Guizhi [tang] is used for taiyang wind-strike with spontaneous sweating, and Mahuang [tang] for cold damage without sweating. Here the pattern says "sweating and aversion to wind," yet the formula contains mahuang; this is probably not the original intent. In the third volume there is the Gegen tang pattern, which says "no sweating and aversion to wind"; it is exactly the same as this formula, and there it is appropriate to use mahuang. What is called Guizhi jia Gegen tang here is probably just Guizhi tang with gegen added.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

*Jiji* describes stretching the neck—like [a bird] that stretches its neck and sways its body as it walks. One whose nape and back are stiff does so when moving. With *jiji* stiffness of the nape and back there should be no sweating; that, on the contrary, there are sweating and aversion to wind means wind-strike with exterior vacuity. Guizhi tang is given to harmonize the exterior, and mahuang and gegen are added to expel wind. However, mahuang governs exterior repletion. The later Gegen tang pattern says, "Taiyang disease, stiff nape and back, *jiji*, no sweating, and aversion to wind: Gegen tang governs it," and its ingredients are exactly the same as this formula. For one without sweating mahuang should be used, but here there is spontaneous sweating, so probably mahuang is not added and only gegen is added.

## Notes

This clause treats taiyang wind-strike with stiffness of the neck and back by adding gegen to Guizhi tang.

- *Jiji*: generally taken to describe stiffness making the neck and back hard to move (its pronunciation is also debated).
- Composition: in the Song edition this formula contains mahuang, but both the note by Lin Yi and others and Cheng Wuji's commentary say that mahuang does not fit a sweating pattern and that the original was probably Guizhi tang with gegen alone. Today it is generally understood as "Guizhi tang plus gegen" without mahuang.
- It pairs with Gegen tang in Clause 31 (stiff nape and back, *jiji*, no sweating, aversion to wind).
