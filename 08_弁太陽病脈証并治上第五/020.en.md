# Clause 20 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Taiyang disease: after sweating has been induced, [sweat] leaks without stopping; the person has aversion to wind, difficult urination, and slight tension of the four limbs, so that it is hard to bend and stretch them. Guizhi jia Fuzi tang (Cinnamon Twig Decoction plus Aconite) governs it. Formula 7.

Guizhi 3 liang (remove the bark); shaoyao 3 liang; gancao 3 liang (mix-fried); shengjiang 3 liang (sliced); dazao 12 pieces (split); fuzi (aconite) 1 piece (blast-fried, remove the skin, broken into eight pieces).

Of the six ingredients above, boil with 7 sheng of water down to 3 sheng, remove the dregs, and take 1 sheng warm. Originally called Guizhi tang; now fuzi is added. Care as in the method above.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

In taiyang disease, when sweating has been induced and the sweat then leaks without stopping, with aversion to wind, the yang qi is insufficient; inducing sweating has made the yang qi even more vacuous, and the skin and interstices are not secured. The *Neijing* says: "The bladder is the official of the regional capital; the fluids are stored there, and when the qi transforms, they come out." Difficult urination is because sweating has lost the fluids and the yang qi is too weak to carry out transformation. The four limbs are the root of all the yang. Slight tension of the four limbs with difficulty in bending and stretching is due to loss of yang and desertion of fluids. The *Zhenjing* says: "When the fluids desert, bending and stretching of the parts attached to the bones is inhibited." Guizhi jia Fuzi tang is given to warm the channels and restore the yang.

## Notes

This clause uses Guizhi tang plus fuzi for a condition in which excessive sweating has made the sweat unstoppable, damaging both yang qi and fluids.

- Leaking sweat and aversion to wind are taken as vacuity of yang (defensive) qi; difficult urination and tension of the limbs as damage to the fluids.
- The idea is that warming and securing the yang with fuzi will stop the sweating and allow the fluids to recover; it is often explained as an example of "rescuing the yang preserves the yin (fluids)."
