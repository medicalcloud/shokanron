# Clause 23 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Taiyang disease contracted eight or nine days earlier, in a malaria-like condition, with fever and aversion to cold, more heat than cold; the person does not vomit, the stool and urine are about normal, and [the attacks] come two or three times a day. If the pulse is faint and moderate, [the patient] is about to recover. If the pulse is faint and there is aversion to cold, both yin and yang are vacuous, and one must not again induce sweating, purge, or induce vomiting. If, on the contrary, the face has a hot color, [the disease] is not yet about to resolve; because [the patient] cannot obtain a slight sweat, the body will certainly itch. Guizhi Mahuang Geban tang (Half-and-Half Cinnamon Twig and Ephedra Decoction) is appropriate. Formula 10.

Guizhi 1 liang 16 zhu (remove the bark); shaoyao, shengjiang (sliced), gancao (mix-fried), and mahuang (remove the nodes), 1 liang each; dazao 4 pieces (split); xingren 24 kernels (soak in hot water, remove the skins and tips and double kernels).

Of the seven ingredients above, with 5 sheng of water first boil the mahuang once or twice, remove the froth on top, add the other ingredients, boil down to 1 sheng 8 ge, remove the dregs, and take 6 ge warm. Originally it said: combine 3 ge of Guizhi tang and 3 ge of Mahuang tang to make 6 ge, and take it in one dose; care as in the method above. Your servants [Lin] Yi and others respectfully note: the Guizhi tang formula is guizhi, shaoyao, and shengjiang 3 liang each, gancao 2 liang, and dazao 12 pieces; the Mahuang tang formula is mahuang 3 liang, guizhi 2 liang, gancao 1 liang, and xingren 70 kernels. Calculating now, taking one third of each of the two decoctions gives guizhi 1 liang 16 zhu, shaoyao, shengjiang, and gancao 1 liang each, dazao 4 pieces, and xingren 23 and one third kernels, which rounded gives 24 kernels—matching the formula. Examined closely, this formula is one third [of each], not half of each; it should be called a "combined-half decoction."

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Eight or nine days of cold damage is the time when the evil makes a second round through the channels, finishes passing through the three yang again, and is about to be transmitted to the three yin. In the order of channel transmission, the three yang are passed through in three days, and on the fourth day [the evil] leaves the yang and enters the yin. If it does not enter the yin, it is about to resolve. If it is transmitted to the yin channels, the three yin are passed through by the sixth day; channel transmission is complete and it should resolve. If it does not resolve and makes a second round, by the ninth day it again finishes the three yang. If the yang does not pass it to the yin, it resolves, but it comes in attacks at fixed times like malaria. More cold means the disease is advancing; more heat means it is retreating. The Classic says: "Less reversal and more heat: the disease will be cured." More cold and less heat means the yang qi is retreating, so [the disease] advances. Here, although there are fever and aversion to cold, there is more heat and less cold; the yang qi is advancing and the evil qi is slight. When the interior is not in harmony there are vomiting and diarrhea; here there is no vomiting and the stool and urine are about normal, so the interior is in harmony. Chills and fever every other day mean the evil qi is deep; once a day means the evil qi has returned to the usual; twice a day means the evil qi is shallow; two or three times a day means the evil qi is slight. The *Neijing* says: "[A pulse that is] large means the evil has arrived; small means it is calm"—that is, when the evil is severe the pulse is large, and when the evil is slight the pulse is faint. Here the attacks are frequent and the pulse faint and moderate, meaning the evil qi is slight and slackened; hence "about to recover." A faint pulse with aversion to cold means both exterior and interior are vacuous. Yang is exterior; yin is interior. A faint pulse is interior vacuity; aversion to cold is exterior vacuity. Because both exterior and interior are vacuous, one must not again induce sweating, purge, or induce vomiting. When yin and yang are both vacuous, the face is green-white; if instead it has a hot color, the exterior has not yet resolved. A hot color is a red color. With a slight sweat there would be harmony; without sweat, the evil qi disperses outward into the skin and causes itching. Guizhi Mahuang Geban tang is given to induce a slight sweat and remove the exterior evil.

## Notes

This clause uses small amounts of Guizhi tang and Mahuang tang combined for a lingering, mild exterior pattern of taiyang disease with malaria-like paroxysms of chills and fever several times a day.

- The clause has three branches: (1) a faint, moderate pulse means spontaneous recovery; (2) a faint pulse with aversion to cold means both yin and yang are vacuous, so sweating, vomiting, and purgation are prohibited; (3) a red face and itching body mean evil remains in the exterior and a slight sweat cannot be obtained, so Guizhi Mahuang Geban tang is used to induce a light sweat.
- Guizhi Mahuang Geban tang is regarded as a representative "slight sweating" method: Guizhi tang regulates the nutritive and defensive, and Mahuang tang induces a light sweat.
- The note by Lin Yi and others points out that, by calculation, the doses are actually one third of each formula, not "half of each."
