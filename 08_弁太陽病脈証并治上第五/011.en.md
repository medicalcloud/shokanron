# Clause 11 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When a patient's body is very hot yet he wants to put on clothes, the heat is in the skin and the cold is in the bone marrow. When the body is very cold yet he does not want clothes near him, the cold is in the skin and the heat is in the bone marrow.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

"Skin" refers to the shallow; "bone marrow" refers to the deep. "Skin" refers to the outside; "bone marrow" refers to the inside. One whose body is hot but who wants clothes has heat in the exterior and cold in the interior. One whose body is cold but who dislikes clothes has cold in the exterior and heat in the interior.

## Notes

This clause distinguishes the true cold or heat of a disease (true cold with false heat, true heat with false cold) by comparing the cold or heat appearing outwardly with what the patient prefers (whether he wants clothes).

- The patient's own sensation (feeling cold or hot) is taken to show the true internal state better than the outward heat or coldness.
- "Skin" and "bone marrow" are generally taken as metaphors for shallow and deep, outside and inside, and Cheng Wuji reads them this way.
