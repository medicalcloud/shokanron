# Clause 22 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

If there is slight cold, Guizhi qu Shaoyao jia Fuzi tang (Cinnamon Twig Decoction minus Peony plus Aconite) governs it. Formula 9.

Guizhi 3 liang (remove the bark); gancao 2 liang (mix-fried); shengjiang 3 liang (sliced); dazao 12 pieces (split); fuzi 1 piece (blast-fried, remove the skin, broken into eight pieces).

Of the five ingredients above, boil with 7 sheng of water down to 3 sheng, remove the dregs, and take 1 sheng warm. Originally called Guizhi tang; now shaoyao is removed and fuzi added. Care as in the method above.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

A pulse that comes rapidly, stops once occasionally, and comes again is called skipping (*cu*). Skipping indicates exuberant yang, and the pulse may be skipping without purgation. But here the pulse is skipping after purgation, so it cannot be taken as exuberant yang. [The Classic says:] "Taiyang disease: when it is purged and the pulse is skipping without chest bind, it is about to resolve." Here, after purgation the pulse is skipping and there is also fullness in the chest, so it cannot be said to be about to resolve. It is because after purgation the yang is vacuous, and the exterior evil gradually enters and lodges in the chest. Guizhi tang is given to disperse the lodged evil and free the yang qi. Shaoyao boosts yin and is unsuitable for those with vacuous yang, so it is removed. When the yang qi is already vacuous and slight aversion to cold is added, a warming preparation must be used to disperse it; therefore fuzi is added.

## Notes

This clause adds fuzi when slight aversion to cold is added to the pattern of Clause 21 (Guizhi qu Shaoyao tang).

- "Slight cold" is generally read as "slight aversion to cold," a sign that yang vacuity has advanced further. Some read it as a faint pulse.
- Cheng Wuji defines the skipping pulse as "rapid with occasional stops" and explains that a skipping pulse after purgation is not exuberant yang but vacuous yang with exterior evil entering.
