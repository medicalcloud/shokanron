# Clause 21 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Taiyang disease: when, after purgation, the pulse is skipping (*cu*) (*cu* is written *zong* in one version) and there is fullness in the chest, Guizhi qu Shaoyao tang (Cinnamon Twig Decoction minus Peony) governs it. Formula 8.

Guizhi 3 liang (remove the bark); gancao 2 liang (mix-fried); shengjiang 3 liang (sliced); dazao 12 pieces (split).

Of the four ingredients above, boil with 7 sheng of water down to 3 sheng, remove the dregs, and take 1 sheng warm. Originally called Guizhi tang; now shaoyao is removed. Care as in the method above.

## Notes

This clause uses Guizhi tang without shaoyao when, after taiyang disease has been mistakenly purged, the pulse becomes skipping (rapid with occasional pauses, or hurried) and the chest becomes full.

- Fullness in the chest is often understood as damage to the yang qi in the chest by mistaken purgation, with the evil beginning to enter the chest.
- It is generally explained that shaoyao, being sour, yin in nature, and astringent, is removed because it may obstruct the yang qi of the chest.
- Cheng Wuji's commentary is attached together with the next clause (see Clause 22).
