# Clause 9 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The time when Taiyang disease tends to resolve is from si to wei.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

*Si* is the time of full yang, when yang qi can be restored. [The six meridians] begin with the Taiyang and end with the Jueyin, and each has three double-hours as its time of resolution: Taiyang from *si* to *wei*, Yangming from *shen* to *xu*, Shaoyang from *yin* to *chen*, Taiyin from *hai* to *chou*, Shaoyin from *zi* to *yin*, Jueyin from *chou* to *mao*. [They are so arranged] because yang moves quickly and yin moves slowly. Yang governs the day; yin governs the night. The times of resolution of the three yang meridians extend from *yin* to *xu* because the way of yang is always in surplus; those of the three yin meridians extend from *hai* to *mao* because the way of yin is always deficient. The *Neijing* says: "The Taiyang within yang communicates with the qi of summer." Thus *si*, *wu*, and *wei* are the times when the Taiyang flourishes.

## Notes

One of the clauses giving, for each of the six meridians, the "time when it tends to resolve." Taiyang disease tends to resolve from *si* to *wei* (roughly 9 a.m. to 3 p.m.), the time of day when yang qi is strongest.

- It reflects the idea of correspondence between the waxing and waning of yang qi in nature and in the body.
- Opinions differ on its clinical importance, but it is read as a guide for observing the course of the disease.
