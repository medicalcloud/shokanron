# Clause 27 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Taiyang disease with fever and aversion to cold, more heat than cold, and a faint, weak pulse: this is absence of yang, and sweating must not be induced. Guizhi er Yuebi yi tang (Two Parts Cinnamon Twig and One Part Maidservant from Yue Decoction) is appropriate. Formula 14.

Guizhi (remove the bark), shaoyao, mahuang, and gancao (mix-fried), 18 zhu each; dazao 4 pieces (split); shengjiang 1 liang 2 zhu (sliced); shigao 24 zhu (crushed, wrapped in cotton).

Of the seven ingredients above, with 5 sheng of water boil the mahuang once or twice, remove the froth on top, add the other ingredients, boil down to 2 sheng, remove the dregs, and take 1 sheng warm. Originally it said: prepare Yuebi tang and Guizhi tang separately, combine them, and drink 1 sheng; now they are combined into one formula, two parts Guizhi tang and one part Yuebi tang. Your servants [Lin] Yi and others respectfully note: the Guizhi tang formula is guizhi, shaoyao, and shengjiang 3 liang each, gancao 2 liang, and dazao 12 pieces; the Yuebi tang formula is mahuang 2 liang, shengjiang 3 liang, gancao 2 liang, shigao half a jin, and dazao 15 pieces. Calculating now, taking one quarter of Guizhi tang gives guizhi, shaoyao, and shengjiang 18 zhu each, gancao 12 zhu, and dazao 3 pieces. Taking one eighth of Yuebi tang gives mahuang 18 zhu, shengjiang 9 zhu, gancao 6 zhu, shigao 24 zhu, and dazao 1 and seven-eighths pieces, [the fraction] discarded. Combining what is taken from the two decoctions gives guizhi, shaoyao, gancao, and mahuang 18 zhu each, shengjiang 1 liang 3 zhu, shigao 24 zhu, and dazao 4 pieces, matching the formula. The old text said "Guizhi three"; since one quarter is now taken, it should say "Guizhi two." The Yuebi tang formula appears among Zhongjing's miscellaneous formulas. The *Waitai miyao* calls it Qipi tang.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Shengjiang (1 liang 3 qian, sliced), dazao (4 pieces, split), mahuang (18 zhu, remove the nodes). [Part of the formula has slipped into the commentary.]

The stomach is the master of the twelve channels. The spleen governs water and grain; it is a humble organ, like a maidservant (*bi*). The *Neijing* says: "The spleen governs moving the fluids for the stomach." This decoction is called Yuepi (Effusing the Spleen) because it effuses the spleen qi and moves the fluids. That a formula in the *Waitai* is also named Yuepi tang has this meaning.

## Notes

This clause uses Guizhi er Yuebi yi tang—Guizhi tang and Yuebi tang (which contains mahuang, shigao, etc.) combined 2:1—for an exterior pattern with fever and aversion to cold, more heat than cold, and some interior heat added.

- The phrase "this is absence of yang, and sweating must not be induced" is difficult. An influential reading treats "a faint, weak pulse: this is absence of yang, and sweating must not be induced" as a parenthetical caution, the formula being for the case of "more heat than cold."
- On *bi* ("maidservant") in Yuebi tang, one view, like Cheng Wuji's, takes it as interchangeable with *pi* ("spleen").
- The note by Lin Yi and others shows the dose calculation and says the old name "Guizhi three" should be corrected to "Guizhi two."
