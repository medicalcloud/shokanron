# Clause 4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

On the first day of cold damage, the Taiyang receives it. If the pulse is quiet, there is no transmission. If there is a strong desire to vomit, or restlessness, and the pulse is rapid and urgent, there is transmission.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The Taiyang governs the exterior. On the first day the Taiyang receives the pathogen, and on the second day it should be transmitted to the Yangming. If the pulse qi is feeble, it is not transmitted to the Yangming. When the stomach meridian receives the pathogen, there is a tendency to vomit. When pathogenic cold is transmitted to the interior, it turns into heat. A strong desire to vomit, restlessness, and an urgent, rapid pulse mean that the Taiyang pathogenic cold has turned into heat and been transmitted to the Yangming.

## Notes

This clause distinguishes, by pulse and signs, whether the disease is "transmitted" from the Taiyang to another meridian.

- If the pulse is quiet (it remains a Taiyang pulse such as floating and tight and does not become rapid and urgent), the disease is still in the Taiyang.
- Nausea, restlessness, and a rapid, urgent pulse are taken as signs that the pathogen has advanced inward and the heat has intensified.
- It is often read as showing that the day-count scheme of transmission ("first day Taiyang, second day Yangming…", as in the "Shanghan li" chapter) should not be applied mechanically, but judged by the pulse and signs.
