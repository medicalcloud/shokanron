# Clause 15 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Taiyang disease: when, after purgation, the qi surges upward, Guizhi tang may be given, using the method above. If there is no upward surging, it must not be given. 4.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Taiyang disease belongs to the exterior; if, on the contrary, it is purged, the interior becomes vacuous and the evil tends to take advantage of the vacuity to be transmitted inward. If the qi surges upward, the interior has not received the evil, and the qi is counterflowing upward to contend with the evil; the evil is still in the exterior. Therefore Guizhi tang should again be given to resolve the exterior. If the qi does not surge upward, the interior is vacuous and cannot contend with the evil, and the evil qi has already been transmitted inward. Therefore Guizhi tang must not be given again to attack the exterior.

## Notes

This clause judges, by the presence or absence of "upward surging of qi," whether Guizhi tang may still be used after taiyang disease has been mistakenly purged.

- "The qi surges upward": often understood to show that, even after mistaken purgation, the upright qi still retains the momentum to move outward and contend with the evil (some take it as a subjective sensation of something rising into the chest).
- If there is surging, the evil is still in the exterior and Guizhi tang is appropriate; if not, the evil has already entered inward and Guizhi tang is not used.
