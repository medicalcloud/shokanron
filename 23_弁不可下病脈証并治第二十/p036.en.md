# §36 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Cold damage with a pulse tight at both yin and yang, aversion to cold, and fever: the pulse is about to reverse. Reversal [of the pulse] is when the pulse first comes large, gradually becomes small, and coming again gradually becomes large—this is its sign. In such a case, when aversion to cold is severe, there is feathery sweating and pain in the throat; if heat predominates, the eyes are red with many vessels and the pupils unclear. If the physician sweats it again, the throat is injured; if it is purged again, both eyes close, with much cold there is diarrhea of undigested grain, with much heat there is stool with pus and blood; if fumigated, the body turns yellow; if hot-compressed, the throat dries. If urination is free, it can be rescued; if urination is difficult, it is perilous.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

A pulse tight at both yin and yang means clear pathogen has struck above and turbid pathogen below; both Taiyang and Shaoyin have contracted pathogen. Aversion to cold is the Shaoyin; fever is the Taiyang. The pulse being about to reverse means the exterior pathogen is about to be transmitted inward. When aversion to cold is severe, it turns to heat, with feathery sweating and pain in the throat, because the Shaoyin vessel follows the throat. When heat predominates, the Taiyang predominates; red eyes with many vessels and unclear pupils are because the Taiyang vessel starts at the eye. Sweating attacks yang, and the Shaoyin heat is thereby effused and rises, so the throat is injured. If purged again, the Taiyang pathogen sinks inward through the deficiency, so both eyes close. When yin pathogen moves downward, cold predominates and there will certainly be diarrhea of undigested grain; when yang pathogen moves downward, heat predominates and there will certainly be stool with pus and blood. Fumigation makes the fire-heat severe, so the body will certainly turn yellow; hot compresses make the fire-heat mild, so the throat will certainly dry. Free urination means the fluids are not yet exhausted, and it can still be rescued; difficult urination means the fluids are already cut off, hard to control and perilous. [The commentary's "巳絶" is read as "已絶."]

## Notes

The clause states that when both Taiyang and Shaoyin have contracted pathogen (the clear and turbid pathogens of paragraph 32 of the "Distinguishing the Pulse" chapter), mistaken sweating, purgation, fumigation, or hot compresses all produce changed patterns, and the prognosis is judged by urination. It is not found in the three yin and three yang chapters.
