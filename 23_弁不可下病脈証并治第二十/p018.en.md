# §18 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the pulse is floating and large, yet the area below the heart is, on the contrary, hard, with heat: if it belongs to the viscera, attack it and do not induce sweating; if it belongs to the bowels, do not make urination frequent—frequent urination makes the stool hard. When sweating is profuse, the heat recovers; when sweating is scant, the stool is difficult. With a slow pulse, it still must not be attacked.

## Notes

The same text as paragraph 26 of the "Distinguishing the Pulse" chapter, where Cheng Wuji's commentary is found.
