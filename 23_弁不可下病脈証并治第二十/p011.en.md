# §11 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The pulse is soft and tight. Soft means the defensive qi is faint; tight means cold in the nutritive. Yang is faint and the defensive is struck by wind: fever with aversion to cold. The nutritive is tight and the stomach qi cold: slight retching and vexation within the heart. The physician says there is great heat, resolves the flesh and induces sweating; yang is lost, vacuous vexation and agitation, distressing glomus and hardness below the heart. Exterior and interior both vacuous and exhausted, dizziness on suddenly rising; guest heat in the skin, sullen and unable to sleep. Not knowing the stomach qi is cold, the tight cold in Guanyuan, with no skill left to apply, he draws water and pours it over the body. The guest heat ceases for the moment, shivering and shaking with cold; heavy bedding is piled on to cover, sweat comes out and the vertex is veiled, the body twitches and shakes again, urination becomes slightly difficult. Cold qi arises through the water, diarrhea of undigested grain without pause; retching turns into, on the contrary, the bowels coming out; tossing and turning without rest; the hands and feet slightly counterflow cold; the body cold and the interior vexed. Late, one tries to rescue it afterward—how can it be called back?

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The stomach is cold and the nutritive cold; yang is faint and struck by wind, with fever, aversion to cold, slight retching, and heart vexation. When the physician does not warm the stomach but, on the contrary, considers it heat and resolves the flesh to induce sweating, the exterior becomes vacuous and yang is lost, with vexation and agitation and glomus and hardness below the heart. The interior was first insufficient; sweating further depletes the exterior, so exterior and interior are both vacuous and exhausted, with dizziness on suddenly rising. Guest heat is in the exterior, and one is sullen and cannot sleep. When the physician does not rescue the interior but only targets the exterior heat, drawing water to pour and wash and drive off the heat, the guest heat easily ceases but the interior cold increases further, with shuddering, shaking, and cold. When heavy bedding is again piled on to cover, the vacuous exterior finally sweats, further depleting the yang qi. *Dian* is the vertex. Veiling at the vertex with shaking and cold of the body and difficult urination are yang collapse. Cold arises through the water: below it becomes undigested grain, above vomiting; outside there is reversal counterflow, inside dryness and vexation, tossing and turning without rest. Even if one wishes to rescue, it cannot be done. The *Bencao* says: "When the disease's momentum has already passed, life will hardly be preserved." [The commentary's "解飢" is read as "解肌" (resolving the flesh).]

## Notes

In the form of five-character rhymed verse, the clause describes the course in which one whose stomach is cold and nutritive and defensive vacuous is mistaken as having heat and sweated, then doused with cold water and covered with bedding to sweat again, and through these compounded errors loses yang qi and reaches a state beyond rescue. It is not found in the three yin and three yang chapters.
