# §10 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The pulse is soft and weak: weak, on the contrary, at the guan; soft, on the contrary, at the top; floating, on the contrary, above; rapid, on the contrary, below. Floating is yang deficiency; rapid is absence of blood. Floating is deficiency; rapid generates heat. Floating being deficiency, there are spontaneous sweating and aversion to cold; rapid being pain, there are shaking and cold shuddering. When feeble and weak are at the guan, there is tension below the chest, dyspnea and sweating with inability to breathe, and within the breathing, pain in the rib-sides; shaking and cold contend, and the form is like malaria. The physician, on the contrary, purged it, thus making the pulse rapid, with fever, manic running about and seeing ghosts, stuffiness below the heart, dribbling urination, great hardness of the lower abdomen, and blood in the urine on urinating.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Weak at the guan means yin qi is weak within; soft at the top means yang qi is weak outside. Floating is deficiency; floating above means the wei-defense is insufficient, hence "yang deficiency." Yang deficient and not securing, the interstices let out sweat with aversion to cold. Rapid is also deficiency; rapid below means the ying-nutrient falls short, hence "blood loss." With blood loss, the bowels and zang organs cannot be warmed and moistened, so the pulse is rapid with pain, shaking, and cold shuddering. Feeble and weak at the guan mean the pathogenic qi has been transmitted inward. When the deficient interior meets the pathogen, there are tension below the chest, dyspnea and sweating, pulling pain below the rib-sides, and shaking and cold like malaria. Here the interior pathogen is not yet excess and the exterior pathogen not yet resolved, yet the physician, on the contrary, purged it, so the interior qi became more deficient and the pathogenic heat sank inward, making the pulse rapid with fever, manic running about and seeing ghosts, and stuffiness below the heart—this is heat sunk into the middle jiao. If the heat qi sinks deeply, it lodges in the lower jiao, making urination dribble, the lower abdomen very hard, and uri[nation bloody]. [In the base the commentary breaks off at the character "小."]

## Notes

The clause states that when one with both exterior yang qi and interior ying-nutrient blood deficient, with malaria-like cold and heat, is mistakenly purged, the pathogenic heat sinks into the middle and lower jiao, producing mania, stuffiness below the heart, dribbling urination, and hematuria.
