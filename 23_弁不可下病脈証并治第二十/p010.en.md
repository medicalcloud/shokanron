# §10 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The pulse is soft and weak: weak, on the contrary, at the guan; soft, on the contrary, at the top; floating, on the contrary, above; rapid, on the contrary, below. Floating is yang vacuity; rapid is absence of blood. Floating is vacuity; rapid generates heat. Floating being vacuity, there are spontaneous sweating and aversion to cold; rapid being pain, there are shaking and cold shuddering. When faint and weak are at the guan, there is tension below the chest, panting and sweating with inability to breathe, and within the breathing, pain in the rib-sides; shaking and cold contend, and the form is like malaria. The physician, on the contrary, purged it, thus making the pulse rapid, with fever, manic running about and seeing ghosts, glomus below the heart, dribbling urination, great hardness of the lower abdomen, and blood in the urine on urinating.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Weak at the guan means yin qi is weak within; soft at the top means yang qi is weak outside. Floating is vacuity; floating above means the defensive is insufficient, hence "yang vacuity." Yang vacuous and not securing, the interstices let out sweat with aversion to cold. Rapid is also vacuity; rapid below means the nutritive falls short, hence "blood loss." With blood loss, the bowels and viscera cannot be warmed and moistened, so the pulse is rapid with pain, shaking, and cold shuddering. Faint and weak at the guan mean the evil qi has been transmitted inward. When the vacuous interior meets the evil, there are tension below the chest, panting and sweating, pulling pain below the rib-sides, and shaking and cold like malaria. Here the interior evil is not yet replete and the exterior evil not yet resolved, yet the physician, on the contrary, purged it, so the interior qi became more vacuous and the evil heat sank inward, making the pulse rapid with fever, manic running about and seeing ghosts, and glomus below the heart—this is heat sunk into the middle burner. If the heat qi sinks deeply, it lodges in the lower burner, making urination dribble, the lower abdomen very hard, and uri[nation bloody]. [In the base the commentary breaks off at the character "小."]

## Notes

The clause states that when one with both exterior yang qi and interior nutritive blood vacuous, with malaria-like cold and heat, is mistakenly purged, the evil heat sinks into the middle and lower burners, producing mania, glomus below the heart, dribbling urination, and hematuria.
