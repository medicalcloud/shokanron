# §1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The pulse is soft and weak: weak, on the contrary, at the guan; soft, on the contrary, at the top; faint, on the contrary, above; rough, on the contrary, below. Faint means yang qi is insufficient; rough means there is no blood. When yang qi is, on the contrary, faint, one is struck by wind, sweats, and, on the contrary, is agitated and vexed. Rough means there is no blood, with reversal and cold. When yang is faint, it must not be purged; purging it causes glomus and hardness below the heart.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

When yang is faint and it is purged, yang qi is already vacuous and yin qi becomes severe within, so there are glomus and hardness below the heart.

## Notes

The opening paragraph of the chapter "Distinguishing Pulse, Signs, and Treatment of Diseases That Must Not Be Purged." The first half is the same as paragraph 5 of the "Must Not Be Sweated" chapter; here purgation is prohibited because purging one with faint yang causes glomus and hardness below the heart.
