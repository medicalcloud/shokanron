# §1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The pulse is soft and weak: weak, on the contrary, at the guan; soft, on the contrary, at the top; feeble, on the contrary, above; rough, on the contrary, below. Feeble means yang qi is insufficient; rough means there is no blood. When yang qi is, on the contrary, feeble, one is struck by wind, sweats, and, on the contrary, is agitated and vexed. Rough means there is no blood, with cold limbs and cold. When yang is feeble, it must not be purged; purging it causes stuffiness and hardness below the heart.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

When yang is feeble and it is purged, yang qi is already deficient and yin qi becomes severe within, so there are stuffiness and hardness below the heart.

## Notes

The opening paragraph of the chapter "Distinguishing Pulse, Signs, and Treatment of Diseases That Must Not Be Purged." The first half is the same as paragraph 5 of the "Must Not Be Sweated" chapter; here purgation is prohibited because purging one with feeble yang causes stuffiness and hardness below the heart.
