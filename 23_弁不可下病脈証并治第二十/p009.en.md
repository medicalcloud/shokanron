# §9 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The pulse is soft and weak: weak, on the contrary, at the guan; soft, on the contrary, at the top; wiry, on the contrary, above; faint, on the contrary, below. Wiry means yang is in motion; faint means yin cold. Above is replete and below vacuous, and one wishes to be warm. Faint and wiry mean vacuity; the vacuous must not be purged. Faint means cough; with cough one spits drool. If purged, the cough stops but diarrhea consequently does not cease; when diarrhea does not cease, there is a feeling in the chest like being gnawed by worms, gruel comes back out as soon as it enters, urination is inhibited, both rib-sides are tense, breathing is difficult, neck and back pull on each other, the arms are numb, there is extreme cold yet, on the contrary, sweating, the body is cold as ice, the eyes are unclear, and speech does not stop; yet grain qi enters abundantly—this is removal of the center (also called dispersion of the center). Though the mouth wants to speak, the tongue cannot move forward.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Purging those of vacuous constitution is double vacuity. The *Nanjing* says: "Filling the replete, emptying the vacuous, harming the insufficient and boosting the surplus—this is the harm done by the middling practitioner."

The *Neijing* says: "When cold is contracted, disease is received; when slight it becomes cough; when severe it becomes diarrhea and pain." When the lung contracts slight cold and there is cough, the pulse too is faint. Purging makes the qi descend; although the cough stops, diarrhea consequently does not cease. When diarrhea does not cease, it robs the upright qi and becomes a dangerous, malign state. A feeling in the chest like being gnawed by worms, gruel coming back out as soon as it enters, inhibited urination, tension of both rib-sides, and difficult breathing mean the interior qi is damaged. Neck and back pulling on each other, numbness of the arms, extreme cold yet sweating, and the body cold as ice mean the exterior qi is damaged. When the damage of exterior and interior reaches its extreme and both yin and yang desert, the eyes are unclear and speech does not stop. The *Nanjing* says: "One who loses yang sees ghosts; one who loses yin goes blind." One whose yin and yang are deserting should be unable to eat; that grain enters abundantly is removal of the center—the stomach qi has been removed. Though the mouth wants to speak, the tongue cannot move forward because the qi has already declined and deserted and cannot move it.

## Notes

A long clause stating that purging one with a pulse of repletion above and vacuity below causes unceasing diarrhea, damage to the qi of exterior and interior, desertion of yin and yang, and finally "removal of the center" (abnormal appetite before the stomach qi is exhausted). Its first half is shared with paragraphs 12–13 of the "Must Not Be Sweated" chapter.
