# §12 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The pulse is floating and large. Floating is qi repletion; large is blood vacuity. Blood vacuity is absence of yin. When solitary yang alone descends to the yin region, the urine should be red and difficult, and the bladder should be vacuous. Now, on the contrary, urination is free and there is great sweating; by rule the defensive should be faint, but now, on the contrary, it is even more replete, and the fluids shoot out in four directions; the nutritive is depleted and the blood exhausted, with dry vexation and inability to sleep; the blood thins and the flesh wastes, forming violent (one version says "black") fluid. If the physician further attacks the stomach with potent drugs, this is double vacuity; the guest yang has its time to depart, and one will certainly discharge something like filthy mud and die.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The defensive is yang; the nutritive is yin. When the defensive qi is strong and replete and the yin blood vacuous and weak, yang takes advantage of yin vacuity and descends to the yin region. The yin region is the lower burner. Yang, being heat, disperses the fluids, so the urine should be red and difficult. Now, on the contrary, urination is free with great sweating because the yin qi is weak within. The classic says: "One whose yin is weak sweats spontaneously." Thus the defensive is not faint but, on the contrary, even more replete; the nutritive is depleted and the blood exhausted, with dry vexation and sleeplessness. "The blood thins, the flesh wastes, forming violent fluid" means the fluids shoot out in four directions. If the physician, on the contrary, purges, further depleting the interior, this is double vacuity; the solitary yang deserts again through purgation, qi and blood are all exhausted, the stomach qi is exhausted within, and one will certainly discharge something like filthy mud and die.

## Notes

The clause states that in one whose yin blood is vacuous with only yang exuberant, the fluids are exhausted through urine and sweat, and if purgatives (potent drugs) are then used to deplete doubly, one discharges stool like filthy mud and dies. It is not found in the three yin and three yang chapters.
