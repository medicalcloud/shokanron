# §12 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The pulse is floating and large. Floating is qi excess; large is blood deficiency. Blood deficiency is absence of yin. When solitary yang alone descends to the yin region, the urine should be red and difficult, and the bladder should be deficient. Now, on the contrary, urination is free and there is great sweating; by rule the wei-defense should be feeble, but now, on the contrary, it is even more excess, and the fluids shoot out in four directions; the ying-nutrient is depleted and the blood exhausted, with dry vexation and inability to sleep; the blood thins and the flesh wastes, forming violent (one version says "black") fluid. If the physician further attacks the stomach with potent drugs, this is double deficiency; the guest yang has its time to depart, and one will certainly discharge something like filthy mud and die.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The wei-defense is yang; the ying-nutrient is yin. When the wei-defensive qi is strong and excess and the yin blood deficient and weak, yang takes advantage of yin deficiency and descends to the yin region. The yin region is the lower jiao. Yang, being heat, disperses the fluids, so the urine should be red and difficult. Now, on the contrary, urination is free with great sweating because the yin qi is weak within. The classic says: "One whose yin is weak sweats spontaneously." Thus the wei-defense is not feeble but, on the contrary, even more excess; the ying-nutrient is depleted and the blood exhausted, with dry vexation and sleeplessness. "The blood thins, the flesh wastes, forming violent fluid" means the fluids shoot out in four directions. If the physician, on the contrary, purges, further depleting the interior, this is double deficiency; the solitary yang deserts again through purgation, qi and blood are all exhausted, the stomach qi is exhausted within, and one will certainly discharge something like filthy mud and die.

## Notes

The clause states that in one whose yin blood is deficient with only yang exuberant, the fluids are exhausted through urine and sweat, and if purgatives (potent drugs) are then used to deplete doubly, one discharges stool like filthy mud and dies. It is not found in the three yin and three yang chapters.
