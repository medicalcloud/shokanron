# §14 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the fuyang pulse is slow and moderate, the stomach qi is normal. When the fuyang pulse is floating and rapid, floating damages the stomach and rapid stirs the spleen; this is not the original disease but the result of the physician's purgation. The nutritive and defensive sink inward, the rapidity first becomes faint, and the pulse, on the contrary, is only floating; the person will certainly have hard stool, and the qi is removed by belching. Why so? Originally the spleen was stirred by the rapid pulse; the rapidity first becomes faint, so one knows the spleen qi is not governing, the stool is hard, and the qi is removed by belching. Now the pulse, on the contrary, is floating and the rapidity changes to faint; the evil qi alone remains, the heart is hungry, but the evil heat does not digest grain, and there are tidal fever and thirst. A rapid pulse should become slow and moderate; when the pulse's measure before and after is as it should be, the patient is hungry. When a rapid pulse is out of season, malign sores arise.

## Notes

The same text as paragraph 24 of the "Distinguishing the Pulse" chapter, where Cheng Wuji's commentary is found.
