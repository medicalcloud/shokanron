# §37 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Cold damage with fever, puffing breath out of the mouth, headache, yellow eyes, and uncontrollable nosebleed: one who craves water will certainly retch; one who dislikes water will have reversal. If purged, sores arise in the throat; if the hands and feet are warm, there will certainly be rectal heaviness with stool containing pus and blood. One with headache and yellow eyes, if purged, will have the eyes close. One who craves water, if purged, will certainly have a reversed pulse, a hoarse voice, and a blocked throat; if sweated, there will be shivering and shuddering, with both yin and yang vacuous. One who dislikes water, if purged, will have interior cold, no desire to eat, and stool with whole undigested grain; if sweated, there will be injury in the mouth, white tongue fur, and vexation and agitation. With a rapid replete pulse and no bowel movement for six or seven days, there will certainly be bloody stool afterward; if sweated, urination will be spontaneously free.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Fever in cold damage is cold turned to heat. Puffing breath out of the mouth means heat lodges in the upper diaphragm. Headache, yellow eyes, and uncontrollable bleeding mean heat steams upward. The *Qianjin* says: "Without yang there is reversal; without yin there is retching." One who craves water and certainly retches has yin vacuity; one who dislikes water and has reversal has yang vacuity. Fever with puffing breath out of the mouth means the throat is already hot; if purged and the fluids lost, sores arise in the throat. The heat descends through interior vacuity; if the heat qi binds within, the hands and feet will certainly be reversal-cold; if the hands and feet are warm, the heat qi does not bind but moves downward, becoming heat-associated diarrhea with rectal heaviness and stool with pus and blood. One with headache and yellow eyes, when purged, has heat qi hidden within, so the eyes close. Craving water is yin vacuity; purging further depletes the interior, the yang qi sinks inward, so the pulse reverses, the voice is hoarse, and the throat blocked. Sweating when yin is vacuous further depletes yang, making both yin and yang vacuous and causing shivering and shuddering. Disliking water is yang vacuity; purging further depletes the stomach qi, and vacuity cold becomes severe within, so the interior is cold and one has no desire to eat. Sweating when yang is vacuous makes the upper burner vacuous and dry, so there are injury and erosion in the mouth, white tongue fur, and vexation and agitation. The classic says: "When the rapid pulse does not resolve and heat combines, grain is dispersed and one is prone to hunger; when by the sixth or seventh day there is no bowel movement, there is static blood." Here a rapid replete pulse with no bowel movement for six or seven days means heat has amassed the blood within; after seven days the evil heat gradually resolves and presses the blood downward, so there will certainly be bloody stool. Sweating with bloody stool makes both yin and yang vacuous. [The commentary's "叶熱" is read as "協熱" (heat-associated). The end of the commentary is somewhat incomplete in meaning.]

## Notes

A long clause describing in detail how, in cold damage with fever, puffing heat from the mouth, headache, yellow eyes, and nosebleed, mistaken purgation or sweating produces different changed patterns depending on whether one craves water (yin vacuity) or dislikes it (yang vacuity). It is not found in the three yin and three yang chapters, and many passages are hard to construe.
