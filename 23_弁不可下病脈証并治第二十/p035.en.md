# §35 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Cold damage with fever, headache, and slight sweating: if sweated, one fails to recognize people; if fumigated, there are panting, no urination, and fullness of the heart and abdomen; if purged, there are shortness of breath, difficult urination, headache, and stiff back; if warm needling is added, there is nosebleed.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Cold damage should have no sweating. Fever, headache, and slight sweating mean the cold evil is turning into heat and about to be transmitted inward. Sweating loses yang and increases heat, so one fails to recognize people. If fumigated with fire, the fire-heat damages qi, consumes the fluids within, and binds into interior repletion, so there are panting, no urination, and fullness of the heart and abdomen. If, on the contrary, it is purged, the interior's fluids are depleted, the evil tries to enter the interior, and outside it stirs the channels, so there are shortness of breath, difficult urination, headache, and stiff back. If warm needling is added, it boosts yang and increases heat, and will certainly stir the blood to cause nosebleed.

## Notes

The clause states that in cold damage at the stage where cold evil is turning to heat, mistakenly using sweating, fire fumigation, purgation, or warm needling each produces a different changed pattern. It is not found in the three yin and three yang chapters and has a form similar to paragraph 28 of the "Must Not Be Sweated" chapter.
