# §9 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Question: When wind and dampness contend and the whole body aches, by rule sweating should resolve it. When it is a time of overcast sky and unceasing rain, and the physician says sweating may be induced, why does the disease not recover when sweating is induced? Answer: When sweating is induced and the sweat is profuse, only the wind qi departs while the damp qi remains; therefore it does not recover. In treating wind-damp, induce sweating so that there is only a faint, slight sweat, and wind and dampness both depart.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

"A time of overcast sky and unceasing rain" makes clear that dampness prevails. The *Neijing* says: "Yang receives wind qi; yin receives damp qi," and also: "Those damaged by wind receive it first above; those damaged by dampness receive it first below." When wind and dampness contend, wind is outside and dampness inside. Profuse sweat means the qi is abrupt; when abrupt, the outer evil exits but the inner evil cannot, so the wind departs and the dampness remains. Slight sweat means the qi is gentle; when gentle, the evils inside and outside all exit, so wind and dampness both depart.

## Notes

Regarding sweating in wind-damp disease, the clause states that profuse sweating removes only the wind and leaves the dampness, so one should sweat gently to produce a slight sweat. It is the same idea as "a faint sweat" in the administration of Guizhi tang.
