# §6 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When one is ill with a hot body and cold feet, stiff and tense neck and nape, aversion to cold, at times a hot head and red face, red eye vessels, only the head and face shaking, sudden clenched jaw, and arched-back rigidity, it is tetany.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Taiyang wind strike is pure wind strike and taiyang cold damage is pure cold damage; neither produces tetany. Only when taiyang wind strike is compounded by contraction of cold-damp does it transform into tetany. A hot body with cold feet means cold-damp damages the lower part. At times a hot head, red face, and red eye vessels mean wind damages the upper part. The head shakes because wind governs movement. Only the head shakes because the head is the meeting place of all yang and wind damages yang. If damaged by pure wind, the body too would move and shake and the hands and feet would twitch; here cold-damp is carried within, so [only] the head shakes. Clenched jaw is because cold governs tension. Sudden clenching means it is not constantly clenched but at times relaxes; if wind and cold contend, the jaw is clenched and never opens. Here wind and dampness are added, so there is sudden clenching. The foot taiyang vessel starts at the inner canthus, ascends the forehead, and crosses at the vertex; its branch enters from the vertex to connect with the brain, emerges and separately descends the nape, follows the inside of the shoulder blade, flanks the spine to reach the lumbus, descends through the buttocks, and reaches the foot. When wind and cold lodge in the channel, the sinews and vessels become tense, so the neck and nape are stiff and tense and the back is arched.

## Notes

The clause lists the typical signs of tetany (stiffness of the neck and nape, clenched jaw, arched back = opisthotonos, etc.). These are sometimes explained as close to signs seen in tetanus or meningitis.
