# §1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Among the taiyang diseases caused by cold damage, the three—tetany (*chi*), dampness, and summerheat stroke (*ye*)—should be discussed separately, but since they are thought to resemble cold damage, they are shown here.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

*Chi* (痓) should be written *jing* (痙); it is a copying error. *Chi* means "bad," not "stiff." The *Neijing* says: "The lung shifts heat to the kidney, which is transmitted to become soft *chi*." "Soft" means the sinews are soft and without strength; *jing* means the bones are stiff and do not follow one's will. *Jing* means stiffness. The *Qianjin* takes rigidity as *chi*. The classic says: "One with stiff and tense neck and nape, clenched jaw, and arched-back rigidity has *chi*." Seen in this light, it is clear that *chi* is the character *jing*.

## Notes

A paragraph serving as the preface of the chapter "Distinguishing Pulse and Signs of Tetany, Dampness, and Summerheat Stroke." It states that tetany (spasm, rigidity), dampness (damp disease), and summerheat stroke (heat disease, close to heatstroke) begin in the form of taiyang disease and resemble cold damage, so they are distinguished here. Many clauses of this chapter also appear in the *Jingui Yaolüe* chapter on tetany, dampness, and summerheat stroke, where formulas are attached. Cheng Wuji argues that "痓" is a miscopying of "痙."
