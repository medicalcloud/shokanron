# §10 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Disease of one with damp constitution with body aches, fever, yellow face with panting, headache, nasal congestion with vexation, but a large pulse, the ability to eat and drink spontaneously, and harmony in the abdomen without disease: the disease is cold-damp in the head, hence the nasal congestion. Putting medicine into the nose will cure it.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Diseases have shallow and deep; signs have inner and outer. This is a case of shallow damp qi. Why? For damp constitutions it does not say "vexing joint pain" but "body aches," meaning the damp qi does not flow into the joints but lodges outside in the muscle surface. It does not say "fever with a body like smoky yellow" but "fever with yellow face and panting," meaning the dampness does not invade the spleen but presses on the upper burner. When yin receives damp qi, the damp evil is deep; now headache, nasal congestion, and vexation mean the dampness lodges in yang and not in yin. The pulse of damp constitutions should be sunken and fine because damp qi flows inward; a large pulse is yang, meaning the dampness does not flow inward but is outside in the exterior. Also, being able to eat and drink spontaneously, with no particular fullness or glomus in the chest and abdomen, is "harmony in the abdomen without disease," so one knows the damp qi is slight and shallow. Medicine is put into the nose to diffuse and drain the cold-damp in the head.

## Notes

The clause states that when damp evil is shallow and confined to the head (upper burner), it is cured by the external method of putting medicine into the nose. The medicine is not specified; later commentators conjecture it was Guadi san blown into the nose, among others.
