# §14 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Taiyang summerheat stroke with fever and aversion to cold, heavy and aching body, and a wiry, fine, scallion-stalk, slow pulse: after urination there is shivering with hair standing on end and counterflow cold of the hands and feet; with slight exertion, the body immediately becomes hot, the mouth opens, and the front teeth are dry. If sweating is induced, the aversion to cold becomes severe; if warm needling is added, the fever becomes severe; if purged repeatedly, the strangury becomes severe.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Diseases may be in the exterior, in the interior, or in both. This is disease of both exterior and interior. Fever, aversion to cold, and a heavy, aching body mean the exterior is struck by summerheat. A wiry, fine, scallion-stalk, slow pulse means the pulse is vacuous from summerheat stroke. Shivering with hair standing on end and counterflow cold of the hands and feet after urination mean the qi of the taiyang channel is insufficient. "With slight exertion the body immediately becomes hot" means that when the yang is taxed and stirred, the summerheat stroke arises at once. The mouth opening and the front teeth dry mean there is heat as well. The *Neijing* says: "With summerheat, sweating and vexation lead to panting and gasping." The mouth opening means panting and gasping; because the panting does not stop, the front teeth dry. If sweating is induced to remove the exterior evil, the outer yang qi is made vacuous, so aversion to cold becomes severe. If warm needling assists yang, fire heat attacks within, so fever becomes severe. If purgation is used to remove interior heat, the interior becomes vacuous and the bladder dry, so strangury becomes severe. [The commentary's "巳" is read as "已."]

## Notes

The clause describes the signs of summerheat stroke in which summer heat has damaged both qi and yin, and states that sweating, warm needling, and purgation are all mistaken treatments. It is the last paragraph of the chapter "Distinguishing Pulse and Signs of Tetany, Dampness, and Summerheat Stroke." The same text appears in the *Jingui Yaolüe* chapter on tetany, dampness, and summerheat stroke.
