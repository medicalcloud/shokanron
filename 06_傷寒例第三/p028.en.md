# §28 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When yang is exuberant and yin vacuous, sweating causes death and purgation cures; when yang is vacuous and yin exuberant, sweating cures and purgation causes death. This being so, how can Shendan (sweating medicine) be mistakenly used to effuse, or Gansui (purgative) be recklessly used to attack? The treatments of vacuity and exuberance are a thousand li apart, and the crux of good or ill fortune responds like shadow and echo—how could it be easy? Moreover, when Guizhi [tang] goes down the throat, one with yang exuberance perishes at once; when Chengqi [tang] enters the stomach, one with yin exuberance thereby dies. The crux of life and death lies in a moment; seeing the end of the body, there is no time to count days. Here yin and yang, vacuity and repletion, intertwine, and their signs are extremely subtle; sweating and vomiting or purgation are opposites, and their calamities are extremely swift. Yet medical skill is shallow and narrow, ignorant of the origin of disease; treatment errs, causing the patient to die, and [the physician] calls it fate. It comes to the point that wronged souls block the road to the underworld and corpses fill the wilderness. How can the benevolent, seeing this, not grieve!

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The exterior is yang; the interior is yin. Where yin is vacuous, yang will surely converge. When evil of yang exuberance takes advantage of interior vacuity and enters the bowels, it is yang exuberance with yin vacuity. The classic says: "A weak chi pulse is called yin insufficiency; yang qi sinks into the yin and there is fever." Purging removes the internal heat and cures it; if, on the contrary, sweating is induced, the fluids are exhausted and one dies. When the yin pulse is insufficient, yang goes to follow it; when the yang pulse is insufficient, yin goes to assail it. When yin evil takes advantage of exterior vacuity and lodges within the nutritive and defensive, it is yang vacuity with yin exuberance. The classic says: "Suppose the cun kou pulse is faint—this is called yang insufficiency; yin qi rises into the yang and there is shivering aversion to cold." Sweating dissipates the exterior cold and cures it; if, on the contrary, it is purged, the upright qi deserts and one dies. The classic says: "Where sweating should be induced, purging it again is adverse; where it should first be purged, inducing sweating instead is adverse."

Shendan is a sweating medicine; Gansui is a purgative. If sweating or purgation is appropriate, the outcome is auspicious; if inappropriate, inauspicious; the response is like a shadow following form or an echo answering sound.

Guizhi tang is a sweating medicine; Chengqi tang is a purgative. The *Jingui Yuhan* says: "When sweating is forcibly induced where it should not be, it robs the fluids, causing withering and death; when purgation is forcibly applied where it should not be, it opens the intestines to throughflux diarrhea, causing incontinence of urine and stool and death."

When a decoction is given inappropriately, calamity appears immediately; how could there be time to count days?

## Notes

The clause emphasizes that the appropriateness of sweating and purgation is exactly opposite in yang exuberance with yin vacuity (interior repletion heat) and yang vacuity with yin exuberance (exterior cold), and sharply criticizes the harm of mistaken treatment and the inexperience of contemporary physicians. Regarding the meaning of "yin and yang" in "when yang is exuberant and yin vacuous, sweating causes death," besides Cheng Wuji's interpretation as exterior and interior, there are various views.
