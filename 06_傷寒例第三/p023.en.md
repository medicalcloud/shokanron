# §23 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When both chi and cun are feeble and moderate, the Jueyin has contracted disease; it should arise on the sixth or seventh day. Because its vessel follows the genitals and connects with the liver, there are vexation and fullness and retraction of the scrotum. When these three meridians have all contracted disease and it has already entered the bowels, purgation will cure it.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Moderate is the wind pulse. A feeble moderate Jueyin pulse means the pathogen has been transmitted to the Jueyin and the heat qi is already intense, approaching wind. It should arise on the sixth or seventh day because the Shaoyin pathogen is transmitted to the Jueyin. Vexation, fullness, and retraction of the scrotum mean heat qi has gathered within.

When the three yin contract pathogen, the disease is in the interior and by rule it should be purged. But among the three yin some are in the meridians, and when in the meridians sweating is appropriate. Hence it is said: "When it has already entered the bowels, purgation will cure it." The classic says: "The practitioner attending a disease must examine both." [The commentary's "巳" is read as "已."]

## Notes

For the sixth day of six-meridian transmission, the clause describes the pulse (feeble and moderate) and signs (vexation and fullness, retraction of the scrotum) of Jueyin disease, and states that disease of the three yin already entered into the bowels is cured by purgation. It pairs with paragraph 20's "disease of the three yang not yet entered into the bowels is treated by sweating."
