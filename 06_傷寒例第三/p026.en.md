# §26 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In general, when people have an illness and do not treat it promptly, enduring it in hope of recovery, it becomes a chronic illness; in children and women it becomes all the worse. When the seasonal qi is disharmonious, one should speak up early, trace the origin of the evil, and while it is in the interstices treat it in good time; then it rarely fails to recover. If the patient endures it and speaks only after several days, the evil qi enters the viscera and is hard to control. This is the essential precaution when there is illness in a household. In general, in making decoctions, one must not avoid morning or night; as soon as illness is perceived, treat it at once; without waiting for morning or evening, it will recover easily. If there is the slightest delay, the disease is transmitted and changes, and even if one wishes to remove and treat it, it will surely be hard to succeed. If the medicine is not taken according to the method and one willfully disobeys the physician, there is no need to treat.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

In general, when one feels unwell, one must urgently seek treatment; if the time is idly prolonged, the evil qi enters deeply and is hard to control again. The *Qianjin* says: "Whenever there is slight suffering and one feels not as usual, one must speak up early. If one endures it without treatment, hoping for spontaneous recovery, in a short while it becomes a chronic illness." This is what is meant.

Children's qi and blood are not yet complete; women have many diseases of the blood chamber. Evil received in them tends to spread.

The interstices are where the fluids seep and drain, within the junctions of the textures. The *Jingui Yaolüe* says: "The *cou* is where the triple burner communicates with and gathers the original true [qi], where blood and qi pour; the *li* are the textures of the skin and the viscera and bowels." When the evil lodges in the skin, the evil qi is floating and shallow and easy to dissipate and effuse; if treated in good time, it rarely fails to recover. The *Jingui Yuhan* says: "Constantly observe [the condition]; while the form and color are not yet diseased and [the evil] has not yet entered the interstices, take needles and medicines in time, regulate oneself, and entrust it to a good physician, and no disease will fail to recover."

When the evil is in the skin, it is outside, belongs to yang, and is easy to treat; when the evil is transmitted into the interior, it is inside, belongs to yin, and is hard to treat. The *Neijing* says: "One good at treatment treats the skin and hair; next, the flesh and skin; next, the sinews and vessels; next, the six bowels; next, the five viscera. Treating the five viscera is half death and half life." In the past, Marquis Huan neglected a slight illness of the skin until it became an illness of the bone marrow. Can one with illness in the household fail to take precautions?

The *Qianjin* says: "Whenever one first feels unwell, one must treat it at once; before it becomes illness, press on with decoctions and food to break the force of its toxin, and it will naturally recover."

Transmission has regularity; transformation has none. Transmission is passing along the channels, as when the taiyang passes to the yangming. Transformation is an irregular change, as when a yang pattern changes to a yin pattern. Once the evil has been transmitted and transformed, the momentum of the disease is deep. The *Bencao* says: "When the disease's momentum is already formed, half recovery may be obtained; when the disease's momentum has already passed, life will hardly be preserved." [The commentary's "巳" is read as "已."]

The *Neijing* says: "One cannot speak of supreme virtue with those bound by ghosts and spirits; one cannot speak of supreme skill with those who hate needles and stones. When the patient does not permit treatment, the disease will certainly not be cured; treating it will have no effect."

## Notes

The clause sets out precepts for treatment: diseases should be treated early, medicine should be used promptly regardless of day or night, and those who do not follow the physician's instructions should not be treated. Cheng Wuji's commentary supplements the importance of early treatment by citing, among others, the story of Bian Que observing the illness of Marquis Huan of Qi.
