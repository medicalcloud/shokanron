# §27 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In general, cold damage disease is mostly contracted from wind and cold. At first the exterior is struck by wind and cold; once it enters the interior it no longer disperses. There is none that, when properly warmed and covered, will not disperse. Even if, not according to the signs, one intends to attack it, one should still first resolve the exterior and then may purge it. If the exterior has already resolved but the interior does not disperse, and without great fullness there is still cold and heat, the disease is not removed. If the exterior has already resolved but the interior does not disperse, with great fullness and great repletion, hardness and dry stool, it may of course be removed by purgation; even after four or five days it cannot cause harm. If purgation is not appropriate and one attacks at once, the interior becomes vacuous and heat enters, with heat-associated diarrhea, vexation and agitation, and countless other changes; light cases become grave, and heavy cases will certainly die.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

In general, diseases of wind strike and cold damage have since antiquity been called cold damage collectively. The *Qianjin* says: "Cold damage disease arises from wind and cold, which enter the interstices and struggle with the essential qi; the nutritive and defensive are one-sidedly separated, the whole body is obstructed, and one falls ill."

It starts at the skin, enters the channels and network vessels, and is transmitted to the viscera and bowels.

When wind and cold first lodge in the skin, if decoctions are promptly given to warm and effuse, and this is appropriate, there is no evil that will not disperse.

If one first resolves the exterior and then purges, there will be no evil transmitted again.

Although the exterior signs have ceased, if the interior has not reached great hardness and fullness, it still must not be purged. This is because the evil has not yet gathered into repletion; purging it makes the interior vacuous, the evil is again not removed, and cold and heat still arise.

No exterior signs outside with hardness and fullness inside means the signs for purgation are all present. The *Waitai* says: "When the exterior is harmonious and the interior diseased, purgation cures it." When the signs for purgation are present, one need not be bound by the number of days.

When purgation is inappropriate, even light cases change in their signs and become hard to treat—how much more so heavy cases!

## Notes

As a principle of treating cold damage, the clause states the order of first resolving the exterior and, once it is resolved, purging interior repletion, and the harm of misjudging the indications for purgation (great fullness and repletion, dry stool).
