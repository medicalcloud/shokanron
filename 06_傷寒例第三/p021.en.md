# §21 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When both chi and cun are sunken and fine, the taiyin has contracted disease; it should arise on the fourth or fifth day. Because its vessel spreads through the stomach and connects with the throat, there are abdominal fullness and dry throat.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

When yang reaches its extreme, yin receives it. When the evil has passed through all three yang, it is next transmitted to the yin channels. Being in yang is being in the exterior; being in yin is being in the interior. When the evil is in the exterior, yang pulses are seen; when in the interior, yin pulses. The yang evil is transmitted to yin and the evil qi sinks inward, so the taiyin contracts disease and the pulse is sunken and fine at both chi and cun. Since it passes from the three yang to the taiyin, it should arise on the fourth or fifth day. When the evil enters yin, it gradually becomes heat; abdominal fullness and dry throat mean the spleen channel is congested and has become hot.

## Notes

For the fourth day of six-channel transmission, the clause describes the pulse (sunken and fine) and signs (abdominal fullness, dry throat) of taiyin disease. Whereas taiyin disease in the main text of the *Shanghan lun* (Clause 273) centers on vacuity-cold diarrhea, here it is depicted with heat signs, based on the febrile-disease scheme of the *Suwen* "Treatise on Heat."
