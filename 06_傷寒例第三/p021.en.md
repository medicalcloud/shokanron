# §21 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When both chi and cun are deep and thready, the Taiyin has contracted disease; it should arise on the fourth or fifth day. Because its vessel spreads through the stomach and connects with the throat, there are abdominal fullness and dry throat.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

When yang reaches its extreme, yin receives it. When the pathogen has passed through all three yang, it is next transmitted to the yin meridians. Being in yang is being in the exterior; being in yin is being in the interior. When the pathogen is in the exterior, yang pulses are seen; when in the interior, yin pulses. The yang pathogen is transmitted to yin and the pathogenic qi sinks inward, so the Taiyin contracts disease and the pulse is deep and thready at both chi and cun. Since it passes from the three yang to the Taiyin, it should arise on the fourth or fifth day. When the pathogen enters yin, it gradually becomes heat; abdominal fullness and dry throat mean the spleen meridian is congested and has become hot.

## Notes

For the fourth day of six-meridian transmission, the clause describes the pulse (deep and thready) and signs (abdominal fullness, dry throat) of Taiyin disease. Whereas Taiyin disease in the main text of the *Shanghan lun* (Clause 273) centers on deficiency-cold diarrhea, here it is depicted with heat signs, based on the febrile-disease scheme of the *Suwen* "Treatise on Heat."
