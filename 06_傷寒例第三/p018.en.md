# §18 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When both chi and cun are floating, the taiyang has contracted disease; it should arise on the first or second day. Because its vessel connects upward with Fengfu, there are pain of the head and nape and stiffness of the lumbus and spine.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The taiyang is the eldest of the three yang, and its qi floats outside. Thus both chi and cun being floating means the evil qi has just entered the skin and is outside in the exterior; it should arise on the first or second day. Fengfu is the name of a point at the center of the nape. The taiyang vessel enters from the vertex to connect with the brain, then emerges and separately descends the nape; thus it connects upward with Fengfu. Its channel follows the inside of the shoulder blade, flanks the spine, and reaches the lumbus; so in disease there are pain of the head and nape and stiffness of the lumbus and spine.

## Notes

Paragraphs 18–23 are a group explaining, by the pulse and course of the channels, the scheme of "six-channel transmission" based on the *Suwen* "Treatise on Heat," in which cold damage passes day by day from taiyang → yangming → shaoyang → taiyin → shaoyin → jueyin. This paragraph describes taiyang disease (days 1–2).
