# §15 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The *Great Treatise on Yin and Yang* says: The qi of spring is warm and mild, the qi of summer hot, the qi of autumn clear and cool, the qi of winter freezing. This is the order of the regular qi of the four seasons. In winter it is severely cold and all things are stored deep; if the noble person keeps secure and close, he will not be damaged by cold. One who is touched and assailed by it is said to have cold damage. Being damaged by the qi of any of the four seasons can cause disease, but cold damage is considered a toxin because it is the most killing and pestilential qi. One who is struck and falls ill at once is said to have cold damage. In one who does not fall ill at once, the cold toxin is stored in the flesh and skin; by spring it transforms into warm disease, and by summer into summerheat disease. Summerheat disease is extreme heat, heavier than warm [disease]. Thus that hardworking people often have warm and heat diseases in spring and summer is all caused by contact with cold in winter; it is not seasonal epidemic qi. As for seasonal epidemics in general: in spring it should be warm but is, on the contrary, very cold; in summer it should be hot but is, on the contrary, very cool; in autumn it should be cool but is, on the contrary, very hot; in winter it should be cold but is, on the contrary, very warm. This is having a qi out of its time; therefore, when within one year the diseases of old and young are largely alike, it is seasonal epidemic qi. To discern disease from the regular qi of the four seasons and the method for seasonal pestilential qi, one should divine by the Dipper calendar. After the Frost's Descent node of the ninth month, it should gradually become cold, becoming very cold toward winter; after the Rain Water node of the first month, [the cold] should resolve. It is called Rain Water because ice and snow melt into rainwater. After the Awakening of Insects node of the second month, the qi gradually becomes mild and warm, very hot toward summer, and turns cool in autumn. From after Frost's Descent until before the Spring Equinox, whoever is touched and assailed by frost and dew, whose body is struck by cold and falls ill at once, is said to have cold damage. In the ninth and tenth months the cold qi is still slight, so the disease is light; in the eleventh and twelfth months the cold is already severe, so the disease is heavy; in the first and second months the cold is gradually about to resolve, so the disease is again light. This is a person who has not adjusted in winter and happens to be damaged by cold, falling ill at once. When winter has out-of-season warmth, it is called winter warmth. The toxin of winter warmth differs greatly from cold damage. Winter warmth also has earlier and later [onsets], overlapping one another, and also light and heavy, and the treatments differ; the signs are as in a later chapter. From after the Beginning of Spring node, if in the meantime there is no sudden great cold and no ice or snow, yet someone falls ill with vigorous heat, this belongs to the yang qi of spring effusing the cold hidden since winter, which transforms into warm disease. From after the Spring Equinox until before the Autumn Equinox node, when the sky has sudden cold, it is all seasonal cold pestilence. In the third and fourth months there may be sudden cold; at that time the yang qi is still weak and is broken by the cold, so the heat of the disease is still light. In the fifth and sixth months the yang qi is already exuberant; broken by cold, the heat of the disease is heavy. In the seventh and eighth months the yang qi is already declining; broken by cold, the heat of the disease is also slight. These diseases resemble warm disease and summerheat disease, but the treatment differs. Every fifteen days one qi is obtained; within the four seasons, each season has six qi, and four times six is called the twenty-four qi. Yet the climate sometimes should arrive but does not, sometimes arrives before it should, and sometimes arrives in excess; all of these become disease qi. But the movement and stillness of heaven and earth, and the drumming of yin and yang, each set right one qi. Thus the warmth of spring becomes the heat of summer; the indignation of autumn becomes the anger of winter. Therefore after the Winter Solstice one yang line rises and one yin line descends; after the Summer Solstice one yang qi descends and one yin qi rises. Thus at the two solstices of winter and summer, yin and yang unite; at the two equinoxes of spring and autumn, yin and yang separate. When yin and yang interchange, people change and fall ill. Hence the noble person nourishes yang in spring and summer and nourishes yin in autumn and winter, following the hard and soft of heaven and earth. The petty person is touched and assailed and will certainly contract sudden illness; one must know in which channel the virulent qi lodges and what disease it produces, and examine in detail to deal with it. Thus, damaged by wind in spring, one will certainly have swill diarrhea in summer; damaged by summerheat in summer, one will certainly have malaria in autumn; damaged by dampness in autumn, one will certainly cough in winter; damaged by cold in winter, one will certainly have warm disease in spring. This is the inevitable way; how can it not be examined and made clear? Cold damage disease deepens day by day, and formulas and treatments are applied accordingly. When people today have cold damage, sometimes it is not treated early at the beginning, sometimes the treatment does not fit the disease, sometimes many days pass in delay and only in distress is the physician informed; and the physician does not treat it in proper sequence, so it does not hit the disease. In all cases one should adjust and compose formulas according to the moment, and then there will be no lack of effect. Now I have searched out and collected Zhongjing's old treatise, recording its signs, pulse diagnosis, voice and complexion, and the true formulas for diseases with divine efficacy, intending to provide against the urgent needs of the age.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Spring and summer are yang. Spring is warm and summer hot because the movement of yang begins in warmth and flourishes in heat. Autumn and winter are yin. Autumn is cool and winter cold because the movement of yin begins in coolness and flourishes in cold.

In the three months of winter pure yin is in charge; yang then hides and is stored; water freezes, the earth cracks, and the cold qi is severe and congealed. At this time, those good at nurturing life keep their comings and goings secure and close, avoid cold and go to warmth, so they are not damaged by cold. Those who wade through cold and are touched and assailed by frost and snow and fall ill are said to have cold damage.

Wind in spring, summerheat in summer, dampness in autumn, cold in winter—these are called the qi of the four seasons.

Heat is yang, and yang governs life; cold is yin, and yin governs killing. Disease from yin cold is the most austere and killing, toxic and pestilential qi.

The *Neijing* says: "[Disease arising] before the Summer Solstice day is warm disease; after the Summer Solstice day, summerheat disease." Diseases of warmth and summerheat originally arise from damage by cold, so the court physicians all call them cold damage.

Disease from irregular climate in the four seasons is called seasonal epidemic qi. Disease caused by the prevailing seasonal qi is not violent pestilential qi, but those affected necessarily share it; therefore within one year the diseases of old and young are largely alike.

The regular qi of the four seasons is wind in spring, summerheat in summer, dampness in autumn, and cold in winter. Seasonal epidemics are the seasonal epidemic qi. Warmth is cold contracted in winter and arising in spring. Pestilence is violent pestilential qi. Divine in advance by the Dipper's orientation, determine the cold and warmth of the season, examine the lightness or heaviness of the evil qi, and treat it. Hence the following text says:

Cold in winter, warmth in spring, heat in summer, and coolness in autumn are the regular qi of the four seasons.

This is being struck by the regular qi of the four seasons and falling ill at once.

This is seasonal epidemic qi, what was said earlier: "in winter it should be cold but is, on the contrary, very warm."

This is warm disease. The *Neijing* says: "Damaged by cold in winter, one will certainly have warm disease in spring."

This is pestilential qi. These several items clarify that, by the earlier Dipper-calendar method, one divines the climate following the seasons, and that the cold or heat and lightness or heaviness of the resulting diseases differ.

There are twelve node qi and twelve center qi, twenty-four in all. The *Neijing* says: "Five days are called a period; three periods are called a qi; six qi are called a season; four seasons are called a year."

It is suspected that a phrase, "sometimes it arrives but does not depart," has been omitted [here]. Checking the *Jingui Yaolüe*: "There is arriving before arriving, arriving without arriving, arriving without departing, and arriving in excess—why? The master said: After the Winter Solstice, at midnight of the *jiazi* day, the shaoyang arises; at the time of the shaoyin, yang begins to be born and heaven obtains warmth. If heaven is warm before *jiazi* is reached, this is arriving before arriving. If *jiazi* is reached but heaven is not yet warm, this is arriving without arriving. If *jiazi* is reached and heaven's great cold does not resolve, this is arriving without departing. If *jiazi* is reached and heaven is as warm as the fifth or sixth month of high summer, this is arriving in excess." The *Neijing* says: "Arriving harmoniously is normal; arriving severely is disease; arriving contrarily is disease; arriving without arriving is disease; arriving before arriving is disease." Seen in this light, the omission is clear.

The *Neijing* says: "Yin and yang are the way of heaven and earth. Clear yang becomes heaven, moving without rest; turbid yin becomes earth, still and unmoving." The qi of yin and yang of heaven and earth drum against each other, producing spring, summer, autumn, and winter, cold, heat, warmth, and coolness, each setting right one qi.

The warmth of spring becoming the heat of summer is going from birth to growth. The indignation of autumn becoming the anger of winter is going from austerity to killing.

In the tenth month all six lines are yin, and the *kun* hexagram is in use. When yin reaches its extreme, yang comes; yang is born at *zi*. After the Winter Solstice one yang line rises and one yin line descends; in hexagrams this is *fu* (Return), meaning yang qi returns. In the fourth month all six lines are yang, and the *qian* hexagram is in use. When yang reaches its extreme, yin comes; yin is born at *wu*. After the Summer Solstice one yang qi descends and one yin qi rises; in hexagrams this is *gou* (Coming to Meet), meaning yin meets yang. The *Neijing* says: "Forty-five days after the Winter Solstice, yang qi rises slightly and yin qi descends slightly; forty-five days after the Summer Solstice, yin qi rises slightly and yang qi descends slightly."

Yang is born at *zi* and yin at *wu*; here yin and yang meet, so it is called union. Yang retreats at *you* and yin at *mao*; here yin and yang turn their backs on each other, so it is called separation. The *Neijing* says: "The arrival of qi is called *zhi* (solstice); the dividing of qi is called *fen* (equinox). At the solstices the qi are the same; at the equinoxes the qi differ."

When the qi of yin and yang of heaven and earth intermix and are not regular, people thereby change and fall ill. The *Neijing* says: "Yin and yang intermix, and change arises from it."

The *Neijing* says: "Those who nurture life must follow the seasons: in spring and summer nourish yang, with coolness and cold; in autumn and winter nourish yin, with warmth and heat. The reason is that one follows its root."

Those who cannot regulate and nourish themselves according to the four seasons and are touched and assailed by cold and warmth will surely develop sudden illness. The physician should apply his mind, examine in detail, and treat it.

In spring, wind qi prevails greatly. When one is damaged by wind in spring, the wind qi communicates with the liver. Since the liver is in its prime in spring, although wind enters it cannot arise at once; it stirs only when the liver declines in summer. Wind spreading causes disease of the extremities, so it should arise in the four limbs; but in summer yang qi is exuberant outside, so the wind cannot effuse outward, and it attacks inward, producing swill diarrhea. Swill diarrhea is diarrhea with undigested rice and grain, yellow in color. In autumn, damp qi prevails greatly. When one is damaged by dampness in autumn, the dampness invades the lung. Since the lung is in its prime in autumn, although dampness enters it cannot arise at once; only when the lung declines in winter does the dampness begin to stir. Rain spreading causes disease of the abdomen, so it should arise as diarrhea; but in winter yang qi is secure within, so the damp qi cannot move downward, and it counterflows upward, producing cough. In summer, summerheat qi prevails greatly. When one is damaged by summerheat in summer, since yin governs the interior in summer, although summerheat enters its momentum cannot yet stir; when in autumn yin comes out and yang governs the interior, the summerheat stirs and is transmitted to yin, producing *jie* malaria. *Jie* attacks once every two days; *nüe* attacks once a day. In winter, cold qi prevails greatly. When one is damaged by cold in winter, since yang governs the interior in winter, although cold enters its momentum cannot yet stir; when in spring yang comes out and yin governs the interior, the cold stirs and is transmitted to yang, producing warm disease. This is the inevitable way in which contracting the regular qi of the four seasons causes disease.

The *Neijing* says: "When it has not yet been three days, sweating will cure it; when it has been three days, draining will cure it." [The commentary's "巳" is read as "已."]

That Zhongjing's book, after a thousand years, is still prominent and used in the world today is due to the efforts of Wang Shuhe.

## Notes

A long paragraph forming the core of the "Examples of Cold Damage" chapter, citing the *Great Treatise on Yin and Yang* to set out a general theory of externally contracted febrile diseases centered on cold damage.

- It distinguishes the normal climate of the four seasons (warm spring, hot summer, cool autumn, cold winter) from "seasonal epidemic qi" (epidemics) caused by unseasonable climate.
- It states the theory of "hidden cold transforming into warmth": cold received in winter that causes illness at once is cold damage; if latent and arising in spring, warm disease; if in summer, summerheat disease. This theory greatly influenced the later development of warm-disease studies.
- It discusses the classification of diseases due to unseasonable climate such as winter warmth and cold pestilence, judging the climate by the twenty-four qi and the Dipper calendar, and the relation of the solstices and equinoxes to yin and yang.
- It quotes the *Suwen* theory of hidden qi of the four seasons, such as "damaged by wind in spring, swill diarrhea arises in summer."
- At the end it describes the abuses of the time, such as delayed treatment or treatment unsuited to the disease, and states the editorial intention of collecting and recording Zhongjing's old treatise. For this reason "Examples of Cold Damage" is often regarded as compiled by Wang Shuhe, and Cheng Wuji too praises Wang Shuhe's achievement in his final note.
- Cheng Wuji's remark "疑漏或有至而不去此一句" suggests that the phrase "sometimes it arrives but does not depart" may have dropped out before the text's "sometimes arrives in excess."
