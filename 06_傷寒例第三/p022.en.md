# §22 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When both chi and cun are sunken, the shaoyin has contracted disease; it should arise on the fifth or sixth day. Because its vessel penetrates the kidney, connects with the lung, and is tied to the root of the tongue, there are dry mouth, parched tongue, and thirst.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The shaoyin is kidney water, whose nature tends downward. When the shaoyin contracts disease, the pulse is sunken at both chi and cun. When the taiyin evil does not recover on the fourth or fifth day, by the fifth or sixth day it is transmitted to the shaoyin; so shaoyin disease should arise on the fifth or sixth day. "When people are damaged by cold it becomes heat disease" means that though it begins as cold it ends as heat. Dry mouth, parched tongue, and thirst in shaoyin disease mean the evil has been transmitted into the interior and the heat qi gradually deepens. [The commentary's "巳" is read as "已."]

## Notes

For the fifth day of six-channel transmission, the clause explains the pulse (sunken) and signs (dry mouth, parched tongue, thirst) of shaoyin disease by the course of the shaoyin channel (kidney, lung, root of the tongue). Like the previous paragraph, it is based on the febrile-disease scheme.
