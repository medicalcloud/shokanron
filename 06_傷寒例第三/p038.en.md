# §38 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

An exuberant pulse with a cold body is contracted from cold damage; a vacuous pulse with a hot body is contracted from summerheat damage. When the pulse is exuberant at both yin and yang and great sweating does not resolve it, it is fatal. When the pulse is vacuous at both yin and yang and the heat does not stop, it is fatal. When the pulse arrives now rapid, now sparse, it is fatal. When the pulse arrives like a twisting rope, death comes that day. With delirious and wild speech, slight body heat, a floating large pulse, and warm hands and feet, one lives; with counterflow cold and a sunken fine pulse, one dies within one day. All the foregoing are the signs of cold damage and heat disease.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The *Neijing* says: "The vessels are the storehouse of blood. When the pulse is replete, the blood is replete; when the pulse is vacuous, the blood is vacuous." Cold damages blood; when evil merges with the blood, blood is exuberant and qi vacuous, so in cold damage the pulse is exuberant and the body cold. Heat damages qi; when evil merges with the qi, qi is exuberant and blood vacuous, so in summerheat damage the pulse is vacuous and the body hot.

A pulse exuberant at both yin and yang should resolve with sweating. If sweating does not resolve it, the evil qi prevails within and the upright qi deserts outward, so it is fatal. The *Neijing* says: "When sweat comes out but the pulse is still agitated and exuberant, it is fatal." The *Qianjin* says: "In heat disease, when sweat has already been obtained but the pulse is still agitated and exuberant, this is the extreme of the yang pulse; it is fatal." [The commentary's "巳得汗" is read as "已得汗."]

A pulse vacuous at both yin and yang means the true qi is weak; unceasing heat means the evil qi prevails. The *Neijing* says: "In warm disease, one with severe vacuity dies." A pulse arriving now sparse, now rapid is fatal because the heavenly true qi of the nutritive and defensive is severed.

[Like a twisting rope] means tense and urgent and not soft; there is no stomach qi within, so one dies within that day.

Delirious and wild speech is yang disease. Slight body heat, a floating large pulse, and warm hands and feet mean pulse and disease correspond. If the body is counterflow cold and the pulse sunken and fine, a yin pulse appears in yang disease; pulse and disease do not correspond, so one dies within one day. The *Nanjing* says: "When the pulse does not correspond to the disease and the disease does not correspond to the pulse, it is a fatal disease." [The commentary's "滎衛" is read as "栄衛" (nutritive and defensive).]

## Notes

The clause describes the difference in pulse between cold damage and summerheat damage, pulses of fatal patterns (remaining exuberant despite sweating, vacuous with unceasing heat, irregular, like a twisting rope, etc.), and the prognosis of patients with delirious speech, concluding the "Examples of Cold Damage" chapter. The closing phrase "all the foregoing are the signs of cold damage and heat disease" marks the end of this account of cold damage and febrile disease.
