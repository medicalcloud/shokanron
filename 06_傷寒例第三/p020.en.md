# §20 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When both chi and cun are wiry, the Shaoyang has contracted disease; it should arise on the third or fourth day. Because its vessel follows the rib-sides and connects with the ears, there are chest and rib-side pain and deafness. When these three meridians have all contracted disease but it has not yet entered the bowels, sweating will cure it.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The *Neijing* says: "The Shaoyang within yang communicates with the qi of spring." The spring pulse is wiry; when both chi and cun are wiry, one knows the Shaoyang has contracted the pathogen. When on the second or third day the Yangming pathogen does not recover, it is transmitted to the Shaoyang, so it should arise on the third or fourth day. Chest and rib-side pain and deafness mean the meridian is congested and not free.

When the three yang contract pathogen, the disease is in the exterior and by rule should be resolved by sweating. But among the three yang some enter the bowels at once, and when they enter the bowels purgation is appropriate. Hence it is said: "When it has not yet entered the bowels, sweating will cure it." [The commentary's "巳" is read as "已."]

## Notes

For the third day of six-meridian transmission, the clause describes the pulse (wiry) and signs (chest and rib-side pain, deafness) of Shaoyang disease, and states that disease of the three yang not yet entered into the bowels is cured by sweating. Since the main text of the *Shanghan lun* prohibits sweating in Shaoyang disease (Clause 265), "may be sweated" here is interpreted as a statement based on the *Suwen* "Treatise on Heat" scheme that groups the three yang together.
