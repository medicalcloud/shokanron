# §31 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In general, when one has a seasonal disease and by the fifth or sixth day is thirsty and wants to drink water but cannot drink much, it should not be given. Why? Because the heat in the abdomen is still slight and cannot disperse it, so it would instead make the person ill. When by the seventh or eighth day there is great thirst with a desire to drink water, it should still be given according to the signs; when giving it, always keep it short of enough and do not let one drink to one's heart's content. If one says one can drink one dou, give five sheng. If after drinking the abdomen is full, urination inhibited, or there is panting or hiccup, it must not be given. If suddenly there is great sweating, it is spontaneous recovery.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

When heat is in the upper burner, there is dispersion-thirst; that is, heat disperses the fluids and the upper burner dries, producing thirst. Great heat can disperse water; slight heat cannot disperse it. If one drinks forcibly, the drink collects and transforms into various diseases. By the seventh or eighth day, yang prevails and the qi warms toward resolution; often there is still great thirst, but water should be given a little at a time to moisten the stomach qi, not drunk to one's heart's content. If after drinking the abdomen is full, urination inhibited, or there is panting or hiccup, water-rheum has collected within and does not dissipate, and no more should be given. Suddenly the yang qi passes, the water qi dissipates, first effusing outward, and there is great sweating and resolution.

## Notes

The clause gives precautions on giving water for thirst during febrile disease: giving much when the heat is slight causes water to collect and produce disease, and even great thirst in the recovery stage should be met sparingly. It has the same purport as Clause 71 of the taiyang chapter and others.
