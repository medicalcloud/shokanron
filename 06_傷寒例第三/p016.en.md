# §16 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Moreover, lands differ in warmth and coolness, height and lowness; the hard or soft nature of things and the diet and dwelling also differ. Therefore the Yellow Emperor raised questions about the four directions, and Qibo set forth the powers of the four treatments, to instruct later worthies and enlighten those not yet awakened. The practitioner attending a disease must examine both.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

In the east the earth's qi is warm, in the south hot, in the west cool, and in the north cold. The northwest is high and the southeast low. This is the difference of lands in warmth and coolness, height and lowness. In the east people dwell at ease and eat fish; in the west they dwell on hills and eat rich food; in the south they live in damp places and like sour food; in the north they live in the open and consume milk. These are the differences of diet and dwelling. In the east, treatment by stone needles is suitable; in the west, by potent drugs; in the south, by fine needles; in the north, by moxibustion. This is the difference in medical treatment among the four directions. In treating disease, the physician should examine what suits the land.

## Notes

The clause states that treatment should take into account regional differences in climate, environment, and way of life. Cheng Wuji's commentary explains it based on the content of the *Suwen* chapter "Different Methods for Different Regions" (stone needles in the east, potent drugs in the west, moxibustion in the north, nine needles in the south, etc.). In that chapter the south is assigned the "nine needles" and the center "guiding and massage," so the commentary's "fine needles" differs in wording.
