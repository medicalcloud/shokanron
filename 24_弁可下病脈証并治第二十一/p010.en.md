# §10 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Question: When a person's illness involves abiding food, how is it distinguished? The master said: When the cun kou pulse is floating and large but, on pressure, on the contrary, rough, and the chi is also slightly rough, one knows there is abiding food. It should be purged; Da Chengqi tang is appropriate.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The cun examines the outside; the chi examines the inside. Floating examines the exterior; sunken examines the interior. A floating large cun kou pulse means qi repletion with blood vacuity. Being, on pressure, rough, and the chi also faint and rough, means abiding food in the stomach with the interior qi not harmonious. Give Da Chengqi tang to purge the abiding food.

## Notes

The clause describes how to distinguish abiding food (undigested stagnant food) by pulse diagnosis. The same text appears in the *Jingui Yaolüe* chapter on abdominal fullness, cold *shan*, and abiding food.
