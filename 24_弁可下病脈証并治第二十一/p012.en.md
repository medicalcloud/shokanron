# §12 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When diarrhea has recovered but recurs at the same year, month, day, and hour, it is because the disease has not been exhausted; it should be purged; Da Chengqi tang is appropriate.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Arising in spring, the liver receives it first; in summer, the heart first; in the extreme yin (late summer), the spleen first; in autumn, the lung first. Suppose disease qi is received in spring; it will certainly damage the liver. When treated it is hard to cure, and when the evil is not exhausted, at the original month and day in spring, inside and outside stimulate each other and the evil will certainly stir again and cause pain. Diarrhea is a disease of the intestines and stomach; the abiding accumulation is not exhausted, so it should be purged away.

"Diarrhea with, on the contrary, a slippery pulse: there is something to be removed; purging cures it; Da Chengqi tang is appropriate." The *Maijing* says: "A slippery pulse is disease of food." Diarrhea with a slippery pulse means abiding food within, hence "there is something to be removed." Give Da Chengqi tang to purge the abiding food. [The second half of this commentary is the text of paragraph 14 and its commentary.]

## Notes

The clause states that recurrence of diarrhea at the same season after recovery is because the root of the disease (abiding accumulation) remains, which should be removed by purgation. The same text appears in the *Jingui Yaolüe*. The second half of Cheng Wuji's commentary is the text and commentary of paragraph 14.
