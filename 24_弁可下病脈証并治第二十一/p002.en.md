# §2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In general, for those who may be purged, decoctions are better than pills or powders. Stop as soon as it hits the disease; it is not necessary to finish the preparation.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

*Tang* (decoction) means *dang* (to wash away). It washes the intestines and stomach, irrigates the viscera and bowels, pushes out the old and dry binding, drives off heat and purges cold, breaks and disperses evil pestilence, regulates and guides, moistens the withered, gladdens the skin, and boosts blood and qi. Water can cleanse all things, so it is better than pills or powders. "Stop as soon as it hits the disease" is as in the Chengqi tang patterns: "If one dose produces a bowel movement, stop the remaining doses," and "If one dose stops the delirious speech, do not take more." This is not finishing the preparation.

## Notes

The clause states the principle that decoctions are best for purgation and that taking should stop once it takes effect. It has the same purport as paragraphs 3 and 4 of the "May Be Sweated" chapter.
