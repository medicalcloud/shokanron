# Clause 85 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

A person with sores (*chuangjia*), even with body pain, must not be made to sweat; if sweat comes out, there will be tetany (*chi*).

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

When the exterior is vacuous and heat gathers, sores arise. Body pain in a person with sores resembles cold damage, but sweating must not be induced. If sweating is induced, the exterior qi becomes ever more vacuous and the momentum of heat ever more severe, generating wind, so it turns into tetany.

## Notes

A person with chronic suppurating sores has qi, blood, and fluids depleted by the pus, so even with body pain resembling an exterior pattern, sweating would leave the sinews unnourished and cause convulsions (tetany); hence it is prohibited.
