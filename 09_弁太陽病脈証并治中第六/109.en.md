# Clause 109 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In cold damage with fever, huddled aversion to cold, great thirst with a desire to drink water, the abdomen will certainly be full; with spontaneous sweating and free urination, the disease is about to resolve. This is the liver overwhelming the lung; it is called "transverse" (*heng*). Needle Qimen. 59.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Fever with huddled aversion to cold in cold damage is a lung disease. Great thirst with a desire to drink water means the liver qi prevails. The *Yuhan* reads "great thirst with a desire to drink vinegar-water," which shows the liver qi prevails. In cold damage, one who wants to drink water recovers; if he does not recover and the abdomen becomes full, the liver is moving to overwhelm the lung and the water cannot move. The Classic says, "Wood moving to overwhelm metal is called transverse." Needling Qimen drains the exuberant liver qi; the qi of liver and lung become even, the water disperses and the fluids flow; outwardly there is spontaneous sweating, inwardly urination becomes free, and it resolves.

## Notes

Paired with Clause 108, this calls "transverse" the case in which the liver (wood) insults the lung (metal), which should normally restrain it. Needling Qimen to drain the liver qi brings sweating and urination and resolves the disease. Many commentators think the word order is disordered.
