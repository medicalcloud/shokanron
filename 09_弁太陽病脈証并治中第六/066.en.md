# Clause 66 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When after sweating there is abdominal distension and fullness, Houpo Shengjiang Banxia Gancao Renshen tang (Magnolia Bark, Fresh Ginger, Pinellia, Licorice, and Ginseng Decoction) governs it. Formula 29.

Houpo half a jin (mix-fried, remove the bark); shengjiang half a jin (sliced); banxia half a sheng (washed); gancao 2 liang; renshen 1 liang.

Of the five ingredients above, boil with 1 dou of water down to 3 sheng, remove the dregs, and take 1 sheng warm, three times a day.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Abdominal distension after vomiting and abdominal fullness after purgation are both repletion—meaning the evil qi has taken advantage of the vacuity to enter the interior and become replete. After sweating, the exterior has already resolved; abdominal distension and fullness are known not to be interior repletion. The fluids of the spleen and stomach are insufficient, the qi is rough and does not flow, and blockage produces fullness. This decoction is given to harmonize the spleen and stomach and lower the qi.

(Nature and flavor) Houpo: bitter, warm. Shengjiang: acrid, warm. Banxia: acrid, neutral. Gancao: sweet, neutral.

The *Neijing* says: "The spleen desires moderation; quickly eat sweet to moderate it, and use bitter to discharge it." The bitterness of houpo discharges the abdominal fullness, the sweetness of renshen and gancao boosts the spleen and stomach, and the acridity of banxia and shengjiang disperses the stagnant qi.

## Notes

The clause on Houpo Shengjiang Banxia Gancao Renshen tang, used when after sweating the spleen-stomach qi is weakened, transformation stagnates, and the abdomen becomes distended and full.

- It is regarded not as replete abdominal fullness (to be purged) but as fullness of "vacuity with repletion mixed in" (qi stagnation).
- With large amounts of houpo, shengjiang, and banxia and small amounts of renshen and gancao, it is generally explained as mainly removing qi stagnation while also supplementing the spleen and stomach.
