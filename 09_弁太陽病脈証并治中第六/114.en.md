# Clause 114 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Taiyang disease: when it is fumigated with fire but no sweat is obtained, the person will certainly become agitated; if it does not resolve when the meridian [period] arrives, there will certainly be blood in the stool. This is called pathogenic fire.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

This is pathogenic fire pressing the blood so that the blood moves downward. When Taiyang disease is fumigated with fire and no sweat is obtained, the heat has no exit; the yin is deficient and receives fire, so there will certainly be agitation. In six days transmission through the meridians is complete; on the seventh day it reaches the Taiyang meridian again, and the heat qi should resolve. If it does not resolve, the heat qi presses the blood to move downward, and there will certainly be *qingxue*. *Qing* means the privy.

## Notes

When fumigation with fire fails to produce sweat and the heat presses inward causing blood in the stool, it is called "pathogenic fire." *Qing* is taken as interchangeable with *qing* 圊 (privy), and *qingxue* is generally understood as blood in the stool (as Cheng Wuji also says).
