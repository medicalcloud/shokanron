# Clause 62 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When after sweating there is body pain and the pulse is deep and slow, Guizhi jia Shaoyao Shengjiang ge yi liang Renshen san liang Xinjia tang (Newly Augmented Cinnamon Twig Decoction with 1 liang each of Peony and Fresh Ginger and 3 liang of Ginseng added) governs it. Formula 25.

Guizhi 3 liang (remove the bark); shaoyao 4 liang; gancao 2 liang (mix-fried); renshen 3 liang; dazao 12 pieces (split); shengjiang 4 liang.

Of the six ingredients above, boil with 1 dou 2 sheng of water down to 3 sheng, remove the dregs, and take 1 sheng warm. Originally called Guizhi tang; now shaoyao, shengjiang, and renshen are added.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Body pain after sweating means the evil qi has not been exhausted. A deep, slow pulse means the nutritive blood is insufficient. The Classic says: "When the pulse is deep, the nutritive qi is faint," and also, "Slow means the nutritive qi is insufficient and the blood scant." Guizhi tang is given to resolve the unexhausted evil, and shaoyao, shengjiang, and renshen are added to boost the insufficient blood.

## Notes

The clause on Guizhi Xinjia tang, used when body pain remains after sweating and the pulse has become deep and slow.

- It is generally understood that sweating has damaged the nutritive blood and qi so that the muscles are not nourished and ache; shaoyao and shengjiang are increased and renshen added to supplement qi and blood.
- The key point is to distinguish by the pulse body pain before sweating (due to exterior evil, floating and tight pulse) from body pain after sweating (due to insufficient nutritive blood, deep and slow pulse).
