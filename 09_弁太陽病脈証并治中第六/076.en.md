# Clause 76 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When after sweating neither water nor medicine can enter the mouth, it is a counterflow [from mistreatment]; if sweating is induced again, there will certainly be vomiting and diarrhea that do not stop. When after sweating, vomiting, or purgation there is vacuity vexation with inability to sleep, in severe cases there will certainly be tossing and turning (*dao* is pronounced like *dao* 到; likewise below), with anguish in the heart (*ao* is the reverse spelling of *wu-hao*, *nong* of *nu-dong*; likewise below): Zhizi Chi tang (Gardenia and Fermented Soybean Decoction) governs it. If there is shortage of qi (shortness of breath), Zhizi Gancao Chi tang (Gardenia, Licorice, and Fermented Soybean Decoction) governs it. If there is vomiting, Zhizi Shengjiang Chi tang (Gardenia, Fresh Ginger, and Fermented Soybean Decoction) governs it. 38.

Zhizi Chi tang
Zhizi (gardenia) 14 fruits (split); xiangchi (fermented soybean) 4 ge (wrapped in cotton).
Of the two ingredients above, with 4 sheng of water first boil the zhizi to obtain 2.5 sheng, add the chi, boil down to 1.5 sheng, remove the dregs, divide into two doses, and give one dose warm. If vomiting occurs, stop the later dose.

Zhizi Gancao tang Chi [Zhizi Gancao Chi tang]
Zhizi 14 fruits (split); gancao 2 liang (mix-fried); xiangchi 4 liang (wrapped in cotton).
Of the three ingredients above, with 4 sheng of water first boil the zhizi and gancao down to 2.5 sheng, add the chi, boil down to 1.5 sheng, remove the dregs, divide into two doses, and give one dose warm. If vomiting occurs, stop the later dose.

Zhizi Shengjiang Chi tang
Zhizi 14 fruits (split); shengjiang 5 liang; xiangchi 4 ge (wrapped in cotton).
Of the three ingredients above, with 4 sheng of water first boil the zhizi and shengjiang down to 2.5 sheng, add the chi, boil down to 1.5 sheng, remove the dregs, divide into two doses, and give one dose warm. If vomiting occurs, stop the later dose.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

When after sweating neither water nor medicine can enter the mouth, it is vomiting counterflow: sweating has lost the yang, and the stomach is vacuous and cold. If sweating is induced again, the yang qi is further damaged and the stomach qi greatly vacuous, so vomiting and diarrhea do not stop.

When after sweating, vomiting, or purgation evil heat takes advantage of the vacuity to lodge in the chest, it is called vacuity vexation. [Vexation] is heat: the chest is vexed and hot, oppressed and unable to discharge it. When heat qi is hidden in the interior, there is a liking for sleep; here the heat qi floats upward and disturbs the yang qi, so there is inability to sleep. The heart dislikes heat; when the heat is extreme, the spirit will certainly be clouded. Therefore in severe cases there is tossing and turning without rest, with anguish and confused oppression in the heart. *Aonong* is what is commonly called *gutu* (restless agitation). The *Neijing* says: "What is high, make it pass out [upward]." Zhizi Chi tang is given to make [the patient] vomit the evil in the chest.

(Nature and flavor) Zhizi: bitter, cold. Xiangchi: bitter, cold.

Sour and bitter cause upwelling and discharge and are yin. Bitter causes upwelling and vomiting; cold overcomes heat. Zhizi combined with chi is fitting as an emetic.

Shortage of qi means heat has damaged the qi; gancao is added to boost the qi. Vomiting means vexation from heat with qi counterflow; shengjiang is added to disperse the qi. With shortage of qi, the qi, assailed by heat, scatters and does not gather, so it is appropriate to supplement with sweet. With vomiting, the qi, assailed by heat, counterflows and does not disperse, so it is appropriate to disperse with acrid.

## Notes

The clause on the Zhizi Chi tang group, used when after sweating, vomiting, or purgation heat is trapped in the chest, causing "vacuity vexation" (anguished oppression in the chest, insomnia).

- The "vacuity" of vacuity vexation is generally understood to mean heat without a tangible evil (such as dry stool or phlegm-water), not weakness of the body.
- Zhizi Chi tang clears depressed heat in the chest; gancao is added for shortness of breath and shengjiang for vomiting.
- From "if vomiting occurs, stop the later dose," it was long regarded as an emetic (as by Cheng Wuji), but since modern times the view that it is not necessarily an emetic has gained ground.
- The formula name "Zhizi Gancao tang Chi" follows the base text (the other sources have "Zhizi Gancao Chi tang").
