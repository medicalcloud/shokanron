# Clause 113 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the form is like cold damage but the pulse is not wiry and tight but weak: one with a weak [pulse] will certainly be thirsty, and if subjected to fire will certainly have delirious speech. One with a weak [pulse], fever, and a floating pulse, when resolved, should recover with sweating.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

"The form is like cold damage" means headache and generalized heat. If the pulse is not wiry and tight, there is no exterior pulse of cold damage. The Classic says, "All weak [pulses] with fever..."; a weak pulse indicates interior heat, hence "one with a weak [pulse] will certainly be thirsty." If subjected to fire qi, the two heats combine and assail the stomach; the stomach becomes agitated and vexed, and there will certainly be delirious speech. When one with a weak pulse and fever obtains a floating pulse, the evil qi has returned to the exterior, and it should resolve with sweating.

## Notes

Even if it looks like cold damage, one whose pulse is weak rather than wiry and tight and who is thirsty has internal heat, as in warm disease, and the clause warns that fire methods will cause delirious speech. If the pulse becomes floating, it resolves from the exterior. The meaning is hard to grasp and interpretations differ.
