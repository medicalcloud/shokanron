# Clause 31 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Taiyang disease with stiffness of the nape and back, *jiji*, no sweating, and aversion to wind: Gegen tang (Pueraria Decoction) governs it. Formula 1.

Gegen 4 liang; mahuang 3 liang (remove the nodes); guizhi 2 liang (remove the bark); shengjiang 3 liang (sliced); gancao 2 liang (mix-fried); shaoyao 2 liang; dazao 12 pieces (split).

Of the seven ingredients above, with 1 dou of water first boil the mahuang and gegen, reducing by 2 sheng; remove the white froth, add the other ingredients, boil down to 3 sheng, remove the dregs, and take 1 sheng warm. Cover to obtain a slight sweat. Otherwise follow the method for Guizhi tang for care and prohibitions. All the decoctions follow this.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Taiyang disease with stiffness of the nape and back, *jiji*, sweating, and aversion to wind is wind-strike with exterior vacuity. Stiffness of the nape and back, *jiji*, with no sweating and aversion to wind is wind-strike with exterior repletion. For exterior vacuity it is appropriate to resolve the flesh; for exterior repletion it is appropriate to induce sweating. Therefore Gegen tang is used to effuse it.

The *Bencao* says: "Light [medicines] can remove repletion; mahuang and gegen are of this kind." Here, because there is wind-strike with exterior repletion, these two medicines are added to Guizhi tang.

## Notes

The clause on Gegen tang for an exterior-repletion pattern with stiffness of the nape and back and no sweating. It pairs with Clause 14 (Guizhi jia Gegen tang: sweating and aversion to wind), the formulas being distinguished by the presence or absence of sweating.

- Gegen tang consists of Guizhi tang plus mahuang and gegen: mahuang induces sweating and gegen relaxes the stiffness of the nape and back.
- It is a widely known formula, including in Japan, and in later times was applied to the early stage of common colds, stiff shoulders, and the like.
