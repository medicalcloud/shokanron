# Clause 50 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

One whose pulse is floating and tight should, by rule, have body pain, and it is appropriate to resolve it by sweating. But if the pulse at the chi position is slow, sweating must not be induced. How is this known? Because the nutritive qi is insufficient and the blood is scant.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The *Zhenjing* says: "One who has been robbed of blood has no sweat." A slow chi pulse means the nutritive blood is insufficient, so sweating must not be induced.

## Notes

Even with the Mahuang tang pattern (floating, tight pulse; body pain), if the chi pulse is slow it shows insufficient nutritive blood, and sweating must not be induced. This rests on the idea that sweat and blood come from the same source (fluids and blood), so sweating someone with scant blood depletes them further ("one robbed of blood has no sweat; one robbed of sweat has no blood").
