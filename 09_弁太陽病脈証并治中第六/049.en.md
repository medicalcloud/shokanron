# Clause 49 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

One whose pulse is floating and rapid should, by rule, recover by sweating. If, after purgation, the body is heavy and there are palpitations, sweating must not be induced; it should resolve when sweat comes out spontaneously. The reason is that the pulse at the chi position is faint, meaning interior vacuity. One must wait until exterior and interior are replete and the fluids spontaneously harmonize; then sweat will come out spontaneously and [the patient] will recover.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The Classic says: "When the pulses are all floating and rapid, there should be fever with shivering aversion to cold," meaning the evil qi is in the exterior; this should recover with sweating. If after purgation the body is heavy with palpitations, the fluids have been damaged and the stomach qi vacuated. If the body is heavy with palpitations but the chi pulse is replete, after purgation the interior is vacuous and the evil qi has taken advantage of the vacuity to be transmitted inward. Here the chi pulse is faint with heavy body and palpitations, so one knows that after purgation the interior is vacuous and the fluids insufficient, but the evil qi has not been transmitted inward and is only in the exterior. But since the fluids are insufficient, sweating must not be induced. One must wait for the interior qi to become replete and the fluids sufficient; then sweat comes out spontaneously and [the patient] recovers.

## Notes

This clause states that even with an exterior pattern, if the interior has become vacuous (faint chi pulse) through mistaken purgation or the like and the fluids are insufficient, sweating must not be induced; one should wait for the upright qi and fluids to recover and sweat to come spontaneously. It is one of the contraindications of sweating and, with Clause 50, an example of judging interior vacuity by the chi pulse.
