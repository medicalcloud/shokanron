# Clause 110 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Taiyang disease on the second day: there is, on the contrary, agitation, but the back is ironed (with heated tiles or the like), causing profuse sweating, and great heat enters the stomach (one version: "within two days the back was ironed with heated tiles, causing profuse sweating, and fire qi entered the stomach"); the water in the stomach is exhausted, there are agitation and vexation, and there will certainly be delirious speech. If after ten-odd days there are shivering and spontaneous diarrhea, it is about to resolve. Hence there is no sweating from the lumbus downward, one wants to urinate but cannot, there is, on the contrary, retching, a tendency to incontinence, aversion to wind on the soles, and hard stool. Urination should be frequent but, on the contrary, is neither frequent nor copious. After a bowel movement the head suddenly aches, and the soles will certainly be hot, because the grain qi flows downward.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

On the second day of taiyang disease the evil is in the exterior and there should be no agitation; that there is, on the contrary, agitation means heat qi is moving in the interior. If, on the contrary, the back is ironed to induce sweating and there is profuse sweating, the stomach becomes dry, fire-heat enters the stomach, the stomach is dry and hot, and there are agitation, vexation, and delirious speech. Shivering and spontaneous diarrhea after ten-odd days mean the momentum of the fire evil has weakened, yin qi is reviving, and the fluids are restored; hence it is about to resolve. When the fire evil leaves and there is heavy sweating, [the patient] recovers. If there is no sweating from the lumbus downward, the fluids cannot pass downward, so one wants to urinate but cannot, and the heat qi counterflows upward, causing retching. A tendency to incontinence and aversion to wind on the soles mean the qi cannot pass downward and is vacuous there. When the fluids percolate one-sidedly so that the stool is hard, urination should be frequent; the Classic says, "When urination is frequent, the stool will certainly be hard." Here fire-heat dries the interior and the fluids cannot pass downward, so urination is neither frequent nor copious. When the fire-heat disperses and the fluids harmonize, the bound, hard stool is moistened and passes spontaneously. The sudden headache after the bowel movement is because, while the stool was hard, the yang qi could not pass downward; once the bowels move, the yang qi descends, the yang in the head becomes vacuous, and it suddenly aches. Grain qi is yang qi. Earlier, when the yang qi did not pass downward, the soles had aversion to wind; now that the yang qi can descend, the soles become hot.

## Notes

From here follow clauses on the deteriorations (fire counterflow) caused by misuse of fire methods (ironing, red-hot needling, moxibustion, and other treatments using fire).

- This clause describes the course of taiyang disease mistakenly treated by ironing, causing profuse sweating, stomach dryness, and delirious speech, and the process of spontaneous recovery after ten-odd days.
- It is a long and difficult clause; because the sequence seems disordered, interpretations differ widely.
