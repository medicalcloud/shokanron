# Clause 80 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In cold damage, when the physician has heavily purged with pills and the generalized heat does not go, with slight vexation, Zhizi Ganjiang tang (Gardenia and Dried Ginger Decoction) governs it. Formula 42.

Zhizi 14 fruits (split); ganjiang 2 liang.

Of the two ingredients above, boil with 3.5 sheng of water down to 1.5 sheng, remove the dregs, divide into two doses, and give one dose warm. If vomiting occurs, stop the later dose.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Pills cannot remove heat; they only damage the upright qi. When the evil qi takes advantage of the vacuity to remain in the chest but has not entered deeply, the generalized heat does not go and there is slight vexation. Zhizi Ganjiang tang is given to make [the patient] vomit the vexation and [boost] the upright qi.

(Nature and flavor) Zhizi: bitter, cold. Ganjiang: acrid, hot.

Bitter brings it up: the bitterness of zhizi makes the vexation vomited. Acrid moistens it: the acridity of ganjiang boosts the qi.

## Notes

When heavy purgation with pills (said to be strong purgatives containing croton, etc.) has left heat in the chest (generalized heat, slight vexation) while also chilling the spleen and stomach, zhizi (clearing heat) and ganjiang (warming the middle) are combined. It is taken as an example of using cold and hot medicines together for a pattern mixing cold and heat.
