# Clause 38 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Taiyang wind-strike with a floating, tight pulse, fever, aversion to cold, body pain, and vexation and agitation without sweating: Da Qinglong tang (Major Green-Blue Dragon Decoction) governs it. If the pulse is faint and weak, with sweating and aversion to wind, it must not be taken; if it is taken, there will be reversal counterflow and twitching of the sinews and flesh. This is a mistake. Da Qinglong tang. 8.

Mahuang 6 liang (remove the nodes); guizhi 2 liang (remove the bark); gancao 2 liang (mix-fried); xingren 40 kernels (remove the skins and tips); shengjiang 3 liang (sliced); dazao 10 pieces (split); shigao the size of a hen's egg (crushed).

Of the seven ingredients above, with 9 sheng of water first boil the mahuang, reducing by 2 sheng; remove the froth on top, add the other ingredients, boil down to 3 sheng, remove the dregs, and take 1 sheng warm to obtain a slight sweat. If the sweating is profuse, dust [the body] with warm powder. If sweating occurs after one dose, stop the later doses; if it is taken again, profuse sweating will cause loss of yang, leading to vacuity, aversion to wind, vexation and agitation, and inability to sleep.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

This is wind-strike showing a cold pulse. Floating means wind, and wind damages the defensive; tight means cold, and cold damages the nutritive. Since both nutritive and defensive are diseased, there are fever, aversion to cold, and body pain. When wind merges into the defensive, the nutritive is weak and the defensive strong; when cold merges into the nutritive, the nutritive is strong and the defensive weak. Here wind and cold both cause damage, so both nutritive and defensive are replete; hence no sweating, with vexation and agitation. Da Qinglong tang is given to induce sweating and remove the wind and cold from the nutritive and defensive. If the pulse is faint and weak, with sweating and aversion to wind, both nutritive and defensive are vacuous; if Qinglong tang is taken instead, there will certainly be loss of yang, or reversal counterflow, and twitching of the sinews and flesh. This is a mistake in treatment.

(Nature and flavor) Mahuang: sweet, warm. Guizhi: acrid, hot. Gancao: sweet, neutral. Xingren: bitter-sweet, warm. Shengjiang: acrid, warm. Dazao: sweet, warm. Shigao (egg-sized): sweet, slightly cold.

Acrid and sweet both disperse. But wind is best dispersed with acrid, and cold best effused with sweet; only acrid and sweet combined can effuse and disperse the wind and cold of the nutritive and defensive. Mahuang, gancao, shigao, and xingren effuse and disperse the cold in the nutritive; guizhi, ginger, and jujube resolve and remove the [wind] in the defensive. [The commentary breaks off here.]

## Notes

The clause on Da Qinglong tang, used when cold tightly binds the exterior and heat is trapped within, causing vexation and agitation.

- It doubles the mahuang of Mahuang tang and adds shigao, inducing strong sweating while clearing internal heat. It is regarded as one of the strongest sweating formulas in the *Shanghan lun*.
- Regarding why it says "wind-strike" yet shows the cold-damage pulse and signs (floating, tight pulse; no sweating), Cheng Wuji explains it as "damage by both wind and cold." This became the basis of the later "three pillars" theory (Guizhi tang = wind damaging the defensive; Mahuang tang = cold damaging the nutritive; Da Qinglong tang = damage by both).
- It strongly warns that using it for a vacuity pattern with a faint, weak pulse, sweating, and aversion to wind causes serious adverse effects such as reversal counterflow and twitching of the sinews and flesh.
