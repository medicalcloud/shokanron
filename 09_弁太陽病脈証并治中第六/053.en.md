# Clause 53 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When in a disease there is constant spontaneous sweating, this means the nutritive qi is in harmony. Though the nutritive qi is in harmony, the exterior is not in accord, because the defensive qi does not harmonize together with the nutritive qi. Since the nutritive moves within the vessels and the defensive moves outside the vessels, effuse the sweat again; when the nutritive and defensive are harmonized, [the patient] recovers. Guizhi tang is appropriate. 19. Use the twelfth formula above.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Wind damages the defensive; cold damages the nutritive. When the defensive has received wind evil but the nutritive is not diseased, the nutritive qi is in harmony. Since the evil has already lodged in the defensive, it cannot harmonize with the nutritive qi, nor can it guard and protect the skin and interstices; hence constant spontaneous sweating. Guizhi tang is given to resolve and disperse the wind evil and harmonize the nutritive and defensive, and [the patient] recovers.

## Notes

This clause gives the grounds for using Guizhi tang for anyone with constant spontaneous sweating, not only in externally contracted disease.

- Spontaneous sweating is explained as due to the defensive qi (which guards the surface) not harmonizing with the nutritive qi (within the vessels).
- Effusing sweat again with Guizhi tang when there is already sweating is in order to harmonize the nutritive and defensive; the clause is often cited to show Guizhi tang's action of "harmonizing the nutritive and defensive."
