# Clause 53 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When in a disease there is constant spontaneous sweating, this means the ying-nutrient qi is in harmony. Though the ying-nutrient qi is in harmony, the exterior is not in accord, because the wei-defensive qi does not harmonize together with the ying-nutrient qi. Since the ying-nutrient moves within the vessels and the wei-defense moves outside the vessels, effuse the sweat again; when the ying-nutrient and wei-defense are harmonized, [the patient] recovers. Guizhi tang (Keishito) is appropriate. 19. Use the twelfth formula above.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Wind damages the wei-defense; cold damages the ying-nutrient. When the wei-defense has received pathogenic wind but the ying-nutrient is not diseased, the ying-nutrient qi is in harmony. Since the pathogen has already lodged in the wei-defense, it cannot harmonize with the ying-nutrient qi, nor can it guard and protect the skin and interstices; hence constant spontaneous sweating. Guizhi tang is given to resolve and disperse the pathogenic wind and harmonize the ying-nutrient and wei-defense, and [the patient] recovers.

## Notes

This clause gives the grounds for using Guizhi tang for anyone with constant spontaneous sweating, not only in externally contracted disease.

- Spontaneous sweating is explained as due to the wei-defensive qi (which guards the surface) not harmonizing with the ying-nutrient qi (within the vessels).
- Effusing sweat again with Guizhi tang when there is already sweating is in order to harmonize the ying-nutrient and wei-defense; the clause is often cited to show Guizhi tang's action of "harmonizing the ying-nutrient and wei-defense."
