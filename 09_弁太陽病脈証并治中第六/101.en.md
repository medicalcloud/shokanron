# Clause 101 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In cold damage or wind-strike with a Chaihu [tang] pattern, as soon as one sign is seen, it is enough; they need not all be present. Whenever a Chaihu tang pattern has been purged, if the Chaihu pattern has not ended, give Chaihu tang again; there will certainly be steaming heat and shivering, then fever again, and with sweating it resolves.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The Chaihu pattern is evil qi between exterior and interior. Whether vexation in the chest without retching, or thirst, or pain in the abdomen, or glomus and hardness below the rib-side, or palpitations below the heart with inhibited urination, or no thirst with slight fever on the body, or cough—as soon as one sign is seen, it is appropriate to treat it with Chaihu tang; one need not wait for all the signs to be present.

The evil being between half exterior and half interior is the Chaihu pattern. If, before interior repletion has formed, the physician purges it at once with medicine, but the Chaihu pattern is still present, the purgation does not count as a mistake, and Chaihu tang may be given again to harmonize and resolve it. When upon taking the decoction the evil qi returns to the exterior, there is steaming heat outside. Since the interior has been vacuated by the previous purgation, when the evil qi tries to get out, there is shivering inside. When the upright qi prevails and yang qi arises, there is fever again, and with sweating it resolves.

## Notes

One of the best-known clauses on the use of Xiao Chaihu tang.

- "As soon as one sign is seen, it is enough; they need not all be present" is often understood to mean that the formula may be used if even one of the main Chaihu signs (alternating chills and fever, bitter fullness in the chest and rib-side, etc.) is clearly seen. Which "one sign" is meant is debated—the main signs of Clause 96, or the "possibly" signs (Cheng Wuji).
- Second half: even after mistaken purgation, Chaihu tang may be given again if the Chaihu pattern remains, and then "shiver-sweating" may occur—shivering, then fever and sweating with recovery.
