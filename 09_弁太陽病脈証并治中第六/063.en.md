# Clause 63 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

After sweating, Guizhi tang (Keishito) must not be used again. When there are sweating and dyspnea without great heat, Mahuang Xingren Gancao Shigao tang (Maokyoninkanzosekkoto; Ephedra, Apricot Kernel, Licorice, and Gypsum Decoction) may be given. Formula 26.

Mahuang (mao) 4 liang (remove the nodes); xingren (kyonin) 50 kernels (remove the skins and tips); gancao (kanzo) 2 liang (mix-fried); shigao (sekko) half a jin (crushed, wrapped in cotton).

Of the four ingredients above, with 7 sheng of water boil the mahuang, reducing by 2 sheng; remove the froth on top, add the other ingredients, boil down to 2 sheng, remove the dregs, and take 1 sheng warm. Originally it said "yellow-ear cup" (*huang'er bei*).

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

For dyspnea after sweating, Guizhi jia Houpo Xingren tang (Keishikakobokukyoninto) should be made, and when sweat comes out the dyspnea is cured. Here there are sweating and dyspnea, meaning the pathogenic qi is severely blocked and Guizhi tang cannot effuse and disperse it; therefore Guizhi tang must not be used again. Sweating and dyspnea with great heat means the internal heat qi is severe. Without great heat, the exterior pathogen must be severe. Mahuang Xingzi Gancao Shigao tang (Maokyuzukanzosekkoto) is given to disperse the pathogen.

(Nature and flavor) Mahuang: sweet, warm. Xingren: sweet, warm. Gancao: sweet, neutral. Shigao: sweet, cold.

The *Neijing* says: "The liver suffers from tension; quickly eat sweet to moderate it." Wind qi communicates with the liver; since pathogenic wind is severe on the outside, a purely sweet preparation is used to effuse it.

## Notes

The clause on Mahuang Xingren Gancao Shigao tang (Ma Xing Gan Shi tang [Makyokansekito]), used when after sweating there are sweating and dyspnea without great heat on the body surface.

- It is generally understood that heat is trapped in the lung, causing dyspnea, and because that heat escapes as sweat, the surface heat is not great ("no great heat"). Cheng Wuji takes "no great heat" to mean that the exterior pathogen is severe, which differs from the current general interpretation.
- As a formula combining mahuang and shigao to clear lung heat and stop dyspnea, it has been widely applied in later times to cough and dyspnea with heat.
- "Originally it said 'yellow-ear cup'" is not well understood; many take it as an interpolation.
