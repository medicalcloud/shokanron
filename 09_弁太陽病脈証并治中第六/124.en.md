# Clause 124 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Taiyang disease of six or seven days with the exterior pattern still present, a feeble and deep pulse, and, on the contrary, no chest bind: when the person develops mania, it is because the heat is in the lower jiao; the lower abdomen should be hard and full. If urination is spontaneously free, [the patient] recovers when blood is purged. The reason is that the Taiyang has followed the meridian and static heat is in the interior. Didang tang (Teitoto; Resistance Decoction) governs it. Formula 64.

Shuizhi (leech) (dry-fried) and mengchong (bochu; gadfly) (remove the wings and legs, dry-fried), 30 each; taoren (tonin) 20 kernels (remove the skins and tips); dahuang (daio) 3 liang (washed in wine).

Of the four ingredients above, boil with 5 sheng of water down to 3 sheng, remove the dregs, and take 1 sheng warm. If there is no purgation, take it again.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The Taiyang is the meridian; the bladder is the bowel. Here the Taiyang has followed the meridian into the bowel. Six or seven days is the time when the pathogenic qi is transmitted inward. A feeble, deep pulse is the pulse of pathogenic qi in the interior. If the exterior pattern is still present, the pathogenic qi is still shallow and should bind in the chest. If it does not bind in the chest and the person develops mania, the heat is bound in the bladder. The Classic says, "Heat binds in the bladder and the person is as if manic"; here there is actual mania, so the heat is deeper. Hard fullness of the lower abdomen with inhibited urination means there is no blood [pattern]; spontaneously free urination makes the blood pattern certain. Didang tang is given to purge the amassed blood.

(Nature and flavor) Shuizhi: salty and bitter, cold. Mengchong: bitter, slightly cold.

(Nature and flavor) Taoren: bitter-sweet, neutral. Dahuang: bitter, cold.

Bitter goes to the blood; salty overcomes blood. The salty bitterness of mengchong and shuizhi removes amassed blood. Sweet relaxes binding; bitter discharges heat. The bitterness of taoren and dahuang purges the bound heat.

## Notes

The clause on Didang tang, used for blood amassment more severe than the Taohe Chengqi tang (Tokakujokito) pattern ("as if manic"), with actual "mania" and hard fullness of the lower abdomen.

- Spontaneously free urination is taken as the mark that it is stagnation of blood (blood amassment) rather than of water.
- Containing leech and gadfly, strong animal blood-breaking medicines, it is regarded as having very strong power to purge static blood.
