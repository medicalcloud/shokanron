# Clause 46 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Taiyang disease with a floating, tight pulse, no sweating, fever, and body pain, unresolved for eight or nine days, with the exterior pattern still present: here the sweat should be effused. After taking the medicine [the signs] are slightly relieved, but the person becomes vexed and the vision dims; in severe cases there will certainly be nosebleed, and with the nosebleed [the disease] resolves. The reason is that the yang qi is heavy. Mahuang tang governs it. 16. Use the fifth formula above.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

A floating, tight pulse, no sweating, fever, and body pain are taiyang cold damage. If the exterior pattern is still present even at eight or nine days, the sweat should still be effused. Having taken a warming, dispersing decoction, even without profuse sweating [the signs] are slightly relieved. Vexation means heat in the body. The evil qi, not resolved by sweating, is constrained and turns into heat, steaming the channels and network vessels and erupting at the flesh and exterior; hence heat and vexation. The liver receives blood and thereby can see. At first the qi damaged the nutritive; when the cold has turned into heat, the blood is assailed by heat and the liver qi is not regulated, so the vision dims. In severe cases the heat is extreme in the channels, pressing the blood to move frenetically and causing nosebleed. With the nosebleed the heat disperses with the blood, and [the disease] resolves. "The yang qi is heavy" means the heat qi is heavy. Mahuang tang is given to resolve the evil of the preceding taiyang cold damage.

## Notes

This clause states that even if taiyang cold damage is prolonged, Mahuang tang may be used to induce sweating while the exterior pattern remains, and that after taking it there may be a nosebleed with which the disease resolves.

- Resolution of the disease by heat leaving through a nosebleed is sometimes called "red sweat" in later literature (see also Clause 47).
- "Mahuang tang governs it" is generally read as following "here the sweat should be effused" (an inverted construction).
