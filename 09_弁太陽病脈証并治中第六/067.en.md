# Clause 67 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In cold damage, after vomiting or purgation, when there are counterflow fullness below the heart, qi surging up into the chest, dizziness on rising, and a deep, tight pulse—[if] sweating is induced, it stirs the channels and the body sways and shakes—Fuling Guizhi Baizhu Gancao tang (Poria, Cinnamon Twig, White Atractylodes, and Licorice Decoction) governs it. Formula 30.

Fuling 4 liang; guizhi 3 liang (remove the bark); baizhu and gancao (mix-fried), 2 liang each.

Of the four ingredients above, boil with 6 sheng of water down to 3 sheng, remove the dregs, and take warm in three doses.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

When after vomiting or purgation the interior is vacuous and the qi counterflows upward, there are counterflow fullness below the heart and qi surging into the chest. When the exterior is vacuous and yang insufficient, there is dizziness on rising. A floating, tight pulse means the evil is in the exterior and sweating should be induced; a deep, tight pulse means the evil is in the interior and sweating must not be induced. If sweating is induced, it stirs the channels and network vessels outwardly and damages the yang qi; when the yang qi is vacuous on the outside, it cannot govern and maintain the vessels, and the body sways and shakes. This decoction is given to harmonize the channels and boost the yang.

(Nature and flavor) Fuling: sweet, neutral. Guizhi: acrid, hot. Baizhu: bitter-sweet, warm. Gancao: sweet, neutral.

When yang is insufficient, supplement it with sweet; fuling and baizhu engender fluids and boost yang. When the interior qi counterflows, disperse it with acrid; guizhi and gancao move yang and disperse qi.

## Notes

The clause on Ling Gui Zhu Gan tang (Fuling Guizhi Baizhu Gancao tang), used when after vomiting or purgation there are fullness below the heart, upward surging of qi, and dizziness on standing up.

- It is generally understood that the spleen-stomach yang has been damaged, water-rheum has collected below the heart, and it surges upward causing dizziness and the like (Cheng Wuji explains it by yang vacuity and qi counterflow).
- "If sweating is induced, it stirs the channels and the body sways and shakes" is taken as a parenthetical remark that mistaken sweating would worsen this pattern; "Fuling Guizhi Baizhu Gancao tang governs it" is read as following "deep, tight pulse."
- It has been widely applied in later times as a representative formula for dizziness and palpitations due to water-rheum.
