# Clause 65 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When after sweating the person has palpitations below the navel, he is about to develop running piglet (*bentun*); Fuling Guizhi Gancao Dazao tang (Poria, Cinnamon Twig, Licorice, and Jujube Decoction) governs it. Formula 28.

Fuling half a jin; guizhi 4 liang (remove the bark); gancao 2 liang (mix-fried); dazao 15 pieces (split).

Of the four ingredients above, with 1 dou of *ganlan* water first boil the fuling, reducing by 2 sheng, add the other ingredients, boil down to 3 sheng, remove the dregs, and take 1 sheng warm, three times a day. Method for making *ganlan* water: take 2 dou of water, put it in a large basin, and lift it up with a ladle [and pour it back] until five or six thousand pearl-like bubbles chase one another on the surface; then take it for use.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Sweat is the fluid of the heart. Palpitations below the navel after sweating mean the heart qi is vacuous and the kidney qi has begun to stir. The accumulation of the kidney is called running piglet; when it attacks, it rises from the lower abdomen to below the heart—the kidney qi counterflowing upward, seeking to overwhelm the heart. Here palpitations below the navel mean the kidney qi has begun to stir; hence "about to develop running piglet." Fuling Guizhi Gancao Dazao tang is given to lower the kidney qi.

(Nature and flavor) Fuling: sweet, neutral. Gancao: sweet, neutral. Dazao: sweet, neutral.

Fuling attacks the kidney evil; guizhi can discharge the running piglet; the sweetness of gancao and dazao nourishes and assists spleen-earth, thereby calming the kidney qi. It is decocted with *ganlan* water because water that has been lifted loses its strength and is chosen so as not to assist the kidney qi.

## Notes

The clause on Ling Gui Gan Zao tang (Fuling Guizhi Gancao Dazao tang), used when after sweating there are palpitations below the navel and an attack of qi surging from the lower abdomen to the chest (running piglet) is about to occur.

- Running piglet refers to attacks in which qi surges from the lower abdomen up to the chest and throat like running pigs. It is generally understood that the heart yang has become vacuous through sweating, so the water qi of the lower burner tends to move upward; fuling moves the water and guizhi lowers the upward surging.
- There is a distinctive instruction to use *ganlan* water (water ladled repeatedly until bubbly); its meaning is debated.
