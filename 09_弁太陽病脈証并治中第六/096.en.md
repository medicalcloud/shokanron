# Clause 96 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Cold damage of five or six days, or wind-strike, with alternating chills and fever, bitter fullness in the chest and rib-side, taciturnity with no desire for food or drink, vexation of the heart and frequent retching—or vexation in the chest without retching, or thirst, or pain in the abdomen, or glomus and hardness below the rib-side, or palpitations below the heart with inhibited urination, or no thirst with slight fever on the body, or cough: Xiao Chaihu tang governs it. Formula 48.

Chaihu half a jin; huangqin 3 liang; renshen 3 liang; banxia half a sheng (washed); gancao (mix-fried) and shengjiang (sliced), 3 liang each; dazao 12 pieces (split).

Of the seven ingredients above, boil with 1 dou 2 sheng of water down to 6 sheng, remove the dregs, decoct again down to 3 sheng, and take 1 sheng warm, three times a day.

If there is vexation in the chest without retching, remove banxia and renshen and add 1 gualoushi (trichosanthes fruit). If there is thirst, remove banxia, add renshen to make 4.5 liang together with the previous amount, and add 4 liang of gualougen. If there is pain in the abdomen, remove huangqin and add 3 liang of shaoyao. If there is glomus and hardness below the rib-side, remove dazao and add 4 liang of muli (oyster shell). If there are palpitations below the heart with inhibited urination, remove huangqin and add 4 liang of fuling. If there is no thirst and slight fever outside, remove renshen and add 3 liang of guizhi; cover warmly, and with a slight sweat [the patient] recovers. If there is cough, remove renshen, dazao, and shengjiang, and add half a sheng of wuweizi and 2 liang of ganjiang.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Diseases may be in the exterior, in the interior, or between exterior and interior. Here the evil qi is between exterior and interior; this is called the half-exterior half-interior pattern. Five or six days is the time when the evil qi is transmitted from the exterior to the interior. "Wind-strike" means cold damage reaching five or six days, [or wind-strike]. The *Yuhan* says, "Wind-strike five or six days, cold damage, alternating chills and fever," meaning either wind-strike or cold damage—not cold damage followed by wind, or wind-strike followed by cold. The Classic says, "In cold damage or wind-strike with a Chaihu pattern, as soon as one sign is seen, it is enough; they need not all be present"—this is precisely what "either wind-strike or cold damage" means. When the evil is in the exterior there is cold; when in the interior, heat. Here the evil is between exterior and interior without a fixed location; hence chills and fever alternate. When the evil is in the exterior, the heart and abdomen are not full; when in the interior, they are distended and full. Here only bitter fullness of the chest and rib-side is mentioned, so one knows the evil qi is between exterior and interior and has not reached fullness of the heart and abdomen. Bitter fullness of the chest and rib-side shows the evil qi is [between] exterior and interior. *Momo* means quiet. When the evil is in the exterior, there is groaning and restlessness; when in the interior, vexation, oppression, and derangement. The *Neijing* says: "When yang enters yin, there is quiet." Taciturnity means the evil is just going from exterior to interior, between the two. When the evil is in the exterior, one can eat; when in the interior, one cannot eat. Not wanting to eat means the evil is between exterior and interior and has not reached the point of being unable to eat. When the evil is in the exterior, there is no vexation or retching; when in the interior, there are vexation, fullness, and retching. Vexation with frequent retching means the evil, in the exterior, is just being transmitted inward. When the evil first enters the interior without a fixed location, where it is transmitted is not uniform; hence the "possibly" signs. "With a Chaihu pattern, as soon as one sign is seen, it is enough" refers to these "possibly" signs.

(Nature and flavor) Chaihu: bitter, slightly cold. Huangqin: bitter, cold. Renshen: sweet, warm. Gancao: sweet, neutral. Banxia: acrid, warm. Shengjiang: acrid, warm. Dazao: sweet, warm.

The *Neijing* says: "When heat runs to excess within, effuse it with bitter." The bitterness of chaihu and huangqin effuses the heat of the transmitted evil. When the interior is insufficient, moderate it with sweet; the sweetness of renshen and gancao moderates the middle and harmonizes the qi. When the evil has half entered the interior, the interior qi counterflows; disperse it with acrid—banxia removes vexation and retching. When the evil is half in the exterior, the nutritive and defensive contend with it; resolve it with acrid and sweet—ginger and jujube harmonize the nutritive and defensive.

On the later modifications:

Vexation in the chest without retching means heat has gathered but the qi is not counterflowing. Sweet makes one full in the middle; since heat is gathering, the supplementing of renshen is not used. Acrid disperses counterflowing qi, but since there is no retching, the acrid warmth of banxia is not used. Heat is best treated with cold, and gathering with bitter; gualoushi, bitter and cold, discharges the accumulated heat in the chest.

Banxia dries the fluids and is unsuitable for those with thirst. Renshen is sweet and moistening, gualougen bitter and cool; they remove heat and engender fluids. These two are fitting.

Huangqin is removed because [its bitter cold] chilling the middle is to be avoided; shaoyao is added to free the blockage.

Sweet makes one full in the middle; for glomus, the sweetness of dazao is removed. Salty softens; for glomus and hardness, the saltiness of muli is added.

When rheum (water) accumulates and does not move, there are palpitations and inhibited urination. The *Neijing* says: "The kidney desires firmness; quickly eat bitter to firm it." Firming the kidney would make the water even firmer, so huangqin is removed. Bland flavor percolates and drains and is yang; the sweet blandness of fuling drains hidden water.

No thirst means the interior is in harmony, so renshen is removed. Slight fever outside means the exterior has not yet resolved; gui is added to effuse sweat.

Cough is counterflowing qi. Sweet obstructs qi, so renshen and dazao are removed. The *Neijing* says: "The lung desires to contract; quickly eat sour to contract it." The sourness of wuweizi contracts the counterflowing qi. When the lung is cold there is cough; disperse it with acrid heat—hence shengjiang is replaced by the heat of ganjiang.

## Notes

The representative clause on Xiao Chaihu tang, the principal formula of shaoyang disease.

- Alternating chills and fever, bitter fullness in the chest and rib-side, taciturnity without desire for food, and vexation with frequent retching are regarded as the four main signs of Xiao Chaihu tang.
- The idea that the evil lies between exterior and interior (half-exterior half-interior) spread through Cheng Wuji's commentary.
- Many concurrent "possibly" signs are listed with corresponding modifications; together with Clause 101 ("as soon as one sign is seen, it is enough"), this makes it known as a formula of wide application.
