# Clause 86 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

A person with nosebleeds (*nüjia*) must not be made to sweat; if sweat comes out, there will certainly be sinking of the forehead, a tense and tight pulse, a fixed stare with inability to blink (*xuan* is pronounced like *huan*, also the reverse spelling of *hu-juan*; likewise below; one version writes *shun*), and inability to sleep.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Nosebleed is loss of blood in the upper burner. If sweating is induced, the fluids of the upper burner are exhausted and the channels and network vessels become dry and rough; hence sinking of the forehead and a tense, tight pulse. All vessels belong to the eyes; when the sinews and vessels are tense, they pull on the eyes, so there is a fixed stare and inability to blink. *Xuan* means blinking and closing the eyes. The *Zhenjing* says: "When yin qi is vacuous, the eyes do not close." Loss of blood is yin vacuity; hence inability to sleep.

## Notes

A person prone to nosebleeds is deficient in blood (yin), so sweating would further deplete fluids and blood, causing a sunken forehead (or, by another reading, sunken vessels at the temples), a tense pulse, a fixed stare, and insomnia; hence it is prohibited. The reading of "額上陥脈急緊" ("sinking of the forehead, tense and tight pulse" or "the vessels on the forehead are sunken and tense") is debated.
