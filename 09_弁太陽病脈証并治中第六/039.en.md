# Clause 39 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Cold damage with a floating, slowdown pulse, no body pain but only heaviness, at times lighter, and no Shaoyin pattern: Da Qinglong tang (Daiseiryuto) effuses it. 9. Use the eighth formula above.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

This is cold damage showing a wind pulse. In cold damage the body aches, but here wind prevails, so the body does not ache. In wind attack the body is heavy, and here, because wind is combined, there are times when it is lighter. When there are no cold limbs, vomiting, or diarrhea—no Shaoyin interior pattern—the wind and cold are severe on the outside. Da Qinglong tang is given to effuse and disperse the wind and cold in the exterior.

## Notes

A Da Qinglong tang clause following Clause 38, showing a different presentation (floating, slowdown pulse; heavy body).

- "No Shaoyin pattern": heaviness of the body is also seen in Shaoyin (yang-deficiency) disease, so this is taken as a caution to confirm the absence of a Shaoyin pattern before using it.
- Pairing Clause 38 (wind attack with a cold pulse) and Clause 39 (cold damage with a wind pulse), Cheng Wuji explains them with the idea of "damage by both wind and cold."
