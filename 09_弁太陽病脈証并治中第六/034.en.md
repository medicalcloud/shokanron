# Clause 34 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Taiyang disease with a Guizhi [tang] pattern: when the physician instead purges it and diarrhea then does not stop, with a skipping pulse (*cu* is written *zong* in one version), the exterior has not yet resolved. When there are dyspnea and sweating, Gegen Huangqin Huanglian tang (Kakkonogonorento; Pueraria, Scutellaria, and Coptis Decoction) governs it. Formula 4.

Gegen (kakkon) half a jin; gancao (kanzo) 2 liang (mix-fried); huangqin (ogon; scutellaria) 3 liang; huanglian (oren; coptis) 3 liang.

Of the four ingredients above, with 8 sheng of water first boil the gegen, reducing by 2 sheng, add the other ingredients, boil down to 2 sheng, remove the dregs, and take warm in two doses.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The Classic says: "When one attacks at once although purgation is not appropriate, the interior becomes deficient, heat enters, and combining with the heat there is diarrhea." A Guizhi pattern means the pathogen is in the exterior; purging it instead vacuates the intestines and stomach, which are then taken over by heat, and the diarrhea does not stop. When the pathogen is in the exterior a yang pulse appears; when it is in the interior a yin pulse appears. Diarrhea with a feeble, slow pulse means the pathogen is in the interior. A skipping pulse means yang exuberance; if the pulse is skipping despite diarrhea, one knows the exterior has not resolved. Where a disease has "sweating and dyspnea," the dyspnea comes with spontaneous sweating, caused by severe pathogenic qi outside. "Dyspnea and sweating" means the sweating comes from the dyspnea, caused by interior heat and qi counterflow. Gegen Huangqin Huanglian tang is given to disperse the exterior pathogen and remove the interior heat.

(Nature and flavor noted in the interlinear notes on the ingredients) Gancao: sweet, neutral. Huangqin: bitter, cold. Huanglian: bitter, cold.

The *Neijing* says: "Sweet disperses and is yang." When the exterior has not resolved, it is dispersed with the sweetness of gegen and gancao. Bitter firms [the interior]; when the interior qi is weak, it is firmed with the bitterness of huangqin and huanglian.

## Notes

The clause on Gegen Huangqin Huanglian tang (Gegen Qin Lian tang), used when a Guizhi tang (Keishito) pattern has been mistakenly purged, so that diarrhea does not stop, the exterior pattern remains, and there are dyspnea and sweating.

- It is regarded as a formula treating exterior and interior together: gegen resolves the exterior, and huangqin and huanglian clear the interior heat.
- In later times it has often been applied as a representative formula for diarrhea with heat.
- Cheng Wuji distinguishes by word order "sweating and dyspnea" (due to exterior pathogen) from "dyspnea and sweating" (due to interior heat).
