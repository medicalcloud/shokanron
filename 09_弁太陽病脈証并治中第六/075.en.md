# Clause 75 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Before the pulse has been taken, the patient crosses his hands over his heart; the master therefore tells him to try coughing, and if he does not cough, he must be deaf in both ears and unable to hear. The reason is that repeated sweating has caused vacuity, which leads to this. After sweating, drinking much water will certainly cause panting; pouring water over [the patient] also causes panting.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Profuse sweating causes loss of yang. One whose yang qi in the chest is insufficient crosses his hands over his heart; the master sees the external sign and knows the yang qi is insufficient. Further, when told to try coughing he does not cough at once, meaning he is deaf; the vacuity of yang qi is clear. Deafness is because the yang qi is vacuous and the essential qi cannot rise to reach the ears.

Panting is a lung disease. Panting from drinking much water means drinking cold things has damaged the lung. Panting from being doused and washed with cold water means bodily cold has damaged the lung.

## Notes

A clause in two parts.

- First part: one whose yang qi is vacuous from repeated sweating covers the chest with the hands (as in Clause 64) and may also become deaf; it is given as an example of detecting vacuity by inspection before taking the pulse.
- Second part: it warns that drinking much cold water or being doused with water after sweating damages the lung and causes panting.
