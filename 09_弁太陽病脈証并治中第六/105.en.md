# Clause 105 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In cold damage of thirteen days, past the meridian [period], delirium is due to heat, and it should be purged with a decoction. If urination is free, the stool should be hard; when, on the contrary, there is diarrhea and the pulse is regular and harmonious, one knows that the physician purged with pills; this is not the right treatment. If there were spontaneous diarrhea, the pulse should be feeble with cold limbs; here, on the contrary, it is harmonious, which means internal excess. Tiaowei Chengqi tang governs it. 55. Use the thirty-third formula above.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

In cold damage of thirteen days, when the second transmission through the meridians is complete, it is called "past the meridian." Delirium is Yangming stomach heat; it should be purged with one of the Chengqi decoctions. If urination is free, the fluids percolate out one-sidedly, so the stool should be hard; that there is diarrhea instead shows that the physician purged with pills. Diarrhea with a feeble pulse and cold limbs is deficiency cold. Here the pulse is regular and harmonious, so it is not deficiency cold, but diarrhea from intestinal deficiency and stomach heat combined. Tiaowei Chengqi tang is given to purge the stomach heat.

## Notes

Even when a patient with delirium and Yangming excess heat has diarrhea from mistaken purgation with pills, if the pulse is regular it is not deficiency cold but remaining internal excess heat, so it should be purged with Tiaowei Chengqi tang. It is taken as an example of distinguishing deficiency and excess by the pulse, not merely by the presence of diarrhea.
