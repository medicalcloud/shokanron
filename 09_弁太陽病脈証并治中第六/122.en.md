# Clause 122 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

A patient's pulse is rapid. Rapid means heat, which should disperse grain and draw food (good appetite); that, on the contrary, he vomits is because sweating has made the yang qi faint and the diaphragm qi vacuous, so the pulse is rapid. This rapidity is visiting heat and cannot disperse grain; because the stomach is vacuous and cold, he vomits.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The yang receives qi in the chest. Inducing sweating vacuates the yang qi outwardly; this is "making the yang qi faint and the diaphragm qi vacuous." Rapid means heat; true heat would disperse grain, but visiting heat cannot. Because sweating damaged the yang qi outwardly and made the stomach vacuous and cold, there is vomiting.

## Notes

A rapid pulse normally indicates heat, but a rapid pulse resulting from yang qi damaged by sweating is "visiting heat" (insubstantial, false heat), and the stomach is actually vacuous and cold, so food is vomited. The clause is taken to show that heat must not be judged from the apparent pulse alone.
