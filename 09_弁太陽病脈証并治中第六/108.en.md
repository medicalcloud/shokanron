# Clause 108 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In cold damage with abdominal fullness, delirious speech, and a cunkou pulse that is floating and tight, this is the liver overwhelming the spleen; it is called "unrestrained" (*zong*). Needle Qimen. 58.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Abdominal fullness with delirious speech is a disease of the spleen and stomach. Floating and tight is the liver pulse. When a spleen disease shows the liver pulse, wood is moving to overwhelm earth. The Classic says, "Water moving to overwhelm fire and wood moving to overwhelm earth are called unrestrained"; this is of that kind. Qimen is the alarm point of the liver; needling it drains the exuberant qi of the liver channel.

## Notes

A clause explaining the course of disease by the five-phase restraint cycle (paired with Clause 109).

- Excessive restraint (overwhelming) of the spleen (earth) by the liver (wood) is called "unrestrained."
- Qimen (LR-14, alarm point of the liver) is needled to drain the exuberant liver qi.
- Explanation by five-phase theory is rare in the *Shanghan lun*, and some regard this as a later addition.
