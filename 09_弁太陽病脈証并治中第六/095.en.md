# Clause 95 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Taiyang disease with fever and sweating: this is weak ying-nutrient and strong wei-defense, which makes sweat come out. To rescue [the patient] from the pathogenic wind, Guizhi tang (Keishito) is appropriate. 47. The formula uses the method above.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

In Taiyang wind attack, when wind merges into the wei-defense, the wei-defense becomes excessive and the ying-nutrient deficient. The ying-nutrient is yin; the wei-defense is yang. Fever with sweating is weak yin and strong yang. The *Neijing* says: "Where yin is deficient, yang will surely gather; hence shallow breathing, periodic heat, and sweating." Guizhi tang is given to resolve and disperse the pathogenic wind and harmonize the ying-nutrient and wei-defense.

## Notes

This clause explains the pathology of fever and sweating in Taiyang wind attack as "weak ying-nutrient and strong wei-defense," and uses Guizhi tang to remove the pathogenic wind and harmonize the ying-nutrient and wei-defense. It is regarded as a theoretical restatement of "the yang is floating and the yin is weak" in Clause 12. "Strong wei-defense" is often understood not as the wei-defensive qi being robust but as the wei-defense being hyperactive after receiving the pathogen (wind).
