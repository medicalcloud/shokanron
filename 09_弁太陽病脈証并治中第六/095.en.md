# Clause 95 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Taiyang disease with fever and sweating: this is weak nutritive and strong defensive, which makes sweat come out. To rescue [the patient] from the evil wind, Guizhi tang is appropriate. 47. The formula uses the method above.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

In taiyang wind-strike, when wind merges into the defensive, the defensive becomes replete and the nutritive vacuous. The nutritive is yin; the defensive is yang. Fever with sweating is weak yin and strong yang. The *Neijing* says: "Where yin is vacuous, yang will surely gather; hence shortage of qi, periodic heat, and sweating." Guizhi tang is given to resolve and disperse the wind evil and harmonize the nutritive and defensive.

## Notes

This clause explains the pathology of fever and sweating in taiyang wind-strike as "weak nutritive and strong defensive," and uses Guizhi tang to remove the evil wind and harmonize the nutritive and defensive. It is regarded as a theoretical restatement of "the yang is floating and the yin is weak" in Clause 12. "Strong defensive" is often understood not as the defensive qi being robust but as the defensive being hyperactive after receiving the evil (wind).
