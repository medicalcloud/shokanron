# Clause 117 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When sweating has been induced with red-hot needling, and the needled spot has been exposed to cold, so that a nodule rises and turns red, there will certainly be running piglet. When qi surges from the lower abdomen up to the heart, apply one cone of moxibustion on each nodule and give Guizhi jia Gui tang (Cinnamon Twig Decoction with Extra Cinnamon), adding a further 2 liang of gui. Formula 61.

Guizhi 5 liang (remove the bark); shaoyao 3 liang; shengjiang 3 liang (sliced); gancao 2 liang (mix-fried); dazao 12 pieces (split).

Of the five ingredients above, boil with 7 sheng of water down to 3 sheng, remove the dregs, and take 1 sheng warm. Originally called Guizhi tang; now gui is added to make 5 liang in full. Gui is added because it can discharge the running-piglet qi.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Inducing sweating with red-hot needling damages the yin blood and startles and stirs the heart qi. When the needled spot is exposed to cold, qi gathers and forms a nodule. The heart qi is startled and becomes vacuous; the kidney qi, riding on the cold qi, stirs and erupts as running piglet. The *Jingui yaolüe* says: "There is a disease called running piglet, which arises from fright." The kidney qi tends to rise and overwhelm the heart, so the qi surges from the lower abdomen up to the heart. First apply moxibustion on the nodule to disperse the cold, and give Guizhi jia Gui tang to discharge the running-piglet qi.

## Notes

When running piglet (attacks of qi surging from the lower abdomen to the chest) arises after sweating induced with red-hot needling, moxibustion is applied to the needle sites and Guizhi jia Gui tang—Guizhi tang with increased guizhi—is used.

- The general interpretation is that increasing the amount of guizhi lowers the surging qi.
- Whether the added "gui" is guizhi (cinnamon twig) or rougui (cinnamon bark) has been debated in later times.
