# Clause 35 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Taiyang disease with headache, fever, body pain, lumbar pain, joint pain, aversion to wind, and dyspnea without sweating: Mahuang tang (Ephedra Decoction) governs it. Formula 5.

Mahuang 3 liang (remove the nodes); guizhi 2 liang (remove the bark); gancao 1 liang (mix-fried); xingren 70 kernels (remove the skins and tips).

Of the four ingredients above, with 9 sheng of water first boil the mahuang, reducing by 2 sheng; remove the froth on top, add the other ingredients, boil down to 2.5 sheng, remove the dregs, and take 8 ge warm. Cover to obtain a slight sweat. There is no need to sip porridge. Otherwise follow the method for Guizhi tang for care.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

This is Taiyang cold damage. Cold damages the ying-nutrient [qi]. Headache, body pain, and lumbar pain extending to pain in the joints mean that the ying-nutrient blood of the Taiyang meridian is not flowing. The *Neijing* says: "When wind and cold lodge in a person, they make the fine hairs stand erect and the skin close, producing heat"—this is cold in the exterior. When wind merges into the wei-defense, the wei-defense becomes in excess and the ying-nutrient deficient, and there is spontaneous sweating with aversion to wind and cold. When cold merges into the ying-nutrient, the ying-nutrient becomes in excess and the wei-defense deficient, and there is no sweating with aversion to wind. Because the ying-nutrient is strong and the wei-defense weak, the qi counterflows and there is dyspnea. Mahuang tang is given to effuse the sweat.

(Nature and flavor) Mahuang: sweet, warm. Guizhi: acrid, hot. Gancao: sweet, neutral. Xingren: acrid, warm.

The *Neijing* says: "When cold runs to excess within, treat with sweet and hot, assisted by bitter and acrid." Mahuang and gancao open the flesh and effuse sweat; guizhi and xingren disperse cold and lower the qi.

## Notes

The clause on Mahuang tang, the representative formula for Taiyang cold damage. It lists eight signs—headache, fever, body pain, lumbar pain, joint pain, aversion to wind, no sweating, and dyspnea—later called the "eight signs of Mahuang."

- Absence of sweating is regarded as the decisive difference from the Guizhi tang pattern (sweating).
- Mahuang and guizhi induce strong sweating, and xingren settles the dyspnea. Because its sweating power is strong, cases in which it must not be used are given (e.g., Clauses 49–50).
- Unlike Guizhi tang, there is no need to sip porridge.
