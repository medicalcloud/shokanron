# Clause 106 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When taiyang disease does not resolve and heat binds in the bladder, the person is as if manic; if the blood descends spontaneously, with its descent [the patient] recovers. When the exterior has not resolved, one must not yet attack; one should first resolve the exterior (later it says, "to resolve the exterior, Guizhi tang is appropriate"). When the exterior has resolved and there is only tense binding of the lower abdomen, then it may be attacked; Taohe Chengqi tang (Peach Kernel Qi-Coordinating Decoction) is appropriate. Formula 56.

Taoren (peach kernel) 50 kernels (remove the skins and tips); dahuang 4 liang; guizhi 2 liang (remove the bark); gancao 2 liang (mix-fried); mangxiao 2 liang.

Of the five ingredients above, boil with 7 sheng of water down to 2.5 sheng, remove the dregs, add the mangxiao, put on the fire again to a slight simmer, take off the fire, and take 5 ge warm before meals, three times a day. There should be slight diarrhea.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

The taiyang is the bladder channel. When the evil heat of the taiyang channel does not resolve and follows the channel into the bowel, it is "heat binding in the bladder." "The person is as if manic" means not yet reaching mania but merely restless. The Classic says, "The person is as if manic because the heat is in the lower burner." The taiyang has much heat; heat in the bladder will surely contend with the blood. If the blood is not amassed but pressed by heat, it descends spontaneously; when the blood descends, the heat leaves with the blood and [the patient] recovers. If the blood does not descend, it is assailed by heat, amassed and accumulated below, causing tense binding of the lower abdomen, and then it may be attacked. Taohe Chengqi tang is given to purge the heat and disperse the blood. The *Neijing* says: "What enters from outside to inside and becomes exuberant inside—first treat the outside, then regulate the inside." This is what is meant.

(Nature and flavor) Taoren: sweet, neutral. Guizhi: acrid, hot.

Moderate with sweet, disperse with acrid. Tense binding of the lower abdomen is moderated with the sweetness of taoren; blood amassed in the lower burner is dispersed with the acrid-hot qi of guizhi. Cold is used to take it. Because the heat is extreme and assails the blood, these two are added to Tiaowei Chengqi tang.

## Notes

The clause on Taohe Chengqi tang, used for the "blood amassment pattern," in which taiyang evil heat has entered the lower burner and bound with the blood.

- Mental restlessness ("as if manic") and tense binding (tension and resistance) of the lower abdomen are taken as its marks; if the blood descends spontaneously, the patient recovers.
- Here again the principle of not attacking while the exterior pattern remains, but purging after resolving the exterior, is shown.
- Consisting of Tiaowei Chengqi tang plus taoren and guizhi, it has been widely applied in later times as a representative formula for purging static blood. The "bladder" in "heat binding in the bladder" is often interpreted broadly as the lower abdomen (lower burner).
