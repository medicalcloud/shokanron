# Clause 48 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Overlap disease of the two yang: when the Taiyang first contracted the disease, sweating was induced, but although sweat came out at first it was insufficient; as a result [the disease] shifted to belong to the Yangming, with continued slight spontaneous sweating and no aversion to cold. If the Taiyang disease pattern has not ended, one must not purge; purging is a mistake. In such a case, a little sweating may be induced. If the facial color is red all over, the yang qi is depressed in the exterior, and it should be resolved and fumigated. If [one says only that] the sweating was insufficient, that is not enough to describe it: the yang qi is depressed and cannot pass outward; where there should be sweating there is none; the person is agitated and vexed, does not know where the pain is—at times in the abdomen, at times in the four limbs, and it cannot be found by pressing—and is short of breath. This is solely because the sweating was insufficient; inducing sweating again will bring recovery. How is it known that the sweating was insufficient? It is known because the pulse is hesitant.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

When Taiyang disease does not resolve and is transmitted to merge into the Yangming while the Taiyang pattern has not ended, it is called overlap disease. Continued slight spontaneous sweating and no aversion to cold mean the Taiyang pattern has ended and the Yangming pattern is complete; by rule it should be purged. If the Taiyang pattern has not ended, the exterior has not resolved, so one must not purge, but should induce a little sweat to resolve the exterior first. The Yangming meridian runs over the face. A face red all over means the yang qi is depressed in the exterior; it should be resolved and fumigated to obtain sweat. "If the sweating was insufficient" is not enough to describe the depression of yang qi; it is simply that where there should be sweating there is none, the yang qi cannot pass out and disperse, the pathogen has no exit and is severely blocked in the meridians, hence dryness and vexation. Because the pathogen moves along the meridians, the pain has no fixed place—now in the abdomen, now in the limbs—and cannot be found by pressing; the shortness of breath is due solely to insufficient sweating, so inducing sweating again brings recovery. The *Neijing* says: "In all disorders, when on palpation the pulse is hesitant, the yang qi is in excess, with generalized heat and no sweating." Thus from the hesitant pulse one knows that the yang qi is blocked and depressed and the sweating insufficient.

## Notes

A long and difficult clause on how to handle "overlap disease" of Taiyang and Yangming when the Taiyang exterior pattern remains.

- While the Taiyang pattern remains, one does not purge even if there are Yangming signs, but induces a little sweating (the principle of treating the exterior before the interior).
- A red face (*yuanyuan zheng chi*) is attributed to yang qi trapped in the exterior unable to disperse, to be resolved by sweating or fumigation.
- Pain without a fixed location and shortness of breath are explained as due to yang qi unable to disperse because of insufficient sweating, and further sweating is said to cure it.
- Many commentators think the text contains corruptions, and interpretations differ.
