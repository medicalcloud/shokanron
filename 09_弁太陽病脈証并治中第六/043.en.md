# Clause 43 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Taiyang disease: when there is slight panting after purgation, it is because the exterior has not yet resolved; Guizhi jia Houpo Xingzi tang (Cinnamon Twig Decoction plus Magnolia Bark and Apricot Kernel) governs it. Formula 13.

Guizhi 3 liang (remove the bark); gancao 2 liang (mix-fried); shengjiang 3 liang (sliced); shaoyao 3 liang; dazao 12 pieces (split); houpo 2 liang (mix-fried, remove the bark); xingren 50 kernels (remove the skins and tips).

Of the seven ingredients above, boil with 7 sheng of water over a gentle fire down to 3 sheng, remove the dregs, and take 1 sheng warm. Cover to obtain a slight sweat.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Severe panting after purgation means the interior qi is greatly vacuous, the evil qi has been transmitted inward, and the upright qi is about to desert. Slight panting after purgation means the interior qi is counterflowing upward, the evil cannot be transmitted inward, and it is still in the exterior. Guizhi tang is given to resolve the exterior, and houpo and xingren are added to lower the counterflowing qi.

## Notes

For slight panting after mistaken purgation while the exterior pattern still remains, houpo and xingren are added to Guizhi tang—the same formula as in Clause 18 (person with panting). Cheng Wuji distinguishes, by the severity of the panting after purgation, whether the evil remains in the exterior or has entered the interior.
