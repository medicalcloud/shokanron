# Clause 43 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Taiyang disease: when there is slight dyspnea after purgation, it is because the exterior has not yet resolved; Guizhi jia Houpo Xingzi tang (Keishikakobokukyoshito; Cinnamon Twig Decoction plus Magnolia Bark and Apricot Kernel) governs it. Formula 13.

Guizhi (keishi) 3 liang (remove the bark); gancao (kanzo) 2 liang (mix-fried); shengjiang (shokyo) 3 liang (sliced); shaoyao (shakuyaku) 3 liang; dazao (taiso) 12 pieces (split); houpo (koboku) 2 liang (mix-fried, remove the bark); xingren (kyonin) 50 kernels (remove the skins and tips).

Of the seven ingredients above, boil with 7 sheng of water over a gentle fire down to 3 sheng, remove the dregs, and take 1 sheng warm. Cover to obtain a slight sweat.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Severe dyspnea after purgation means the interior qi is greatly deficient, the pathogenic qi has been transmitted inward, and the healthy qi is about to desert. Slight dyspnea after purgation means the interior qi is counterflowing upward, the pathogen cannot be transmitted inward, and it is still in the exterior. Guizhi tang (Keishito) is given to resolve the exterior, and houpo and xingren are added to lower the counterflowing qi.

## Notes

For slight dyspnea after mistaken purgation while the exterior pattern still remains, houpo and xingren are added to Guizhi tang—the same formula as in Clause 18 (person with dyspnea). Cheng Wuji distinguishes, by the severity of the dyspnea after purgation, whether the pathogen remains in the exterior or has entered the interior.
