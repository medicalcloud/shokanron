# Clause 91 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In cold damage, when the physician has purged and there follows incessant diarrhea with undigested food and body pain, the interior must urgently be rescued. When afterward there is body pain but the stool has spontaneously become regular, the exterior must urgently be rescued. To rescue the interior, Sini tang is appropriate; to rescue the exterior, Guizhi tang is appropriate. 45. Use the twelfth formula above.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

In cold damage, when after purgation there follows incessant diarrhea with undigested food and body pain, the interior must urgently be rescued because the interior qi is insufficient; it must be rescued first, and Sini tang given urgently. Once the stool has spontaneously become regular and one knows the interior qi is harmonized, then Guizhi tang is given urgently to rescue the exterior. Body pain is exterior evil. The *Neijing* says: "When a disease arises with insufficiency, treat it as branch and root, first treating the branch and afterward the root." Here cold is regarded as the sudden [condition].

## Notes

When an exterior pattern (body pain) and a serious interior pattern (diarrhea with undigested food) coexist, if the interior is urgent and severe, the interior is rescued first (Sini tang) and the exterior after the interior is regulated (Guizhi tang). This clause shows the principle of urgency in treatment and is regarded as an important exception to "exterior first, then interior" (Clause 90). "Diarrhea with undigested food" is taken to indicate yang vacuity of the spleen and kidney.
