# Clause 111 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When taiyang wind-strike is forced to sweat with fire, the evil wind receives fire-heat, and the blood and qi overflow and lose their normal measure. The two yang fumigate and scorch each other, and the body turns yellow. When yang is exuberant, there tends to be nosebleed; when yin is vacuous, urination is difficult. When yin and yang are both vacuous and exhausted, the body becomes withered and dry; there is sweating only from the head, stopping at the neck; abdominal fullness, slight panting, dry mouth, festering throat, or no bowel movement; after a long time, delirious speech; in severe cases, even hiccup; agitated movements of the hands and feet, fiddling with the clothes and groping at the bed. If urination is free, the person can be treated.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Wind is a yang evil; through the qi of fire-heat, the evil wind becomes even more severe, presses on the blood and qi, makes them overflow, and makes them lose their normal measure. Wind and fire qi are called "the two yang." When the two yang fumigate and scorch each other and the heat erupts outward, the body will certainly turn yellow. If the heat assails the channels and network vessels, it is yang exuberance; the external heat presses the blood upward, and there will certainly be nosebleed. If the heat assails the interior, it is yin vacuity; with internal heat there will certainly be difficult urination. If the heat consumes the blood and qi so that they become scant, both yin and yang are vacuous. When blood and qi are vacuous and scant, they cannot nourish the body, which therefore becomes withered and dry. The three yang channels reach the neck; the three yin reach the chest and return. Sweating only from the head, stopping at the neck, means the heat qi flames upward and assails the yang but not the yin. The *Neijing* says: "All distension and enlargement of the abdomen belong to heat." Abdominal fullness with slight panting means heat qi is constrained within. The *Neijing* says, "When fire qi erupts within, above there are a dry mouth and festering throat," meaning fire-heat fumes upward. When heat qi is above and does not descend, the stool does not become hard; if heat qi descends into the stomach and consumes the fluids, the stool becomes hard—hence "or no bowel movement." After a long time the stomach becomes dry and hot, and there will certainly be delirious speech. The *Neijing* says, "When the disease is deep, the voice becomes hiccup." When fire qi is extreme and the upright qi counterflows chaotically, there is hiccup. The *Neijing* says, "The four limbs are the root of all yang"; when yang is exuberant, the four limbs are replete. Because fire-heat is extreme, there are agitated movements of the hands and feet, fiddling with the clothes and groping at the bed—derangement. Free urination means the fire is not yet violent and the fluids not yet exhausted, so it can still be treated.

## Notes

This clause describes the grave deterioration that follows forcing sweat in wind-strike with fire: wind (yang) and fire (yang) combine and the heat becomes extreme, leading to jaundice, nosebleed, difficult urination, delirious speech, and fiddling with clothes and groping at the bed (a grave state of disordered consciousness).

- The final sentence, "if urination is free, the person can be treated," is valued as judging prognosis by whether the fluids are not yet exhausted, as shown by the urine.
