# Clause 98 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When six or seven days after contracting the disease, with a slow, floating, and weak pulse, aversion to wind and cold, and warm hands and feet, the physician has purged two or three times, and there are inability to eat, fullness and pain below the rib-side, yellowing of the face, eyes, and body, stiffness of the neck and nape, and difficult urination: if Chaihu tang is given, there will afterward certainly be heaviness below (tenesmus). For one who originally had thirst and vomits after drinking water, Chaihu tang is not suitable; eating grain causes hiccup.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Six or seven days after contracting the disease, with a slow, floating, and weak pulse, aversion to wind and cold, and warm hands and feet, the evil qi is half exterior, half interior and has not yet become repletion. Purging two or three times instead vacuates the stomach qi and damages the fluids; the evil accumulates in the interior, so there is inability to eat and fullness and pain below the rib-side. The stomach is vacuous and steamed by heat, which erupts outward, so the face, eyes, and body all turn yellow. Stiffness of the neck and nape means the exterior has still not resolved. Difficult urination means the fluids have been lost inside. Although it was originally a Chaihu tang pattern, the interior is vacuous and the lower burner qi rough, causing difficult urination; if Chaihu tang is given, it further drives off the fluids, and afterward there will certainly be heaviness below. Vomiting not caused by drinking water is the Chaihu tang pattern; vomiting originally caused by drinking means water has collected below the heart. The *Jingui yaolüe* says: "One who is first thirsty and then vomits has water collected below the heart; this belongs to rheum." Vomiting after drinking water is due to collected water; hiccup after eating grain is due to accumulated food; neither suits Xiao Chaihu tang. Both are warnings about Chaihu tang and must be known.

## Notes

A clause showing cases in which Chaihu tang must not be used.

- One whose spleen and stomach have become vacuous through mistaken purgation, with jaundice and difficult urination, may resemble the Chaihu tang pattern (fullness and pain below the rib-side), but giving the bitter, cold Chaihu tang would further damage the spleen and stomach and cause tenesmus.
- Chaihu tang is also unsuitable for one who is thirsty but vomits after drinking water (collected water-rheum).
- It is read as a caution to distinguish the Xiao Chaihu tang pattern from similar patterns of spleen-stomach vacuity cold and water-rheum.
