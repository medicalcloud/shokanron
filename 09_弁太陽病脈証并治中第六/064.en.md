# Clause 64 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When, after excessive sweating, the person crosses his hands over his heart, has palpitations below the heart, and wants to have it pressed, Guizhi Gancao tang (Cinnamon Twig and Licorice Decoction) governs it. Formula 27.

Guizhi 4 liang (remove the bark); gancao 2 liang (mix-fried).

Of the two ingredients above, boil with 3 sheng of water down to 1 sheng, remove the dregs, and take in a single dose.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Excessive sweating is loss of yang. The yang receives qi in the chest. Because the yang qi in the chest is insufficient, the patient crosses his hands over his heart, has palpitations below the heart, and wants it pressed. Guizhi Gancao tang is given to regulate the insufficient qi.

(Nature and flavor) Guizhi: acrid, hot. Gancao: sweet, neutral.

The acridity of guizhi goes to the lung and boosts qi; the sweetness of gancao enters the spleen and moderates the middle.

## Notes

This clause uses Guizhi Gancao tang, consisting only of guizhi and gancao, when excessive sweating has damaged the heart yang, causing palpitations with a desire to press the chest.

- Covering the chest with the hands and wanting pressure are taken as characteristics of vacuity (in repletion, pressure is disliked).
- The combination of guizhi and gancao, called "acrid and sweet transform into yang," is regarded as the basic combination for supplementing heart yang and is contained in many formulas, such as Ling Gui Zhu Gan tang.
