# Clause 32 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In combined disease of taiyang and yangming, there will certainly be spontaneous diarrhea; Gegen tang governs it. Formula 2. (One version says to use Gegen Huangqin Huanglian tang.)

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

In cold damage there is combined disease (*hebing*) and overlap disease (*bingbing*). When taiyang disease does not resolve and merges into the yangming, it is called overlap disease. When [two or] three channels all receive the evil and are diseased together, it is called combined disease. In combined disease the evil qi is severe. That taiyang-yangming, taiyang-shaoyang, and yangming-shaoyang combined diseases are all said to have "certainly spontaneous diarrhea" is because when the evil qi merges into the yin, the yin becomes replete and the yang vacuous, and when the evil qi merges into the yang, the yang becomes replete and the yin vacuous. When cold evil qi is severe and lodges in the two yang, the two yang are replete on the outside and do not govern the interior, so the interior qi is vacuous and there must be diarrhea. Gegen tang is given to disperse the severe evil in the channels.

## Notes

This clause uses Gegen tang for "combined disease," in which the taiyang and yangming channels are diseased at the same time, with diarrhea.

- Cheng Wuji's commentary summarizes the distinction between combined disease (two or more channels affected at onset) and overlap disease (a disease of one channel extending to another before it resolves).
- The exterior evil is exuberant and its effect reaches the interior causing diarrhea; dispersing the exterior is expected to stop the diarrhea too.
