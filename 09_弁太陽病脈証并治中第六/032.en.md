# Clause 32 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In combined disease of Taiyang and Yangming, there will certainly be spontaneous diarrhea; Gegen tang (Kakkonto) governs it. Formula 2. (One version says to use Gegen Huangqin Huanglian tang [Kakkonogonorento].)

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

In cold damage there is combined disease (*hebing*) and overlap disease (*bingbing*). When Taiyang disease does not resolve and merges into the Yangming, it is called overlap disease. When [two or] three meridians all receive the pathogen and are diseased together, it is called combined disease. In combined disease the pathogenic qi is severe. That Taiyang-Yangming, Taiyang-Shaoyang, and Yangming-Shaoyang combined diseases are all said to have "certainly spontaneous diarrhea" is because when the pathogenic qi merges into the yin, the yin becomes excessive and the yang deficient, and when the pathogenic qi merges into the yang, the yang becomes excessive and the yin deficient. When cold pathogenic qi is severe and lodges in the two yang, the two yang are in excess on the outside and do not govern the interior, so the interior qi is deficient and there must be diarrhea. Gegen tang is given to disperse the severe pathogen in the meridians.

## Notes

This clause uses Gegen tang for "combined disease," in which the Taiyang and Yangming meridians are diseased at the same time, with diarrhea.

- Cheng Wuji's commentary summarizes the distinction between combined disease (two or more meridians affected at onset) and overlap disease (a disease of one meridian extending to another before it resolves).
- The exterior pathogen is exuberant and its effect reaches the interior causing diarrhea; dispersing the exterior is expected to stop the diarrhea too.
