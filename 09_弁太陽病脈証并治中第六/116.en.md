# Clause 116 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

With a feeble, rapid pulse, be careful not to apply moxibustion. When the fire becomes an pathogen, there is vexation counterflow; pursuing deficiency and chasing excess, the blood scatters within the vessels; though the fire qi is slight, its internal attack is powerful, scorching the bones and damaging the sinews, and the blood is hard to restore. With a floating pulse, it is appropriate to resolve by sweating; if moxibustion with fire is used, the pathogen has no exit and grows exuberant through the fire, and from the lumbus downward the disease will certainly be heavy and numb; this is called fire counterflow. When it is about to resolve spontaneously, there must first be vexation, and with the vexation there is sweating and resolution. How is this known? Because the pulse is floating, one knows that sweating will resolve it.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

A feeble, rapid pulse is heat. Moxibustion removes cold but cannot disperse heat; therefore be careful not to apply it. If moxibustion is applied instead, the heat becomes severe through the fire and finally there is vexation counterflow. Moxibustion originally pursues [supplements] deficiency, but here it also chases heat into excess. Heat damages the blood, and the added fire qi scatters the blood within the vessels. Qi warms and blood moistens; when qi and blood are dispersed, they cannot moisten the sinews and bones, so the bones are scorched, the sinews damaged, and the scattered blood hard to restore.

A floating pulse means [the pathogen] is in the exterior, to be resolved by sweating. When the physician applies moxibustion with fire to obtain sweat but no sweat is obtained, the pathogen has no exit, and the added fire qi assists it, so the heat becomes ever more severe. The upper half of the body corresponds to the yang of heaven, the lower half to the yin of earth. The nature of fire is to flame upward, so below the lumbus the yin qi alone governs; hence the heaviness and numbness from the lumbus downward.

Vexation is heat. When the pathogenic qi returns to the exterior, there is vexation and heat, and with sweating it resolves. Because the pulse is floating, the pathogen has returned to the exterior.

## Notes

A clause on deterioration from misuse of moxibustion (fire counterflow).

- It strongly warns that moxibustion for someone with a feeble, rapid pulse (said to indicate yin deficiency or deficiency heat) lets the heat damage the blood so that the sinews and bones are no longer nourished.
- Moxibustion for an exterior pattern leaves the pathogen no exit, causing heaviness and numbness below the lumbus. If the pulse is floating, there is a chance of spontaneous resolution with vexation followed by sweating.
