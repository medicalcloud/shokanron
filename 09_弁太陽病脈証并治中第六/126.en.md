# Clause 126 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Cold damage with heat and fullness of the lower abdomen: urination should be inhibited, but when now on the contrary it is free, there is blood; it should be purged, and no other medicine may be used. Didang wan (Resistance Pill) is appropriate. Formula 66.

Shuizhi 20 (dry-fried); mengchong 20 (remove the wings and legs, dry-fried); taoren 25 kernels (remove the skins and tips); dahuang 3 liang.

Pound the four ingredients above and divide into four pills. Boil one pill with 1 sheng of water, take 7 ge, and drink it. Within a full day blood should be passed; if not, take it again.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Heat with fullness of the lower abdomen in cold damage means blood is amassed in the lower burner. If the heat amassed the fluids so that they did not flow, urination would be inhibited. When the heat does not amass the fluids but amasses the blood so that it does not move, urination is spontaneously free; this is blood amassment, which should be purged with Taoren Chengqi tang or Didang tang. But here there is no yellow body or black stool, nor forgetfulness or mania—it has not yet become severe—so other swift, drastic medicines must not be used. Didang wan may be given to purge it a little.

(Nature and flavor) Shuizhi: bitter, cold. Mengchong: bitter, slightly cold.

## Notes

For blood amassment that is milder—only fullness of the lower abdomen without grave signs such as mania—Didang wan, Didang tang made into pills and boiled before taking, is used. Making it into pills is said to make its action gentler. *Zuishi* means a full day and night (24 hours).
