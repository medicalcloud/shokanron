# Clause 82 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Taiyang disease: when after sweating, although sweat has come out, [the disease] has not resolved, and the person still has fever, palpitations below the heart, dizziness, twitching of the body's flesh, and swaying as if about to fall to the ground (*pi* is written *pi* 僻 in one version), Zhenwu tang (True Warrior Decoction) governs it. Formula 43.

Fuling, shaoyao, and shengjiang (sliced), 3 liang each; baizhu 2 liang; fuzi 1 piece (blast-fried, remove the skin, broken into eight pieces).

Of the five ingredients above, boil with 8 sheng of water down to 3 sheng, remove the dregs, and take 7 ge warm, three times a day.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Fever persisting after sweating without resolution means the evil qi has not yet resolved. Palpitations below the heart, dizziness, twitching of the flesh, and swaying as if about to fall to the ground mean that sweating has caused loss of yang. Interior vacuity causes palpitations; upper vacuity causes dizziness; channel vacuity causes twitching and swaying of the body. Zhenwu tang is given to govern it, warming the channels and restoring the yang.

## Notes

The clause on Zhenwu tang, used when excessive sweating has made the yang qi vacuous and water qi has stirred, causing palpitations, dizziness, trembling of the body, and unsteadiness.

- Centered on fuzi, which warms kidney yang, and fuling and baizhu, which move water, it is regarded as the representative formula for water collection due to yang vacuity ("yang vacuity with water flooding"). It also appears in the shaoyin chapter (Clause 316).
- "Still has fever" is often interpreted not as a remaining exterior pattern but as vacuous yang floating outward.
- "Zhenwu" was originally "Xuanwu" (the god of the north and water), changed to "Zhenwu" in the Song dynasty to avoid a taboo name.
