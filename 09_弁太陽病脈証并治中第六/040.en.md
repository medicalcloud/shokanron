# Clause 40 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When in cold damage the exterior has not resolved and there is water qi below the heart, with dry retching, fever and cough, and possibly thirst, or diarrhea, or choking, or inhibited urination with lower abdominal fullness, or panting, Xiao Qinglong tang (Minor Green-Blue Dragon Decoction) governs it. Formula 10.

Mahuang (remove the nodes), shaoyao, xixin (asarum), ganjiang, gancao (mix-fried), and guizhi (remove the bark), 3 liang each; wuweizi (schisandra) half a sheng; banxia half a sheng (washed).

Of the eight ingredients above, with 1 dou of water first boil the mahuang, reducing by 2 sheng; remove the froth on top, add the other ingredients, boil down to 3 sheng, remove the dregs, and take 1 sheng warm. If there is thirst, remove banxia and add 3 liang of gualougen (trichosanthes root). If there is slight diarrhea, remove mahuang and add raohua (wikstroemia flower) the size of a hen's egg, roasted until red. If there is choking, remove mahuang and add 1 piece of fuzi, blast-fried. If there is inhibited urination with lower abdominal fullness, remove mahuang and add 4 liang of fuling. If there is panting, remove mahuang and add half a sheng of xingren with skins and tips removed. However, raohua does not treat diarrhea and mahuang governs panting; since these words say the opposite, it is doubtful that they are Zhongjing's intent. Your servants [Lin] Yi and others respectfully note: the main purpose of Xiao Qinglong tang is to treat water. Further, according to the *Bencao*, raohua precipitates the twelve kinds of water; if the water is removed, the diarrhea stops. Further, according to the *Qianjin*, for one whose body is swollen, mahuang should be used, but xingren is used instead because mahuang effuses the yang. Proved in this way, how could it not be Zhongjing's intent?

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

When in cold damage the exterior has not resolved and there is water-rheum below the heart, water and cold contend, the lung becomes cold and its qi counterflows; hence dry retching, fever, and cough. The *Zhenjing* says, "Cold in the body and drinking cold things damage the lung"—that is, the two colds affect each other, damaging both inside and outside, so the qi counterflows and moves upward. Xiao Qinglong tang is given to induce sweating and disperse the water. When water qi soaks inward, where it is transmitted is not fixed, so there are the "possibly" signs; the formula is modified according to the signs to resolve and transform it.

(Nature and flavor) Mahuang: sweet, warm. Shaoyao: sour, slightly cold. Wuweizi: sour, warm. Ganjiang: acrid, hot. Gancao: sweet, neutral. Guizhi: acrid, hot. Banxia: acrid, slightly warm. Xixin: acrid, warm.

When cold evil is in the exterior, only sweet and acrid can disperse it; the acrid-sweetness of mahuang, guizhi, and gancao effuses and disperses the exterior evil. When water collects below the heart and does not move, the kidney qi becomes dry. The *Neijing* says: "The kidney suffers from dryness; quickly eat acrid to moisten it." The acridity of ganjiang, xixin, and banxia moves the water qi and moistens the kidney. Cough, counterflow, and panting mean the lung qi is counterflowing. The *Neijing* says: "The lung desires to contract; quickly eat sour to contract it." The sourness of shaoyao and wuweizi contracts the counterflowing qi and calms the lung.

On the modifications:

When there is diarrhea, the exterior must not be attacked; sweating would certainly cause distension and fullness. Mahuang effuses the yang, and when water soaks into the stomach it will certainly cause diarrhea. Raohua precipitates the twelve kinds of water; when the water is removed, the diarrhea stops. If there is thirst, banxia is removed and 3 liang of gualougen added. Acrid dries and bitter moistens; banxia is acrid and drying, [harming] the fluids, and unsuitable for those with thirst, so it is removed. Gualou is bitter in flavor and engenders fluids, so it is added.

The Classic says: "When water meets cold qi, being chilled they must contend, and the person chokes." Fuzi is added to warm and disperse the water-cold. If a patient with cold is again made to sweat, the stomach becomes cold and he will certainly vomit roundworms; [so] mahuang is removed, as sweating is to be avoided.

When the lower burner does not move, there are inhibited urination and lower abdominal fullness. Mahuang effuses the fluids outward and is unsuitable; fuling drains water accumulated below, so adding it is appropriate. If there is panting, mahuang is removed and half a sheng of xingren, skins and tips removed, is added. The *Jingui yaolüe* says: "Because the person's body is swollen, mahuang is not added but xingzi is, because mahuang effuses the yang." Panting with labored breathing and a swollen body is a disease of water qi involving both root and branch.

## Notes

The clause on Xiao Qinglong tang, used when there is exterior cold with water qi (water-rheum) collected below the heart (the epigastrium), causing dry retching, fever, and cough.

- It is regarded as the representative formula for "external cold with internal rheum," and in later times has been widely applied to cough, panting, and copious thin phlegm.
- It is generally explained that mahuang and guizhi resolve the exterior; ganjiang, xixin, and banxia warm and disperse the water-rheum; and wuweizi and shaoyao contract the lung qi. Cheng Wuji's commentary is to much the same effect.
- Modifications are given for the signs listed as "possibly." Against a remark within the modifications doubting "raohua" and "removing mahuang and adding xingren" ("doubtful that they are Zhongjing's intent"), Lin Yi and others cite the *Bencao* and *Qianjin* to argue that they do in fact accord with Zhongjing's intent.
