# Clause 61 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When, after purgation, sweating is again induced, and during the day there is restlessness with inability to sleep, but at night [the patient] is calm and quiet, with no vomiting, no thirst, no exterior pattern, a deep and feeble pulse, and no great heat in the body, Ganjiang Fuzi tang (Kankyobushito; Dried Ginger and Aconite Decoction) governs it. Formula 24.

Ganjiang (kankyo) 1 liang; fuzi (bushi) 1 piece (used raw, remove the skin, cut into eight pieces).

Of the two ingredients above, boil with 3 sheng of water down to 1 sheng, remove the dregs, and take in a single dose.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Purgation vacuates the interior; sweating vacuates the exterior. When sweating follows purgation, both exterior and interior are deficient. Yang flourishes in the daytime; the yang tries to recover but, being deficient, cannot overcome the pathogen, and the healthy qi and pathogen contend, so during the day there are restlessness and inability to sleep. At night yin flourishes, and the deficient yang cannot contend with it, so at night there is calm and quiet. No vomiting and no thirst mean there is no heat in the interior. No great heat in the body means there is no heat in the exterior. Further, with no exterior pattern and a deep, feeble pulse, one knows that the yang qi is greatly deficient and the yin-cold qi prevails. Ganjiang Fuzi tang is given to drive back the yin and restore the yang.

(Nature and flavor) Ganjiang: acrid, hot. Fuzi (1 piece): acrid, hot.

The *Neijing* says: "Where cold excess prevails, calm it with acrid and hot." Since deficiency cold is very severe, an acrid-hot preparation is used to overcome it.

## Notes

This clause has Ganjiang Fuzi tang, consisting only of dried ginger and aconite, taken in a single dose by a patient whose yang qi has suddenly become deficient after mistaken purgation followed by sweating.

- The contrast between restlessness by day and calm by night is characteristic; it is generally explained that by day the weakened yang, aided by the yang of nature, contends with the pathogen, but at night it cannot.
- It is often cited as an example of a differential procedure: excluding the three yang patterns—no vomiting (not Shaoyang), no thirst (not Yangming), no exterior pattern (not Taiyang)—and judging yang deficiency from the deep, feeble pulse.
- It is Sini tang (Shigyakuto) without licorice; taking it in a single dose is said to restore the yang urgently.
