# Clause 92 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When in a disease there are fever and headache but the pulse is, on the contrary, deep, and [the disease] does not get better, with body pain, the interior should be rescued; the Sini tang formula is appropriate.

Gancao 2 liang (mix-fried); ganjiang 1.5 liang; fuzi 1 piece (used raw, remove the skin, broken into eight pieces).

Of the three ingredients above, boil with 3 sheng of water down to 1 sheng 2 ge, remove the dregs, and take warm in two doses. For a strong person, a large piece of fuzi and 3 liang of ganjiang may be used.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Fever and headache are exterior disease. A pulse that is, on the contrary, deep is an interior pulse. The Classic says: "When there is disease in the exterior, the pulse should be floating and large; now the pulse is, on the contrary, deep and slow, so one knows it will be cured." When an exterior disease appears with an interior pulse, it should get better; if it does not, there is internal vacuity with severe cold. Sini tang is given to rescue the interior.

## Notes

Even with an exterior pattern of fever and headache, if the pulse is deep (indicating interior yang vacuity), the interior should take priority and be rescued with Sini tang. It is taken as an example of giving precedence to the pulse over the signs ("discarding the signs and following the pulse"). It is often read in connection with Clause 301 (Mahuang Fuzi Xixin tang), shaoyin disease with fever.
