# Clause 103 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Taiyang disease more than ten days past the channel [period]: if, after it has instead been purged two or three times, the Chaihu pattern is still present four or five days later, first give Xiao Chaihu tang. If the retching does not stop, there is tension below the heart (one version says "the retching stops and there is slight relief"), and depressed, slight vexation, [the disease] has not yet resolved; give Da Chaihu tang (Major Bupleurum Decoction) to purge it, and [the patient] will recover. Formula 53.

Chaihu half a jin; huangqin 3 liang; shaoyao 3 liang; banxia half a sheng (washed); shengjiang 5 liang (sliced); zhishi 4 pieces (mix-fried); dazao 12 pieces (split).

Of the seven ingredients above, boil with 1 dou 2 sheng of water down to 6 sheng, remove the dregs, decoct again, and take 1 sheng warm, three times a day. One version adds 2 liang of dahuang; if it is not added, it probably would not be Da Chaihu tang.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

When many days have passed and repeated attacks and purgations have not ended the Chaihu pattern, one should still first give Xiao Chaihu tang to resolve the exterior. This is what the Classic means by "whenever a Chaihu tang pattern has been purged, if the Chaihu pattern has not ended, give Chaihu [tang] again." If the retching stops, exterior and interior are harmonized. If the retching does not stop and there is depressed, slight vexation, the interior heat is already severe and bound in the stomach. Da Chaihu tang is given to purge the interior heat, and [the patient] recovers.

(Nature and flavor) Chaihu: sweet, neutral. Huangqin: bitter, cold. Shaoyao: sour, slightly cold. Banxia: acrid, warm. Shengjiang: acrid, warm. Zhishi: bitter, cold. Dazao: sweet, warm. Dahuang: bitter, cold.

The bitterness of chaihu and huangqin enters the heart and breaks the heat; the sour-bitterness of zhishi and shaoyao causes upwelling and discharge and supports the yin. Acrid disperses; the acridity of banxia disperses the counterflowing qi. Acrid and sweet harmonize; the acrid-sweetness of ginger and jujube harmonizes the nutritive and defensive.

## Notes

The clause on Da Chaihu tang, used when the shaoyang pattern remains while heat has begun to bind and form repletion in the interior (stomach and intestines).

- It shows the order: first give Xiao Chaihu tang; if the retching still does not stop and there are tension below the heart and vexation (interior repletion added), purge with Da Chaihu tang.
- Da Chaihu tang is Xiao Chaihu tang minus renshen and gancao, plus shaoyao and zhishi (and dahuang); it is regarded as a formula for overlap disease of shaoyang and yangming.
- In the Song edition the formula lacks dahuang, with the note "One version adds 2 liang of dahuang; if it is not added, it probably would not be Da Chaihu tang." Today it is generally included.
