# Clause 56 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In cold damage with no bowel movement for six or seven days, headache, and heat, give Chengqi tang. If the urine is clear (one version says the stool is green), one knows [the evil] is not in the interior but still in the exterior, and sweating must be induced. If there is headache, there will certainly be nosebleed. Guizhi tang is appropriate. 22. Use the twelfth formula above.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

No bowel movement for six or seven days with headache and heat is why it would be appropriate to purge. If the urine is clear, one knows there is no heat in the interior, so one must not purge. The Classic says: "When urination is frequent, the stool will certainly be hard; there may be no bowel movement for ten days without discomfort." All the more, when there is no bowel movement for six or seven days but the urine is clear, the evil cannot be blamed on the interior; it is still in the exterior. Guizhi tang is given to resolve the exterior. If the headache does not stop, the exterior has not ended; [the heat] is severely constrained in the channels, presses the blood to move frenetically, and rises to cause nosebleed.

## Notes

This clause distinguishes, by the color of the urine, whether constipation with headache and fever should be purged (interior heat) or sweated (exterior pattern).

- If the urine is red and concentrated, it is interior heat and Chengqi tang is used; if the urine is clear, the heat is not in the interior but in the exterior, and Guizhi tang is used.
- "If there is headache, there will certainly be nosebleed" is often understood to mean that if exterior heat is strong and the headache persists, a nosebleed may occur. "Guizhi tang is appropriate" is generally read as following "sweating must be induced" (an inverted construction).
