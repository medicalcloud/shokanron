# Clause 97 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the blood is weak and the qi exhausted, the interstices open; the pathogenic qi thereupon enters, contends with the healthy qi, and binds below the rib-side. The healthy qi and the pathogen contend apart, so chills and fever alternate, ceasing and recurring at fixed times, with taciturnity and no desire for food or drink. The zang-fu organs are connected with each other, and the pain must be lower; the pathogen is high and the pain is low, so it causes retching (one version says, "the zang-fu organs are at odds; the disease must be lower, with pain in the rib-side and diaphragm"). Xiao Chaihu tang (Shosaikoto) governs it. If after taking Chaihu tang (Saikoto) there is thirst, it belongs to the Yangming and is treated by its method. 49. Use the formula above.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

A person's qi and blood wax and wane with the times. When the moon's disk is empty, the blood is weak and the qi exhausted, and the interstices are open and loose. When the pathogenic qi takes advantage of the deficiency to harm a person, [it enters] deep. The *Zhenjing* says: "When the moon's disk is empty, the sea water swells in the east, a person's blood and qi are deficient, the wei-defensive qi departs and the body is left alone, the flesh diminishes, the skin slackens, the interstices open, the hair thins, the skin texture is thin, and dirt falls off. If at this time one meets a robber wind, it enters deep"—this is it. Because of deficiency of the healthy qi, the pathogen goes from exterior to interior, binds below the rib-side, and contends separately with the healthy qi, producing alternating fever and chills and taciturnity without desire for food or drink. "Lower" means from outside to inside. The meridians and collaterals are connected with the zang-fu organs, and the qi, following the meridians, must be transmitted inward; hence "the pain must be lower." (*Tong*, pain, is written *bing*, disease, in one version.) The pathogen being in the upper jiao is "the pathogen is high"; the pathogen gradually being transmitted inward is "the pain is low." The interior qi contends with the pathogenic qi and counterflows upward, so it causes retching. Xiao Chaihu tang is given to resolve the half-exterior half-interior pathogen.

When after taking Xiao Chaihu tang the exterior pathogen has ended but there is thirst, the interior pathogen has been transmitted to the Yangming; treat it [by the method] of Yangming.

## Notes

A clause explaining, from the standpoint of pathology, how the Xiao Chaihu tang pattern arises.

- It explains that when qi and blood are weak and the interstices open, the pathogen enters and contends with the healthy qi below the rib-side, producing alternating fever and chills and the like.
- "The pathogen is high and the pain is low" is variously interpreted, e.g., the pathogen is in the gallbladder (above) and the pain extends to the abdomen (below).
- If thirst appears after taking Xiao Chaihu tang, the pathogen has moved to the Yangming, so it is treated by the method of Yangming disease.
