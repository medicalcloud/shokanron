# Clause 144 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

A woman with wind-strike, after seven or eight days, subsequently has chills and fever occurring at fixed times, and her menses happen to stop: this is heat entering the blood chamber; the blood must be bound, so it is like malaria, with attacks at fixed times. Xiao Chaihu tang governs it. Formula 10.

Chaihu half a jin; huangqin 3 liang; renshen 3 liang; banxia half a sheng (washed); gancao 3 liang; shengjiang 3 liang (sliced); dazao 12 pieces (split).

Of the seven ingredients above, boil with 1 dou 2 sheng of water down to 6 sheng, remove the dregs, decoct again down to 3 sheng, and take 1 sheng warm, three times a day.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Seven or eight days of wind-strike is the time when the evil qi is transmitted inward. When there were originally no chills and fever but subsequently there are chills and fever and the menses happen to stop, the exterior evil has taken advantage of the vacuity of the blood chamber to enter it, contending with the blood so that the blood binds and does not move; therefore the menses stop. Blood and qi contend separately with the evil, causing malaria-like chills and fever with attacks at fixed times. Xiao Chaihu tang is given to resolve the evil transmitted through the channels.

## Notes

Xiao Chaihu tang is used for heat entering the blood chamber when the menses stop midway and periodic malaria-like chills and fever occur. It is taken as the basis for using Xiao Chaihu tang for heat entering the blood chamber, and in later times it has been applied to fevers around menstruation and the like.
