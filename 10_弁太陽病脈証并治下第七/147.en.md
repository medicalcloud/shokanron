# Clause 147 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Cold damage of five or six days: when, after sweating has been induced and then purgation applied, there are fullness and slight binding of the chest and rib-side, inhibited urination, thirst without retching, sweating only from the head, alternating chills and fever, and vexation of the heart, this has not yet resolved; Chaihu Guizhi Ganjiang tang (Bupleurum, Cinnamon Twig, and Dried Ginger Decoction) governs it. Formula 13.

Chaihu half a jin; guizhi 3 liang (remove the bark); ganjiang 2 liang; gualougen 4 liang; huangqin 3 liang; muli 2 liang (dry-fried); gancao 2 liang (mix-fried).

Of the seven ingredients above, boil with 1 dou 2 sheng of water down to 6 sheng, remove the dregs, decoct again down to 3 sheng, and take 1 sheng warm, three times a day. With the first dose there is slight vexation; with the next dose, sweat comes out and [the patient] recovers.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Five or six days into cold damage, after sweating and purgation, the evil should resolve. Here, fullness and slight binding of the chest and rib-side, inhibited urination, thirst without retching, sweating only from the head, alternating chills and fever, and vexation of the heart mean the evil qi is still between half exterior and half interior and has not yet resolved. Fullness and slight binding of the chest and rib-side, chills and fever, and vexation are evil between half exterior and half interior. Inhibited urination with thirst means the fluids were lost through sweating and purgation and the interior is dry. If heat had consumed the fluids so that urination is inhibited with thirst, the person would certainly retch; here there is thirst without retching, so one knows it is not interior heat. In cold damage, sweating brings harmony; here there is sweating only from the head and none elsewhere, meaning the fluids are insufficient and the yang is vacuous above. Chaihu Guizhi Ganjiang tang is given to resolve the evil of exterior and interior, restore the fluids, and assist the yang.

(Nature and flavor) Chaihu: bitter, neutral. Guizhi: acrid, hot. Ganjiang: acrid, hot. Gualougen: bitter, cold. Huangqin: bitter, cold. Muli: salty, cold. Gancao: sweet, neutral.

The *Neijing* says: "When heat runs to excess within, effuse it with bitter." The bitterness of chaihu and huangqin resolves the evil transmitted from the exterior. Acrid and sweet disperse and are yang; the acrid sweetness of guizhi and gancao disperses the evil in the exterior. Soften with salty; the saltiness of muli disperses the fullness of the chest and rib-side. Moisten with acrid; the acridity of ganjiang secures the sweat due to yang vacuity. Since the fluids are insufficient and there is thirst, firm it with bitter; the bitterness of gualou[gen] engenders fluids.

## Notes

The clause on Chaihu Guizhi Ganjiang tang, used when after sweating and purgation the shaoyang evil remains (alternating chills and fever, fullness and slight binding of the chest and rib-side, vexation) together with insufficient fluids or collected water (inhibited urination, thirst, sweating from the head).

- It is explained variously as "shaoyang with water-rheum" or "gallbladder heat with spleen cold," the coexistence of shaoyang evil with taiyin (spleen) cold or water-rheum; interpretations differ among commentators.
- It is Xiao Chaihu tang minus renshen, banxia, shengjiang, and dazao, plus guizhi, ganjiang, gualougen, and muli. In later times it has been widely applied to chronic low fever, thirst, night sweats, and the like.
