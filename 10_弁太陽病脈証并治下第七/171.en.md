# Clause 171 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In overlap disease of taiyang and shaoyang, with hardness below the heart, stiffness of the neck and nape, and dizziness, one should needle Dazhui, Feishu, and Ganshu, and be careful not to purge. 33.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Glomus and hardness below the heart with dizziness are shaoyang; stiffness of the neck and nape is taiyang. Needling Dazhui and Feishu drains the taiyang evil because the taiyang vessel descends the nape and flanks the spine. [Needling] Ganshu drains the shaoyang evil because the gallbladder is the bowel of the liver. Taiyang is in the exterior and shaoyang in the interior, so this is a half-exterior half-interior pattern. The fifth pattern above (Clause 142) says, "Sweating must not be induced; if sweating is induced, there will be delirious speech," meaning that attacking the taiyang evil by sweating makes the shaoyang evil even more severe, invading the stomach and certainly causing delirious speech. Here it says "be careful not to purge," meaning that if one attacks the shaoyang evil [by purging], the taiyang evil takes advantage of the vacuity to enter the interior and will certainly form chest bind. The Classic says, "When overlap disease of taiyang and shaoyang is instead purged, chest bind forms."

## Notes

Like Clause 142, this treats overlap disease of taiyang and shaoyang with acupuncture. Whereas Clause 142 prohibits sweating, this one prohibits purgation. It is taken as an example of using acupuncture for an overlap disease in which both sweating and purgation are prohibited.
