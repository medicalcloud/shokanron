# Clause 171 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In overlap disease of Taiyang and Shaoyang, with hardness below the heart, stiffness of the neck and nape, and dizziness, one should needle Dazhui, Feishu, and Ganshu, and be careful not to purge. 33.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Stuffiness and hardness below the heart with dizziness are Shaoyang; stiffness of the neck and nape is Taiyang. Needling Dazhui and Feishu drains the Taiyang pathogen because the Taiyang vessel descends the nape and flanks the spine. [Needling] Ganshu drains the Shaoyang pathogen because the gallbladder is the bowel of the liver. Taiyang is in the exterior and Shaoyang in the interior, so this is a half-exterior half-interior pattern. The fifth pattern above (Clause 142) says, "Sweating must not be induced; if sweating is induced, there will be delirium," meaning that attacking the Taiyang pathogen by sweating makes the Shaoyang pathogen even more severe, invading the stomach and certainly causing delirium. Here it says "be careful not to purge," meaning that if one attacks the Shaoyang pathogen [by purging], the Taiyang pathogen takes advantage of the deficiency to enter the interior and will certainly form chest bind. The Classic says, "When overlap disease of Taiyang and Shaoyang is instead purged, chest bind forms."

## Notes

Like Clause 142, this treats overlap disease of Taiyang and Shaoyang with acupuncture. Whereas Clause 142 prohibits sweating, this one prohibits purgation. It is taken as an example of using acupuncture for an overlap disease in which both sweating and purgation are prohibited.
