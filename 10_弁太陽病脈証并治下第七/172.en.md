# Clause 172 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In combined disease of Taiyang and Shaoyang with spontaneous diarrhea, give Huangqin tang (Scutellaria Decoction); if there is vomiting, Huangqin jia Banxia Shengjiang tang (Scutellaria Decoction plus Pinellia and Fresh Ginger) governs it. 34.

Huangqin tang
Huangqin 3 liang; shaoyao 2 liang; gancao 2 liang (mix-fried); dazao 12 pieces (split).
Of the four ingredients above, boil with 1 dou of water down to 3 sheng, remove the dregs, and take 1 sheng warm, twice by day and once at night.

Huangqin jia Banxia Shengjiang tang
Huangqin 3 liang; shaoyao 2 liang; gancao 2 liang (mix-fried); dazao 12 pieces (split); banxia half a sheng (washed); shengjiang 1.5 liang (3 liang in one formula; sliced).
Of the six ingredients above, boil with 1 dou of water down to 3 sheng, remove the dregs, and take 1 sheng warm, twice by day and once at night.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Spontaneous diarrhea in combined disease of Taiyang and Yangming is in the exterior; Gegen tang should be given to induce sweating. Spontaneous diarrhea in combined disease of Yangming and Shaoyang is in the interior; Chengqi tang may be given to purge it. Spontaneous diarrhea in this combined disease of Taiyang and Shaoyang is half exterior, half interior, unsuitable for sweating or purgation, so Huangqin tang is given to harmonize and resolve the half-exterior half-interior pathogen. Vomiting means the stomach qi is counterflowing, so banxia and shengjiang are added to disperse the counterflowing qi.

(Nature and flavor) Huangqin: bitter, cold. Gancao: sweet, neutral. Shaoyao: sour, neutral. Dazao: sweet, warm.

When deficient and not excess, firm it with bitter and contract it with sour—the bitter sourness of huangqin and shaoyao firms and contracts the qi of the intestines and stomach. When weak and insufficient, supplement with sweet—the sweetness of gancao and dazao supplements and secures the weakness of the intestines and stomach.

## Notes

This clause uses Huangqin tang for diarrhea in combined disease of Taiyang and Shaoyang, and Huangqin jia Banxia Shengjiang tang when vomiting accompanies it.

- Cheng Wuji explains the diarrhea of combined diseases by contrast: Taiyang-Yangming (Gegen tang), Yangming-Shaoyang (Chengqi tang), Taiyang-Shaoyang (Huangqin tang).
- Huangqin tang is regarded as a formula for heat diarrhea in which Shaoyang heat has reached the intestines; in later times it was called "the ancestral formula for treating dysentery."
