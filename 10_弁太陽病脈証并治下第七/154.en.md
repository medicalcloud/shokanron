# Clause 154 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Glomus below the heart that is soft on pressure, with a floating pulse at the guan position: Dahuang Huanglian Xiexin tang (Rhubarb and Coptis Heart-Draining Decoction) governs it. Formula 17.

Dahuang 2 liang; huanglian 1 liang.

Steep the two ingredients above in 2 sheng of boiling water (*mafei tang*) for a short while, wring out and remove the dregs, and take warm in two doses. Your servants [Lin] Yi and others have examined this in detail: in all versions Dahuang Huanglian Xiexin tang has two ingredients. But the later Fuzi Xiexin tang uses dahuang, huanglian, huangqin, and fuzi; probably the preceding formula also contained huangqin, and the later one merely added fuzi. Hence it later says of Fuzi Xiexin tang, "originally said to add fuzi."

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Hardness below the heart, painful on pressure, with a deep guan pulse, is replete heat. Glomus below the heart, soft on pressure, with a floating guan pulse, is vacuous heat. Dahuang Huanglian tang guides away the vacuous heat.

(Nature and flavor) Dahuang: bitter, cold. Huanglian: bitter, cold.

The *Neijing* says: "When fire-heat receives evil, heart disease arises." Bitter enters the heart; cold removes heat. The bitter cold of dahuang and huanglian guides and drains the vacuous heat below the heart. It is only steeped in boiling water because only its thin qi is taken, to discharge vacuous heat.

## Notes

The clause on Dahuang Huanglian Xiexin tang, used for glomus due to heat (heat glomus).

- Glomus that is soft on pressure (without repletion) with a floating guan pulse is regarded as formless heat trapped below the heart.
- Steeping briefly in boiling water instead of decocting (the *mafei tang* steeping method) is explained as taking only the qi of the bitter, cold medicines to clear heat above without strongly purging the intestines and stomach.
- Lin Yi and others speculate, from the relation with Fuzi Xiexin tang in the next clause, that this formula also originally contained huangqin. In later times it is often used with huangqin added, as San Huang Xiexin tang.
