# Clause 148 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Cold damage of five or six days with sweating from the head, slight aversion to cold, cold hands and feet, fullness below the heart, no desire to eat, hard stool, and a fine pulse: this is slight yang binding; there must be an exterior and also an interior. A deep pulse also means [the evil] is in the interior. Sweating is due to slight yang [binding]. If it were pure yin binding, there could be no external pattern; all would have entered the interior. This is half in the interior and half outside. Although the pulse is deep and tight, it cannot be shaoyin disease. The reason is that yin [disease] cannot have sweating; here there is sweating from the head, so one knows it is not shaoyin. Xiao Chaihu tang may be given. If [the patient] does not feel clear, a bowel movement will resolve it. 14. Use the tenth formula above.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Five or six days of cold damage is when the evil should be transmitted inward. Sweating from the head and slight aversion to cold mean the exterior has still not resolved. Cold hands and feet, fullness below the heart, no desire to eat, hard stool, and a fine pulse mean the evil is bound in the interior. Hard stool is yang binding. Although this evil heat has been transmitted inward, it still carries exterior evil outside, so the heat binding is still shallow; hence "slight yang binding." A deep pulse means [the evil] is in the interior, but if it were pure yin binding, there would be no exterior pattern of head sweating and aversion to cold. The yin vessels all reach the neck and chest and return; they do not rise to run over the head. Since there is sweating from the head, one knows it is not shaoyin. Xiao Chaihu tang is given to remove the half-exterior half-interior evil. If after taking the decoction the external pattern has ended but [the patient] does not feel clear, the interior heat has not been removed; giving a decoction to produce slight diarrhea will cure it. Hence "a bowel movement will resolve it."

## Notes

A differential clause: although there are signs resembling shaoyin disease (cold hands and feet, fine and deep pulse), sweating from the head shows it is not shaoyin but "slight yang binding" straddling exterior and interior, and Xiao Chaihu tang is used.

- It distinguishes "slight yang binding" (mild binding by heat, with exterior signs) from "pure yin binding" (binding by cold, without exterior signs).
- The key differential point is that there is no sweating from the head in shaoyin (yin) disease.
