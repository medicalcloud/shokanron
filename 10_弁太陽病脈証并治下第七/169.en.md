# Clause 169 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Cold damage without great heat, with dry mouth and thirst, vexation of the heart, and slight aversion to cold on the back: Baihu jia Renshen tang governs it. 31. Use the formula above.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

"Without great heat" means no great heat on the body. Dry mouth with thirst and vexation should be yangming disease, but slight aversion to cold on the back means the exterior has not entirely ended, so it belongs to the taiyang. The back is yang. Aversion to cold on the back with harmony in the mouth (no thirst) is shaoyin disease, for which Fuzi tang should be given. Here the mouth is dry and thirsty, so although the back has aversion to cold, it is interior heat, and the aversion to cold is not severe; hence "slight aversion to cold." Baihu tang is given to harmonize the exterior and disperse heat, and renshen is added to stop thirst and engender fluids.

## Notes

Even with slight aversion to cold on the back, if there are dry mouth, thirst, and vexation, it is taken as exuberant yangming heat with qi and fluids damaged by sweating, and Baihu jia Renshen tang is used. The aversion to cold on the back is distinguished from the shaoyin Fuzi tang pattern (harmony in the mouth) by the presence or absence of thirst (Cheng Wuji).
