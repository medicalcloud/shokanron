# Clause 141 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When a disease is in the yang, it should be resolved by sweating; if, on the contrary, cold water is sprayed or poured over [the patient], the heat is suppressed and cannot leave, there is ever greater vexation, gooseflesh rises on the skin, and [the patient] thinks of drinking water but, on the contrary, is not thirsty: take Wenge san (Clamshell Powder). If not better, give Wuling san. For cold-repletion chest bind without heat signs, give the three-ingredient Xiao Xianxiong tang, using the sixth formula above. Bai san (White Powder) may also be taken. 7. (One version says to give the three-ingredient Xiao Bai san.)

Wenge san
Wenge (clamshell) 5 liang.
Make the one ingredient into powder and take one square-inch spoonful mixed with boiling water; use 5 ge of water.

Wuling san
Zhuling 18 zhu (remove the black skin); baizhu 18 zhu; zexie 1 liang 6 zhu; fuling 18 zhu; guizhi half a liang (remove the bark).
Make the five ingredients into powder, pound it further with a pestle in a mortar, and take a square-inch spoonful mixed with white drink, three times a day. Drink plenty of warm water; when sweat comes out, [the patient] recovers.

Bai san
Jiegeng (platycodon) 3 fen; badou (croton seed) 1 fen (remove the skin and core, dry-fry until black, grind until like grease); beimu (fritillaria) 3 fen.
Make the three ingredients into powder, add the badou, pound it further with a pestle in a mortar, and take it mixed with white drink—half a qianbi for a strong person, less for a weak one. If the disease is above the diaphragm there will certainly be vomiting; if below the diaphragm, certainly diarrhea. If there is no diarrhea, give a cup of hot porridge; if the diarrhea is excessive and does not stop, give a cup of cold porridge. When the body is hot and the gooseflesh does not resolve, and [the patient] wants to pull clothes over himself, if he is sprayed or washed with water, the heat is further suppressed and cannot get out; where there should be sweating and there is none, there is vexation. If, after sweating, there is pain in the abdomen, give 3 liang of shaoyao, as in the method above.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

A disease in the yang is evil in the exterior; by rule it should resolve with sweating. If instead cold water is sprayed or poured to wash [the patient], the heat is held by the cold water and cannot get out, so it attacks the interior instead, with ever greater vexation. Gooseflesh on the skin means water-cold qi lodges in the skin. Thinking of drinking water means there is heat in the interior; not being thirsty, on the contrary, means cold is in the exterior. Wenge san is given to disperse the water-cold qi in the exterior. If it does not get better, water and heat are contending and about to be transmitted inward, so Wuling san is given to induce sweating and harmonize it. At first the heat was in the exterior; restrained by the water-cold, it could not drain outward, attacked the interior, and bound in the chest and diaphragm, with hardness and pain below the heart. Since this is originally water-cold hiding heat and forming repletion, it is called cold-repletion chest bind. "No heat signs" means there is no heat outside and the heat is entirely contained in the interior. Xiao Xianxiong tang is given to purge and expel it. Bai san purges heat, so it may also be used to attack.

(Nature and flavor) Wenge: salty, cold.
Salty goes to the kidney; [thus] it can overcome the water qi.

(Nature and flavor) Jiegeng: acrid-bitter, slightly warm. Badou: neutral, warm. Beimu: acrid-bitter, neutral.
Acrid disperses and bitter discharges. The bitter acridity of jiegeng and beimu is used to lower qi; the acridity of badou is used to disperse repletion.

## Notes

A long clause on the changes after the mistaken procedure of cooling an exterior pattern with cold water, and the three corresponding formulas (Wenge san, Wuling san, Bai san).

- When cold water traps the heat in the exterior, with vexation and gooseflesh, Wenge san is used; if not better, Wuling san.
- For "cold-repletion chest bind," with cold and water bound in the chest and no heat signs, the three-ingredient Xiao Xianxiong tang or Bai san is said to be used. Since the bitter, cold Xiao Xianxiong tang does not suit a case without heat, an influential reading follows the note "one version says to give the three-ingredient Xiao Bai san" and uses only Bai san (jiegeng, badou, beimu).
- Bai san is a drastic formula containing croton; it is said to cause vomiting for disease above the diaphragm and purgation for disease below it, its action being adjusted with hot or cold porridge.
