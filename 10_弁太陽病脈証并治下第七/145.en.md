# Clause 145 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

A woman with cold damage and fever, whose menses happen to come, who is clear during the day but has delirium in the evening, as if seeing ghosts: this is heat entering the blood chamber. If the stomach qi and the upper two jiao are not violated, she will certainly recover spontaneously. 11.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Fever in cold damage means the cold has already become heat. When the menses happen to come, the blood chamber is empty, and the pathogenic heat takes advantage of the deficiency to enter it. Delirium in the daytime would mean the pathogen lodges in the bowel and the yang contends. Here she is clear in the daytime but has delirium in the evening as if seeing ghosts, meaning the pathogen has not entered the bowel but the blood chamber, and the yin contends. Delirium from exuberant yang should be purged, but this is heat entering the blood chamber, so purgative medicine must not be given to violate the stomach qi. When heat entering the blood chamber has bound the blood into excess heat, Xiao Chaihu tang (Shosaikoto) is given to disperse the pathogen and induce sweating; here, although heat has entered the blood chamber, it is not lodged and bound, so sweating medicine must not be given to violate the upper jiao. When heat entering the blood chamber causes fullness of the chest and rib-side like chest bind, Qimen may be needled; here, although heat has entered the blood chamber, there is no fullness or binding, so Qimen must not be needled to violate the middle jiao. She will certainly recover spontaneously because, as the menses flow, the heat leaves with the blood; when the blood has been discharged, the pathogenic heat is completely removed and she recovers. Sweating violates the upper jiao because sweating stirs the wei-defensive qi, and the wei-defensive qi issues from the upper jiao. Needling Qimen violates the middle jiao because needling Qimen stirs the ying-nutrient qi, and the ying-nutrient qi issues from the middle jiao. The *Maijing* says, "If the stomach qi and the upper two jiao are not violated, she will certainly recover spontaneously"—how could this refer only to medicines and not to needles?

## Notes

Even with heat entering the blood chamber, if the menses come smoothly and the blood is discharged, the heat leaves with the blood; so if one watches the course without damaging the healthy qi by purgation, sweating, or the like, she will recover spontaneously. Delirium that appears at night is taken as a sign that the pathogen is in the blood (yin) aspect. "The upper two jiao" are generally taken as the upper and middle jiao; Cheng Wuji explains that sweating violates the upper jiao and needling Qimen the middle jiao.
