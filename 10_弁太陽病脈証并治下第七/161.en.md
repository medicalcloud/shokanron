# Clause 161 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In cold damage, when after sweating, or vomiting, or purgation [the disease] has resolved, but glomus and hardness below the heart remain and the belching does not go away, Xuanfu Daizhe tang (Inula and Hematite Decoction) governs it. Formula 23.

Xuanfuhua (inula flower) 3 liang; renshen 2 liang; shengjiang 5 liang; daizhe (hematite) 1 liang; gancao 3 liang (mix-fried); banxia half a sheng (washed); dazao 12 pieces (split).

Of the seven ingredients above, boil with 1 dou of water down to 6 sheng, remove the dregs, decoct again down to 3 sheng, and take 1 sheng warm, three times a day.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Although the major evil has resolved, because sweating, vomiting, and purgation were applied, the stomach qi is weak and not yet harmonized, and vacuous qi counterflows upward; hence glomus and hardness below the heart and belching that does not go away. Xuanfu Daizheshi tang is given to lower the vacuous qi and harmonize the stomach.

(Nature and flavor) Xuanfuhua: salty, warm. Renshen: sweet, warm. Shengjiang: acrid, warm. Banxia: acrid, warm. Daizheshi: bitter, cold. Dazao: sweet, warm. Gancao: sweet, neutral.

Hardness means the qi is firm; salty flavor can soften it—the saltiness of xuanfu softens the hardness of the glomus. Vacuity makes the qi float; heavy preparations can settle it—the heaviness of daizheshi settles the vacuous counterflow. Acrid disperses—the acridity of shengjiang and banxia disperses the vacuous glomus. Sweet moderates—the sweetness of renshen, gancao, and dazao supplements the weak stomach.

## Notes

The clause on Xuanfu Daizhe tang, used when sweating, vomiting, or purgation has resolved the evil but the stomach qi is weakened and its qi counterflows, leaving glomus below the heart and persistent belching. It is regarded as composed of xuanfuhua and daizheshi to lower the counterflowing qi, with renshen, gancao, and dazao to supplement the stomach. In later times it has been widely applied to belching, hiccup, vomiting, and the like. The formula name "Xuanfu" follows the base text's character 復 (generally written 覆).
