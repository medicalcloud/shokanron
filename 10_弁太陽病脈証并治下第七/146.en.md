# Clause 146 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Cold damage of six or seven days with fever, slight aversion to cold, vexing pain of the limb joints, slight retching, propping binding below the heart, and the external pattern not yet gone: Chaihu Guizhi tang (Bupleurum and Cinnamon Twig Decoction) governs it. Formula 12.

Guizhi (remove the bark); huangqin 1.5 liang; renshen 1.5 liang; gancao 1 liang (mix-fried); banxia 2.5 ge (washed); shaoyao 1.5 liang; dazao 6 pieces (split); shengjiang 1.5 liang (sliced); chaihu 4 liang.

Of the nine ingredients above, boil with 7 sheng of water down to 3 sheng, remove the dregs, and take 1 sheng warm. Originally called Renshen tang: made as in the method for Guizhi tang, adding banxia, chaihu, and huangqin, and again as in the method for Chaihu tang. Now renshen is used to make a half-preparation.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Six or seven days of cold damage is when the evil should be transmitted inward. *Zhi* means scattered. Retching with binding below the heart is an interior pattern; by rule the interior should be attacked. Fever, slight aversion to cold, and vexing pain of the limb joints mean the external pattern has not yet gone, so the interior must not be attacked. Chaihu Guizhi tang is given to harmonize and resolve it.

## Notes

For a mild simultaneous taiyang exterior pattern (fever, slight aversion to cold, vexing joint pain) and shaoyang pattern (slight retching, propping binding below the heart), Chaihu Guizhi tang—half doses of Guizhi tang and Xiao Chaihu tang combined—is used. As a representative combined formula for overlap disease of taiyang and shaoyang, it has been widely applied in later times. The amount of guizhi is not written in the base text (it is generally read as 1.5 liang, listed with the other ingredients).
