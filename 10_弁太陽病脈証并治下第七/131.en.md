# Clause 131 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When a disease arising in the yang is, on the contrary, purged, heat enters and thereby chest bind forms. When a disease arising in the yin is, on the contrary, purged (one version writes "sweats"), thereby glomus (*pi*) forms. Chest bind forms because purgation was applied too early. In chest bind, the nape is also stiff, like soft tetany; when it is purged, there is harmony. Da Xianxiong wan (Major Chest-Draining Pill) is appropriate. Formula 1.

Dahuang half a jin; tinglizi (lepidium/descurainia seed) half a sheng (dry-fried); mangxiao half a sheng; xingren half a sheng (remove the skins and tips, dry-fried until black).

Of the four ingredients above, pound and sift two; add the xingren and mangxiao, grind together until like grease, mix with the powder, and take a piece the size of a pellet. Separately pound gansui (kansui) into powder, one qianbi; with 2 ge of white honey and 2 sheng of water, boil down to 1 sheng, and take warm in a single dose. Purgation occurs after one night; if there is no purgation, take it again—purgation is taken as the effect. Prohibitions as in the method for medicines.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Fever with aversion to cold arises in the yang; if it is instead purged, the yang evil in the exterior enters the interior and binds in the chest, forming chest bind. Aversion to cold without fever arises in the yin; if it is instead purged, the yin evil in the exterior enters the interior and binds below the heart, forming glomus.

In chest bind the nape is stiff because the evil is bound in the chest, the chest and diaphragm are bound and full, and below the heart is tense and replete, so one can look up but cannot bend forward; this nape stiffness resembles soft tetany. Da Xianxiong wan is given to purge the binding and discharge the fullness.

(Nature and flavor) Dahuang: bitter, cold. Tingli: bitter, cold. Mangxiao: salty, cold. Xingren: bitter-sweet, warm.

The bitter saltiness of dahuang and mangxiao serves to purge heat; the bitter sweetness of tingli and xingren serves to discharge fullness. Gansui is chosen for its direct penetrating [force], white honey for its moistening and freeing [force]; all are things that purge and discharge fullness and repletion.

## Notes

A clause on how chest bind and glomus form, and on Da Xianxiong wan for chest bind with stiffness of the nape and back.

- Purging an exterior (yang) pattern too early lets heat enter the chest, forming chest bind; purging [a disease arising in the yin] forms glomus (blockage below the heart). What "yang" and "yin" refer to is debated.
- Da Xianxiong wan is Da Xianxiong tang plus tinglizi, xingren, and white honey made into a pill; it is regarded as gently purging a binding located higher up (chest, nape).
