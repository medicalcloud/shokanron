# Clause 149 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Cold damage of five or six days with retching and fever: the Chaihu tang pattern is complete. If it is purged with another medicine and the Chaihu pattern is still present, give Chaihu tang again. Although it has already been purged, this is not a mistake; there will certainly be steaming heat and shivering, then fever and sweating, and it resolves. If there are fullness, hardness, and pain below the heart, this is chest bind; Da Xianxiong tang governs it. If there is only fullness without pain, this is glomus; Chaihu [tang] is not suitable, and Banxia Xiexin tang (Pinellia Heart-Draining Decoction) is appropriate. Formula 15.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Five or six days of cold damage is when the evil is half exterior, half interior. Retching with fever is a pattern of evil half exterior, half interior; this is the Chaihu pattern being complete. When it is purged with another medicine but the Chaihu pattern has not ended, it is not a mistake, and giving Chaihu tang again cures it. If after purgation the evil qi is transmitted inward [the following happens]. When the evil is half exterior, half interior, both yin and yang have evil, and when after purgation the evil qi is transmitted inward, there is also a difference between yin and yang. If after purgation the yang evil is transmitted inward, it binds in the chest and forms chest bind, since the chest is the aspect where the yang receives qi; Da Xianxiong tang is given to purge the binding. When the yin evil is transmitted inward, it remains below the heart and forms glomus, since below the heart is the aspect where the yin receives qi; Banxia Xiexin tang is given to free the glomus. The Classic says, "When a disease arising in the yang is instead purged, heat enters and chest bind forms; when a disease arising in the yin is instead purged, glomus forms"—this is what is meant.

(Nature and flavor) Banxia: acrid, neutral. Huangqin: bitter, cold. Ganjiang: acrid, hot. Renshen: sweet, warm. Huanglian: bitter, cold. Dazao: warm, sweet. Gancao: sweet, neutral.

Acrid enters the lung and disperses qi; the acridity of banxia disperses bound qi. Bitter enters the heart and discharges heat; the bitterness of huangqin and huanglian drains the heat of the glomus. The spleen desires moderation; quickly eat sweet to moderate it—the sweetness of renshen, gancao, and dazao moderates it.

## Notes

A clause contrasting three courses after the Chaihu tang pattern has been mistakenly purged (Chaihu pattern remaining, chest bind, glomus), and using Banxia Xiexin tang for glomus.

- Fullness, hardness, and pain below the heart are chest bind (Da Xianxiong tang); fullness without pain is glomus (Banxia Xiexin tang).
- Banxia Xiexin tang combines cold medicines (huangqin, huanglian) and hot medicines (banxia, ganjiang), with renshen, gancao, and dazao to supplement the middle; it has been widely applied in later times as the representative formula for glomus below the heart (epigastric blockage).
- The base text lacks the Banxia Xiexin tang formula (composition and preparation) here. For the formula in the other sources, see the notes of this unit.
