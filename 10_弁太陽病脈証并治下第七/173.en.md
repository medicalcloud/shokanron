# Clause 173 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Cold damage with heat in the chest, evil qi in the stomach, pain in the abdomen, and a desire to vomit: Huanglian tang (Coptis Decoction) governs it. Formula 35.

Huanglian 3 liang; gancao 3 liang (mix-fried); ganjiang 3 liang; guizhi 3 liang (remove the bark); renshen 2 liang; banxia half a sheng (washed); dazao 12 pieces (split).

Of the seven ingredients above, boil with 1 dou of water down to 6 sheng, remove the dregs, and take warm, three times by day and twice at night. Doubtfully a formula of Zhongjing.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

When in a person with dampness, after purgation, there is something like a coating on the tongue, it is because there is heat in the cinnabar field and cold in the chest; the evil qi has entered the interior with heat below and cold above. This, by contrast, is cold-damage evil qi transmitted inward with cold below and heat above. Evil qi in the stomach prevents yin and yang from interacting: the yin cannot rise and governs alone below, causing cold below with abdominal pain; the yang cannot descend and governs alone above, causing heat in the chest with a desire to vomit. Huanglian tang is given to raise and lower the qi of yin and yang.

(Nature and flavor) Huanglian: bitter, cold. Gancao: sweet, neutral. Ganjiang: acrid, hot. Guizhi: acrid, hot. Renshen: sweet, warm. Banxia: acrid, warm. Dazao: sweet, warm.

Heat above is discharged with bitter—the bitterness of huanglian lowers the yang. Cold below is dispersed with acrid—the acridity of guizhi, ganjiang, and banxia raises the yin. The spleen desires moderation; quickly eat sweet to moderate it—the sweetness of renshen, gancao, and dazao boosts the stomach.

[The words "take twice at night" have slipped into the commentary.]

## Notes

The clause on Huanglian tang, used for a pattern of "heat above and cold below"—heat in the chest (above) and cold in the stomach and intestines (below)—with abdominal pain and nausea.

- It is Banxia Xiexin tang with huangqin replaced by guizhi and more huanglian, regarded as treating cold and heat at the same time.
- "Doubtfully a formula of Zhongjing" after the formula is regarded as a later note.
