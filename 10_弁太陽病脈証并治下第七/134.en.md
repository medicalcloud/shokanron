# Clause 134 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Taiyang disease with a floating, stirred, and rapid pulse. Floating means wind; rapid means heat; stirred means pain; rapid means deficiency. When there are headache, fever, slight night sweats, and, on the contrary, aversion to cold, the exterior has not yet resolved. If the physician instead purges it, the stirred, rapid pulse turns slow, there is resisting pain within the diaphragm (one version says "headache with immediate dizziness"), the stomach is empty and deficient, visiting qi stirs the diaphragm, there are shortness of breath, restlessness, and anguish in the heart; the yang qi sinks inward, and below the heart becomes hard as a result—this is chest bind, and Da Xianxiong tang (Taikankyoto; Major Chest-Draining Decoction) governs it. If chest bind does not form, but there is sweating only from the head and none elsewhere, stopping at the neck, with inhibited urination, the body will certainly turn yellow. Da Xianxiong tang. 2.

Dahuang (daio) 6 liang (remove the bark); mangxiao (bosho) 1 sheng; gansui (kanzui) 1 qianbi.

Of the three ingredients above, with 6 sheng of water first boil the dahuang down to 2 sheng, remove the dregs, add the mangxiao, boil once or twice, add the gansui powder, and take 1 sheng warm. When there is satisfactory diarrhea, stop the later dose.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Stirred and rapid are both yang pulses; the pathogen should be regarded as in the exterior. Sweating during sleep is called night sweats; when the pathogenic qi is half exterior, half interior, there is no aversion to cold. Here, with headache, fever, slight night sweats, and on the contrary aversion to cold, the exterior has not yet resolved and sweating should be induced. If the physician instead purges, vacuating the stomach qi, the exterior pathogen takes advantage of the deficiency and sinks. When the pathogen is in the exterior, yang pulses appear; when in the interior, yin pulses. Because the pathogenic qi sinks inward, the stirred, rapid pulse turns slow. That the floating pulse alone does not change is because the pathogen binds in the chest and the yang of the upper jiao is bound, so the pulse cannot become deep. Visiting qi is external pathogen that takes advantage of the emptiness of the stomach to enter the interior and bind in the chest and diaphragm. Resisting pain within the diaphragm is visiting qi stirring the diaphragm. The *Jingui yaolüe* says: "Shortness of breath, not enough to breathe, is excess." Shortness of breath, dryness and vexation, and anguish in the heart all mean that pathogenic heat has become excess. When the yang qi sinks inward, the qi cannot pass through the diaphragm, and it blocks below the heart, becoming hard, full, and painful—chest bind forms. Da Xianxiong tang is given to purge the bound heat. If the stomach is empty and the yang qi sinks inward but does not bind in the chest and diaphragm and instead descends into the stomach, then if the whole body sweats, the heat passes out and there is no yellowing. If there is sweating only from the head and not the body, stopping at the neck, with inhibited urination, the heat cannot pass out, and there will certainly be yellowing.

(Nature and flavor) Dahuang: bitter, cold. Mangxiao: salty, cold. Gansui: bitter, cold.

Dahuang is called "the general"; with its bitterness it sweeps clean. Mangxiao, also called xiaoshi, can soften hardness with its saltiness. Between them is gansui to free water; like a meridian for water, its qi can penetrate directly through the binding. As the three ingredients for draining the chest, they are fitting.

## Notes

The clause on Da Xianxiong tang, used for "chest bind"—hardness and pain below the heart—formed when mistaken purgation of an exterior pattern lets the yang qi (heat) sink inward and bind with water.

- Da Xianxiong tang consists of dahuang, mangxiao, and gansui; it is regarded as a drastic formula that strongly purges the binding of heat and water. When satisfactory diarrhea occurs, it is stopped.
- If chest bind does not form but the heat enters the stomach, with sweating only from the head and inhibited urination, the trapped heat causes jaundice.
