# Clause 177 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Cold damage with a knotted and regularly intermittent pulse and palpitations of the heart: Zhi Gancao tang (Shakanzoto; Honey-Fried Licorice Decoction) governs it. Formula 39.

Gancao (kanzo) 4 liang (mix-fried); shengjiang (shokyo) 3 liang (sliced); renshen (ninjin) 2 liang; shengdihuang (shojio; fresh rehmannia) 1 jin; guizhi (keishi) 3 liang (remove the bark); ejiao (akyo; donkey-hide gelatin) 2 liang; maimendong (bakumondo; ophiopogon) half a sheng (remove the core); maren (hemp seed) half a sheng; dazao (taiso) 30 pieces (split).

Of the nine ingredients above, with 7 sheng of clear wine and 8 sheng of water first boil eight of them down to 3 sheng, remove the dregs, add the ejiao and dissolve it completely, and take 1 sheng warm, three times a day. Also called Fumai tang (Fukumyakuto; Pulse-Restoring Decoction).

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Of the knotted and regularly intermittent pulses, one that beats, stops midway, and can return by itself is called bound; one that cannot return by itself is called intermittent. They are due to blood and qi being deficient and debilitated so that they cannot continue. Palpitations in the heart show that the genuine qi is deficient within. Zhi Gancao tang is given to boost deficiency and supplement blood and qi, [restoring the pulse].

(Nature and flavor) Gancao: sweet, neutral. Shengjiang: acrid, warm. Guizhi: acrid, hot. Renshen: sweet, warm. Shengdihuang: sweet, cold. Ejiao: warm, sweet. Maimendong: sweet, neutral. Maziren (mashinin): sweet, neutral. Dazao: sweet, warm.

Supplementation can remove weakness: the sweetness of renshen, gancao, and dazao supplements the insufficient qi, and the acridity of guizhi and shengjiang boosts the healthy qi. The *Shengji jing* says: "When fluids are consumed and dispersed, there is withering; the five zang organs wilt and weaken, and the ying-nutrient and wei-defense dry up and cease to flow; warm preparations serve to moisten them." The sweetness of maren, ejiao, maimendong, and dihuang moistens the meridians, boosts the blood, restores the pulse, and reaches the heart.

## Notes

The clause on Zhi Gancao tang (Fumai tang), used for a knotted and regularly intermittent pulse (arrhythmia) with palpitations.

- It is attributed to deficiency of both qi and blood (yin and yang), so that the pulse cannot continue and the heart is not nourished; it is explained as renshen, gancao, and guizhi supplementing qi (yang) and dihuang, ejiao, maimendong, and maren supplementing blood (yin).
- Decocting in clear wine and water is characteristic. It has been widely applied in later times as the representative formula for arrhythmia and palpitations.
