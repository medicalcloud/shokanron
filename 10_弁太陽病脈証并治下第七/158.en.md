# Clause 158 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In cold damage or wind-strike, the physician instead purged, and the person has diarrhea dozens of times a day with undigested grain, thunderous rumbling in the abdomen, glomus, hardness, and fullness below the heart, dry retching, and vexation without rest. The physician, seeing glomus below the heart, said the disease was not exhausted and purged again, and the glomus became even worse. This is not bound heat; it is only that the stomach is vacuous and visiting qi counterflows upward, causing the hardness. Gancao Xiexin tang (Licorice Heart-Draining Decoction) governs it. Formula 21.

Gancao 4 liang (mix-fried); huangqin 3 liang; ganjiang 3 liang; banxia half a sheng (washed); dazao 12 pieces (split); huanglian 1 liang.

Of the six ingredients above, boil with 1 dou of water down to 6 sheng, remove the dregs, decoct again down to 3 sheng, and take 1 sheng warm, three times a day. Your servants [Lin] Yi and others respectfully note: in the method of Shengjiang Xiexin tang above, it was originally called "Lizhong Renshen Huangqin tang." Examined in detail, Xiexin [tang] treats glomus, and glomus qi arises from [disease] arising in the yin; thus the three Xiexin formulas—Banxia, Shengjiang, and Gancao—are all based on Lizhong [tang], and each formula must contain renshen. That it is absent from Gancao Xiexin [tang] now is because it has dropped out. Further, in the *Qianjin* and the *Waitai miyao*, this formula used to treat cold damage with worm-eating (*ni shi*) always contains renshen; there is no doubt it dropped out.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

"Cold damage or wind-strike" means either cold damage or wind-strike. The evil qi was in the exterior, but the physician instead purged it, vacuating the intestines and stomach so that the qi sank inward. Diarrhea dozens of times a day with undigested grain and thunderous rumbling mean that after purgation the interior is vacuous and the stomach weak. Glomus and hardness below the heart, dry retching, and vexation without rest mean the stomach is empty and visiting qi counterflows upward. Xiexin tang is given to attack [the glomus], and gancao is added to supplement the vacuity. In the former case the stomach became vacuous after sweating, damaging the yang qi outside, so shengjiang was added; here the stomach became vacuous after purgation, damaging the yin qi inside, so gancao is added.

## Notes

The clause on Gancao Xiexin tang, used when repeated mistaken purgation has greatly weakened the stomach qi, causing violent diarrhea, abdominal rumbling, glomus and hardness below the heart, dry retching, and vexation.

- It is Banxia Xiexin tang with increased gancao, regarded as emphasizing supplementation of stomach vacuity.
- The Song edition formula lacks renshen, but Lin Yi and others regard this as an omission and say it should be added; today it is generally used with renshen.
- "Cold damage with worm-eating" (*ni shi*) in the note by Lin Yi and others is thought to refer to the fox-creeper disease (*huhuo*) for which the *Jingui yaolüe* uses Gancao Xiexin tang (see the notes for Chapter 10).
