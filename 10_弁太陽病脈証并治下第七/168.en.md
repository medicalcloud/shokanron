# Clause 168 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When in cold damage, after vomiting or purgation, [the disease] has not resolved after seven or eight days, heat is bound in the interior, both exterior and interior are hot, with occasional aversion to wind, great thirst, a dry tongue with vexation, and a desire to drink several sheng of water, Baihu jia Renshen tang governs it. Formula 30.

Zhimu 6 liang; shigao 1 jin (crushed); gancao 2 liang (mix-fried); renshen 2 liang; jingmi 6 ge.

Of the five ingredients above, with 1 dou of water boil until the rice is cooked and the decoction is done, remove the dregs, and take 1 sheng warm, three times a day. This formula may be taken only after the Beginning of Summer and before the Beginning of Autumn; it must not be taken after the Beginning of Autumn. In the first, second, and third months it is still bitterly cold, and it must not be given then either; if given, it causes vomiting, diarrhea, and abdominal pain. It also must not be given to any who have lost blood or are vacuous. For those who get abdominal pain and diarrhea from it, simply warm them and they should recover.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Seven or eight days after vomiting or purgation it should resolve; it has not, and the heat is bound in the interior. Exterior heat is heat of the body; interior heat is internal heat. Originally, after vomiting and purgation, the evil qi took advantage of the vacuity to sink inward and become bound heat. If there were no exterior heat but only interior heat, the evil heat would bind into repletion. Here the exterior heat has not yet ended, so there is occasional aversion to wind. If the evil qi were purely in the exterior, the aversion to wind would be constant; if purely in the interior, there would be no aversion to wind at all. From the occasional aversion to wind one knows there is heat in both exterior and interior. When evil heat binds into repletion, there is no great thirst; when evil heat is diffuse, there is thirst. Here, although the heat is bound in the interior and both exterior and interior are hot, it has not yet bound into repletion; the evil qi is diffuse and fumigates and steams the burners and diaphragm, hence great thirst, a dry tongue with vexation, and a desire to drink several sheng of water. Baihu jia Renshen tang is given to disperse the heat and engender fluids.

## Notes

The clause on Baihu jia Renshen tang, used when yangming heat is exuberant (great thirst, dry tongue, vexation, copious drinking) and spread through exterior and interior, but has not yet become bowel repletion (dry stool).

- "Occasional aversion to wind" is regarded as dislike of wind because heat makes one sweat and opens the interstices, distinguished from an exterior pattern.
- The note after the formula, cautioning against use outside summer (especially in cold seasons) or in weak patients, is often regarded as a later addition.
