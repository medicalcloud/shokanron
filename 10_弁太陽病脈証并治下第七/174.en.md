# Clause 174 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Cold damage of eight or nine days: wind and dampness contend, the body aches with vexation, one cannot turn over by oneself, there is no retching and no thirst, and the pulse is floating, vacuous, and rough: Guizhi Fuzi tang (Cinnamon Twig and Aconite Decoction) governs it. If the person's stool is hard (one version says "hard below the navel and below the heart") and urination spontaneously free, Qu Gui jia Baizhu tang (minus Cinnamon Twig plus White Atractylodes) governs it. 36.

Guizhi Fuzi tang
Guizhi 4 liang (remove the bark); fuzi 3 pieces (blast-fried, remove the skin, broken); shengjiang 3 liang (sliced); dazao 12 pieces (split); gancao 2 liang (mix-fried).
Of the five ingredients above, boil with 6 sheng of water down to 2 sheng, remove the dregs, and take warm in three doses.

Qu Guizhi jia Baizhu tang
Fuzi 3 pieces (blast-fried, remove the skin, broken); baizhu 4 liang; shengjiang 3 liang (sliced); gancao 2 liang (mix-fried); dazao 12 pieces (split).
Of the five ingredients above, boil with 6 sheng of water down to 2 sheng, remove the dregs, and take warm in three doses. After the first dose, the person's body feels as if numb; after about half a day take it again, and when all three doses are finished the person seems veiled (dizzy); do not be alarmed. This is because fuzi and zhu together course within the skin, expelling water qi that has not yet been removed, causing this. By rule, 4 liang of gui should be added. This is originally one formula with two methods: with hard stool and spontaneously free urination, gui is removed; with stool that is not hard and inhibited urination, gui should be added. Three pieces of fuzi are probably too many; for the weak and for women after childbirth, it is appropriate to reduce the dose.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

When cold damage or wind-strike reaches seven or eight days, the time of the second round through the channels, the evil qi is mostly in the interior and the body need not suffer pain. Here, after many days, the body again aches with vexation and one cannot turn over by oneself; this is wind and dampness contending. Vexation is wind; body pain with inability to turn over is dampness. The Classic says, "Wind makes [the pulse] floating and vacuous," and the *Maijing* says, "When the pulse comes rough, one suffers from cold-damp." No retching and no thirst mean there is no evil in the interior. A floating, vacuous, and rough pulse with aching and vexation of the body shows that the wind-damp is only in the channels. Guizhi Fuzi tang is given to disperse the wind-damp in the exterior.

Gui induces sweating and drives off fluids. Here urination is free and the stool hard, meaning the fluids are insufficient, so gui is removed and zhu added.

(Nature and flavor) Guizhi: acrid, hot. Fuzi: acrid, hot. Shengjiang: acrid, warm. Gancao: sweet, warm. Dazao: sweet, warm.

Wind in the exterior is dispersed with the acrid sweetness of guizhi and gancao. Dampness in the channels is expelled with the acrid heat of fuzi. The acrid sweetness of ginger and jujube moves the nutritive and defensive, frees the fluids, and harmonizes the exterior.

## Notes

The clause on Guizhi Fuzi tang, used when wind and damp evils are bound in the exterior and joints so that body pain prevents turning over (wind-damp), and its variant Qu Gui jia Baizhu tang (Baizhu Fuzi tang).

- Guizhi Fuzi tang is Guizhi tang without shaoyao, with increased guizhi and fuzi; it warms and disperses wind and damp.
- For one with hard stool and free urination, the fluids are insufficient, so guizhi, which induces sweating, is removed and baizhu added.
- The statement that numbness or dizziness after taking it is a reaction (*mingxuan*) of the medicine expelling water qi and need not alarm one is often cited as an example of a post-dosing reaction.
