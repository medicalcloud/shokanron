# Clause 143 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

A woman with wind-strike, fever, and aversion to cold, whose menses happen to come: when seven or eight days after contracting it the heat is gone, the pulse slow, and the body cool, but there are fullness below the chest and rib-side like chest bind and delirious speech, this is heat entering the blood chamber. One should needle Qimen, taking [the evil] according to its repletion. 9.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Fever and aversion to cold in wind-strike are exterior disease. If the menses had not come, when the exterior evil was transmitted inward it would enter the bowel and not the blood chamber. Because the menses happened to come, the blood chamber was empty; at seven or eight days, when the evil qi is transmitted inward, it does not enter the bowel but takes advantage of the vacuity to enter the blood chamber. The heat being gone, the pulse slow, and the body cool mean the evil qi has sunk inward and the exterior pattern has ended. Fullness below the chest and rib-side like chest bind, with delirious speech, means the heat has entered the blood chamber and the interior is replete. Qimen is the alarm point of the liver, and the liver governs the blood; needling Qimen drains the heat of the blood chamber. Examine which channel's qi is replete, and drain according to that repletion.

## Notes

One of the clauses (143–145) on the pattern of "heat entering the blood chamber," in which menstruation coincides with the course of an externally contracted disease and the evil heat enters the "blood chamber" (the uterus, or, by other views, the liver or penetrating vessel). Qimen (the alarm point of the liver) is needled to drain the heat.
