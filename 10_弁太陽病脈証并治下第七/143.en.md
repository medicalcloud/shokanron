# Clause 143 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

A woman with wind attack, fever, and aversion to cold, whose menses happen to come: when seven or eight days after contracting it the heat is gone, the pulse slow, and the body cool, but there are fullness below the chest and rib-side like chest bind and delirium, this is heat entering the blood chamber. One should needle Qimen, taking [the pathogen] according to its excess. 9.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Fever and aversion to cold in wind attack are exterior disease. If the menses had not come, when the exterior pathogen was transmitted inward it would enter the bowel and not the blood chamber. Because the menses happened to come, the blood chamber was empty; at seven or eight days, when the pathogenic qi is transmitted inward, it does not enter the bowel but takes advantage of the deficiency to enter the blood chamber. The heat being gone, the pulse slow, and the body cool mean the pathogenic qi has sunk inward and the exterior pattern has ended. Fullness below the chest and rib-side like chest bind, with delirium, means the heat has entered the blood chamber and the interior is in excess. Qimen is the alarm point of the liver, and the liver governs the blood; needling Qimen drains the heat of the blood chamber. Examine which meridian's qi is in excess, and drain according to that excess.

## Notes

One of the clauses (143–145) on the pattern of "heat entering the blood chamber," in which menstruation coincides with the course of an externally contracted disease and the pathogenic heat enters the "blood chamber" (the uterus, or, by other views, the liver or penetrating vessel). Qimen (the alarm point of the liver) is needled to drain the heat.
