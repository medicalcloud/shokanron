# Clause 166 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When a disease is like the Guizhi [tang] pattern, but the head does not ache and the nape is not stiff, the cun pulse is slightly floating, there are glomus and hardness in the chest, and qi surges up to the throat so that one cannot breathe, this is cold in the chest; it should be vomited, and Guadi san (Melon Stalk Powder) is appropriate. Formula 28.

Guadi (melon stalk) 1 fen (dry-fried until yellow); chixiaodou (rice bean) 1 fen.

Pound and sift the two ingredients above separately into powder, then mix them together and take one qianbi. Boil 1 ge of xiangchi with 7 ge of hot water into a thin gruel, remove the dregs, take the liquid and mix it with the powder, and take warm in a single dose. If there is no vomiting, add a little at a time, and stop once there is satisfactory vomiting. For all who have lost blood or are vacuous, Guadi san must not be given.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

"A disease like the Guizhi pattern" means fever, sweating, and aversion to wind, i.e., evil in the exterior. With headache and a stiff nape, the Guizhi tang pattern would be complete. If the head does not ache and the nape is not stiff, the evil is not in the exterior but has been transmitted inward. Floating means in the exterior; deep means in the interior. Here the cun pulse is slightly floating, so the evil is neither in the exterior nor the interior but in the chest. Since the chest corresponds to the exterior, one knows that when the evil is in the chest it resembles the Guizhi pattern and the cun pulse is slightly floating. From the glomus and hardness in the chest, surging up to the throat, and inability to breathe, one knows that cold evil lodges in the chest and not in the exterior. The *Qianjin* says: "When qi floats in the upper part, stuffing the heart and chest, with fullness in the chest, vomiting it will cure it." Guadi san is given to make [the patient] vomit the evil in the chest.

(Nature and flavor) Guadi: bitter, cold. Chixiaodou: sour, warm.

What is high, make it pass out—with the bitterness of guadi and fermented soybean. What is above, make it well up—with the sourness of chixiaodou. The *Neijing* says: "Sour and bitter cause upwelling and discharge and are yin."

## Notes

The clause on Guadi san, which treats by vomiting a patient whose chest is blocked by a substantial evil such as phlegm, with difficulty breathing and qi surging upward.

- It resembles the Guizhi tang pattern (fever, sweating), but is distinguished by the absence of headache and nape stiffness, a slightly floating cun pulse, and glomus in the chest with breathing difficulty.
- The "cold" in "cold in the chest" is often interpreted as a substantial evil such as phlegm-rheum.
- Guadi san is the representative emetic of the *Shanghan lun*, with cautions such as stopping once vomiting occurs and not using it for those who have bled or are weak.
