# Clause 129 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

What is called visceral bind? Answer: It is like chest bind, but eating and drinking are as usual, there is diarrhea from time to time, the cun pulse is floating, and the guan pulse is small, fine, deep, and tight; it is called visceral bind. When the tongue has a white, slippery coating, it is difficult to treat.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

In chest bind the evil binds in the chest; in visceral bind the evil binds in the viscera. Both arise after purgation, when the evil qi takes advantage of the vacuity to enter the interior. When after purgation the evil qi enters and binds with the yang, it is chest bind, because the yang receives qi in the chest. When it binds with the yin, it is visceral bind, because what the yin receives enters the five viscera. Qi should flow but is blocked, hence pain. When the evil binds in the yang aspect, the yin qi cannot pass upward; when it binds in the yin aspect, the yang qi cannot pass downward; therefore in both there is hardness and pain below the heart. From a floating cun pulse and a deep guan pulse one knows the evil is bound in the yang. From a floating cun pulse and a small, fine, deep, tight guan pulse one knows the evil is bound in the yin. Since the yin is bound but not the yang, even with binding pain below the heart, eating and drinking remain as usual. The yin qi takes advantage of intestinal vacuity to descend, so there is spontaneous diarrhea from time to time. Yin resolves when it obtains yang; visceral bind is easier to treat when there are many heat signs. A white, slippery tongue coating means the evil qi is bound and the chest is also cold; hence "difficult to treat."

## Notes

The definition of visceral bind: like chest bind with pain below the heart, but eating and drinking are normal, there is occasional diarrhea, and the guan pulse is small, fine, deep, and tight.

- Whereas chest bind is a binding of yang (heat), visceral bind is yin (cold) bound in the viscera, regarded as a grave disease of yang vacuity with yin exuberance.
- A white, slippery tongue coating shows severe cold and is considered difficult to treat.
