# §1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Dual-yang combination disease: when taiyang first contracted the disease, sweating was induced, but the sweat came out first without thoroughness, and so it shifted to belong to the yangming, with continuing spontaneous slight sweating and no aversion to cold. If the taiyang disease pattern has not ceased, it must not be purged; purging it is adverse; in such a case slight sweating may be induced. If the complexion is a uniform bright red, yang qi is depressed in the exterior, and it should be resolved and fumigated. If the incomplete sweating is not a slight matter, the yang qi is depressed and cannot pass out; one should sweat but does not, and the person is vexed and agitated, does not know where the pain is—now in the abdomen, now in the limbs—and it cannot be found by pressing; the person is short of breath and only sits. This is because the sweating was not thorough; further sweating will cure it. How does one know the sweating was not thorough? Because the pulse is rough.

## Notes

The same content as Clause 48 of the middle taiyang chapter, where Cheng Wuji's commentary is found. The chapter "Distinguishing Pulse, Signs, and Treatment of Diseases After Sweating" regathers clauses on conditions following sweating from the three yin and three yang chapters, and most of the following paragraphs overlap with those clauses.
