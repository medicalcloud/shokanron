# 傷寒論十巻目録・傷寒論薬方目録 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Table of Contents of the *Shanghan lun* in Ten Scrolls; Table of Contents of Medicinal Formulas of the *Shanghan lun*

(The lists of chapter and formula names are left as in the original and not translated. Below are translations of the explanatory sentences within the lists.)

"此下二十五方……": The following twenty-five formulas, although they appear under the corresponding patterns, are mostly written in the form of modifications and seem not to be given in full detail; therefore the formulas are listed again in the final scroll.

"已上十巻内計方一百一十二道": In all, within the above ten scrolls there are 112 formulas.

"此経方剤……": The formulas of this classic all follow the ancient measures of zi, zhu, fen, and liang, which differ from today's. For example, "fuju" (chewing) is today's cutting to the size of hemp seeds or beans. "One sheng" is today's large white cup. As for "zhu," six zhu make one fen, that is, two and a half qian; twenty-four zhu make one liang. "Three liang" is today's one liang. "Two liang" is … [breaks off here]

## Notes

The table of contents and table of formulas of Cheng Wuji's annotated edition. Since the lists of chapter and formula names are enumerations of proper names, the translation leaves them in the original and translates only the explanatory sentences.

- The annotated edition consists of ten scrolls, and the chapters in each scroll are indicated; the structure is the same as the twenty-two chapters of the Song edition.
- The formula table records the number of formulas per scroll, totaling 112. Twenty-five formulas written as modifications are relisted together in the final scroll.
- The final explanatory sentence describes the conversion of ancient and modern weights and measures, a document showing Song-to-Ming ideas of conversion such as "three liang is today's one liang." Because the next leaf of the base is missing, it breaks off at "云二両即."
