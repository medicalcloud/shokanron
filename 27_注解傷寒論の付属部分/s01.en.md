# 図解運気図 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Illustrated Diagrams of the Circulation of Qi

The classic says: "The workings of prevalence and retaliation of the qi of heaven and earth do not take visible form in the signs," and the pulse-diagnosis method says: "The changes of heaven and earth cannot be diagnosed by the pulse." This is what is meant. [The pulse] follows where the qi is, expected on the left or right. Following the qi brings harmony; going against it brings disease. One whose positions are interchanged falls ill; one who fails to keep one's position is in danger; one whose cun and chi are reversed dies; one whose yin and yang cross dies. The classic says: "Crossing of yin and yang means that in a year when yang should be on the left it is instead on the right, and when yin should be on the right it is instead on the left. One whose left and right cross dies. If only one side is so and it is not a crossing, it is called non-correspondence." Only the eight years *yin, shen, si, hai, chen, xu, chou, wei* have this correspondence. "Reversal of cun and chi is fatal" means that in a year when yin should be at the cun it is instead seen at the chi, and when yang should be at the chi it is instead seen at the cun. One whose cun and chi are reversed dies; if only one is so and it is not a reversed appearance, it is called non-correspondence. Only the four years *zi, wu, mao, you* correspond to this. Now, following the orthodox *Suwen*, I draw the diagrams directly, and as for the pulse method, first fix the year to know its qi, observe what appears in correspondence on left and right, and only then speak of life and death. In general, under the two governances, southern and northern, of the three yin in heavenly control and earthly spring above and below, when the cun and chi of the two hands, right or left, do not correspond, these are all pulses that sink downward—sunken when the hand is turned up, and when the hand is turned down the sunken becomes floating, fine, or large. Not understanding this method is like crossing a deep abyss or sea asking for the ford—how is that not foolish? One may toil until white-haired without understanding clearly—how much less in a mere ten days or a month. I too have included the methods of placement: adding and overlaying the five circulations and six qi, the three yin and three yang, root and branch, the southern and northern governances, heavenly control and earthly spring, and governed diseases, I have readily made diagrams so that their meaning is easy to grasp. How then could one fail to reach the intention of the sages?

The five circulations and six qi governing disease, and yin and yang, vacuity and repletion, do not go beyond these diagrams. The classic says: "Above is heaven; below is earth. 'Circling heaven' means heaven circles." Regarding the positions of the five phases: heaven hangs down the six qi; earth spreads the five phases. Heaven follows earth and turns leftward; earth receives heaven and revolves eastward. After the wood circulation, heaven's qi always has a surplus; the surplus qi is not added to sovereign fire but steps back one step and is overlaid on ministerial fire. Thus every five years it has already retreated one position and moved to the right. Therefore it is said: "Circling heaven left and right, with surplus it meets again." *Hui* means encounter. The way of heaven and earth always completes in five years; by the surplus qi it shifts and overlays, meets again with the seats of the five phases, and forms the law of the year. "Circling heaven" means heaven circling the positions of earth, not the six qi of the circling heaven. The classic, on the method of overlaying, says: "First fix the year to know its qi; observe what appears in correspondence on left and right; and only then speak of life and death."

[This text is difficult, and the translation is tentative. In particular, phrases such as "右曰随其気所在" and "区区白首不能暁明也況因旬月邪僕亦留入式之法" are hard to construe.]

## Notes

The explanatory text of the "Illustrated Diagrams of the Circulation of Qi" placed at the beginning of Cheng Wuji's annotated edition (the edition collated by Wang Jichuan). It sets out the theory of the five circulations and six qi based on the seven chapters on circulation of qi in the *Suwen* (the idea that the arrangement of the qi of heaven and earth is determined by the year's stem and branch and manifests in the pulse and disease), and has no direct relation to the text of the *Shanghan lun*.

- The diagrams themselves are not in the Kanripo text, so only the explanatory text before and after them appears here.
- "Southern and northern governance," "reversal of chi and cun," and "crossing of yin and yang" are terms from the *Suwen* chapters such as "Great Treatise on the Essentials of Supreme Truth" and "Great Treatise on the Movement of the Five Circulations."
- It is regarded as a document showing the background of the period from the Song onward, when circulation-of-qi theory was widely adopted in medicine.
