# §4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the disease has various repletions on the chest (one version writes "cold"), with depressed pain in the chest, inability to eat, a desire to have someone press on it yet, on the contrary, drool and spittle, diarrhea more than ten times a day, the pulse, on the contrary, slow, and the cun kou pulse slightly slippery: this may be treated by vomiting; with vomiting, the diarrhea stops.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

Various repletions on the chest are phlegm repletion, depressed heat, or bound cold. The chest is depressed and painful and one cannot eat. Wanting someone to press it yet, on the contrary, having drool and spittle [means this]: if the evil were below, pressing it would make the qi descend and there would be no drool or spittle; one who on pressing, on the contrary, has drool and spittle—one knows the evil is in the chest. The classic says: "Diarrhea with a slow, slippery pulse is internal repletion." Now there is diarrhea more than ten times a day, with the pulse, on the contrary, slow and the cun kou pulse slightly slippery; this is repletion above, so it may be treated by vomiting. The *Yuhan* says: "When exuberance above does not cease, induce vomiting to seize it; abiding food in the upper stomach duct should be vomited; abiding food in the middle or lower stomach duct should be purged." Abiding food in the upper stomach duct should be vomited. The *Neijing* says: "What is high, take it up and out; what is low, draw it down and exhaust it."

## Notes

The clause states that even with diarrhea, if there is replete evil on the chest, removing the upper repletion by vomiting also stops the diarrhea. It is not found in the three yin and three yang chapters. Cheng Wuji cites the *Suwen* "Great Treatise on Yin and Yang Correspondences": "What is high, take it up and out."
