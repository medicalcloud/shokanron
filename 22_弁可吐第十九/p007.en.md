# §7 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the disease has counterflow cold of the hands and feet and the pulse suddenly becomes bound, it is because guest qi is in the chest. When there are fullness and vexation below the heart and a desire to eat but inability to eat, the disease is in the chest, and it should be treated by vomiting.

## Translation of Cheng Wuji's commentary

This is the same as the Guadi san pattern in the jueyin section of the sixth scroll. There it says "the pulse is suddenly tight"; here it says "the pulse suddenly becomes bound"—only this differs. Tight is internal repletion; suddenly tight means the repletion is not yet deep and the evil is in the chest. Bound is bound repletion; suddenly bound means the binding is not yet deep and the evil is in the chest. Therefore the pattern and treatment are the same.

## Notes

The same content as Clause 355 of the jueyin chapter (the Guadi san clause), differing only in giving the pulse as "suddenly bound" rather than "suddenly tight." Cheng Wuji's commentary explains this difference. It is the last paragraph of the chapter "May Induce Vomiting."
